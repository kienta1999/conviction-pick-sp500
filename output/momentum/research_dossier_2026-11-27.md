**Backlog / order book — not applicable.** Railroads don't carry a backlog; freight is booked days/weeks ahead. Closest proxy: Q3 2026 operating revenue \$7.2B (+15% YoY); freight revenue ex-fuel surcharge grew 6% to a record \$5.6B; 6% volume growth. Merger-related: 500+ customer support letters to the STB representing >30% of UNP volume; ~30 customers actively assisting integration planning.

**Category position — effectively co-#1 in US freight rail.** UP 2025 operating revenue ~\$24.5B vs BNSF ~\$23.4B — UP leads on revenue, BNSF leads on volume/ton-miles (BNSF holds 60% of the intermodal market and 53% where the two compete head-to-head, per UP's own merger-facts page). UP has the industry's best operating ratio among Class Is (59.8% in 2025; Q3 2026 adjusted OR 58.8%). Post-merger with Norfolk Southern, the combined carrier would control ~50% of US Class I-originated rail shipments.

**Irreplaceability — 9/10.** Rail is a natural monopoly/duopoly: you cannot build a competing transcontinental railroad, and shippers served by a single railroad have no alternative for bulk/intermodal long-haul. Substitution exists at the margin (trucking for short-haul, pipelines for crude) but not for the core network. The merger would deepen this: single-line coast-to-coast service eliminating interchange handoffs. Threat: regulators could impose conditions, and BNSF/CSX remain competitors on overlapping lanes.

**Recent catalysts (last ~3 months).** Q3 2026 (22 Oct 2026): record net income \$2.1B (+16%); adjusted EPS \$3.53 (+15%); revenue \$7.2B (+15%); dividend raised 3% (20th consecutive annual increase). Merger: STB merits review underway — opposition comments due 18 Nov 2026, replies due 16 Feb 2027; CEO Vena said "I am 99.999% sure that it is going to be approved" (Q3 call); expects first merged-company earnings in H2 2027. At RailTrends (23 Nov 2026) the two CEOs cited 2,000+ stakeholder support letters and ~\$2B planned integration investment. Capex raised to \$3.5B for 2026. FY2026 outlook reaffirmed: high-single-digit reported EPS growth vs \$11.98 in 2025.

**Risks.** Merger is the whole upside and it is contested: 8 senators (4R/4D) urged the STB to reject it on 18 Nov 2026; Marathon Petroleum also urged rejection; BNSF is actively opposing; STB decision not expected until 2027. A block or heavy conditions would deflate the premium. Diesel guided at \$4.85/gal for Q4 — a bigger YoY drag than Q3's 140bp OR hit. Coal and automotive volumes soft; intermodal strength may not persist if consumer demand cools. CFO Jennifer Hamann retiring early 2027. At 20.7x forward PE, the merger premium is arguably already in the price.

**Shortage score: 5/10. Verdict:** no shortage — the bet is a regulatory one (STB approval of the first transcontinental railroad) plus best-in-class operating leverage; explosive only if the merger clears cleanly in 2027.

## 9. EMR — Emerson Electric (\$157.23; rev +4.5% TTM; fwd PE 19.6; next earnings 2027-02-02)

**Shortage thesis — a genuine demand surge in automation for power, LNG, and semiconductors that supply is struggling to meet.** Q4 FY2026 (reported 4 Nov 2026): underlying orders +14% (Software & Systems +18%, Intelligent Devices +13%); Test & Measurement orders +14%, including semiconductor orders +59%; Middle East orders +42%, North America +15%. Growth verticals (power, LNG, semis, life sciences) grew 21% in FY2026 and are 27% of sales; Q4 sales in those verticals +25%, led by LNG +68%, semiconductors +43%, power +24%. Emerson's ~\$2.5B power business grew 25%, its control-systems portion ~45%. CEO: power activity spreading "from fleet modernization to brownfield and greenfield projects." This is AI-infrastructure-driven capex (data-center power, LNG export build-out, fab construction) hitting a long-cycle automation supplier — the closest thing to a structural shortage in this batch.

**Backlog / order book — re-verified fresh from primary sources (Q4 FY2026 earnings, 4 Nov 2026): backlog \$8.2B, up 11% YoY (excluding AspenTech), book-to-bill 1.01.** New detail: the share of backlog expected to convert within 12 months fell to 70% from a longstanding ~75% — the book is lengthening into longer-cycle projects. At ~\$19B annual sales, \$8.2B backlog is roughly 5 months of revenue — but the funnel and lengthening cycle matter more than the absolute number.

**Category position — leader in its niches, not #1 overall.** Industrial automation is fragmented: Siemens leads global sales, followed by Schneider, ABB, Rockwell, and Emerson. But EMR is a leader in process automation niches: in distributed control systems (DCS), Emerson holds ~6.7% overall but ~29% of the oil & gas DCS niche (Verified Market Research, Feb 2026); in process automation software it is described as "the dominant force... specifically within the chemical and life sciences verticals." Its DeltaV platform is the standard in process industries.

**Irreplaceability — 7/10.** A DCS is not a commodity: ripping out DeltaV from a running refinery, LNG train, or fab means re-validating safety systems, retraining operators, and risking downtime — switching costs are enormous and measured in years. Substitutes exist (Honeywell Experion, ABB, Siemens PCS, Yokogawa) and open-architecture / software-defined trends are the long-term threat, but Emerson's installed base plus AspenTech software lock-in (annual contract value \$1.7B, +10.1%) is a real moat.

**Recent catalysts (last ~3 months).** Q4 FY2026 (4 Nov 2026): adjusted EPS \$1.86 (+15%), beat \$1.85 consensus; revenue \$5.20B (+7%), beat \$5.12B; adjusted segment EBITA margin 29.1% (+160bp); FY2026 adjusted EPS \$6.59 (+10%, sixth straight year of double-digit growth); FCF \$3.5B (+9%). FY2027 guidance: ~5% sales growth, adjusted EPS ~\$7.25 (+10%), segment EBITA margin ~29%, FCF \$3.8–3.9B; ~\$3.8B shareholder returns planned (\$2.5B buybacks + \$1.3B dividends). Dividend raised 7% to \$0.595/quarter. \$1B buybacks completed in FY2026 (7.2M shares retired). Shares traded near the 52-week high (~\$168.80) after the print vs \$157.23 close on 25 Nov — the stock has pulled back ~7% from the post-earnings high into this screen.

**Risks.** FY2027 guidance (+5% sales) is modest vs the +14% order growth — orders may be long-cycle and slow to convert; the backlog's 12-month conversion rate already slipped to 70%. Power/LNG/semi capex is cyclical; a data-center or LNG FID slowdown would hit the funnel (\$13B is pipeline, not orders). Broad automation market is fragmented and competitive; Honeywell is now a pure-play automation rival (~\$20B revenue) and Siemens/ABB are larger. Valuation 19.6x forward is not cheap for 5% guided growth; the "explosive" case needs orders to keep compounding at double digits.

**Shortage score: 8/10. Verdict:** the only name in the batch with a quantified, growing backlog (\$8.2B, +11%) riding a genuine AI-power/LNG automation surge — the strongest momentum-shortage fit, with the caveat that FY2027 guided growth (+5%) lags the order boom.

## 10. V — Visa (\$424.39; rev +12.2% TTM; fwd PE 24.8; next earnings 2027-01-26)

**Shortage thesis — fails on its own terms.** Visa sells network capacity that is effectively infinite — it processed 74.2 billion transactions in fiscal Q4 2026, up 10% YoY, with no capacity constraint cited anywhere. Global payments volume +9% in constant dollars, cross-border volume ex-intra-Europe +14%, US payments volume +10% (Visa Q4 FY2026 earnings release, 27 Oct 2026). Value-added services grew 31% in constant dollars to \$4 billion in the quarter and 30% to \$14 billion for FY2026 — a product-mix shift, not a scarce good. No sold-out capacity, no extending lead time, no price increase forced by scarcity.

**Backlog / order book — not applicable.** Visa has no order book. Closest forward indicator: roughly 25% of Visa's payments volume is up for renewal in fiscal 2027. That is double-edged — renewal is an opportunity to attach more value-added services, but also when client incentives spike: client incentives surged 21% to \$5.1 billion in Q4 FY2026, outpacing 13% net revenue growth. No contracted future revenue number disclosed.

**Category position — clear #1.** Visa held 62.27% of combined Visa + Mastercard purchase volume worldwide in the twelve months to 30 Jun 2026 (\$15.34 trillion vs Mastercard's \$9.29 trillion), and 70.96% in the US in calendar Q2 2026 (Axis Intelligence Research, updated 27 Oct 2026). On the broader Nilson measure, Visa holds ~52% of US card purchase volume vs Mastercard ~25% and Amex ~19.5%.

**Irreplaceability — 8/10.** The moat is the two-sided network: ~4.5 billion cards in force, acceptance in 200+ countries, VisaNet's authorization/clearing/settlement with fraud and chargeback infrastructure no single merchant or bank can replicate cheaply. But the bypass threat is concrete and live: (a) stablecoins — Visa itself reports stablecoin settlement at a \$25 billion+ annualized run rate with 225+ linked card programs; (b) real-time domestic rails and account-to-account payments that bypass card networks entirely; (c) Big Tech wallets (Apple Pay, Google Pay) that sit between Visa and the consumer. Visa's answer is to integrate rather than fight — stablecoin settlement pilots, an OpenAI agentic-commerce partnership — which preserves relevance but concedes that the toll is contestable at the margin. No single customer can replace Visa; the system as a whole slowly can.

**Recent catalysts (last ~3 months).** Q4 FY2026 beat, 27 Oct 2026: net revenue \$12.1 billion (+13%), non-GAAP EPS \$3.48 (+17%, vs \$3.44 expected); FY2026 net revenue \$45.9 billion (+15%), described by management as the strongest growth in 15+ years ex-pandemic/acquisitions. Dividend raised 16% to \$0.780/share quarterly; \$5.2 billion returned via buybacks + dividends in the quarter. FY2027 guidance: adjusted net revenue and EPS growth both low double digits to low teens. Value-added services guided to low-to-mid-20% growth in FY2027.

**Risks.** DOJ antitrust suit (filed 24 Sep 2024, SDNY 24-cv-7214) alleging Visa monopolized US debit network services; motion to dismiss denied 23 Jun 2025 — case proceeds. Credit Card Competition Act and Regulation II / Illinois interchange restrictions could force routing to rival networks and cap fees. The FY2027 renewal cycle (25% of volume) pressures margins via client incentives, already growing faster than revenue. Stablecoin / real-time-rail disintermediation of the highest-margin cross-border flows over time. Valuation: fwd PE 24.8 with low-double-digit growth leaves little room for a miss.

**Shortage score: 2/10. Verdict:** a durable toll-road compounder, not a shortage bet — steady 9–13% volume growth with no scarcity premium to re-rate it explosively.

## 11. ABNB — Airbnb (\$172.15; rev +14.9% TTM; fwd PE 27.1; next earnings 2027-02-11)

**Shortage thesis — a genuine but soft supply/demand tension, not a hard shortage.** US short-term-rental supply growth has stalled: AirDNA reported US listings grew just 1.6% YoY in 2026, "the slowest pace since the 2021 reopening", with demand still growing 1.4–2.1% and occupancy floored near 57% for two years (VRMA 2026 / AirDNA, Nov 2026). Against that flat supply, Airbnb's Q3 2026 demand surged: Nights and Seats Booked +16% to 155 million, accelerating from +10% in Q2, with lead times extending "across major travel corridors" (earnings call transcript, 5 Nov 2026). ADR rose 4% to \$179. But supply is host-driven and can respond (hosts list in weeks), and regulation caps (Paris/London 90–120 nights/year) constrain supply by law rather than by Airbnb's capacity.

**Backlog / order book — no backlog; unearned fees are the proxy.** Unearned fees — service fees guests have already paid for trips not yet taken — were \$1,536 million at 30 Sep 2026, down 16% YoY even as bookings grew 21%, explained by Reserve Now, Pay Later shifting payment timing, not by weak forward demand. Forward guidance: Q4 2026 revenue guided to \$3.22–3.28 billion (+16–18%), GBV growth mid-teens, Nights and Seats growth at least low double digits; full-year 2026 revenue growth raised to the high teens with adjusted EBITDA margin ~36%.

**Category position — clear #1 in short-term rentals, with share still rising.** ~52% of US nights booked vs Vrbo 14% and direct/other channels 33% (AirDNA via VRMA 2026). In professionally managed US rentals, Airbnb hit 51% of reservations and 43% of revenue in Q2 2026, up from 47%/36% a year earlier. Globally 9 million+ listings in 220+ countries vs Vrbo's ~2 million in 190 countries, ~44% of global STR market share (Lodgify, Nov 2026).

**Irreplaceability — 6/10.** The moat is demand aggregation: 66% of nights are now booked through the app (up from 62%), app nights +29% YoY, first-time bookers +13% (fastest since early 2022). Hosts cannot replicate that demand pool themselves — but they can and do multi-home: only 28% of listings worldwide are on two or more channels, yet Vrbo, Booking.com and direct bookings remain live alternatives, and Airbnb itself is piloting direct-booking links at a reduced 6–10% host fee (vs standard 15.5%), effectively conceding that hosts want to bypass it. Guests can substitute hotels trivially — and Airbnb's own hotel push (thousands of boutique hotels added, hotel nights growing ~3x faster than home nights) shows it knows the boundary is porous.

**Recent catalysts (last ~3 months).** Q3 2026 blowout, 5 Nov 2026: revenue \$4.87 billion (+19%, fastest in 3+ years, vs \$4.75 billion expected), EPS \$3.16 (+43%, vs \$2.85 expected), net income \$1.9 billion (39% margin), GBV \$27.7 billion (+21%), adjusted EBITDA \$2.5 billion (+22%, 52% margin). Shares rose ~12% on the print. Guidance raised: full-year revenue growth to high teens, adjusted EBITDA margin to ~36%. \$12 billion new buyback authorization (on top of \$2.7 billion remaining). Seventeen desks raised targets on 6 Nov 2026 alone (Citi \$193→\$227, Mizuho \$200→\$245 and named top pick, Evercore \$200→\$230, Wedbush \$200→\$237); Bernstein raised to \$234 on ~20 Nov 2026. AI: support assistant now resolves ~40% of customer issues without a human; AI search launched in the US 30 Sep 2026; a full AI travel agent is planned for H1 2027. Hotels expansion accelerating; Reserve Now, Pay Later now covers ~40% of GBV.

**Risks.** The Q3 beat was flattered: Reserve Now, Pay Later added a 3-point net lift to nights; excluding it, bookings grew ~13%, not 16% — and cancellation rates rose to 19% from 16% as pay-later adoption widened. The benefit is expected to peak in Q3 and fade in Q4. New businesses (Experiences, Services, hotels) are "unlikely to be material over the next 3 to 5 years." Regulation: city-level caps and registration regimes can remove supply overnight; US international inbound demand is soft (AirDNA: international traveller demand 12% below last spring, Canada −32% vs 2024). Take rate fell to 17.6% in Q3; stock-based compensation \$462 million in one quarter; FCF margin slipped to 29% from 33%. Valuation after the run: shares +53.6% YTD through 16 Nov 2026; ~43x trailing P/E vs a ~5-year average near 26x.

**Shortage score: 5/10. Verdict:** the strongest demand-acceleration print in the batch, with real supply tightness behind it — but a third of the bookings beat was pay-later timing, so the explosive case needs Q4 to prove the acceleration is organic.

## 13. CBOE — Cboe Global Markets (\$351.80; rev +13.4% TTM; fwd PE 22.7; next earnings 2027-02-05)

**Shortage thesis — the closest thing in the batch to a true shortage: demand for SPX options is surging and only one venue on earth can legally supply them.** Index options ADV rose 24% YoY to 6.1 million contracts in Q3 2026, with 0DTE SPX options at a record 65% of overall SPX activity; SPX 0DTE ADV hit a record 3.2 million contracts, +34% YoY (Q3 2026 earnings call, 30 Oct 2026). Retail SPX 0DTE volume jumped 46% YoY (vs +20% institutional) and retail is now an estimated 58% of SPX 0DTE volume, up from 53% a year ago. SPX options set a record 970.6 million contracts in 2025, ADV 3.9 million (+25%, fourth straight record year) (Cboe/S&P DJI release, 29 Sep 2026). XSP volume more than doubled YoY after the June 2026 repeal of the pattern-day-trader rule; Mini-SPX averaged 251,000 contracts/day; overnight SPX volume +117%. Management also noted the SPX complex "has not undergone a meaningful pricing adjustment for several years" — pricing power is unexercised headroom.

**Backlog / order book — not applicable; volume is the backlog, and it is reported daily.** The forward book is the license itself: on 28–29 Sep 2026 Cboe and S&P Dow Jones Indices signed a 25-year extension of the exclusive licensing agreement, keeping Cboe as the sole venue for SPX options (plus Mini-SPX, VIX and related S&P options) through 2051, replacing rights that previously ran only to 2032 (company press release, 29 Sep 2026; 8-K). Royalty terms are unchanged for 2026, with new per-contract fees from 1 Jan 2027, described by management as a de minimis impact on 2027 growth. The 25-year runway is the durable forward revenue visibility no other name in this batch can claim.

**Category position — #1 where it counts.** Cboe operates the largest options exchange in the US and held 30.5% combined US options market share in Q3 2026 — #1 among exchange operators, ahead of Nasdaq and NYSE/ICE venues. In US equities it is #3 (on-exchange share 8.9%, down from 9.8%; off-exchange 21.3%, up from 17.9%). But the strategically decisive position is absolute: 100% of SPX options and VIX options trade only on Cboe. No #2 exists for SPX.

**Irreplaceability — 10/10.** The moat is a signed contract, not a network effect that can erode: exclusive SPX/VIX rights through 31 Dec 2051, a 40+ year partnership dating to the 1983 SPX launch. No customer can build its own SPX options market — the index is S&P DJI's IP and the license is exclusive. Substitutes exist at the edges (SPY/QQQ ETF options, Nasdaq-100 options, OTC variance swaps, futures), but none replicates SPX's cash-settled, European-style, Section 1256 tax treatment and deep 0DTE liquidity. The live bypass threat is regulatory rather than commercial: Cboe and allies sued the CFTC over its approval of perpetual futures on single stocks; Coinbase, Kalshi and Kraken's parent are backing CFTC oversight. Nothing can route around SPX itself before 2051.

**Recent catalysts (last ~3 months).** Q3 2026 beat, 30 Oct 2026: net revenue \$712.2 million (+18%), adjusted diluted EPS \$3.63 (+36%, vs \$3.43 expected), GAAP EPS a record \$4.08 (helped by the Cboe Australia sale gain). KPI binary options (prediction-market contracts on reported corporate metrics — EPS, revenue, Nvidia data-centre revenue, Tesla Model 3/Y production, 100+ indicators across 23 companies) received SEC approval mid-October, listed 9 Nov 2026, with Robinhood as first retail distributor (fees waived through year-end), Interactive Brokers and Schwab cited as incoming; mini-Russell 2000 binaries also launched 9 Nov. Capital return: buyback authorization increased \$550 million on 22 Oct 2026 (capacity ~\$1 billion); plan to repay \$650 million of senior notes in Q1 2027; \$2.7 billion adjusted cash, 0.7x leverage. Volatility tailwind: shares rose 10.03% in the first 12 days of November to \$369.19 as the VIX spiked above 28. Cboe Clear Europe cleared 1.5 billion+ client equity trades in 2026 to date, beating its 2022 full-year record of 1.49 billion.

A volatility collapse, a retail drawdown, or a regulatory clampdown on 0DTE would hit the growth engine directly. The 2027 royalty reset (new per-contract S&P DJI fees from 1 Jan 2027) is described as de minimis, but the exact economics are not detailed until February. Prediction-market competition: rival exchanges are already filing to list KPI binaries; the SEC approval order notes standardized terms, so Cboe's first-mover edge may compress to distribution rather than product. Multi-listed options share is under pressure (combined options share 30.5% vs 30.9% YoY; US equities on-exchange share fell to 8.9%); the growth is concentrated in the proprietary index complex, which is also the concentration risk. Fwd PE 22.7 is the cheapest in the batch, but the stock has already run hard in November (VIX spike) — entry after a vol spike is the wrong side of the trade if vol mean-reverts.

**Shortage score: 7/10. Verdict:** the purest shortage-monopoly in the batch — record 0DTE demand for a product only Cboe may sell, now contracted to 2051 — but the engine runs on retail speculation and volatility, so size it as a momentum trade, not a buy-and-forget compounder.
