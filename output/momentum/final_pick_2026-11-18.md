## One-paragraph thesis

Amphenol is the only name in a thin field that satisfies all three legs of the momentum doctrine at once: the world's largest interconnect maker, riding a genuine structural shortage in high-speed AI interconnect, with a product its hyperscaler customers cannot in-source. Record Q3 orders of $12.17B (+99% YoY, book-to-bill 1.19) show demand still arriving faster than Amphenol can ship, AI is now ~36% of total company revenue, and the CommScope CCS acquisition makes it architecture-neutral — it wins whether the rack goes copper or optical. The stock is up only +15.0% over 12 months and sits ~16% off its early-October high after a post-earnings pullback, so the market is still pricing a cyclical connector maker, not the number-one supplier into a multi-year AI interconnect buildout.

## The shortage + backlog evidence

- Record orders of $12.17B in Q3 2026, +99% YoY and +13% sequentially — a record for the fourth straight quarter — book-to-bill 1.19:1, positive in nearly every end market, with $5.7B added to backlog year-to-date (Amphenol Q3 2026 release/call, Oct 28, 2026; verified Phase 3.5).
- IT datacom grew 30% sequentially in Q3 (twice the guided pace) and is now 48% of the company; AI is ~three-quarters of IT datacom sales, ~36% of total company revenue — disclosed for the first time. IT datacom +117% YoY / +77% organic.
- Connector lead times hit record highs in 2025 (Supplyframe Commodity IQ Lead Time Index 166.6 in April 2025, highest on record); hyperscale buildout continues at 130–140 new facilities/year.
- Q3 2026 was the first $10B quarter: sales $10.23B (+65% YoY, +37% organic), adjusted EPS $0.80 (+74%), beating the July guide ceiling by the widest dollar margin of the cycle. Guidance raised: Q4 sales $10.3–10.5B, adjusted EPS $0.81–0.83; FY2026 sales $36.9–37.1B, adjusted EPS $2.82–2.84.
- CommScope CCS 2026 outlook raised again to $5.1B sales (from $4.1B at close), $0.18 EPS accretion, low-20s operating margin; CCS IT datacom now ~half its sales.
- Capital return: dividend +20% to $0.15/share (post-split) and a new 3-year $4B buyback authorised alongside Q3. Goldman Sachs added APH to its US Conviction List (Nov 2, 2026).

## Why it's the category leader

Amphenol is the world's largest interconnect manufacturer: >31% of North America circular connectors, >12.5% of fiber-optic connectors, >36% of military connectors (2025, GMI), ~12.4% global cable-assembly share. Next-largest rivals TE Connectivity and Molex are both smaller in the high-speed datacom niche, and neither matches Amphenol's breadth across copper + optical + power + cable plant. The $10.5B CommScope CCS acquisition (closed Jan 2026) added the fiber-optic capability Amphenol previously lacked.

## Why it's irreplaceable

Hyperscalers do NOT make their own connectors: low-margin, high-precision, high-volume manufacturing with no strategic reason to insource, and the connector is a small share of system cost. The moat is qualification/design-win lock-in — interconnects are designed into specific server/GPU platforms and re-qualified per generation — plus manufacturing scale and precision (high-speed copper at 224G) that few firms have, plus breadth no single rival matches. The substitute threat is architectural (copper vs optical), not vendor-level — and Amphenol now sells both, which is precisely the point of the CommScope deal. The real long-term threat is a shift to co-packaged optics / silicon photonics reducing discrete interconnect content per rack — a multi-year risk, not a near-term one. Irreplaceability 8/10; disintermediation risk low, per all four lenses.
