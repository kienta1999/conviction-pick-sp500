## Why it's the category leader

Essentially 100% of US index options trade on Cboe (index-options market share ~98.3–98.5% through 2026, per Cboe monthly volume statistics updated 4 Nov 2026); total options share ~30%, US equities exchange share ~9%. In fungible multi-listed options it is one of several venues at ~23% — but in its proprietary niche it is the only venue. Q3 2026: net revenue \$712.2m (+18% YoY, beat ~\$700m consensus), adjusted diluted EPS \$3.63 (+36%, beat \$3.43 consensus by 5.8%), GAAP EPS \$4.08 (+43%, a record); DataVantage net revenue \$179.1m (+13%, record); Global FX +23%.

## Why it's irreplaceable

The moat is contractual exclusivity, not technology: the 25-year extension of the exclusive S&P Dow Jones Indices licence through 2051 (announced 29–30 Sep 2026 — corrected per Phase 3.5 verification; the dossier first said 30 Oct) means no broker, exchange or hyperscaler can legally list SPX options anywhere else. The only theoretical bypass is a substitute index (Nasdaq-100 options, CME's S&P futures/options complex), but SPX is the benchmark for US equity hedging and 0DTE liquidity is self-reinforcing — the deepest pool attracts the next trade. Both B and C rated disintermediation risk low. The honest caveat: the licence is a contract, not a law of physics; S&P raised royalty terms (management called the 2027 impact "de minimis"); and event contracts face an unsettled SEC/CFTC turf question.
