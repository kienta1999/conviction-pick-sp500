## Single-pick ballot — Panelist A (Supply-chain analyst)

- Top pick: APH
- Runner-up: MU
- Thesis (3-5 sentences): APH is the cleanest booked shortage in the dossier: demand is not a forecast, it is orders already placed at a record pace, in a component category where lead times are stretching to about 50 weeks and hyperscalers are securing capacity years ahead. Unlike the memory names, APH's scarcity is diversified across end markets and confirmed by an actual Q3 2026 beat-and-raise on 2026-10-28, not a pending print. Its AI content per system is also rising structurally, with TPU backplane connector content expected to more than double versus prior generations. That combination — booked demand, lengthening lead times, and rising content per system — gives APH explosive upside without betting on a single commodity price staying at a record.
- Key shortage/backlog evidence: Q3 2026 orders of \$12.17B, up 99% year over year, at a 1.19x book-to-bill against \$10.23B of sales — a record bookings quarter, with AI at about three-quarters of IT Datacom revenue.
- Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk: medium — and why: hyperscalers do not generally manufacture connectors themselves, and the qualified supplier base for high-speed, backplane and fiber interconnect at AI scale is tiny, which protects APH. The risk is real but bounded: customers dual-source aggressively between APH, TE Connectivity, Molex and others, and Google's TPU backplane co-design shows customers own the architecture, so APH can lose share at a renewal even if it cannot be fully bypassed.
- 12-18 month return scenario: rough base / bull case: base about +25% to +35%, roughly in line with the dossier's +27.9% analyst upside, if the record order book converts and fiber lead times stay extended; bull about +50% or more if TPU9i content and multi-year hyperscaler LTAs keep bookings above sales through 2027.
- Top risk: Derived demand — if hyperscaler capex pauses or neocloud funding tightens, the longer-dated order book is the first thing the market questions, and Q3 growth already relied heavily on acquisitions.
