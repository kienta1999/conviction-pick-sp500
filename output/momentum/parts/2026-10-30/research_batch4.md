## 3. FCX — Freeport-McMoRan (copper mining)

### 1. Shortage thesis
Yes — the strongest commodity-level shortage thesis in the batch, with a company-specific twist. Structural drivers: (a) IEA Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2026: mined copper ~25% short of primary supply requirements in 2035 under stated policies (down from ~30% in the prior outlook); BNEF Transition Metals Outlook 2026 (2026-10-20): $332bn investment gap, annual shortfall up to 20 Mt by 2050 (~44% of ~46 Mt demand), average new-mine development 17 years. (b) Concentrate scarcity here and now: annual benchmark treatment/refining charges (TC/RCs) fell to zero in 2026, spot TC/RCs negative in 2025 (~−$60/t), signalling acute concentrate shortage; copper hit a COMEX record $6.83/lb on 2026-09-22 (Silmaril/MDPI summaries, Sep–Oct 2026). Offsetting nuance: the ICSG near-term refined-metal balance is contested — one summary cites a 150 kt deficit for 2026 (reversing a prior surplus call), another cites a 96 kt surplus for 2026 and 377 kt surplus for 2027 as idled supply returns. The honest framing is: concentrate/mined-copper scarcity now, refined-metal balance uncertain for 2027 — the long shortage thesis is structural, the 12-month price thesis is not guaranteed.

### 2. Backlog / order book
**Not applicable in industrial form — miners do not carry an order backlog; not found, and none should be invented.** The forward-visibility equivalents are sales guidance and the production ramp: Q3 2026 sales guidance cut on 2026-10-27 to ~3.0bn lb copper for 2026 (from 3.1bn lb in July) and 3.7bn lb for 2027 (from 3.8bn lb), with 2028 held at 4.1bn lb; Q4 2026 sales guided at 875m lb copper, 300k oz gold, 22m lb molybdenum. ### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk

No copper consumer (wire, grid, data-centre builder) can realistically build its own Grasberg. Substitution threats are real but slow and price-gated: aluminium substitution in some power/transmission uses, thrifting/design efficiency, and recycling (secondary supply is explicitly netted out of the IEA's 25% shortfall measure). The more immediate "disintermediation" is geopolitical/operational: Indonesia (Grasberg licence, smelter obligations, export rules) and Chile/Peru permitting can strand or tax the resource — the Sep 2025 Grasberg mud rush that killed seven workers and shut the district is the live example. ## 4. NUE — Nucor (steel)

### 1. Shortage thesis
Yes, but policy-created as much as geology-created: a tariff-shielded domestic shortage. Drivers: (a) **Section 232 50% tariffs** on steel articles have collapsed finished-steel import share (TIKR, Mar 2026: from ~25% to an estimated ~14% by November, freeing ~4m tons of sheet demand for domestic mills) and lifted domestic prices — Nucor raised spot hot-rolled coil offers eight weeks running to $1,230/short ton by early Oct 2026 (DK Street Journal, 2026-10-05), up >$300 since Oct 2025; Q2 average selling price $1,145/ton (+$71 QoQ). (b) **Demand surge that domestic capacity is straining to meet:** record mill shipments three quarters running (Q3: >7.2m tons), with infrastructure, reshoring, data centres, grid, energy/LNG, shipbuilding and defence all cited on the Q3 call (2026-10-27/28). (c) **Data-centre steel as a new structural leg:** data centres = ~15% of Nucor's total backlog, and management claims Nucor can supply/manufacture ~95% of the steel inside and outside a data centre. (d) **New-capacity absorption:** West Virginia sheet mill first heat + initial coils shipped in Q3; Crawfordsville (Indiana) towers & structures at full production in Q3. This is a demand-plus-protection story, not a raw-material scarcity story — scrap (the EAF feedstock) is not reported as constrained.

### 2. Backlog / order book
- **Steel Products backlogs +~25%** (overall, per Fabricated Construction Products EVP John Hollatz on the Q3 2026 call, 2026-10-27), with pricing improvements extending into 2027.
- **Towers & structures backlog more than doubled since start of 2026**, extending into early 2028 (Q3 call summary, MarketBeat 2026-10-27); Crawfordsville at full production in Q3, plus $165m announced to expand Indiana/Alabama towers capacity ~50%, lifting projected long-term EBITDA contribution to ~$200m.
- **Data centres ~15% of companywide backlog** (CEO Leon Topalian, Q3 call).
- **Steel Mills backlogs entering 2026 were +40% YoY** (TIKR, Mar 2026, citing Q4 2025 call) — older context, not a Q3 figure; the Q3 call did not give a single group-wide dollar backlog, so no group-wide dollar figure is asserted. No backlog horizon beyond towers (early 2028) and steel-products pricing (into 2027) is disclosed.

### 3. Category position
**#1 in the US, by a clear margin.** Nucor is "the nation's largest steel producer" and "the largest producer of steel and scrap metal in North America" (FinancialContent, Jan 2026; Manufacturing Dive, Jul 2026). Market-share sizing (FinancialContent, Jan 2026): Nucor ~18.1%, vs Steel Dynamics ~10.3% and the combined Nippon Steel / U.S. Steel ~9.1% — i.e. Nucor is ~1.8x its nearest rival. It is also the pioneer and scale leader of the EAF mini-mill model (first EAF mini-mill, Darlington SC, 1969), which gives a structural cost and carbon advantage over blast-furnace integrated mills, and the most diverse product portfolio in North America (sheet, plate, bar, structural, joists/deck, tubular, metal buildings, insulated panels, towers).

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk
**Moderate. Irreplaceability score: 5/10.**
Moat = scale + geographic footprint (mills near customers and scrap sources, minimising logistics) + product breadth (the "95% of a data centre" claim) + EAF cost flexibility (scale production up/down with demand, unlike blast furnaces). None of this is a patent or regulatory filing lock-in: steel is a fungible commodity graded to spec, and the tariff shield — not Nucor technology — is what currently keeps imports out. Customers (construction, auto, appliance OEMs) can and do dual-source between Nucor, Steel Dynamics, Cleveland-Cliffs and imports; they cannot "in-source" a steel mill cheaply, but they do not need to — switching mills is a procurement decision, not a re-qualification project. The genuine bypass threat is policy reversal (Section 232 rollback from 50%) or import circumvention (Topalian flagged a "massive surge from South Korea" despite tariffs on the Q3 call; finished imports are down >13% YoY and 24% vs 2024 but rising in recent months). Score reflects commodity fungibility discounted by scale/footprint.

### 6. Risks
- **Cyclicality + tariff-policy beta is the whole thesis in reverse:** If Section 232 is scaled back (a risk flagged as early as Feb 2026 FT reporting, per TIKR) or imports surge around it (South Korea surge already flagged), domestic HRC pricing and the import-share collapse unwind quickly; consensus already models 2026 EPS $18.88 (+137%) flattening to $19.68 (2027) and $18.68 (2028) per DK Street Journal's summary — i.e. the market expects a plateau, not a breakout, after 2026.
- **Valuation already pricing the tariff trade:** Stock +38% YTD at Jul 2026 (TIKR) and +81.8% over the year to early Oct 2026 per DK Street Journal (vs Steel Dynamics +60.7%); screen analyst upside is only +4.8% at $275.29 despite the Q3 beat — the explosive move may already have happened.
- **Input-cost / downstream squeeze:** Steel Products plants ran near 65% utilisation vs 88% in mills (DK Street Journal, Oct 2026) because the rising mill price is an input cost downstream; conversion costs ticked up in Q3 on overtime/energy at high utilisation.
- **Demand concentration in lumpy projects:** Data centres (15% of backlog), border wall (~1,400 miles remaining × ~1,500 tons/mile per Q3 call), infrastructure and LNG are policy/project-dependent; delays hit backlog conversion, not just new orders.
- **Capex overhang:** Even falling capex ($2.5bn in 2026) remains elevated vs the $1.6bn quarterly FCF run-rate if shipments normalise.

### 7. Shortage score and verdict
**Shortage score: 7/10.** Verdict: A tariff-walled domestic-capacity squeeze with record shipments, a +25% downstream backlog and a data-centre leg — real shortage pricing, but the wall is a policy choice, not geology, so the premium can be legislated away as fast as it was created.
