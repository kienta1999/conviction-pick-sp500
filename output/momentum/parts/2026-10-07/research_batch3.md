## KLAC — KLA Corporation (semiconductor process control)

**1. Shortage thesis.** Driver: wafer-fab process-control demand intensity rising at the leading edge, not just capacity adds. Per the FQ4 FY26 call (7/28/2026), KLA raised the 2026 wafer-fab-equipment outlook to the low-$150B range (from just above $140B); HPC, HBM, EUV adoption in DRAM, and hybrid bonding are "driving higher process control intensity" with growth visibility into calendar 2027. Advanced-packaging process-control revenue is expected to approach ~$1.1B in calendar 2026 — "more than a 70% year-over-year increase." CFO Bren Higgins on the call: "strong customer momentum that has accelerated over the past 3 months and is reflected in our system backlog and sales funnel." It is a structural intensity story (each EUV/HBM node needs more inspection steps per wafer) more than a capacity-sold-out story — equipment "sold out" language not found; the closest physical evidence is the 60% backlog jump below.

**2. Backlog.** $12.57B at FY26 end (6/30/2026), up from $7.86B a year earlier — **+60% YoY** (company IR release, 7/28/2026; tradersunion/equityswarm coverage 10/6/2026). Delivery horizon not disclosed in sources found.

**3. Category position.** #1, near-monopoly in most sub-segments: ~73.8% of the global metrology/inspection market, up from ~50% in 2010 (ainvest, 5/2026); ~60% brightfield / >50% darkfield wafer inspection, 45% CD metrology, 40% overlay, **80%+ reticle inspection** (TradingView/GuruFocus 2025). Applied Materials is the #2 (dominant in defect *review* ~90% historically; SEMVision >70% of defect-review per Applied commentary) but "holds a much smaller position overall" in process control. Gartner-based estimate puts KLA at ~62% of leading-edge (3nm/2nm) process-control spend (rocsf.org.tw analysis, 4/2026).

**4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation.** Customers (TSMC, Samsung, Intel) cannot realistically in-source: decades of defect-classification data ("algorithmic moat"), tool-referenced fab infrastructure that requires compatibility, and the physics/optics expertise behind it. Only ~4–5 firms on earth compete meaningfully (KLA, Applied, ASML-metrology-adjacent, Onto Innovation, Hitachi High-Tech). Teradyne is pushing into memory *test* (> $200M/qtr memory revenue) — adjacent segment, not core inspection. **Irreplaceability: 9/10.**

**5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months).** FQ4 FY26 beat (7/28/2026): revenue $3.66B +15.2% YoY, non-GAAP EPS $1.05 +11.7% (beat ~5%), gross margin 62.4% at upper end of guide; guided FQ1 FY27 revenue ~$4.0B (±$200M) and EPS $1.16 — implying continued quarter-to-quarter growth into 2027. WFE market outlook raised. Services revenue +17% YoY (~23–24% of total — recurring floor).

**6. Risks.** China = 28% of 2025 revenue per 10-K — export-control headline risk is the swing factor. Valuation: Zacks downgraded Strong Buy→Hold on 9/30/2026 citing valuation after the run (avg target $222.22 vs ~$196–200 price). FY27 gross-margin guide flat at ~61.5% (expansion stalled). Insider selling ~$112.3M over 12 months. Equipment remains cyclical; a memory or capex pause compresses bookings fast.

**Earnings-quality flag — RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN.** 10-K (6/30/2026, filed ~8/2026): net receivables $2,889.2M vs $2,263.9M at 6/30/2025 — **+27.6%**, vs FY26 revenue growth of +12%. Quarter-sequence shows the spike concentrated in the June quarter ($2,304.5M at 3/31 → $2,889.2M at 6/30). One-line verdict: **mostly benign billing mechanics, worth watching** — receivables are tracking the +60% backlog surge and Q4-heavy system shipments rather than deteriorating collections, but the gap is large enough to monitor DSO on the FQ1 FY27 report; cash+securities ($4.9B) and strong FCF make distress implausible.

**Shortage score: 8/10.** Backlog +60% and rising per-wafer process-control intensity is as close to a structural shortage signal as wafer equipment gets; docked points because it is intensity-driven, not physical capacity-sold-out, and China exposure is real.

**Verdict:** The purest "AI chips need more inspection per wafer" play; near-monopoly economics with a swelling order book — but the run has priced in perfection, and China export policy is the single-point-of-failure.

## APH — Amphenol (interconnect: connectors, cable assemblies, sensors)

**1. Shortage thesis.** Driver: AI-datacenter interconnect — "the central nervous system of AI" (CEO Adam Norwitt). Every accelerator rack is dead without high-speed copper/fiber/power interconnect; customers are ordering 23% more than Amphenol ships. Q2 2026 (reported 7/29/2026): **record orders $10.732B (+94% YoY)**, book-to-bill 1.23. IT datacom (the AI-wiring business) is now 43% of sales, +89% YoY (+63% organic), ~4× its size two years ago. Norwitt at a recent Citi conference noted the company announces none of its customer commitments — including **long-term non-cancelable orders** — i.e., contracted demand the backlog numbers understate. Inference build-out "requires an unprecedented level of interconnect intensity" (management).

**2. Backlog / bookings.** Amphenol does not disclose a dollar backlog; the book-to-bill is the proxy: Q2 2026 orders $10.7B vs sales $8.76B = **1.23:1**, orders +94% YoY. Not found: booking-duration horizon.

**3. Category position.** #1 in the consolidated connector space by market cap (~3× TE Connectivity per ainvest, 7/2026) and the clear AI-interconnect leader: IT datacom is Amphenol's largest end market at 43% of sales. In the broad connector market the top-5 hold ~67% (HTF, 2026): TE Connectivity ~16.7%, Amphenol ~9%, Molex (Koch/private) 7.1% (Bishop, older). Direct AI-datacenter competitor: TE's Digital Data Networks +34% YoY in FQ3 2026 with bookings +70% YTD — growing fast off a smaller base; Marvell is the silicon-side threat (data-center revenue +46% YoY to $2.17B) rather than a cable-assembly competitor.

**4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation.** Hyperscalers cannot realistically in-source: connector/cable manufacturing is high-precision, capital-intensive, qualification-gated across thousands of SKUs, and scale-driven — the exact manufacturing capability hyperscalers don't have. Management explicitly works across GPU, ASIC, and XPU architectures: "all these architectures require interconnects," so architecture churn doesn't strand them. The one structural threat is the **copper→optics shift**: the OCI MSA (Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI, formed 3/12/2026) is standardizing optical scale-up to 3.2 Tb/s, and Broadcom/Marvell co-packaged-optics (CPO) capture value at the silicon-optics interface — but the physical optical cable/assembly layer still must be manufactured and installed, and Amphenol's CommScope acquisition (see below) bought real fiber-optics capability ($1.2B+ Q2 sales, IT datacom ~50% of its 2026 mix, in customer talks on optics-in-scale-up). **Irreplaceability: 7/10** — best-in-class manufacturer, but the technology vector (copper vs. optics) matters more than for any other name in this batch.

**5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months).** Record Q2 2026 (7/29/2026): sales $8.76B +55% (organic +30%), adj EPS $1.35 +67%, adj operating margin record 29.8% (+420 bps). Q3 2026 guide (raised bar): sales $9.3–9.4B (+50–52%), adj EPS $1.40–1.42 (+51–53%). CommScope integration: 2026 sales raised to $4.6B, $0.30 EPS accretion. 2-for-1 stock split completed early September 2026. 18 analysts Moderate Buy, avg target $96.85 (~11% above $87.23 on 10/5/2026, MarketBeat).

**6. Risks.** Valuation is the core risk: ~55× forward earnings at the summer peak (ainvest), still >42× per MarketBeat; debt/equity 133% post-CommScope; insider selling 2.1M shares ($172.3M) over 90 days. A hyperscaler capex deceleration turns the inventory/capacity build into an overhang. Optical transition (CPO) could shift value from cable assemblies toward silicon vendors. Trade ~3% below all-time high — expectations are for near-perfect execution.

**Earnings-quality flags: none.** Clean.

**Shortage score: 9/10.** The strongest demand signal in the batch — +94% orders, 1.23 book-to-bill, long-term non-cancelable commitments, 4× IT-datacom growth in two years. It is a *demand surge*, not a capacity-constrained shortage, so one point off.

**Verdict:** The closest thing to an order-backed (not vibes-backed) AI infrastructure compounder — customers are contractually front-running supply; the only real questions are valuation (42–55× earnings) and the copper-to-optics technology arc, not demand.

## CAT — Caterpillar (heavy equipment; Power & Energy / backup gensets)

**1. Shortage thesis.** Driver: datacenter power-generation equipment — backup gensets and turbines are a genuine physical shortage. Q2 2026 (reported 8/4/2026): **power-generation sales to users +72%** on large generator sets and turbines for data centers; Power & Energy segment sales $8.238B (+17%), power generation $3.098B (+29%), segment profit +30% to $2.027B (per the 10-Q via PR Newswire compilation). Corroborating industry evidence: Cummins' order book for large gensets extends into **2H 2028**, with "demand for large generator sets [having] outpaced manufacturing capacity" — customers taking smaller configurations to get anything at all; Cummins signed a multi-year agreement with a global hyperscaler covering several GW of future backup-generator demand (Cummins Q2 2026, 8/4/2026). An industry bottleneck memo (jameswong2011/investmentvault, GitHub, ~9/2026) puts CAT at ~60% of 2026 genset demand and 18–24-month reciprocating-engine lead times — treat as secondary-source, not primary. CAT is tripling engine capacity vs 2024 and restarting 1.5 GW of 10-MW medium-speed gas recips in Q4 2026.

**2. Backlog.** **$72B at Q2 2026 end, +$9B sequentially, +92% YoY** (August 4, 2026 earnings call). 59% scheduled for delivery within 12 months; Power & Energy customers placing orders **as far out as 2030** (dollarstockclub/earnings-call summary; TheStreet, 9/30/2026). CEO Joe Creed (8/4/2026): had "not seen demand slowing in the data center market."

**3. Category position.** #1 globally in construction/mining/power-generation equipment; ~$377B market cap, and in the datacenter-genset layer the oligopoly is effectively 4 majors (CAT, Cummins, Rolls-Royce Power Systems, INNIO/Waukesha) plus Solar Turbines (a CAT subsidiary) on the turbine side. Cummins is the closest comp: Power Systems record $2.26B (+19% YoY), EBITDA margin 24.5%. CAT's $72B backlog and the reported ~60% share of 2026 genset demand make it the default winner of the backup-power buildout.

**4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation.** Hyperscalers cannot build their own large gensets — capital-intensive manufacturing, EPA Tier-4/emissions certification, and a global dealer service network (CAT just agreed to acquire the Fabick dealer network, ~37 locations, announced 9/29/2026) are not software skills. The only longer-term disintermediation path is design-side: Meta-style "two-nines" reliability architectures that drop N+1 gensets entirely (per the bottleneck memo) — a 2028+ demand-shape risk, not a supply-side threat. **Irreplaceability: 8/10** in the backup-power layer (construction/mining core is more cyclical, but that's not the shortage thesis).

**5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months).** Record quarter: first $20B+ quarter in company history ($20.5B, +24%); adj EPS $8.17 (+73%) vs $6.20 consensus — a massive beat. Full-year 2026 sales guidance raised to mid-to-high-teens growth. BofA maintained Buy, $989 target, citing 283 MW of AI-backed power orders (TheStreet, 9/30/2026). $1B new Sanford, NC plant announced 9/30/2026 (compact track loaders/telehandlers — capacity story). Planned Fabick dealer acquisition (10/2/2026) tightens distribution.

**6. Risks.** Valuation and expectations: 34.5× trailing earnings vs 22.4 for the S&P 500; stock +70% over the past year, pulled back ~8% in a month on state/local regulatory scrutiny of datacenter construction (TX, NY). Construction/mining is still ~1/3 of revenue — cyclicality is dormant, not dead. Tariff headwind $1.3–1.5B. Insider selling noted. If hyperscaler capex pauses, the 2030-dated orders are the first thing renegotiated.

**Earnings-quality flags: none.** Clean.

**Shortage score: 9/10.** The most literal physical shortage in the batch — customers cannot get the gensets they want for ~2 years and are buying smaller ones instead; $72B backlog growing $9B/quarter, booked to 2030.

**Verdict:** A 100-year-old industrial company re-priced as AI power infrastructure — the shortage is physical and dated, but at 34.5× earnings on a cyclical base, you're paying for the datacenter narrative to never pause.

## CDNS — Cadence Design Systems (EDA software / chip-design IP)

**1. Shortage thesis.** Driver: AI chip-design EDA demand — "shortage" in software form: every leading-edge AI chip must be designed in Cadence or Synopsys tools; there is no third option at scale. Evidence of demand intensity rather than supply constraint: **record backlog $8.1B**, with $4.2B expected to be recognized in the next 12 months against a ~$6.3B annual guide — i.e., ~2/3 of the year's revenue already contracted (Q2 2026 PR, 7/27/2026). Agentic-AI demand accelerator: FBN Securities (initiation, Outperform, $375 target, ~10/1/2026): "autonomous agents invoke Cadence's physically accurate engines more often than human designers do" — AI-driven design flows increase engine consumption rather than cannibalizing seat licenses (management, Q2 call). IP segment +40% YoY; Systems Design & Analysis +37% (Hexagon/BETA CAE).

**2. Backlog.** **$8.1B quarter-end (6/30/2026)**, up from $8.0B in Q1 and $7.8B at FY25 end — modest +3.8% growth vs the explosive backlog moves at KLAC/CAT; $4.2B contracted within 12 months.

**3. Category position.** EDA duopoly (+1): Synopsys ~31%, Cadence ~30%, Siemens EDA ~13% — the big three control ~70–85% of global EDA revenue depending on definition (shashi.co, 7/2026; heygotrade, 6/2026). Nvidia, Apple, Qualcomm, Broadcom use all three on the same chip — Cadence owns analog/custom (Virtuoso) and digital implementation (Innovus); nobody tapes out a modern chip without them.

**4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation.** Effectively non-disintermediable: chip-design flows take years to validate; switching costs run to "engineering decades." The Kimi K3 event (7/17/2026 — Moonshot's AI autonomously designed a functional chip, briefly denting CDNS/SNPS) is actually the bull case: autonomous design agents run *on top of* Cadence's engines and consume more of them. Hyperscalers designing their own chips (Google TPU/Broadcom) still pay the EDA toll. **Irreplaceability: 10/10** — the tightest chokepoint in the batch: "a backlog eventually clears and a duopoly doesn't" (OrderFlow).

**5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months).** Beat-and-raise Q2 (7/27/2026): revenue $1.584B +24.2% (beat ~0.5%), non-GAAP EPS $2.11 +27.9% (beat $2.05), non-GAAP op margin 45.5%. **Guidance raised**: FY26 revenue $6.26–6.34B (+19% vs prior +16–18%), non-GAAP EPS $8.05–8.15, OCF $2.0B. Fourth straight beat-and-raise. TSMC expanded partnership (9/23/2026): tool certification across A14 (1.4nm) and N2P, 3DFabric support; Intel IP agreement. FBN Securities initiation Outperform/$375 (~10/1/2026).

**6. Risks.** Valuation: ~70× trailing P/E (finnhub), PEG ~3.52, $359 vs 52wk high $416.69 — priced for flawless compounding. Export-control overhang: Cadence pled guilty (7/2025) to conspiracy to violate export controls ($140M penalties, 3-year federal probation to ~7/2028); China was 13% of FY25 revenue (15% in Q2 2026) and advanced-node EDA flows face U.S. licensing frameworks. Hexagon D&E acquisition ($3.1B — $2.2B cash + shares) cut cash to $1.44B and lifted debt to $2.5B principal; leverage is a monitoring point. CEO sold 25,005 shares ($10.38M) on 6/2/2026. Backlog growth (+3.8%) is far tamer than the momentum names above — the explosive-repricing case is thinner.

**Earnings-quality flags:**
- **RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN.** 10-Q (6/30/2026): receivables $1,065.0M vs $944.9M at 12/31/2025 (+12.7%) vs revenue +24.2% YoY (Q2) / +17.6% (H1). One-line verdict: **benign** — receivables growth trails revenue growth, and deferred revenue jumped ($778.4M → $1,025.1M current), meaning customers are prepaying faster than receivables build; looks like subscription-billing timing, not collection weakness. OCF $635M and FCF $582M in the quarter corroborate.
- **INVENTORY_BUILD.** 10-Q: inventories $390.4M vs $303.5M at 12/31/2025 (+28.6%); cash-flow statement shows a -$107M inventory use in H1 2026 vs +$7.5M in H1 2025. One-line verdict: **benign** — tiny relative to $1.58B quarterly revenue; the build is hardware (Palladium emulation systems) staged ahead of a record hardware quarter, i.e., deliberate inventory for confirmed demand, not channel stuffing.

**Shortage score: 7/10.** Demand is structurally captive and the backlog covers ~2/3 of guided revenue, but this is a subscription moat, not a supply-constrained shortage — the "explosive return" mechanism here is multiple-on-compounder, not scarcity repricing.

**Verdict:** The single best irreplaceability in the batch (a toll on every AI chip on earth) with a clean beat-and-raise record — but backlog growth is modest, valuation is rich, and the export-control probation + China exposure are the live tail risks; it's a compounder, not a shortage repricing.
