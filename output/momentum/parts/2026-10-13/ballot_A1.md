## (1) SINGLE-PICK ballot

- Top pick: MU
- Runner-up: GOOGL
- Thesis (3-5 sentences): Micron is the only name where a hard shortage, a hard contract book, and multi-year visibility all coincide — HBM is sold out for calendar 2026, most of 2027 is already contracted, and management said 2027 and 2028 will be "even tighter" than 2026 with no meaningful new fab volume until late 2028. That scarcity is not spot pricing alone: 26 take-or-pay Strategic Customer Agreements convert it into ~$150B of remaining performance obligations covering >35% of estimated revenue through 2030, some into 2031. At a screen forward P/E of 4.8 and a $989.04 price versus a $1,555.13 mean target, the market is still pricing a normal memory cycle while the contract structure is explicitly designed to damp that cycle. If the floors hold, earnings visibility to 2030 supports re-rating as well as earnings growth — the explosive combination in this dossier.
- Key shortage/backlog evidence: ~$150B RPO under 26 take-or-pay SCAs, with $32B in customer financial commitments (mostly cash deposits), >75% of 2027 output already committed, and HBM sold out for 2026.
- Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk: low — Hyperscalers cannot in-source leading-edge DRAM/HBM (no process technology, fabs cost tens of billions and take years) and are instead prepaying Micron $32B to secure supply, the opposite of disintermediation; the dossier scores irreplaceability 6/10 because Samsung and SK hynix are real substitutes within the 3-player oligopoly and share shifts each generation (Micron HBM share 21%→18% in Q2), but a customer can switch supplier, not bypass memory or build it itself.
- 12-18 month return scenario: rough base / bull case — Base: +40% to +55% (toward the $1,555.13 screen target) if SCA floors and the $61.5B FQ1 FY27 revenue guide deliver as contracted. Bull: +70% to +90% if 2027 tightness lifts minimum prices on renewal/expansion and HBM4 qualification gains share back.
- Top risk: Memory cyclicality — massive industry capex (Micron FY27 capex guided >$50B in some summaries in the dossier) lands as new supply in 2028-29, and because pricing is doing most of the work, any ASP rollover hits EPS non-linearly despite the take-or-pay floors.

### Top 3 — detail

**1. MU:** The single most compelling data point is the contract stack itself: ~$150B of RPO the CFO called "inherently conservative" (minimum prices only), backed by $32B in customer commitments, alongside >40-week DRAM lead times and sold-out HBM. No other supplier in the dossier has customers prepaying at that scale to lock multi-year supply. 12-18mo scenario: base +40% to +55% toward the $1,555.13 screen target; bull +70% to +90% if 2027-28 tightness raises floored prices and committed volumes expand.

**2. GOOGL:** Google Cloud RPO of $514B at Q2 2026 — up from $106B a year earlier and $462B in Q1, with just over 50% expected to convert within 24 months — is the largest multi-year revenue-visibility figure in the dossier, and management said demand is outpacing capacity despite $195-205B of 2026 capex guidance. Alphabet is also the in-sourcer, not the victim: its own TPUs displace rivals internally and are now sold externally. 12-18mo scenario: base +20% to +25% toward the $429.47 screen target as RPO converts (cloud revenue already +82% YoY in Q2); bull +35% to +45% if external TPU system sales and RPO conversion accelerate faster than the capex/depreciation drag.

**3. KLAC:** KLA has the purest hard backlog among the equipment names: $12.57B in its FY2026 10-K, up 60% from $7.86B, with management "virtually sold out," average lead times ~12 months and some products at 18-24 months, and ~52-62% share of process control at 9/10 irreplaceability — no fab can in-source yield inspection at 2nm. 12-18mo scenario: base +20% to +25% toward the $232.77 screen target as H2 2026 revenue runs ~+20% vs H1 on long-lead supply; bull +40% to +50% if 2027 growth is at least as strong as 2026 as guided and advanced-packaging process control (~$1.1B in 2026, +>70% YoY) compounds.

Deliberately left out of top 10: NEM, CSCO, EW, VRTX — NEM has no order book, sells a fungible commodity into spot markets, and its own 2026 production is guided down; CSCO's $46.7B RPO grew only 7% YoY and hyperscalers demonstrably bypass it with white-box/merchant silicon and Nvidia Spectrum-X; EW discloses no backlog and its demand surge is gated by hospital cath-lab capacity, not by an Edwards order book; VRTX discloses no backlog and has patent scarcity (9/10 irreplaceability) rather than a supply shortage, with ~75% penetration already in core CF markets.
