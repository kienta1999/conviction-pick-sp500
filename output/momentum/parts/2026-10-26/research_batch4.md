## 1. AME — AMETEK, Inc. (Electronic Instruments Group + Electromechanical Group)

### 1. Shortage thesis — WEAK / INDIRECT. A demand-surge compounder in niche instruments, not a structural-shortage owner

AMETEK's core products — process, test and measurement instruments, precision motion / thermal / medical components — are not themselves in structural shortage. No evidence was found of AMETEK being sold out, rationing customers, extending lead times industry-wide, or raising prices because it cannot build product. What is real is a broad, multi-market demand surge hitting many of its niches at once, which management tied on the Q2 2026 call to "AI/semiconductor metrology, data-center/power infrastructure build-outs, defense modernization, commercial aerospace and MedTech" (Q2 2026 call summary, 4 Aug 2026).

Concrete evidence of demand, not shortage:

- Record orders of **$2.3 billion in Q2 2026, +28% YoY, organic +25%**, following Q1 organic orders +22% — CEO David Zapico, Q2 2026 earnings call, 4 Aug 2026: "Orders were again exceptional in the quarter with continued broad-based growth across all AMETEK divisions… leading to a record backlog of $4.11 billion."
- Record Q2 sales $2.04B (+15% YoY, organic +10%), record operating income $544M, core margin 27.1% (+110 bps) — same call/release, 4 Aug 2026.
- Product vitality cited at ~25% (new products) in call summaries — demand is being met by new product, not by scarcity pricing.

Distinction that matters for the doctrine: AMETEK is a *beneficiary* of shortages and build-outs elsewhere (semiconductor capacity, data-centre power, defence) in the same way Arista is in Batch 1 — it sells picks into those build-outs. Its growth algorithm is historically ~mid-single-digit organic + disciplined M&A, not price torque on a scarce input. The screen's TTM revenue growth of only 7.5% (vs Q2's stronger print, which annualises a step-up) is consistent with that: strong for AMETEK, not explosive-shortage economics.

## 2. FCX — Freeport-McMoRan Inc. (Copper, gold, molybdenum)

### 1. Shortage thesis — YES for copper concentrate and for ex-US refined metal; CONTESTED for global refined copper in 2026 — and Freeport's own mine is part of the shortage

Driver: Electrification + AI data-centre / grid demand against a mine-supply pipeline that cannot respond quickly — declining ore grades, rising capital costs and ~decade-long development timelines (IEA critical-minerals analyst Shobhan Dhir, Dec 2025: demand could outstrip supply "by as much as 30 percent" by 2035, and "Copper is the one we're really concerned about… a really challenging mineral to ramp up supply quickly") — compounded by an acute, named supply shock: Freeport's own Grasberg.

Concrete evidence:

- **Grasberg mud rush, Sep 2025:** ~800,000 tonnes of wet material flooded the Grasberg Block Cave (GBC), which accounted for ~70% of previously forecast PTFI production; force majeure was declared, 2026 output was guided down by ~1.7B lb of copper vs prior forecasts in analyst summaries, and the mine is still ramping — Q3 2026 mill throughput averaged **140,000 t/day, ~67% of pre-incident normalised rates** (70,000 t/day from GBC), with ~80% targeted by mid-2027 and near-full capacity only by end-2027 (Freeport operational update, 2 Oct 2026).
- **Bank deficit forecasts (the bull cut):** UBS raised copper forecasts to $13,000/t by Dec 2026 and widened expected deficits to **230kt in 2025 and 407kt in 2026**, citing Grasberg, slower Chilean recovery and Peru protests (reported Dec 2025, TS2 summary of UBS). ICSG's earlier October forecast was a **150kt refined deficit in 2026**, flipped from a previously expected 209kt surplus (ICSG, Oct 2025, via multiple reports).
- **Concentrate market is unambiguously tight — the strongest shortage datum:** annual treatment/refining charges (TC/RCs) at **$0/t or negative** in Aug 2026 monitoring, LME cash-to-3M premium $207.50/t, because smelter capacity has outrun mine output — "low TC/RCs are likely to persist over the medium term" per IEA-noted consensus (Discovery Alert concentrate-tightness tracker, Aug 2026 update). Smelters bidding feedstock fees to zero is a physical shortage signal that does not depend on any bank's balance model.
- **Price confirmation:** US copper settled at a record **$6.89/lb on 9 Sep 2026, +40% YoY** (The Inference, 10 Oct 2026, citing Freeport's 2 Oct update / Reuters); Freeport's Q3 2026 realised copper price is expected to **exceed $6.50/lb** and Q2 realised was $6.17/lb, +36% YoY (Freeport update, 2 Oct 2026; Q2 results, Jul 2026).
- **Counter-evidence — state it, do not bury it:** ICSG's **14 Oct 2026** forecast revised the refined balance to a **surplus of ~104kt in 2026 and ~368kt in 2027** (refined production +0.4% in 2026, +3.4% in 2027 vs use +1.5% / +2.5%), and Reuters reported ~70% of the world's visible stocks sitting in Comex warehouses with US on/off-exchange inventories ~1.6Mt after tariff front-running (Comex stocks +~750% since end-Feb 2025). Goldman Sachs has likewise argued a 2026 surplus caps sustained prices above ~$11,000/t. Honest synthesis: the *global refined* balance is near-flat and disputed; the *deliverable ex-US and concentrate* markets are tight, and the long-run (2030s) structural deficit is broadly agreed. The momentum bet is therefore partly a location/timing squeeze, not a pure 2026 physical deficit.

### 2. Backlog / order book — Not applicable / not disclosed (miners sell into spot/LME-linked contracts); visibility is reserves + guidance instead

- Freeport discloses **no order backlog** — copper is sold under annual/spot contracts priced off LME/Comex, so there is no backlog figure to quote. **Formal backlog: not found, because it does not exist in this business model.**
- Visibility proxies that do exist: proven & probable copper reserves of **112 billion lb at 31 Dec 2025** (plus 165B lb in incremental mineral resources) — ~25 years of reserves (Freeport company profile / BMO conference profile, Feb 2026; Morningstar/PitchBook summaries agree on ~25 years).
- 2026 consolidated copper sales guidance held at **3.1B lb** (cut from 3.4B lb in Apr 2026 on the Grasberg ramp delay; maintained in Q2 and in the 2 Oct 2026 update). Q3 2026: production ~830M lb, sales expected ~750M lb (see Special section — that gap is the inventory story).
- Growth visibility: leach innovation run-rate ~200M lb/yr rising toward ~300M lb/yr by end-2026 (Q2 call); Bagdad (Arizona) doubling — final investment decision due by end-2026, preliminary capex ~$4.5B; El Abra (Chile) expansion in preparation (Freeport updates, Jul–Oct 2026).

### 3. Category position — #1 in the United States by a wide margin; one of the world's largest, NOT global #1

- **United States: clear #1.** Freeport's own "America: Copper Champion" disclosure (fcx.com, 2026): its mines account for **~60% of US copper production in 2025** and **~70% of US refined copper production**; it operates one of only two active US copper smelters; Morenci (Arizona, 72%-owned) is the largest US copper mine at ~700M lb produced in 2025.
- **Globally:** Freeport describes itself as "one of the world's largest publicly traded copper producers" and "the world's largest producer of molybdenum" (Freeport profile, Feb 2026). It sold ~1.1Mt of copper (its share) in 2025 across 10 mines — Grasberg (49%), Cerro Verde, Peru (55%), Morenci (72%) are three of the world's largest (PitchBook/Morningstar profile, Oct 2026). Chile's state-owned Codelco remains the world's largest copper producer overall, so FCX is **not global #1 in copper**; it is the largest US producer and the largest listed pure-play-scale copper name. A precise global % share figure was **not found**.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk — Irreplaceability 8/10 (asset irreplaceability; the product is a commodity)

Moat: (i) The orebodies themselves — Grasberg, Morenci and Cerro Verde are tier-one, long-life, low-cost (at normal operating rates) deposits that cannot be recreated at any price on any investable timeline; 112B lb of reserves is the moat. (ii) Copper the metal has no scalable substitute for power transmission, motors and data-centre distribution — aluminium substitutes only at the margin with weight/efficiency penalties. (iii) Customers cannot in-source: wire/cable makers, utilities, EV and data-centre builders cannot acquire or permit a tier-one mine; the in-sourcing question that threatens NVDA/ANET simply does not apply to a miner. (iv) US strategic premium: ~60% of US production + one of two US smelters makes Freeport the default beneficiary of any tariff / domestic-supply policy (US market premium over LME already observed in retrieved coverage).

Limits: copper is priced as a commodity, so Freeport captures scarcity only through the *price*, never through a proprietary premium — BHP, Southern Copper, Antofagasta and Codelco metal is perfectly substitutable for Freeport metal, and if any of them (or Grasberg's own recovery) restores supply, the scarcity rent evaporates for all of them simultaneously. Jurisdiction is the other limit: 49% of Grasberg means Indonesian state partnership, export/smelter mandates and a life-of-resource extension application now pending with the Indonesian government (Q2 call, Jul 2026).

**Irreplaceability score: 8/10** — the reserves and the metal are irreplaceable this decade; the company's share of the scarcity rent is not proprietary and lasts only as long as the market stays tight.

### 6. Risks

- **The shortage has a printed expiry date — Freeport's own.** Management targets Grasberg at ~80% by mid-2027 and near-full by end-2027; the April guidance cut (2026 sales 3.4B → 3.1B lb; H2 Grasberg 85% → 65% of capacity) already slipped once, and Morgan Stanley downgraded to Equal Weight / $66 on that slip (Oct 2026 coverage). Every quarter of successful ramp adds supply into the very market whose tightness is the thesis — FCX volume recovery and copper price are negatively correlated from here.
- **Balance-forecast risk:** if ICSG's 14 Oct 2026 surplus (104kt 2026 / 368kt 2027) and the ~1.6Mt of tariff-front-run US inventory prove right, the ex-US squeeze can unwind fast if US stocks are released or tariff policy changes — copper is the most macro/geopolitically reflexive metal in the complex.
- Cost inflation at low operating rates: Q2 unit net cash costs $1.97/lb, +74% YoY; Q3 guided ~5% above the $2.00/lb July estimate on deferred gold credits (2 Oct 2026 update). Fixed costs deleveraged over two-thirds throughput flatter no one if price falls.
- Indonesia concentration and politics: Grasberg is ~half of reserves on the retrieved profile; extension approval, domestic smelting obligations (Eastern Java), and PTFI ownership terms are sovereign decisions, not company decisions.
- Valuation has run ahead of slower targets: trailing P/E ~36.4x vs a 5-year median of ~29x, one DCF fair value at $49.75, and ~$46M of insider sales over 12 months with no insider buying (The Inference, 10 Oct 2026, secondary) — against a frozen-screen fwd P/E of 15.03 that itself assumes 2027–28 run-rate volumes and elevated prices both arrive.

### SPECIAL — Earnings-quality flag: INVENTORY_BUILD

What the screen is seeing, verified against the filing (Freeport Q2 2026 10-Q, balance sheet at 30 Jun 2026 vs 31 Dec 2025):

| Inventory line (10-Q) | 30 Jun 2026 | 31 Dec 2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product | $3,363M | $3,332M | +$31M |
| Materials & supplies, net | $2,924M | $2,738M | +$186M |
| Mill & leach stockpiles (current) | $1,577M | $1,423M | +$154M |
| Long-term mill & leach stockpiles | $1,074M | $1,173M | -$99M |

Total inventory on the standardised series was **$7.864B at Q2 2026, +13.09% YoY** (MacroTrends summary of filings). Note the composition: finished *product* inventory is essentially flat (+$31M) — the build is in **mill & leach stockpiles (ore) and materials & supplies**, i.e. upstream of saleable metal, not unsold cathode piling up.

Why it built — three operational, disclosed mechanisms, all pointing the same way:

1. **Production is running ahead of sales because the smelter, not the mine or the customer, was the bottleneck.** PTFI's Eastern Java smelter was suspended after the Sep 2025 Grasberg incident and only restarted in late Aug 2026 (Freeport update, 2 Oct 2026). Ore and concentrate produced in the interim had nowhere to be converted/shipped — it sits in stockpiles.
2. **The production-vs-sales gap is explicit in the company's own updates:** Q3 2026 production ~830M lb vs sales expected ~750M lb (an ~80M lb in-quarter build), and **60,000 oz of refined gold sales deferred from Q3 into Q4** (Freeport, 2 Oct 2026) — deferred, with a named destination quarter, which also mechanically lifted Q3 unit costs ~5% via lost by-product credits. Q2 showed the mirror image of the same disruption (sales 710M lb on ramping GBC output that doubled from ~34k t/day in April to ~69k t/day in June).
3. **Demand failure is contradicted by price:** a company building inventory because it cannot sell does not realise >$6.50/lb (Q3E) / $6.17/lb (Q2, +36% YoY) in a market with $0/negative TC/RCs and record settlements. Stockpiled ore at Grasberg is also partly structural — leach/mill stockpiles are a normal, disclosed balance-sheet category for Freeport in every period (they fell on the long-term line even as the current line rose).

Residual caution: materials & supplies +$186M in six months is consistent with ramp/repair activity (Block Cave material-handling upgrades complete early 2027) but is the line to watch — if stockpiles keep building *after* the smelter is back and gold deferrals clear, the benign reading expires. The 27 Oct Q3 print is the verification date.

**One-line verdict — INVENTORY_BUILD: Benign / operational — the build is ore stockpiles and supplies (+$154M current stockpiles, product inventory flat at +$31M) caused by the Eastern Java smelter suspension and an explicit 80M lb production-over-sales gap plus 60k oz of gold deferred to Q4, in a market realising >$6.50/lb; it is unsold *feedstock awaiting processing*, not unsold metal awaiting a buyer.**

### 7. Shortage score and verdict

**Shortage score: 8/10.**
Verdict: FCX is the batch's purest *price-torque* shortage play — concentrate at $0 TC/RCs, a record $6.89/lb price and its own Grasberg outage tightening the market it sells into — but the refined-balance data are now contested (ICSG Oct 2026 surplus) and Freeport's own ramp to near-full capacity by end-2027 is the shortage's scheduled end, so this is a 12–18 month squeeze trade on irreplaceable reserves, not a permanent scarcity franchise.

## 3. LLY — Eli Lilly and Company (Incretins: Mounjaro / Zepbound / Foundayo)

### 1. Shortage thesis — YES, but in transition: the US injectable shortage has formally eased; the binding constraint is now global capacity, and Lilly's revenue is still capacity-gated

Driver: Tirzepatide (Mounjaro for type 2 diabetes, Zepbound for obesity) demand from a patient pool measured in the hundreds of millions globally, against peptide/API manufacturing that takes 18–24 months per capacity tranche to build and validate. For 2023–25 this was a literal shortage (FDA shortage listings, capped launches); the 2026 form is subtler and, for momentum purposes, nearly as powerful: **Lilly can only grow as fast as it can build.**

Concrete evidence:

- Capacity-gating, quantified (secondary analysis of Lilly disclosures, TickerXRay deep dive, 2026): "Lilly's manufacturing capacity in 2025 supported roughly $30 billion in incretin franchise revenue. The 2026 capacity ramp is targeting $45–50 billion supportable revenue. The 2027 ramp targets above $60 billion… Lilly's revenue trajectory through 2027 is largely a function of capacity, not demand… Most pharma equities are demand-constrained; Lilly is supply-constrained."
- Management's own dose math: Lilly expects to produce **at least 1.6x the salable incretin doses in H1 2026 vs H1 2025** (CEO David Ricks, Q4 2025 call, Feb 2026) — a supply target, not a demand forecast, as the growth plan.
- The easing datum, stated honestly: Lilly confirmed **US supply across all tirzepatide dose forms was available throughout Q4 2025**, resolving the intermittent US shortages (Q4 2025 call summary). So the shortage score must not be built on a US shortage that no longer exists; it rests on (a) ex-US launches still constrained/sequenced, (b) every dose of new capacity being absorbed immediately (Q2 2026 volume +60% YoY — see §5), and (c) a new demand step-change arriving before capacity catches up: Medicare obesity coverage from 1 Jul 2026 (see §5).
- Scale of the response is itself shortage evidence: **>$50B committed to US manufacturing since 2020** (Lilly, Feb 2025: +$27B for four new US sites), plus Alzey, Germany ($2.5B), Ireland ($1.8B), Netherlands ($3B) and the Lebanon, Indiana API site more than doubled from $3.7B to **$9B** for tirzepatide API, with manufacturing starting end-2026 and ramping through 2028 (Lilly releases / Nasdaq-Zacks summary). Companies do not commit $50B+ against demand they expect to be able to meet from existing plants.

### 2. Backlog / order book — Not disclosed (pharma sells through wholesalers/PBMs); visibility proxies are guidance, dose capacity and prescription share

- **Formal backlog / order book: not found — Lilly does not disclose one**, and any "backlog" figure for LLY should be treated as fabricated. Prescriptions are filled through retail/specialty pharmacy and LillyDirect, not booked as orders.
- Visibility proxy 1 — guidance: FY2026 revenue guidance **raised to $85.0–$87.0B** on 5 Aug 2026 (Q2 release), with underlying non-GAAP EPS guidance raised $2.78 at midpoint (reported range $35.50–$36.50 after $3.03/share of acquired IPR&D charges). This was the second raise pattern of the year — Q1 2026 guidance had already gone to $82–85B (BetaFinch summary of Q1).
- Visibility proxy 2 — contracted demand step-change: Medicare Bridge coverage (from 1 Jul 2026, $50/month copay, running through end-2027) with **~20M patients potentially eligible** on Lilly's framing and ~700,000 seniors already started by 21 Sep 2026 (Ricks, CNBC, 21 Sep 2026 — see §5).
- Visibility proxy 3 — LillyDirect surpassed **1 million patients in 2025** (Q4 2025 call summary) — a direct channel whose refill base behaves more like a subscription book than spot pharma sales, though no contracted-revenue figure is disclosed for it.

### 3. Category position — Clear #1 in incretins / obesity-diabetes, and pulling away from #2 Novo Nordisk

- **US incretin (obesity + diabetes) share: Lilly 60.9% vs Novo Nordisk 38.8%** — Lilly Q2 2026 earnings presentation, via BioNixus market-share summary (Oct 2026). Q1 2026 cut was similar (60.1%).
- Head-to-head Q2 2026: Zepbound **$4.93B, +46% YoY**, while Novo's injectable Wegovy was flat at constant exchange rates with US sales -22% (BioNixus, Oct 2026). Combined Mounjaro + Zepbound were **$14.9B in Q2 2026 alone** (TradingKey summary of the 5 Aug 2026 release) — roughly 65% of Lilly's total revenue.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk — Irreplaceability 8/10

Moat: (i) Patent-protected molecule + best-in-class efficacy — tirzepatide's dual GIP/GLP-1 action has beaten every head-to-head challenger published so far, including its only scaled rival's next product (see §3). (ii) Manufacturing as moat: in a supply-gated market, Lilly's $50B+ head start in API/injectable/oral capacity *is* market share — Novo lost the share lead substantially on capacity and execution, and a new entrant needs both a molecule that beats 25.5% weight loss *and* billions in validated plants. (iii) No in-sourcing threat in the industrial sense: Lilly's "customers" are patients, physicians and payers — none can manufacture a patented incretin; PBMs can negotiate price and formulary position but cannot bypass the supplier of the drug itself. LillyDirect (1M+ patients) further disintermediates the *channel*, in Lilly's favour.

Concrete threats (substitution, not in-sourcing): Novo Nordisk is a real, scaled #2 with an oral head start and signalled 2027 Wegovy price cuts; a credible pipeline queue — Amgen MariTide (monthly dosing, Phase 3), Pfizer's Metsera-derived monthly candidate (Phase 3), Roche CT-388, Viking VK2735 — attacks on dosing convenience rather than efficacy first; compounders/telehealth copies persist at the cash-pay fringe; and semaglutide generics are already live ex-US (Canada May 2026, Brazil Aug 2026), pressuring Novo first but repricing the whole class's ex-US expectations.

**Irreplaceability score: 8/10** — patients and payers cannot make, bypass or in-source tirzepatide, and Lilly's capacity lead compounds its efficacy lead; the score is capped by a genuine #2 and a funded field of next-gen challengers arriving 2027–28.
