## SINGLE-PICK BALLOT
- **Top pick:** VRTX
- **Runner-up:** ISRG
- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** VRTX is the only name in the 15 with a legally enforced, sole-supplier monopoly nobody can copy, in-source, or route around — patients, payers and physicians cannot manufacture a patented CFTR corrector, and no substitute therapy class exists. That moat sits on a durable balance sheet ($13.6B cash + marketable securities at Q2, before the Crinetics deal) and a 38% operating margin on the frozen screen, not on peak-cycle commodity pricing like MU/SNDK at ~81–87% gross margins. ISRG is the close runner-up on training/installed-base lock-in, but its lock-in is already being litigated ($450M antitrust settlement) and challenged by newly cleared rivals, whereas VRTX's core patents run past 2037. Under this lens, quality is a monopoly that survives a capex pause — VRTX does not need hyperscaler spending to persist.
- **Key shortage/backlog evidence:** Sole source of disease-modifying CFTR modulators eligible to treat ~95% of US CF patients after the Apr 2026 ALYFTREK/TRIKAFTA label expansion (~90% of the CF population in Morningstar's framing), with no approved rival modulator.
- **Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk:** low — patent estate to post-2037, two decades of CFTR science and registry/clinician lock-in; customers cannot in-source a small-molecule corrector and no substitute class exists. The moat does not automatically transfer to IgAN/pain, but the core franchise itself is not disintermediable.
- **12-18 month return scenario:** Base ~+11% to the $571.46 target mean (frozen price $513.31), driven by CF durability plus the povetacicept FDA target action date of 30 Nov 2026; bull ~+30% toward the $665 Morgan Stanley target cited in the dossier if povetacicept is approved and non-CF revenue meets the ≥$500M FY26 guide.
- **Top risk:** Concentration — 96% of Q2 product sales from one disease (CF), so any CF safety, pricing or patent event is company-level; TRIKAFTA already fell -2.1% in Q2 as ALYFTREK cannibalised it, and povetacicept approval rests on a proteinuria surrogate with confirmatory data still to come.

### Top 3 — detail

**1. VRTX:** The single most compelling moat datum is the near-monopoly itself: ~90%+ of eligible CF patients, eligibility expanded to ~95% in the US in Apr 2026, and Grand View's assessment of near-monopoly control until generic entry post-2037 — there is no second source to shift share to, unlike every memory, networking and connector name here. Rough 12-18 month scenario: base ~+11% to the $571.46 target mean; bull ~+30% toward $665 if the 30 Nov 2026 povetacicept decision lands and the $10B Crinetics diversification does not dilute the franchise story.

**2. ISRG:** The moat is physical and human: 12,094 da Vinci systems installed at 30 Sep 2026 (+12% YoY, including 2,023 da Vinci 5), 84% recurring revenue in Q3, ~57–60% global surgical-robot revenue share — roughly 7–8x the #2 — compounded by decades of surgeon training/muscle memory that hospitals cannot build themselves. Rough 12-18 month scenario: base ~+5% to the $478.30 target mean (frozen price $453.90), reflecting a pre-paid valuation at fwd P/E 36.52; bull ~+15–20% if worldwide procedure growth holds near the guided 14–15% for 2026 and dV5 placements (546 in Q3 vs 427 a year earlier) keep compounding the instrument base despite the deliberate 3–5% per-procedure price cut.

**3. WST:** The moat is regulatory text: a stopper/closure is specified in the drug's filing, so switching supplier post-approval requires re-validation — customers are locked in for the product lifecycle, reinforced by >90% participation in new biologic launches and nearly 800 Annex 1 conversion projects in hand at Q2 (+~50% YoY) with coated-component lead times of 6–9 months. Rough 12-18 month scenario: base ~+6% to the $416.40 target mean (frozen price $393.23) — the shortage is already priced at fwd P/E 39.39; bull ~+15–20% if Annex 1 conversions (less than 15% of a ~6 billion component upgrade potential realised) and GLP-1 demand convert the project pipeline into sustained HVP growth (+19.4% in Q2) faster than the guide.

### Deliberately left out of top 10

- **SNDK:** #5 in NAND at 11.4% share — the industry is hard to enter, but SanDisk specifically is substitutable ton-for-ton by Samsung, SK hynix, Micron, Kioxia and rising YMTC; no certification lock-in comparable to CUDA or HBM qualification, and 84.6% gross margin is peak-cycle, not quality.
- **ANET:** Irreplaceability 5/10 — EOS is real, but hyperscalers can white-box/SONiC, and NVIDIA Spectrum-X bundling already cost it the #1 data-centre Ethernet slot (IDC Q1 2026: NVDA 21.5% vs ANET 20.7%); a switch is replaceable at each refresh.
- **CSCO:** Irreplaceability 5/10 — #1 in overall switching but only #3 in the AI data-centre fabric behind NVDA and ANET, fighting hyperscalers who design their own silicon and buy Broadcom merchant silicon; incumbency in campus does not make it irreplaceable where the growth is.
- **CF:** Irreplaceability 7/10 on plant cost, but nitrogen is a fungible commodity — buyers can switch to Nutrien, Koch, Yara or imports ton-for-ton, CF's edge is a gas-cost spread not a proprietary product, and the shortage premium is war/Hormuz-contingent and had already faded back to pre-conflict prices by end-Q2.
- **FANG:** Irreplaceability 5/10 — Tier-1 Midland acreage is scarce, but crude is the ultimate fungible product, price is set by OPEC/war/inventories not by FANG, and its largest competitors (Exxon, Chevron) are themselves the in-sourcers who drill their own Permian barrels.
