## SINGLE-PICK BALLOT
- **Top pick:** MU
- **Runner-up:** SNDK
- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** Under this lens — shortage/backlog evidence and multi-year revenue visibility above all — Micron is the only name in the 15 with a contracted book that reaches to 2030. Its HBM, conventional DRAM and NAND lines are simultaneously short, new fab supply does not arrive in volume until H2 2027 (Idaho, Tongluo) or 2030 (New York), and customers have converted that scarcity into take-or-pay commitments rather than spot orders. SanDisk is the closest peer on contracted visibility (≥$93.9B of floor-price NBMs), but it is a No. 5, NAND-only supplier, whereas Micron sits in the three-firm HBM/DRAM oligopoly across all three memory types. The caveat is priced into the conviction, not ignored: 87% gross margin is a peak-cycle marker, so this is a contracted-shortage trade for 12–18 months, not a claim that peak margins are permanent.
- **Key shortage/backlog evidence:** ~$150B of remaining performance obligations under 26 Strategic Customer Agreements, backed by $32B in customer financial commitments (mostly cash deposits), with 75% of fiscal 2027 shipments already committed and ~35% booked through 2030 (Micron fiscal Q4 2026 disclosure, 30 Sep 2026).
- **Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk:** medium — only three firms on earth make HBM (SK hynix, Samsung, Micron) and leading-edge fabs cost $20B+ and take 3–5 years, so customers cannot in-source or bypass Micron this cycle, and custom HBM4E co-design with NVIDIA deepens qualification lock-in; but Micron is No. 3 (~18–21% HBM share) and customers can credibly shift share to the other two makers at each generation's qualification.
- **12-18 month return scenario:** Base ~$1,350–$1,400 (+48% to +53% from the frozen $912.27) if FY2027 committed volumes ship at SCA floor/ceiling pricing and margins merely hold near the guided ~86% level; bull ~$1,562 (+71%, the frozen screen's analyst target mean) if HBM shortfalls (~19% in 2027 per JPMorgan) keep pricing at the ceiling end and HBM4 share gains toward Micron's ~23% DRAM share.
- **Top risk:** Peak-cycle reversal — simultaneous Micron/Samsung/SK hynix capacity additions are the historical recipe for oversupply 18–24 months out (retrieved coverage already flags Counterpoint expecting DRAM prices to fall ~23% in 2028), and at 87% gross margin Micron has the furthest to fall when ASPs turn; SCA price ceilings also cap upside torque in the meantime.
- **Conviction (1-10):** 9

### Top 3 — shortage/backlog detail and scenarios

**1. MU** — The single most compelling data point is the contracted book itself: ~$150B RPO under 26 take-or-pay Strategic Customer Agreements with $32B of customer financial commitments, 75% of fiscal 2027 shipments committed and capacity ~35% booked through 2030, against JPMorgan-estimated HBM shortfalls of ~20% (2026), 19% (2027) and 16% (2028). No other name converts shortage into multi-year contracted revenue at this scale or duration. Scenario (frozen price $912.27): base ~$1,350–$1,400 (+48% to +53%); bull ~$1,562 (+71%, screen target mean).

**2. SNDK** — SanDisk's New Business Model agreements total a minimum of $93.9B at floor pricing (Q4 FY2026 call, 5 Aug 2026), covering about half of expected fiscal 2027 bits and, on the earlier disclosure, roughly two-thirds of planned volume through fiscal 2028 with >$11B in financial guarantees — on top of industry reporting that all 2026 NAND capacity is sold out and 2027 is being pre-booked, with TrendForce expecting NAND undersupply of ~4–5% through 2026. The visibility is real but shorter and narrower than Micron's (NAND only, No. 5 share at 11.4%). Scenario (frozen price $1,263.29): base ~$1,650 (+31%) if floor-price NBM volumes deliver without a 2027 ASP rollover; bull ~$2,173 (+72%, screen target mean) if enterprise SSD contract price strength (+23–28% QoQ forecast for Q4 2026) persists into 2027 bookings.

**3. APH** — Amphenol took in record Q2 2026 orders of $10.7B (+94% YoY) at a 1.23:1 book-to-bill for a second consecutive quarter above 1.20x, with orders running ~23% faster than shipments — the cleanest order-book evidence of demand exceeding qualified capacity in the non-memory names, and Q3 guidance ($9.3–$9.4B) sits below Q2 order intake, so forward cover is building. The discount versus MU/SNDK is duration: Amphenol discloses no multi-year contracted backlog or "booked through" date, so visibility is quarters, not years. Scenario (frozen price $76.03): base ~$95 (+25%) on continued >1.1x book-to-bill and CommScope CCS accretion; bull ~$103 (+35%, screen target mean) if AI datacom (89% YoY growth, ~43% of sales) sustains its pace.

### Deliberately left out of the top 10
CF, FANG, META, ANET and AME. CF (98% utilisation, 4.0–4.5M tons of traded urea removed) and FANG (Gulf shut-ins, near-zero OPEC spare capacity) have real shortages but no order book at all — CF visibility is one-to-two-quarter fill programs and FANG's ~20 years of drilling locations is undeveloped resource, not backlog — and both shortages are war/Hormuz-contingent with prices already partly faded by end-Q2 (CF) or forecast to fade into 2027 (FANG). META is the shortage *buyer*, not a supplier (shortage score 4/10, no backlog, $135–$145B of capex is spend-side visibility only). ANET discloses only $6.9B of deferred revenue and roughly two quarters of visibility — the weakest contracted profile among the AI-hardware names. AME does hold a record $4.11B backlog, but that is only ~0.5x annual sales (about two quarters) with a shortage score of 4/10 — a demand surge, not scarcity — so it loses the last top-10 slot to names with longer or harder visibility.
