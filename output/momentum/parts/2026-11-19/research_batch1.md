## ANET — Arista Networks

### 1. Shortage thesis

Yes — AI datacenter buildout, specifically high-speed Ethernet fabrics for GPU clusters. CEO Jayshree Ullal on the Q2 FY2026 call (4 Aug 2026): "The industry is going to have a 2-year problem. I don't think we get out of it as an industry until 2028." Management describes FY2026 guidance as explicitly supply-constrained: "if the supply was to release a little more, there is an opportunity to do better in the year." Concrete evidence: memory is secured for 2026 with visibility into 2027, purchase commitments rose to \$11.4B (see §2), and lead times run about 52 weeks with reservation needs beyond that, per pre-Q3 coverage; qualification cycles have stretched from 2–4 quarters to 6–8 quarters. Arista targets at least \$3.5B of AI fabrics revenue in FY2026 (CEO verbally cited at least \$3.6B) and ~\$7B in 2027 — a doubling. Etherlink AI fabrics are in use at 100+ customers. Ullal also said Arista's networks should support more than 10 gigawatts of AI deployments by end-2027 (Q3 call, 3 Nov 2026).

### 2. Backlog / order book

Arista does not report a conventional backlog total. Proxies, Q3 2026 (quarter ended 30 Sep 2026, reported 3 Nov 2026):

- Total deferred revenue: \$6,839.5M at 30 Sep 2026 vs \$6,865.9M at 30 Jun 2026 — first sequential decline since coverage began; \$1,715.0M of Q3 revenue (46.9%) came from the opening deferred balance. Year-end 2025 balance was \$5,372.4M, so deferred is still up ~27% year-to-date.
- Purchase commitments (Arista's own upstream orders, mainly chips plus a multi-year memory agreement): \$11.4B at 30 Sep 2026 vs \$9.7B at 30 Jun 2026 and \$6.8B at year-end 2025; commitments due beyond 12 months jumped to \$2.1B from \$0.3B in June. ~80% expected to be used within 12 months.
- Inventory: \$3.2B vs \$2.5B in Q2.
- Visibility: management cites demand visibility for the next two quarters, but has now guided five quarters out (first 2027 guidance).

### 3. Category position

Not #1 overall. IDC Quarterly Ethernet Switch Tracker, Q2 2026: total Ethernet switch market \$18.9B (+43.4% YoY); datacenter segment \$12.3B (+64.5% YoY). Datacenter segment shares: Nvidia #1 at 20.4%, Arista 18.7%, Cisco 18.2%. Arista is #1 in the hyperscale/cloud-native niche by reputation and holds ~90.9% of its revenue from datacenter; Cisco remains #1 in the overall Ethernet switch market (28.7% share, \$5.4B in Q2 2026). In Q1 2026 Nvidia held 21.5% of the datacenter segment vs Arista 20.7% — the lead has flipped between the two during 2026. 800GbE ports were 41.2% of datacenter revenues in Q2 2026, Arista's sweet spot.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk

Moat: EOS software + cloud-grade operating model, qualification with hyperscalers, and a lead in 800G/1.6T Etherlink fabrics (Tomahawk 6-based, up to 1.6T). Threats are concrete and named: (a) Nvidia's Spectrum-X, which IDC calls "the preferred network interconnect for large-scale AI training," went from under 4% to ~21% datacenter share in about a year; (b) hyperscalers can and do buy Nvidia's integrated GPU-plus-network stack, or Broadcom-based white-box/OEM alternatives (HPE/Juniper, Cisco Silicon One); (c) scale-up Ethernet (the open-standard challenge to Nvidia NVLink) will not produce material revenue until 2028, per Ullal on the Q3 call — so the highest-value interconnect layer is still Nvidia's. Arista buys merchant silicon from Broadcom rather than owning the chip, so its edge is software/systems, not silicon. Customer concentration is high (Microsoft and Meta are the notable large customers).

**Irreplaceability score: 7/10.**

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)

- Q3 2026 (3 Nov 2026): revenue \$3.66B, +58.5% YoY — fastest growth since IPO — beating ~\$3.33B consensus by 9.8% and the ~\$3.3B guide by 10.8%; non-GAAP EPS \$1.20 vs ~\$1.08 expected; non-GAAP gross margin 64.0%, operating margin 49.1%.
- Q4 2026 guide: revenue \$3.9–4.0B, non-GAAP EPS \$1.26–1.30; FY2026 revenue raised for the fourth time this year to ~\$13.3B (+48% YoY).
- First 2027 guidance (3 Nov 2026): revenue ~\$18.5B (+38–40%), well above Bloomberg consensus ~\$16.4B; AI networks ~\$7B, cognitive campus ~\$2B (+60%).
- Shares fell 9.5% on 5 Nov 2026 to \$161.57 despite the beat/guide — a valuation/guidance-quality reaction, not a miss.
- 12 Nov 2026: Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang resigned; Evercore called Arista "the clearest beneficiary" of the disruption in AI networking (gap to Nvidia's datacenter share only ~1.7 pp). Shares rose 6.3% on 12 Nov 2026, also lifted by CoreWeave raising its outlook and disclosing a \$132.5B backlog.
- Balance sheet: cash and securities ~\$14.4B, no debt; \$1.5B buyback plan (May 2025) has \$817.9M remaining.

### 6. Risks

Nvidia Spectrum-X share gains and vertical integration; Cisco's Silicon One resurgence; hyperscaler concentration (Microsoft, Meta); supply costs — gross margin guide of 62–64% already absorbs memory/silicon cost increases, with pricing relief only "towards the end of the year or more like next year"; deferred revenue flat/down sequentially raises a question about order intake durability; valuation ~mid-30s forward P/E with insiders having sold \$1.56B in twelve months with no purchases (per SEC-API filing analysis, Nov 2026); AI capex slowdown would hit a 58% grower hardest.

### 7. Shortage score and verdict

**Shortage score: 9/10. Verdict: The purest AI-networking shortage play in the batch — demand is outrunning supply into 2028 and the 2027 guide is far above consensus — but Nvidia, not Arista, owns the #1 datacenter share and the scale-up layer.**

## APH — Amphenol

### 1. Shortage thesis

Yes — AI datacenter interconnect: high-speed copper, optical and power interconnects inside AI racks. Demand is surging faster than Amphenol can ship: IT datacom grew 30% sequentially in Q3 2026, twice the guided rate, and management said AI drove the vast majority of the sequential gain. CEO Adam Norwitt on the Q3 call (28 Oct 2026): "The revolution in AI has created a unique opportunity for Amphenol." Concrete evidence: orders have exceeded sales for three straight quarters, adding \$5.7B to backlog year-to-date; on the call, management was asked about the backlog extending "a little bit more than historical." No explicit lead-time figure was found in the sources reviewed — lead-time extension is inferred from the book-to-bill, not quoted. AI is now ~75% of IT datacom sales (~36% of the whole company), up from roughly half in 2025.

### 2. Backlog / order book

Amphenol does not disclose an aggregate backlog total — its 10-Q states a substantial portion of remaining performance obligations will be fulfilled within three months, so the aggregate is not disclosed. Reported order flow, Q3 2026 (reported 28 Oct 2026):

- Orders: \$12.17B, +99% YoY, +13% sequentially — a record for a fourth straight quarter.
- Book-to-bill: 1.19:1, positive in nearly every end market (Q2 2026 was 1.23:1 on \$10.7B of orders).
- Orders exceeded sales by ~\$1.94B in Q3 (\$12.17B vs \$10.23B sales); \$5.7B added year-to-date through Q3.
- How far out it is booked: not disclosed in the filing; the 10-Q's "substantial portion within three months" language implies a short-dated book, so the backlog is better read as a flow (book-to-bill >1) than a multi-year pile.

### 3. Category position

#1 in interconnect overall by revenue scale, but not a monopolist — the global connector market is an oligopoly. Third-party estimates (2025): Amphenol ~15% of industrial connectors (vs TE Connectivity 13%, Molex 7.5%) and 36.2% of military connectors (vs TE 14.4%); IBISWorld (Oct 2026) names Amphenol as the largest US wire-connector manufacturer. In connectors generally, TE Connectivity is often cited as the largest single player (~16.8% in one industrial segment estimate vs Amphenol 14.2%), so #1 vs #2 depends on the segment cut — Amphenol leads in defence/military and is co-leader in datacom interconnect. In its growth engine, IT datacom, it competes with TE, Molex, Samtec and others; its edge is breadth across copper, optical and power.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk

Moat: breadth (copper + fibre + power, so it wins whichever interconnect architecture prevails, including co-packaged optics), qualification/design-in with hyperscalers and OEMs, 52 acquisitions absorbed over four decades, and the CommScope fibre business (see §5). Limits: connectors are a multi-supplier business — TE Connectivity, Molex (Koch), Aptiv, Samtec all qualify at the same customers; hyperscalers dual- and triple-source interconnects by design; a shift to optical/CPO could favour silicon-photonics players (Broadcom, Marvell, Coherent) over a connector incumbent. Customers cannot realistically "build connectors themselves" at scale, but they can and do switch suppliers and squeeze pricing. No single customer concentration figure was found in the sources reviewed.

**Irreplaceability score: 6/10.**

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)

- Q3 2026 (28 Oct 2026): first \$10B quarter — sales \$10,229.7M, +65% reported / +37% organic, beating the July guide ceiling by \$830M; adjusted EPS \$0.80, +74%, beating the Street by ~9.6%. IT datacom +117% YoY / +77% organic, now 48% of sales; adjusted operating margin 29.7%.
- Q4 2026 guide: sales \$10.3–10.5B, adjusted EPS \$0.81–0.83; FY2026 guide sales \$36.9–37.1B, adjusted EPS \$2.82–2.84.
- CommScope CCS acquisition (closed 9 Jan 2026, announced 12 Jan 2026, ~\$10.5B cash): 2026 sales outlook raised again to \$5.1B and \$0.18 EPS accretion; its IT datacom sales expected to more than double in 2026 to ~half its revenue.
- 2-for-1 stock split effective 3 Sep 2026 (all per-share figures restated).
- Q3 also: acquired Empire Electronics (~\$320M annual sales); signed deals to acquire AdvancedPCB, Dexgreen and Humanetics Sensors for a combined ~\$1.6B (AdvancedPCB and Humanetics expected to close Q4 2026, Dexgreen Q1 2027); higher dividend; new \$4B buyback authorisation for 2027 noted in coverage.
- Free cash flow \$1.6B in Q3, 80% of net income (vs 68% in Q2).

### 6. Risks

Cyclicality — if hyperscaler capex pauses, a 1.19 book-to-bill can unwind fast and the short-dated book offers little cushion; Q4 datacom guided to only mid-single-digit sequential growth, a sharp deceleration from +30% in Q3; CommScope integration and leverage — total debt ~\$18.7B after the deal, ~\$200M quarterly net interest; broadband/communications-networks end market down 28% organically in Q3; multi-supplier competition (TE, Molex) and architecture shift to optical/CPO; valuation — the stock has already re-rated on the AI narrative and the Q3 beat was partly a one-off step-change in datacom mix.

### 7. Shortage score and verdict

**Shortage score: 8/10. Verdict: Record orders and a 1.19 book-to-bill on AI interconnect demand, yet connectors remain a multi-supplier, qualification-driven business rather than a monopoly — the shortage is real but the moat is breadth, not exclusivity.**

## MSFT — Microsoft

### 1. Shortage thesis

Yes — AI cloud compute capacity. Demand for Azure exceeds the capacity Microsoft can bring online: all three hyperscalers said in late October 2026 they cannot add capacity as fast as customers sign for it. Channel checks (UncoverAlpha, Oct 2026): "cloud capacity is still sold out, and if anything, the sold-out window is getting longer"; Amazon CEO Andy Jassy reportedly corrected "out of capacity until 2028" to "beyond 2028, end of 2028, maybe." Microsoft-specific evidence: Azure grew 48% in Q1 FY2027 and is guided to 49–50% in Q2, yet commercial RPO was flat sequentially because recognised revenue is catching up with the backlog; Microsoft "expects capacity constraints at least through the end of 2026," and some outside capacity deliveries were late in Q1 FY2027, cutting capex. AWS raised reserved GPU pricing 15% on 7 Oct 2026, the fourth consecutive quarterly hike (cumulative +40–75% in 2026) — a market-wide price signal of scarcity. Capex: \$45.2B in Q1 FY2027 (including finance leases), with more than \$50B guided for Q2; cash paid for property and equipment doubled to \$39.4B.

### 2. Backlog / order book

- Commercial remaining performance obligation (RPO): \$677B at 30 Sep 2026, +73% YoY, but flat vs \$678B at 30 Jun 2026 (Q1 FY2027, reported 28 Oct 2026). Commercial bookings fell 12% YoY (−11% constant currency) against a prior-year quarter that included very large Azure commitments; management said underlying commercial sales motions grew, helped by one large Azure commitment signed in the quarter.
- Duration: weighted average ~2.3 years; ~30% expected to be recognised in the next 12 months. Longer-dated RPO grew 98% YoY; near-term RPO grew 29%.
- Concentration: OpenAI alone was \$10.0B of Q1 FY2027 revenue (10.8% of total revenue; \$24.1B in all of FY2026, ~\$6.0B/quarter) and was ~45% of RPO at Dec 2025 after contracting an additional \$250B of Azure services (announced 28 Oct 2025). The RPO is all Microsoft commercial, not Azure alone.

### 3. Category position

#2 in cloud infrastructure, not #1. Synergy Research Group, Q3 2026 (29 Oct 2026): AWS 26%, Microsoft (Azure) 19%, Google Cloud 16% of a \$167.7B quarterly cloud infrastructure market (+55% YoY). Azure is growing faster than AWS (48% vs AWS +39% to \$46.0B; Google Cloud +103% to \$30.7B). Microsoft holds the largest disclosed contracted backlog (\$677B vs AWS ~\$578B and Google Cloud \$560.8B), though scopes differ. Azure revenue was disclosed in dollars for the first time: \$33.2B in the quarter. Microsoft's distribution moat is Microsoft 365/Windows/developer relationships plus the OpenAI partnership.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk

Moat: enterprise distribution (Office 365, Windows, GitHub, developer tools), the OpenAI partnership (exclusive-ish cloud provider; OpenAI is both the largest customer and a strategic dependency), global footprint (60+ regions), and its own silicon (Maia 200 deploying in Azure; Maia 300 previewed) reducing dependence on Nvidia. Threats: customers can multi-cloud or move to AWS/GCP — switching costs are real but not prohibitive, and Google Cloud is growing twice as fast; OpenAI is itself building with AMD (warrants over up to 160M shares, gigawatt-scale commitments) and others, so the largest customer is diversifying away; hyperscaler customers (Meta, Google, Amazon) are all building their own AI chips (Trainium, TPU, Maia), which caps Microsoft's pricing power on compute; the AI model layer is not Microsoft's — it rents OpenAI's models. The "in-sourcing" risk runs the other way for Microsoft: it is the in-sourcer (Maia), not the victim.

**Irreplaceability score: 8/10.**

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)

- Q1 FY2027 (28 Oct 2026): revenue \$92.3B (+19% YoY), beating ~\$90.6B consensus; adjusted EPS \$4.89 vs ~\$4.72; Azure +48% constant currency (guide 44–45%), first dollar disclosure \$33.17B; operating margin 49.8%. Q2 FY2027 guide: revenue \$96.0–97.1B, Azure +49–50% constant currency.
- New segment structure from 2 Sep 2026: "Agents and Infra" (83.2% of revenue, 52.8% operating margin) vs "Devices and Consumer."
- Free cash flow fell 27% to \$18.6B as capex absorbed the beat — the market's main debate point.
- Nov 2026 context: Nvidia's Q3 FY27 (17 Nov 2026) printed \$115.8B revenue (+103% YoY) with Vera Rubin racks running at Azure, Google Cloud, Oracle and others — confirming the demand backdrop — but Nvidia's CEO resigned 12 Nov 2026, a governance shock to the AI supply chain Microsoft depends on.
- Not found in sources reviewed: a specific new Maia 300 order or datacenter-opening announcement in the last ~3 months; Maia 300 was previewed pre-earnings (Goldman preview, 28 Oct 2026).

### 6. Risks

Backlog stall: RPO flat sequentially and bookings −12% — the "contracted demand" story is pausing even as recognised revenue accelerates; OpenAI concentration (10.8% of revenue, ~45% of RPO at Dec 2025) is a single-counterparty risk, and OpenAI's own financing is unproven at this scale; capex intensity — \$45.2B in one quarter, >\$50B guided next, with FCF down 27%; capacity-delivery delays; Google Cloud growing 2x faster and AWS still larger; AI-capex ROI question — if enterprise AI monetisation disappoints, the \$677B RPO includes multi-year commitments that could be renegotiated; valuation at a ~\$506 screen price after a 48% Azure print leaves little room for a bookings disappointment.

### 7. Shortage score and verdict

**Shortage score: 8/10. Verdict: Azure is growing 48% against a \$677B backlog it cannot convert fast enough, but the backlog is flat sequentially and one customer is ~11% of revenue — a capacity shortage with a concentration asterisk.**

## MA — Mastercard

### 1. Shortage thesis

No. Mastercard sells payment-network access, not a physical product — there is no capacity constraint, lead time, or sold-out inventory. Its "demand surge" is steady consumer spending and cross-border travel, not a supply shortage: gross dollar volume +7%, purchase volume +9%, cross-border volume +11% in Q3 2026. The closest analogue to scarcity is pricing power: domestic assessments +13%, cross-border assessments +16% and transaction processing +14%, all above the underlying volume growth — i.e. Mastercard is raising take rate, not rationing supply. Value-added services (+19%) are the growth engine, not a constrained good. This is a demand-compounding story, not a shortage story.

### 2. Backlog / order book

Not applicable — Mastercard has no backlog, bookings, or remaining performance obligations in the sense used for hardware/cloud names. The nearest equivalents, Q3 2026 (reported 29 Oct 2026): 3.8 billion Mastercard- and Maestro-branded cards issued (cards +5% YoY); switched transactions +9%; gross dollar volume \$3.0 trillion in the quarter. Revenue visibility comes from multi-year network deals and services contracts, but no backlog figure is disclosed — not found.

### 3. Category position

#2 in the global card duopoly, behind Visa. Twelve months to 30 Jun 2026 (Axis Intelligence, updated 27 Oct 2026, from Visa/Mastercard filings): Visa payments volume \$15,337B (62.27%) vs Mastercard purchase volume \$9,293B (37.73%) of the \$24,630B combined; Visa leads 1.65x on volume but only 1.27x on net revenue, because Mastercard earns 30.1% more per dollar of volume (37.75 vs 29.01 basis points). US share: Visa 70.96% vs Mastercard ~29%; outside the US, Visa 56.02% and Mastercard is gaining. Globally including UnionPay: Visa ~38.7%, UnionPay ~33.2%, Mastercard ~25% of purchase transactions (Nilson Report H1 2024). Together Visa + Mastercard process ~85% of global non-Chinese card volume. Mastercard is growing faster where it matters: cross-border +11–14% vs Visa mid-single digits; value-added services +19–22% vs Visa ~10%.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk

Moat: two-sided network effects (3.8B cards, millions of merchants, bank issuers), brand/trust, fraud and tokenisation infrastructure, and the regulatory/licensing web — effectively a duopoly with Visa that no new entrant has cracked at scale. Disintermediation threats are real but slow-moving: real-time account-to-account rails (UPI, Pix, FedNow, SEPA Instant), stablecoin settlement, and agentic commerce (Google's Universal Commerce Protocol, live in the US on waitlist, global rollout late 2026) could route around card rails; the Credit Card Competition Act and a US merchant settlement (10 bp cut in average US credit interchange, five-year cap; final-approval hearing Nov 2026) could compress take rate. Mastercard's response is to buy/partner into the threat: BVNK stablecoin infrastructure acquired 3 Aug 2026 for \$1.4B cash (plus up to \$300M earn-out); OpenAI and Tencent agentic-commerce partnerships; OKX stablecoin card; JPMorgan-backed Firmly AI shopping agents. Customers (banks, merchants) cannot realistically build a rival global network; they can only steer volume to Visa or to alternative rails at the margin.

**Irreplaceability score: 9/10.**

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)

- Q3 2026 (29 Oct 2026): net revenue \$9.8B, +14% (as reported and currency-neutral), beating ~\$9.65B consensus; adjusted EPS \$5.31, +21%, beating ~\$5.16 consensus by 3.3%; net income \$4.6B (+18%); adjusted operating margin 60.5%; payment network revenue +11% (\$5.7B), value-added services +19% (\$4.1B). Contactless is 81% of in-person switched purchase transactions.
- FY2026 guidance reaffirmed: net revenue growth at the high end of low double digits (currency-neutral, ex-inorganic); Q4 revenue and expense growth both guided to the high end of low double digits.
- Analyst upgrades after Q3: KBW raised its price target to \$750 from \$685 (2 Nov 2026, Outperform); TD Cowen, Wells Fargo, RBC, Barclays and Susquehanna also raised targets (\$700–750 range); consensus ~\$698, "Moderate Buy" (MarketBeat).
- Capital returns: \$3.0B of buybacks in Q3 (5.3M shares) plus \$763M dividends; \$11.9B buybacks in the first nine months of 2026; \$4.9B buyback authorisation remaining at 26 Oct 2026; quarterly dividend \$0.87 (paid 9 Nov 2026, ~0.5% yield).
- BVNK acquisition closed 3 Aug 2026; OpenAI/Tencent agentic-commerce partnerships and OKX stablecoin card announced in the period.

### 6. Risks

No shortage thesis — the main risk to this panel's framing is that MA simply does not belong in an explosive shortage bet; growth is low-double-digit, not 40–100%. Consumer-spending slowdown would hit volumes; intra-Europe cross-border softened in Q3 (new EU customs fee, promo timing, lower crypto purchases); interchange regulation (US merchant settlement final approval, Nov 2026; Credit Card Competition Act; UK £5B collective action; merchant suits in the Netherlands/Portugal; Block and Intuit seeking >\$5B damages); stablecoin and real-time-payment disintermediation over 5–10 years; nine-month operating cash flow down 6% while buybacks plus dividends exceeded operating cash flow and debt rose ~\$4.9B; valuation ~34x P/E with only ~8% upside to consensus target (~\$706 vs \$645.73 screen price).

### 7. Shortage score and verdict

**Shortage score: 2/10. Verdict: No shortage at all — a steady duopoly compounder whose moat is network effects, not scarcity, so it does not fit an explosive shortage thesis.**
