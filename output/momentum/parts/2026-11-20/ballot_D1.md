# Panelist D — Contrarian / Risk Skeptic — Momentum ballot, 2026-11-20

## Single-pick ballot

- **Top pick:** APH
- **Runner-up:** WAB
- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** Amphenol is the highest-scored shortage in the dossier (9/10), and the shortage is physical and slow to fix: at 224 Gb/s per lane, traffic must move off PCB traces into engineered cable assemblies, and fibre preform capacity has not kept pace with demand since mid-2025, with new preform plants taking up to two years to build. Orders are running ahead of shipments for a third straight quarter, customers are reserving capacity well into next year, and AI is about 75% of an IT Datacom segment that is 48% of sales — so the demand is concentrated exactly where the bottleneck sits. The skeptic's check cuts in APH's favour on entry: shares fell about 5.7% in the week after the Q3 print, so the record beat is not being chased at a fresh high the way the fully-priced names in this field are. Unlike the software and networking names, Amphenol is architecture-neutral across copper, power, active copper cable and fibre, so it wins whichever interconnect architecture the hyperscalers settle on.
- **Key shortage/backlog evidence:** Q3 2026 orders of USD 12.17B, up 99% year over year and 13% sequentially — a record — with a 1.19:1 book-to-bill in Q3, following 1.23:1 in Q2, 1.24:1 in Q1 and 1.31:1 in Q4 2025.
- **Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk:** Medium — and why: high-speed interconnect is designed in and qualified jointly with the chip vendor and hyperscaler, and a failed link can take down an entire training run, which is real lock-in; but hyperscalers deliberately qualify multiple suppliers (TE Connectivity is the live second source), there is no software lock-in, and co-packaged optics / silicon photonics could over time reduce discrete connector content per rack. No customer can in-source decades of signal-integrity process knowledge at 224G overnight, but neither does APH hold a monopoly — breadth, not exclusivity, is the moat.
- **12-18 month return scenario:** Base case roughly +25% to +35%: the order book converts, IT Datacom keeps growing at a multiple of the group rate, and the post-print pullback proves to be the entry rather than the start of a de-rating. Bull case roughly +50% to +70%: fibre and high-speed interconnect lead times stay stretched through 2027, hyperscaler capex does not pause, and the market re-rates APH from a connector supplier to the tollbooth on AI rack build-out.
- **Top risk:** AI concentration — IT Datacom is 48% of sales and about 75% AI, so a hyperscaler capex pause hits APH immediately, and some longer-dated orders may come from less-capitalised neocloud buyers who could cancel.
- **Conviction (1-10):** 7

**1. **2. **3. MSFT — Microsoft.** The single most compelling data point is the disconnect: Azure grew 48% year over year to USD 33.2B and commercial RPO stands at USD 677B, yet the stock's 12-month return is only +1.9% — the market is pricing the capex bill (about USD 170B of calendar-2026 spending, Q2 capex expected above USD 50B) and not the sold-out capacity, with channel checks saying cloud capacity is sold out into/beyond 2028. Base case roughly +15% to +25% over 12–18 months as Azure growth converts the RPO and the capex-payback fear fades; bull case roughly +35% to +50% if capacity comes online faster than feared and the market re-rates Azure's 48% growth instead of discounting it.
