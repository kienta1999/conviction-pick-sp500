## APH — Amphenol (screen price `USD 75.61`)

### 1. Backlog/bookings — CONFIRMED

Q3 2026 orders were a record `USD 12.17 billion`, up 99% YoY and 13% sequentially, with a book-to-bill ratio of 1.19:1. Stated verbatim by CFO Craig Lampo on the Q3 2026 earnings call, 28 Oct 2026, and repeated in the company release and multiple transcripts.

Sources: Amphenol Q3 2026 earnings call transcript, 28 Oct 2026 (AlphaStreet / Motley Fool); Morningstar/Dow Jones, 28 Oct 2026.

### 2. Shortage-driver — CONFIRMED (one wording nuance)

CEO Adam Norwitt on the same 28 Oct 2026 call: "The IT datacom market represented 48% of our sales in the 3rd quarter. Sales in this important market grew by a very strong 117% in US dollars and 77% organically... AI... now does represent approximately 3 quarters of our IT datacom sales."

Nuance: the +117% figure is the growth of the whole IT Datacom market, not of AI-related datacom revenue alone. The dossier's phrasing ("AI-related datacom revenue +117%") conflates the two. The underlying numbers — 48% of sales, ~75% AI, +117% IT Datacom growth — are each confirmed as stated by the company.

Sources: Amphenol Q3 2026 earnings call transcript, 28 Oct 2026 (AlphaStreet; Zacks call highlights, 29 Oct 2026).

### 3. Catalyst + date — CONFIRMED

Q3 2026 results reported 28 Oct 2026: record sales of `USD 10.23 billion` (+65% YoY); adjusted diluted EPS `USD 0.80` vs `USD 0.73` expected (Zacks consensus; FactSet via Morningstar also `USD 0.73`); FY2026 guidance raised to sales of `USD 36.9–37.1 billion` and adjusted EPS `USD 2.82–2.84`.

Sources: Amphenol Q3 2026 release/call, 28 Oct 2026; Morningstar/Dow Jones, 28 Oct 2026; Zacks, 29 Oct 2026.

### 4. Moat — UNVERIFIED

The "~33% market share in the AI datacom segment" figure traces to a single secondary estimate — an AInvest analysis (2025, repeated Jan 2026) — syndicated by low-quality content farms (e.g. a Jan 2026 "Guymon Daily Herald" market-wire piece). No primary source, industry tracker (IDC/Dell'Oro/Gartner), or company disclosure corroborates a 33% share in an "AI datacom interconnect" segment, and the segment itself has no standard definition. The "short list of firms (Amphenol, TE Connectivity, Molex) can qualify high-speed interconnect at 224G" phrasing comes from the same AInvest article, not from a technical or industry primary source. The 224G engineering premise itself is real (Amphenol's Paladin HD supports 224 Gb/s; TE and Molex are recognised high-speed suppliers), but the exclusivity claim — that only these three can qualify at hyperscale volume — has no independent confirmation I could find.

Related but different data point, not the same claim: Global Market Insights credits Amphenol with over 31% share of the North America circular connector market in 2025 — a different, narrower segment.

Sources checked: AInvest, "Amphenol's AI Data Center Play" (2025) and "Amphenol: The Company Wiring the AI Boom" (2026); Global Market Insights, North America circular connector market (2026).

## WAB — Wabtec (screen price `USD 280.73`)

### 1. Backlog/bookings — CONFIRMED

Total backlog `USD 31.44 billion` at 30 Sep 2026 (`USD 31,435 million` in the release), up 22.9% YoY from `USD 25.58 billion` (`USD 25,577 million`). 12-month backlog `USD 8.99 billion`, up 8.8% YoY. Freight multi-year backlog `USD 25.77 billion`; Transit `USD 5.67 billion`.

Sources: Wabtec Q3 2026 press release, 21 Oct 2026 (SEC 8-K exhibit, sec.gov); Zacks/Nasdaq, 21 Oct 2026.

### 2. Shortage-driver — CONFIRMED

CEO Rafael Santana on the Q3 2026 call, 21 Oct 2026, verbatim: coverage "is the strongest it's ever been," defined as "having orders in hand to match what I call our product lead times. So you think about for long lead time products like new locomotives, that means having orders 24, 36 months out."

Sources: Wabtec Q3 2026 earnings call transcript, 21 Oct 2026 (Motley Fool; TradingKey transcript); TipRanks/Bullish call summaries, 21–22 Oct 2026.

### 3. Catalyst + date — CONFIRMED

Q3 2026 results reported 21 Oct 2026: adjusted EPS `USD 2.83`, up 22.0% YoY, beating the `USD 2.72` Zacks consensus (one vendor, TradingView/Seeking Alpha, cited `USD 2.73` — consensus varies slightly by provider; the beat is confirmed either way). Full-year 2026 adjusted EPS guidance raised to `USD 10.85–11.05` (from `USD 10.60–10.90`). Sales were `USD 3.12 billion` (+8.1% YoY), a small miss vs `USD 3.18 billion` consensus — the dossier records this correctly.

Sources: Wabtec Q3 2026 press release, 21 Oct 2026 (Business Wire / wabteccorp.com / SEC 8-K); Zacks, 21 Oct 2026.

### 4. Moat — CONFIRMED (scope caveat)

Wabtec and Progress Rail (Caterpillar/EMD) are the two suppliers of new heavy-freight diesel-electric locomotives to North American Class I railroads. CPKC's 2026 fleet renewal (Jan 2026 Progress Rail release; Texas Rail Advocates) describes Wabtec and Progress Rail as "the world's two largest locomotive manufactures" and its only two motive-power suppliers, ordering 70 Wabtec ET44AC and 65 Progress Rail SD70ACe-T4 Tier 4 units. Industry coverage (Rolling Stock World, Jan 2026) likewise frames the North American new-locomotive market as Wabtec plus Progress Rail.

Caveat: the "only two" framing holds for heavy-freight diesel-electric locomotives. Siemens Mobility builds passenger locomotives (Charger) in the US, and smaller builders (Brookville, National Railway Equipment) build switchers and rebuilds — none is a qualified heavy-freight mainline OEM. No single primary source states "only two qualified heavy-freight OEMs" in those words; the claim is supported by the procurement record, not by an explicit industry census.

Sources: Progress Rail press release, 21 Jan 2026; Texas Rail Advocates / CPKC, 2026; Rolling Stock World, Jan 2026.

## MSFT — Microsoft (screen price `USD 498.30`)

### 1. Backlog/bookings — CONFIRMED

Commercial remaining performance obligation (RPO) `USD 677 billion` at 30 Sep 2026, up 73% YoY, with a weighted average duration of ~2.3 years; roughly flat sequentially vs `USD 678 billion` at 30 Jun 2026. About 30% expected to be recognised as revenue in the next 12 months.

Sources: Microsoft Q1 FY2027 earnings release, 28 Oct 2026 (Form 8-K Ex. 99.1); Microsoft Q1 FY2027 earnings call transcript, 28 Oct 2026 (Motley Fool); FourWeekMBA analysis of Microsoft 10-Q filings, 28 Oct 2026.

### 2. Shortage-driver — PARTIALLY CONFIRMED

- Azure demand exceeds supply: CONFIRMED. CFO Amy Hood on the Q1 FY2027 call, 28 Oct 2026: "customer demand continues to exceed available supply," with some third-party capacity deliveries delayed into H2 FY2027. Directions on Microsoft (Nov 2026) likewise notes "another acknowledgement by Microsoft that Azure demand is still exceeding supply."
- Azure revenue +48% YoY to `USD 33.2 billion` (`USD 33,165 million`): CONFIRMED. Microsoft Q1 FY2027 release, 28 Oct 2026.
- "Capacity is sold out into/beyond 2028": UNVERIFIED as an Azure claim — and the specific quote is misattributed. The "beyond 2028 / end of 2028, maybe" line is AWS CEO Andy Jassy speaking about AWS capacity, relayed by Ben Horowitz on the All-In podcast and quoted in Uncover Alpha's Q3 2026 channel-check report (Oct 2026). Uncover Alpha's own framing is industry-wide ("cloud capacity is still sold out, and if anything, the sold-out window is getting longer"), and Google's Sundar Pichai separately said 2027 compute requests are "incredibly difficult" — but no source I found puts an Azure-specific sold-out horizon at 2028. The dossier attributes the 2028 limb to Uncover Alpha channel checks; that source supports a general cloud-capacity squeeze, not an Azure 2028 sell-out.

Sources: Microsoft Q1 FY2027 earnings call, 28 Oct 2026; Directions on Microsoft Compass Point, Nov 2026; Uncover Alpha Q3 2026 channel checks, Oct 2026; TechShorts/Reuters on Jassy's AWS remarks, Oct 2026.

### 3. Catalyst + date — CONFIRMED

Q1 FY2027 results reported 28 Oct 2026: revenue `USD 92.3 billion` (+19% YoY); non-GAAP EPS `USD 4.89` (GAAP `USD 4.92`) vs `USD 4.73` expected per LSEG (Zacks consensus was `USD 4.72` — consensus varies slightly by vendor; the beat is confirmed either way). Azure `USD 33.2 billion`, +48% YoY.

Sources: Microsoft Q1 FY2027 earnings release and call, 28 Oct 2026 (microsoft.com); Zacks, 29 Oct 2026; ODaily/LSEG summary, 28 Oct 2026.

### 4. Moat — CONFIRMED (factual limb only)

The factual limb is confirmed as Microsoft's own claim: Microsoft's Foundry pages (ai.azure.com; azure.microsoft.com) state Foundry is used by "80% of the Fortune 500" (and by 80,000+ enterprises / 120,000+ customers, per the product pages). The qualitative limb — that the M365/Windows/identity + Azure stack is "the hardest stack in software to rip out" — is a judgement, not a verifiable fact; no source ranks enterprise-stack switching costs that way. Treat it as analyst opinion, not a confirmed fact.

Note: the product has been rebranded from "Azure AI Foundry" to "Microsoft Foundry" (Ignite, Nov 2025); the 80% figure is carried on the current Foundry pages.

Sources: Microsoft Foundry product pages (ai.azure.com; azure.microsoft.com/en-us/products/ai-foundry), accessed Nov 2026.

## Material corrections / flags for the panel

1. **APH claim 2 wording:** +117% is total IT Datacom market growth, not AI-only revenue growth. The numbers are right; the label is imprecise.
2. **APH claim 4:** the 33% share and the 224G short list rest on one secondary estimate (AInvest) with no primary corroboration. Do not cite as established fact.
3. **MSFT claim 2:** the "sold out into/beyond 2028" line belongs to AWS (Jassy), not Azure. Azure demand exceeding supply is confirmed; an Azure 2028 sell-out is not.
4. **Consensus figures vary by vendor** (WAB `USD 2.72` vs `USD 2.73`; MSFT `USD 4.73` vs `USD 4.72`); neither changes any beat/miss verdict.
5. No contradictions found: all hard numbers (orders, backlog, RPO, sales, EPS, guidance) matched the dossier exactly.
