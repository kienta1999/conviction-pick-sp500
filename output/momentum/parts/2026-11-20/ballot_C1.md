# Panelist C — Quality / Moat & Irreplaceability — Ballot, 2026-11-20

## Single-pick ballot

- **Top pick:** WAB
- **Runner-up:** MSFT
- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** Wabtec is the only name in the field where the moat, the backlog, and the customer lock-in all point the same way. It is one of only two qualified heavy-freight locomotive OEMs in North America, with FRA/AAR certification, crashworthiness and emissions qualification that takes years to replicate, and railroads standardise fleets around its GE Evolution platform. Its backlog is the largest and fastest-growing in the dossier, giving 24–36 months of coverage on long-lead locomotive programs — the strongest coverage management says it has ever had. Unlike the AI names, no customer can in-source the product: no Class I railroad builds its own locomotives, and deferring a purchase still feeds Wabtec's modernisation and services revenue. The explosive upside comes from converting that record book at a 22.9% operating margin that is still expanding.
- **Key shortage/backlog evidence:** Total backlog of USD 31.44B at 30 Sep 2026, up 22.9% year over year, with 24–36 months of coverage for long-lead locomotive programs — the strongest order coverage management has ever reported.
- **Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk:** Low. Only two qualified heavy-freight locomotive OEMs exist in North America (Wabtec and Progress Rail/Caterpillar-EMD); certification, installed-base fleet standardisation, and capital intensity block entry, and no railroad can in-source locomotive manufacturing. The realistic bypass is deferral or modernisation instead of new builds — which still flows to Wabtec — plus a longer-term technology shift to battery/hydrogen locomotives, where Wabtec is itself developing prototypes.
- **12-18 month return scenario:** Base case roughly +15–20%, driven by backlog conversion, FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance of USD 10.85–11.05, and continued international orders. Bull case roughly +35–40% if international locomotive orders accelerate and the market re-rates the book toward analyst targets (Stephens USD 340, Citi USD 334, against a USD 280.73 screen price).
- **Top risk:** Cyclicality — locomotive orders track rail volumes and Class I capex, North American railcar builds were cut to ~23,000 units in 2026 (down 26% from 2025), and delivery timing already slipped from Q3 into Q4 2026.
- **Conviction (1-10):** 8

**3. Base case roughly +12–18% as HVP growth compounds; bull case roughly +30% if GLP-1 volumes keep growing at 30%+ and the 18–24 month capacity lag turns today's demand into a genuine shortage premium.
