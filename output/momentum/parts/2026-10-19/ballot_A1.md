# Panelist A — Supply-Chain Analyst Ballot — 2026-10-19

Lens: Weight shortage/backlog evidence and multi-year revenue visibility above all. A filed, take-or-pay, deposit-backed order book outranks a demand narrative; a sold-out input that customers cannot in-source outranks a merely popular product.

## SINGLE-PICK BALLOT:

- **Top pick:** MU
- **Runner-up:** GOOGL
- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** Micron is the only name in the field scoring 10/10 on shortage with a contracted book to match it — calendar-2026 HBM is sold out, management can meet only 50–66% of key customers' demand, and shortages are guided to intensify through FY2027 and FY2028 with meaningful new fab output not arriving until mid-CY2027. That scarcity is already monetised, not hoped for: Q4 FY2026 revenue was $54.23bn (+379% y/y) on ASP-led growth, Q1 FY2027 is guided to $61.5bn at ~86.25% gross margin, and TrendForce still forecasts Q4 2026 DRAM contract prices +10% to +15% q/q (+15% to +20% including HBM). At $878.84 and 4.2x forward P/E against a $1,560.57 analyst target (+77.6%), the market is paying a cyclical multiple for what the contracts say is a multi-year allocation regime. If even half the RPO converts at guided margins, the earnings base reprices before supply can answer.
- **Key shortage/backlog evidence:** ~$150bn of remaining performance obligations from 26 Strategic Customer Agreements (up from ~$100bn / 16 SCAs one quarter earlier), take-or-pay on volume, backed by $32bn of customer financial commitments — mostly cash deposits — with >75% of planned FY2027 shipments already committed.
- **Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk:** medium — Hyperscalers cannot in-source DRAM/HBM (a fab is $20bn+, 3–5 years, plus decades of TSV/yield IP, and Micron is co-qualified into specific accelerator platforms), so bypass is impossible; the real risk is share-shifting inside the 3-player oligopoly, proven by Samsung recovering HBM share from 21% in Q1 to 33% in Q2 2026, and by Nvidia imposing a common TSMC base-die standard for custom HBM across all three suppliers.
- **12-18 month return scenario:** Base ~+35% to +50% if SCA floor pricing and guided margin hold through FY2027; bull ~+78% to the $1,560.57 consensus target, with Rosenblatt's $1,900 target as the stretch case if HBM tightness extends through 2027 as secondary coverage claims.
- **Top risk:** Peak-cycle reversal in 2027–28 — Idaho/Taiwan/Singapore capacity plus simultaneous Samsung/SK hynix expansion lands into an ASP-led earnings base, and the same operating leverage that produced +256% FY2026 revenue growth runs in reverse; near-term, Rubin HBM4 validation delays can push HBM revenue right.
- **Conviction (1-10):** 9

**Top 3 — detail:**

**1. MU:** The single most compelling data point is the jump in one quarter from ~$100bn to ~$150bn of RPO across 26 take-or-pay SCAs, with customer cash commitments rising to $32bn — customers are prepaying for allocation in a market where Micron meets half or less of key demand and 2026 HBM, including HBM4, is sold out. That is multi-year visibility (~3/4 of SCA revenue has a defined pricing framework, some through 2031), not a spot-price bet. 12–18mo scenario: base +35% to +50%, bull +78% to $1,560.57.

**2. GOOGL:** Alphabet's $514bn Google Cloud backlog at end-Q2 2026 — up >$50bn sequentially, >5 years of current quarterly Cloud revenue ($24.8bn), with just over 50% expected to convert to revenue within 24 months — is the largest hard backlog in the field, and management is explicit that revenue "would have been higher" if it could meet demand, forcing third-party capacity as a bridge and a $195–$205bn capex guide. At $340.59 the shortage is funded by the Search cash engine rather than by hope. 12–18mo scenario: base +15% to +20% on steady backlog conversion, bull +26% to the $430.40 target ($450 Evercore stretch).

**3. NVDA:** Nvidia has ~$1tn of Blackwell + Vera Rubin purchase-order visibility through 2027 (management-stated, not a filed backlog — hence #3, not #1, on this lens), corroborated by $279bn of supply and capacity commitments at 2026-07-26, up from $119bn one quarter earlier — it is pre-buying HBM and CoWoS years out because its own inputs are the bottleneck, with HBM4 yields already delaying the Rubin ramp per TrendForce. Customers ordering 18–24 months out with Blackwell sold out through mid-2026 is genuine allocation, even if the headline number is softer evidence than MU's or GOOGL's filed books. 12–18mo scenario: base +25% to +35%, bull +54% to the $328.72 target.

Deliberately left out of top 10: WST, VRTX, EW — WST (shortage 6/10) discloses no backlog and trades at 38.4x with only +7.5% analyst upside despite filing-locked moat; VRTX (shortage 3/10) is a patent monopoly with no shortage in its 96%-of-revenue CF business and no order book; EW (shortage 4/10) has an under-treated patient reservoir, not a product shortage or backlog, plus a fresh SAPIEN M3 delivery-system FDA early alert.
