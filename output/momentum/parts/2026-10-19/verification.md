## MU — Micron Technology

### Claim 1 — Backlog / SCAs / deposits

**Verdict: CONFIRMED, with nuance.**

- 26 Strategic Customer Agreements (SCAs), ~$150B remaining performance obligations, $32B in customer financial commitments: Micron FQ4 FY2026 earnings call, 2026-09-30 — CEO Sanjay Mehrotra and CFO Mark Murphy, as reported by Zacks (2026-10-01), CNBC via AI Stock Wire (2026-10-01), and the call transcript (MarketBeat, 2026-09-30). SCAs are multi-year take-or-pay agreements, estimated at >35% of revenue through 2030, some extending into 2031.
- Deposits actually held at FQ4-end: ~$12.7B — CONFIRMED independently of the 2026-10-18 verification. CFO Murphy on the 2026-09-30 call: "Customer cash deposits on our balance sheet at the end of fiscal Q4 were $12.7 billion." Micron Form 10-K, filed 2026-10-09 (fiscal year ended 2026-09-03): cash deposits received in 2026 of $12.75B under the SCAs; balance-sheet noncurrent customer contract liabilities of $12.895B at 2026-09-03 (vs $568M at FQ3-end).

**Nuances that change how the claim should be stated:**
1. Do NOT write "$32B in deposits." $32B is total customer *financial commitments* ("the vast majority" in cash deposits, per management). Cash actually received and held at FQ4-end was $12.7B. The 10-K adds a further $7B of letters of credit issued or contractually committed to be issued by third-party banks (contingent on customer default/breach; not cash held and not revenue).
2. The ~$150B RPO counts ONLY SCAs with a determined pricing framework (fixed, or floor-and-ceiling bands — about three-quarters of the agreements), at committed minimum volumes and minimum pricing. Murphy called the basis "inherently conservative." It therefore understates total SCA volume, and it is not comparable to the ~$100B figure cited at FQ3, which was measured on a different basis (minimum-price cumulative revenue for 14 of 16 agreements).
3. Deposits are unrestricted cash while held, but are returnable over the latter half of each agreement's term if customers meet minimum purchases; per the 10-K, nearly all are scheduled for repayment between 2029 and 2031. They are recorded as financing cash flow, not operating cash flow or revenue.

### Claim 2 — Shortage / HBM

**Verdict: CONFIRMED, with a timing nuance.**

- CY2026 HBM sold out: Micron earnings materials from December 2025 onward state the entire calendar-2026 HBM supply, including HBM4, is committed under completed price-and-volume agreements; corroborated by multiple 2026 secondary summaries of management remarks.
- CY2027 HBM: On the FQ4 call (2026-09-30), management said agreements are complete for "the vast majority" of calendar-2027 HBM bit supply at significantly / "much higher" prices vs 2026 (Zacks, 2026-10-01; The Register, 2026-10-01: customers will pay "much higher prices"). Management also said >75% of total planned 2027 output (all memory, not HBM alone) is already committed, that talks have shifted to 2028, and that supply-demand will be tighter in CY2027 and CY2028 than in 2026, with no line of sight to balance.
- Only ~3 firms make HBM at scale: CONFIRMED as industry consensus — SK hynix, Samsung, Micron (Counterpoint Q2 2026 HBM revenue shares ~50% / 33% / 18% respectively, cited across October 2026 coverage; AInvest, IndMoney, Exoswan all state "only three companies manufacture HBM at scale").

**Nuance:** The "can satisfy only 50–66% of key customers' demand" figure is real but is NOT an FQ4 (2026-09-30) statement. It originates in Mehrotra's remarks around the FQ2 FY2026 report in March 2026 (Reuters reporting, summarised 2026-03-24: key customers receiving only 50% to two-thirds of requirements). The final writeup should date it ("management said in March 2026 it could meet only 50–66% of key customers' demand") rather than presenting it as a fresh FQ4 disclosure. The FQ4-era equivalents are the >75%-of-2027-output-committed figure and "tighter in 2027 and 2028, no line of sight to balance."

### Claim 7 — $500B / $1T visibility vs filing RPO; supply commitments

**Verdict: CONFIRMED — the claim's distinction is correct and is the single most important labelling point for NVDA.**

- Management visibility: At GTC 2026 (March 2026), CEO Jensen Huang said NVIDIA had seen ~$500B of "very high confidence demand / purchase orders" for Blackwell + Rubin through 2026 a year earlier, and now saw **at least $1T through 2027** (DataCenterDynamics reporting of the GTC keynote, March 2026). CFO Colette Kress confirmed the $1T covers Blackwell + Vera Rubin plus related networking only, excluding e.g. Groq LPUs and standalone CPUs. This is a keynote/management demand-visibility statement. It appears in no SEC filing as backlog or RPO.
- Filing RPO: NVIDIA Form 10-Q for the quarter ended 2026-07-26 (filed 2026-08-26, accession 0001045810-26-000075): "As of July 26, 2026, revenue related to remaining performance obligations from contracts greater than one year in length was $3.2 billion, which included $3.0 billion from deferred revenue and $244 million that has not yet been billed or recognized as revenue."
  - Nuance for fair statement: the $3.2B filing RPO has a narrow statutory perimeter (contracts >1 year; largely support/software-type obligations) — it is not a like-for-like "total GPU backlog" either. The correct final-writeup formulation is the claim's own: "$1T is management visibility, not a filed backlog; NVIDIA does not disclose a GPU order backlog in its 10-Q, and its filed RPO line is ~$3.2B." Do not present $3.2B as "the real backlog" any more than $1T should be presented as filed RPO.
- Supply/capacity commitments ~$279B: CONFIRMED — 10-Q / CFO commentary as of 2026-07-26: supply and capacity commitments $279B, up from $119B in Q1 FY27, primarily memory and manufacturing capacity for current and future Data Center products (Zacks, 2026-09; TokenPost/CryptoBriefing filing summaries, October 2026). Total listed commitments in the CFO commentary table were $366B, of which the $279B supply/capacity line is the largest component.

### Claim 8 — ASIC trap and receivables quality

**Verdict: CONFIRMED, with a maturity nuance on one ASIC.**

- Receivables / DSO — CONFIRMED from the Q2 FY27 filing and CFO commentary (2026-08-26): net accounts receivable $63.059B at 2026-07-26, up from $40.7B at Q1 FY27-end — i.e. **+55% QoQ** while revenue rose +18% QoQ (filing-based analysis, Beancount, 2026-08-26; Zacks and Motley Fool, September 2026, report the same levels). DSO rose from 45 days in Q1 to 60 days in Q2, which NVIDIA itself attributes to extended payment terms on large, multi-quarter agreements with certain investment-grade customers; the 10-Q notes some such customers receive terms of 90 days to one year. Five direct customers represented 70% of the receivables balance.
  - Do not confuse the bases: vs the January 2026 fiscal year-end balance of $38.466B, the rise is ~+64% over six months. "+55% QoQ" and "+$24.6B in H1" are both correct on their stated bases.
  - Cash-quality corroboration: Q2 operating cash flow fell to $24.1B from $50.3B in Q1 despite higher revenue/net income ($59.7B), with the receivables build absorbing ~$22.3B in the quarter.
- Hyperscaler / customer ASICs — CONFIRMED as shipping or in production for four of the five named programmes:
  - Google TPU: shipping and externally sold — TPU v7 (Ironwood) in market; Alphabet recognised TPU *system-sale* revenue for the first time in Q2 2026 (Alphabet call, 2026-07-22).
  - AWS Trainium: in volume — 500,000+ Trainium2 deployed, Trainium3 generally available (industry ASIC survey, mid-2026; AWS disclosures).
  - Microsoft Maia 200: in production — Microsoft states Maia 200 serves Microsoft Copilot in production and is deploying across the US, Europe and Australia; Maia 300 was announced 2026-10-12 as the future primary Azure inference platform (Moor Insights & Strategy, October 2026, reporting Microsoft's disclosures).
  - Meta MTIA: deployed/in production — Meta states it runs hundreds of thousands of MTIA chips for inference; MTIA 300 in production for ranking/recommendation (Meta engineering disclosures, March–September 2026; Reuters reporting via July 2026 coverage of the volume-production ramp).
  - OpenAI–Broadcom "Jalapeño": **taped out, not yet shipping in volume.** Unveiled 2026-06-24: design-to-tape-out in nine months with Broadcom (silicon) and Celestica (systems); engineering samples running workloads in the lab; initial deployment targeted for end of 2026 (OpenAI/Broadcom announcement, 2026-06-24; Reuters reporting same date).
  - Wording fix: the claim as drafted already separates "shipping" from "taped out" — preserve that separation exactly. Do not upgrade Jalapeño to "shipping," and note the shipping ASICs are concentrated in inference / internal workloads (MTIA: ranking, recommendation, GenAI inference; Maia: inference) — none is yet a demonstrated substitute for NVIDIA in frontier training, which is the correct scope of the "trap."
