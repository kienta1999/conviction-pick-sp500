## KLAC — KLA Corporation (semiconductor process control: inspection & metrology)

### 1. Shortage thesis — INDIRECT / second-order, with the hardest backlog evidence in the batch
Driver: the same AI/WFE shortage as LRCX/AMAT, expressed as *process-control intensity* — every new node, EUV layer, HBM stack and advanced-packaging step needs disproportionately more inspection/metrology, and KLA is the near-monopoly supplier of that layer. The shortage is in chips and cleanroom capacity; KLA sells the yield assurance without which new capacity does not ramp.
- Management raised the calendar-2026 WFE outlook to the low-$150B range (from >$140B) at the fiscal Q4 call (28 Jul 2026, Zacks/secondary call summaries), expecting H2 CY26 growth of ~20% over H1 on improving supply availability, with momentum continuing into CY27.
- Advanced packaging process control: CY26 revenue expectation raised to ~$1.1B, >+70% YoY (management, Jul 2026) — the AI-packaging vector is now a material, separately-guided business.
- Management described unprecedented customer urgency to secure slots/deliveries and industry capacity constraints limiting how fast demand can be met (call summaries, 2026). Note the offset: on an earlier call (Q1 FY26) management said lead times had normalised to ~7–9 months — KLA's own supply is a constraint being worked down, not a sold-out wall.

Moat: the strongest in this batch. Fabs cannot in-source inspection/metrology — no chipmaker builds its own leading-edge inspection fleet — and KLA's advantage compounds: decades of defect-classification data and algorithms, tools qualified as the reference platform in customer flows, an installed base generating ~23% of revenue as recurring service, and process-control intensity that rises mechanically with each node/HBM/packaging step. Concrete threats are peer/niche, not customer in-sourcing: AMAT in integrated metrology, ASML/HMI in e-beam, Hitachi High-Tech in CD-SEM, Onto/Nova/Camtek in packaging/macro inspection — each contests a step, none contests the portfolio. ## CSCO — Cisco Systems, Inc. (networking / Silicon One / security & observability)

### 1. Shortage thesis — PARTIAL: real AI-order surge, but the shortage also hits Cisco as a *buyer*
Driver: hyperscaler AI-backend networking (Silicon One switching/routing, optics) plus an enterprise campus refresh cycle. Cisco's AI demand is genuine and accelerating — but unlike memory or etch, networking supply is not sold out at Cisco's layer; the binding shortages (memory, components) sit *upstream* of Cisco and raise its costs and working capital (see addendum).
- Fiscal Q4 2026 (company release via PR Newswire, 12 Aug 2026): record revenue $17.3B (+18% YoY); **AI infrastructure orders from hyperscalers of $4.0B in Q4 alone, taking FY26 AI orders to $9.3B — 4.5× FY25** — against an initial FY26 target of $5B that management had already raised to $9B in May and still beat. Total product orders +35% YoY; networking revenue $9.79B (+28%).
- Conversion is happening, not just booking: ~$4.0B of AI infrastructure revenue was recognised in FY26, and FY27 AI infrastructure revenue is guided to **$7.5B** (management, Aug 2026).
- Input-side shortage evidence: Dell'Oro Group (Sep 2026, on 2Q CY26 campus switching) reported component shortages affected campus switch availability, drove ASPs +19%, and that memory shortages are expected to shape the market into 2027 — Cisco gained campus share in that constrained quarter, i.e. supply management, not scarcity rent, is the edge.

### 2. Backlog / order book
- **RPO $46.734B at 25 Jul 2026, +7% YoY** (company Q4/FY26 release): product RPO $23.436B (+9%), services RPO $23.298B (+6%); deferred revenue $29.781B. Duration: total RPO ≈ 0.74× FY26 revenue ($63.3B) — visibility is real but RPO grew only +7% against +18% Q4 revenue growth, so the book is *not* building the way KLAC's or APH's orders are.

### 3. Category position — #1 overall in its category, #3 in the AI segment that matters

- But in data-centre Ethernet switching — the AI-backend battleground — IDC data for Q1 CY26 (via secondary summaries) puts **Nvidia #1 at 21.5%, Arista #2 at 20.7%, Cisco #3 at 17.8%**. Cisco is winning hyperscaler AI orders from a #3 segment position, which is both the opportunity (share to take) and the fragility (no incumbent's lock on the growth segment).

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation — score 5/10
Moat: enormous installed base, IOS/NX-OS familiarity, bundled security/observability (Splunk), Silicon One programmability across switching and routing, channel and services reach — sticky in enterprise/campus, where refresh orders grew (campus orders +20% in Q4, Morningstar summary). Hyperscalers do not in-source switch *silicon* fabrication, but they in-source system design and network OS — which is where much of Cisco's historical margin lived.
