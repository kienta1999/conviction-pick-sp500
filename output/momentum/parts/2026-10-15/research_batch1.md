### 7. Shortage score: 9/10

## MU — Micron Technology (DRAM / HBM / NAND)

### 1. Shortage thesis — YES, the doctrine's prototype, now stronger
Driver: HBM + conventional DRAM + datacentre SSD demand from AI buildout; HBM consumes disproportionate wafer capacity, starving conventional supply, and new cleanroom capacity cannot add meaningful output until after late 2028 (management, 30 Sep 2026 call summaries).
- CEO Sanjay Mehrotra (earlier in 2026, reported widely): Micron could satisfy only ~50–66% of key customers' HBM demand.
- FQ4 FY26 call (30 Sep 2026): no line of sight to supply–demand balance; calendar 2027 and 2028 expected tighter than 2026 despite DRAM bit shipments growing low-20s% and NAND mid-20s%.
- HBM: 2026 supply sold out; "vast majority" of calendar-2027 HBM supply already agreed, at significant price increases vs 2026; HBM revenue growth outpaced total revenue growth in FQ4.
- Pricing: DRAM prices +high-teens QoQ and NAND ~+30% QoQ in FQ4 FY26 (call summaries).

### 2. Backlog / order book — best in class
- **~$150B RPO** disclosed 30 Sep 2026 (FQ4 FY26 results/call), across **26 Strategic Customer Agreements (SCAs)**, all take-or-pay, RPO counted only where a pricing framework (fixed or floor/ceiling) is determined and based on committed volumes at minimum pricing — described by CFO Mark Murphy as inherently conservative.
- SCAs cover >35% of estimated revenue through 2030 (some beyond 2030/into 2031); **$32B in customer financial commitments**, mostly cash deposits (~$12.3B of deposits received in FQ4 alone per call summaries); **>75% of calendar-2027 output already committed**, customer discussions now centred on 2028. RPO ($150B) exceeds all of FY26 revenue ($133.2B).

### 3. Category position — #3, in a 3-player oligopoly
- DRAM (Q1/Q2 2026, Counterpoint/TrendForce via press): Samsung #1 ~38–39%, SK hynix #2 ~25–29%, **Micron #3 ~22–24%**; the three control ~90%+ of DRAM.
- HBM: SK hynix #1 ~50% (Q2 2026, down from 58% Q1), Samsung #2 ~33%, **Micron #3 ~18–21%**. Micron is the only US-based HBM maker — a strategic, not share, leadership point.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation — score 8/10
Moat: Only ~3 firms on earth make HBM at scale; fabs cost tens of billions (Micron FY27 capex guided >$50B), process/yield know-how and Nvidia/AMD qualification lock-in are multi-year, and customers are prepaying $32B rather than attempting in-sourcing — a hyperscaler cannot in-source a memory fab realistically, and none has tried. Concrete threat: share loss *within* the oligopoly (Samsung's HBM share doubled YoY to 33% in Q2 2026; SK hynix holds ~50% and the lead Nvidia HBM4 allocation), plus CXMT reaching ~10% of conventional DRAM (Q2 2026) at the low end. Bypass risk is low; relative-share risk is real.

### 5. Recent catalysts (~3 months)
- 30 Sep 2026: FQ4 FY26 beat — revenue $54.2B (+379% YoY, +31% QoQ) vs ~$51.1B consensus, non-GAAP EPS $33.42 vs ~$31.6 consensus, gross margin ~87%; FQ1 FY27 guidance $61.5B ±$1.5B / ~86.25% GM / $38.15 ±$1.00 EPS; FY26 revenue $133.2B (+256%), adj. EPS $75.52 (+811%).
- Sep 2026: Citi raised target $1,150 → $1,300 (Buy, DRAM pricing/supply tightness); UBS reaffirmed $1,625 target (press summaries).
- HBM4 in volume production for Nvidia's Vera Rubin platform; HBM4E ramp planned 2027; datacentre SSD revenue >$10B in FQ4, >10× YoY.
- Capital return flagged from 9 Dec 2026 as CHIPS restrictions ease (call commentary reported in press).

### 6. Risks
Memory cyclicality (2022–23 precedent of losses after a boom) if AI capex pauses; earnings are price-led and the market already applies a peak-cycle discount (~5× forward P/E on the screen despite record margins); >$50B FY27 capex creates the very supply that ends the shortage post-2028; Samsung/SK hynix share competition in HBM4; customer concentration in a handful of hyperscalers/Nvidia; CXMT/YMTC at the commodity end; stock volatility (a ~28% drawdown in Jul 2026 within this uptrend).

### 7. Shortage score: 10/10
**Verdict:** The textbook case, upgraded — sold-out HBM through 2026, most of 2027 contracted at higher prices, and a $150B take-or-pay RPO larger than a full year's (record) revenue converts the old spot-cycle story into contracted visibility.

### 3. Category position — clear #1

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation — score 7/10 — THE TRAP IS LIVE HERE

Open-source Triton/PyTorch abstraction is slowly eroding CUDA lock-in for inference. Training and frontier work remain Nvidia's; the bypass is partial, workload-specific, and compounding — hence 7, not 9–10.

**Verdict:** Still the shortage's toll-booth — $96B quarter, $108B guided, allocation-constrained racks — but it is the one name here whose biggest customers are demonstrably building the substitute, so the moat question, not demand, decides the holding period.

## LRCX — Lam Research Corporation (etch & deposition equipment)

### 1. Shortage thesis — INDIRECT / second-order, strong but not "sold out"
Driver: AI-driven wafer-fab-equipment (WFE) demand — every HBM/DRAM/NAND/logic expansion needs disproportionately more etch and deposition (3D NAND layer counts, GAA transistors at 2nm, HBM stacking, advanced packaging). The shortage is in *chips and cleanroom space*, and Lam sells the picks into it.
- Management raised calendar-2026 WFE three times in 2026: $135B (Jan) → $140B with upside bias (Apr) → **low-$150B range with upside bias** (29 Jul 2026, FQ4 call), saying fab-readiness/cleanroom constraints, not demand, are what hold WFE below the ~$150B+ the chip revenue math would imply — i.e. demand for equipment exceeds the industry's ability to absorb it.
- CFO Doug Bettinger (Citi conference, 9 Sep 2026, via press): 2nm GAA needs more etch/deposition; every 100k wafer starts of that capacity adds ~$1B to Lam's served market; Lam's gross margin at a 20-year high.
- Advanced packaging revenue guided to >70% YoY growth potential (management commentary summarised Aug 2026); NAND systems revenue doubled QoQ in the June quarter.

### 2. Backlog / order book
- **Formal backlog: not found** — Lam does not report an order backlog figure. Proxies from the FQ4 FY26 release (quarter ended 28 Jun 2026): **deferred revenue $2.43B**, up from $2.22B in March, +$213M QoQ, driven by customer downpayments; plus ~**$490.2M** of estimated future revenue in shipments to Japan customers held in inventory at cost pending acceptance (vs $434.3M in March). Combined visible pre-recognition book ≈ $2.9B — small vs a $8.1B single-quarter guide, so visibility rests on the WFE outlook and customer behaviour (management, Jul 2026: customers with multiyear fab plans are "working with us to secure equipment orders" for cleanroom space as it comes online), not on a disclosed backlog.

### 3. Category position — #1 in etch, #2 in WFE overall
- Etch: ~39–45% global share (multiple analyst/press summaries; ~40–50% in key segments), #1, ahead of Tokyo Electron and Applied Materials. Deposition: strong #2-type position (~17% in one summary) behind Applied Materials.
- Overall WFE: #2 behind Applied Materials (AMAT has ~1/3 more revenue and a much larger services arm); Lam is #1 by market cap among the pair as of Oct 2026 press. Lam's SAM is slightly above mid-30s% of WFE in 2026, trending toward its high-30s% goal (management, Apr/Jul 2026). Screen: sub-industry rank 1 in Semiconductor Materials & Equipment, 1.08× the #2 by market cap.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation — score 8/10
Moat: Fabs cannot in-source etch/deposition tools — no chipmaker builds its own leading-edge etch fleet; the supplier set is a tight oligopoly (Lam / AMAT / Tokyo Electron) protected by process-recipe co-development with customers, atomic-level process IP, installed-base service lock-in (Customer Support was a record >$2.4B in the June quarter), and qualification cycles that make a qualified etch process effectively designed-in for a node's life. Concrete threat is *peer* substitution, not customer in-sourcing: AMAT/TEL can win individual process steps node-by-node, and Lam's share must be re-won at each transition (GAA, backside power, 400+ layer NAND) — it is currently winning (SAM expanding, third straight year of WFE outperformance guided), but the moat is execution-renewed, not permanent.

### 5. Recent catalysts (~3 months)
- 29 Jul 2026: FQ4 FY26 record — revenue $6.72B (+15.1% QoQ), GAAP EPS $1.81 / non-GAAP $1.82, GAAP GM 51.7%; **September-quarter guidance $8.10B ±$400M (+~20% QoQ), GM 52% ±1%, EPS $2.15 ±$0.15**; WFE outlook raised to low-$150Bs (above).
- Sep 2026: Citi-conference commentary (9 Sep) on 2nm etch intensity and 20-year-high gross margins; headcount +1,800 in the June quarter to support manufacturing/installation — hiring into demand.
- 1 Oct 2026: positive read-through trade — Micron's >$50B FY27 capex plan lifted equipment names, Lam +~1.4% that session (press).
- Next print 21 Oct 2026 (screen: 6 days out) is the near-term catalyst/risk event.

### 6. Risks
WFE cyclicality — equipment is the most cyclical layer; a memory/logic capex digestion cuts orders first and hardest; China was 26% of June-quarter revenue (34% in March) and export-control tightening is a standing threat; fab-readiness constraints can push revenue right even when demand is real; valuation (screen forward P/E ~25.6, trailing ~55; thinnest analyst upside of the big equipment names in Oct press); customer concentration in TSMC/Samsung/SK hynix/Micron capex plans; the thesis is a derivative of MU/Samsung capex, so a break in the memory shortage breaks LRCX with a lag.

### 7. Shortage score: 7/10
**Verdict:** No sold-out book of its own, but the cleanest second-order shortage play here — #1 in etch, SAM expanding, WFE raised three times in seven months to low-$150B, and a +20% QoQ guide into the September quarter.
