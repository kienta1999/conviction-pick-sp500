Sources: Wabtec Q3 2026 earnings release, Oct 21, 2026 (via Business Wire / FinancialContent republication); Zacks, Oct 21, 2026 ("Total backlog reached \$31.44 billion as of Sept. 30, 2026, up 22.9% from \$25.58 billion a year earlier"; "The 12-month backlog increased 8.8% to \$8.99 billion"); Motley Fool Q3 2026 call transcript takeaways, Oct 21, 2026 (same figures).

### Claim 3 — Wabtec holds ~70% of the North American diesel-electric locomotive market vs ~30% for Caterpillar's Progress Rail / EMD

**Verdict: UNVERIFIED**

I could not verify the ~70% / ~30% split as a current statement about Wabtec from any primary source (company filing, earnings call, or reputable press). The only source I found stating exactly "up to a 70% market share" for the GE side and "an approximate 30% market share" for Caterpillar-owned EMD is a low-quality mirror page reproducing the old Wikipedia "GE Transportation" article — i.e. a pre-2019 figure about GE Transportation before Wabtec acquired it in 2019, not a current Wabtec figure. That provenance makes the claim as stated unreliable, and it should not be cited as a current market-share fact.

What the better-sourced evidence actually says — and it points higher than 70% for Wabtec, on a different basis:

- Progress Rail's own 2023 Delaware complaint, as quoted by Railway Age (updated Feb 27, 2026) and FreightWaves (Sep 21, 2023), alleged Wabtec had "manufactured and sold approximately 75% of active diesel long-haul freight locomotives in North America, and approximately 90% of new long-haul freight locomotives that comply with Tier IV" standards. That is an adversarial plaintiff's allegation, not a neutral market-share measurement, and it is about the active fleet / new Tier IV units, not the whole diesel-electric market.
- Wabtec itself said at RailTrends 2021 (via Trains, reported Dec 8, 2021) that ~75% of operated locomotives in North America were produced by the company and GE Transportation (which it acquired in 2019).
- A 2025 market-research report (Dataintelo, diesel-electric locomotive market) claims Wabtec commands "over 85% market share in new Class I locomotive deliveries" in North America — a third-party estimate, not a company disclosure, and again a new-delivery basis.

Correct figure: none established. The direction of the evidence is that Wabtec is the dominant supplier and the ~70% figure, if anything, understates its position on the new-build / Tier IV bases most often cited — but no verified current ~70% / ~30% split exists. Recommendation: drop the precise split from the memo, or replace it with the sourced formulation that Wabtec and Progress Rail form a North American duopoly for new high-horsepower freight locomotives, with Wabtec the dominant supplier (per the Progress Rail complaint's ~75% of the active diesel long-haul fleet / ~90% of new Tier IV units, attributed as an allegation).
