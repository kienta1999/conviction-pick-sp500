# Momentum research — batch 3 — 2026-11-07

Tickers: V, CME, VRSN, UNP, WAB. Research date 2026-11-07. All five reported Q3 / fiscal-Q4 results 2026-10-21 to 2026-10-27, so figures below are from those releases and calls unless dated otherwise. Note on framing: none of these five is a classic physical-shortage name — the shortage lens fits WAB and VRSN (contractual pricing power / multi-year order book) far better than V, CME or UNP, which are toll / volume franchises. Scores below are shortage scores, not quality scores.

## V — Visa Inc.

### 1. Shortage thesis

No structural shortage. Visa sells network capacity that scales at near-zero marginal cost; there is no sold-out capacity, lead time or rationing. The demand-side driver is secular, not scarce: global payments volume +9% constant dollars in fiscal Q4 2026, cross-border volume ex-intra-Europe +14%, processed transactions 74.2 billion (+10%) in the quarter and 281.3 billion (+9%) for FY2026 (Visa earnings release, 2026-10-27). Commercial payments volume grew 12% for the year — "the fastest annual growth rate in eight years, excluding the post-pandemic recovery" (Q4 call). Visa Direct topped 15 billion transactions / about \$1.5 trillion in volume for the year. This is durable volume growth, not supply-constrained pricing power.

### 2. Backlog / order book

Not applicable — no order book. The closest visibility metric is the transaction base: ~80% processing penetration globally, and FY2027 guidance of low-double-digit revenue and operating-expense growth on a non-GAAP adjusted constant-dollar basis ex-acquisitions (earnings deck, 2026-10-27). No backlog figure is reported.

### 3. Category position

Yes — #1. Visa held 62.27% of combined Visa + Mastercard purchase volume worldwide in the 12 months to 2026-06-30 (\$15.34 trillion vs Mastercard \$9.29 trillion), and 70.96% in the US in calendar Q2 2026 (Axis Intelligence analysis of both companies' filings, updated 2026-10-27; Nilson Report Feb 2026: 70.38% US share in 2025). Together Visa + Mastercard process ~85% of global non-China card volume.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk

Moat: two-sided network effects (issuers, acquirers, merchants, cardholders in 200+ countries), VisaNet scale, brand/trust, and value-added services (revenue \$14.2 billion in FY2026 per 10-K summary). The concrete threat is real, not hypothetical: stablecoins and real-time rails (UPI, Pix, FedNow) bypass card interchange. Stablecoin payment volume reached \$33 trillion in 2025 (+72% YoY, per Blockonomi citing Morgan Stanley/Citrini, Feb–Mar 2026); Druckenmiller said in April 2026 stablecoins could dominate payments within 15 years; Visa and Mastercard stocks fell 18–23% from peaks in early 2026 on stablecoin + proposed 10% credit-card rate-cap fears. Visa's response is to be the connector, not the coin: multi-coin/multi-chain, Open USD support, stablecoin advisory practice launched Dec 2025, and CEO McInerney: "Our role is not to pick winners" (earnings call, July 2026). Jefferies argues the threat is overstated — consumer adoption is the binding constraint and merchants would have to share savings as discounts. Hyperscalers cannot in-source this: no single merchant or cloud provider can replicate a global two-sided acceptance network and issuer relationships.

**Irreplaceability: 8/10** — near-irreplaceable today, docked for a credible 10–15-year rail-substitution path.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)

- Fiscal Q4 2026 (2026-10-27): net revenue \$12.1 billion (+13%), adjusted EPS \$3.48 (+17%, beat \$3.44 consensus), FY2026 revenue \$45.9 billion (+15%, "strongest growth in over 15 years ex-post-pandemic/acquisitions"), adjusted EPS \$13.27 (+16%).
- Dividend raised 16% to \$0.78/quarter (payable 2026-12-01, record 2026-11-10); \$4.0 billion buybacks in Q4, \$20.5 billion in FY2026, \$24.4 billion authorization remaining.
- Agreed to acquire fraud-intelligence provider BioCatch for \$2.4 billion cash, expected to close by end of fiscal Q2 2027.
- Shares slipped after hours despite the beat (closed \$401.86 on 2026-10-27, −0.57%) — guidance seen as moderate.

### 6. Risks

Stablecoin / real-time-rail disintermediation and interchange regulation (10% rate-cap proposal; interchange litigation — \$1.6 billion additional accruals in FY2026, ~\$15.9 billion unresolved claims per 10-K summary, Nov 2026); consumer-spending cyclicality; Middle East conflict pressuring CEMEA cross-border travel (management assumption: volumes stay indexed to Q4 levels); total shareholder returns (\$25.6 billion) exceeded FY2026 free cash flow (~\$22.1 billion) by ~\$3.5 billion.

## CME — CME Group Inc.

### 1. Shortage thesis

No physical shortage; the "shortage" is of hedging capacity in volatile markets, which is demand-driven and mean-reverts. Q3 2026 ADV hit a record 29.4 million contracts (+16% YoY), with double-digit volume growth across all six asset classes; market-data revenue hit a record \$243 million (+20%, 34th consecutive quarter of growth). The driver is persistent geopolitical uncertainty and rate/volatility hedging demand, plus international ADV of 9 million (+22%). There is no capacity constraint — CME can always list more contracts — so there is no pricing power from scarcity; average rate per contract actually fell to \$0.679 from \$0.702 (−3.3%).

### 2. Backlog / order book

Not applicable — no order book. Visibility proxy: October ADV running +17% vs last year's record Q4, and nine-month revenue \$5,323.6 million (+9.3%), putting 2026 on course for a fifth consecutive record year (Aardvark Labs recap, Oct 2026). CME does not guide volume or revenue.

### 3. Category position

Yes — #1. "The world's largest derivatives marketplace" / largest derivatives exchange operator by volume; Q1 2026 ADV was 36.2 million contracts (+22%). ICE is #2 in derivatives overall but leads in specific benchmarks (Brent, TTF); Cboe leads equity options/VIX. CME dominates US rates (SOFR, Treasuries), equity-index futures (E-mini S&P 500), ags and WTI.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk

Moat: benchmark ownership (SOFR, Treasury, E-mini contracts are the reference prices the world hedges against), clearinghouse network effects, \$100 billion/day average capital-efficiency savings for clients (Q3 2026 — a record), and deep open interest that cannot be moved without losing liquidity. Threats: (a) perpetual futures / crypto-native venues and 24/7 trading could siphon crypto and eventually rates volume if US regulation opens that door; (b) BGC Group competing in rates, ICE in energy; (c) clients could clear bilaterally or via other CCPs, but only at much higher margin cost. Hyperscalers are not a threat — they are customers (Google Cloud is CME's migration partner, not a rival exchange). The Google Cloud migration itself is the in-sourcing-adjacent risk in reverse: CME is outsourcing its own infrastructure to Google.

**Irreplaceability: 8/10** — benchmarks + clearing moat are extremely sticky; docked for venue competition at the margin.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)

- Q3 2026 (2026-10-21): revenue \$1,737.3 million (+13%, highest Q3 on record, second-highest quarter ever), adjusted EPS \$3.02 (+13%, beat ~\$2.98–\$2.99 consensus), adjusted operating margin 69.4% (+100 bps).
- Cloud migration milestones (2026-10-12): initial Globex-in-cloud performance testing showed faster round-trip times; Aurora Cloud Region construction ahead of schedule, client testing targeted mid-2027, livestock migration late 2027; CME "the only exchange to offer clients the choice of both co-location and cloud-managed services."
- New products: Bitcoin Cash and Uniswap futures launched October 2026 (crypto ADV averaged 195.8k contracts in Q3); AI-compute futures tied to Nvidia GPU rental costs; tungsten (critical minerals) futures; baseball futures launched 2026-10-12.
- Wells Fargo initiated coverage Overweight, \$301 price target, October 2026.

### 6. Risks

Volume is procyclical with volatility — a calm, low-rate-vol regime cuts ADV and revenue with high operating leverage; rate-per-contract erosion (−3.3% YoY); perpetual-futures regulation could either unlock or cap new volume; leadership transition (new CEO expected early 2026 per reports); premium valuation (~22x earnings) with a ~4.3% all-in dividend yield doing much of the return work.

## VRSN — VeriSign Inc.

### 1. Shortage thesis

A different kind of shortage: there is exactly one .com. VeriSign is the sole registry operator — a regulated monopoly, not a capacity shortage. Demand is surging again: record 13.1 million new .com/.net registrations in Q3 2026 (vs 10.6 million a year ago, 12.7 million in Q2), net adds of 3.36 million in the quarter, domain base 182.5 million (+6.1% YoY). Management credits registrar customer-acquisition efforts, its own marketing, and AI-tool adoption driving new business starts. Pricing power is contractual, not market-driven: the .com wholesale fee rises 7% to \$10.97 on 2026-11-01 (from \$10.26), the first of up to four annual 7% steps in the current six-year cycle (to ~\$13.45 by 2029 if all taken). Demand elasticity measured −0.04 over 2020–2026 — near-perfect inelasticity.

### 2. Backlog / order book

No order book, but the closest equivalent is deferred revenue: \$1.46 billion at 2026-09-30, up \$79 million from year-end 2025 (Q3 release, 2026-10-22). Registrations are prepaid for 1–10 years, so cash precedes revenue recognition. Domain-base growth guidance was raised and narrowed to 6.5–7.0% for 2026.

### 3. Category position

Yes — #1 by definition. Sole registry operator for .com and .net under ICANN / US Dept. of Commerce (NTIA) agreements; .com registry agreement runs to 2026-11-30 (renewal deadline 2030-11-30 per FY2024 10-K). No #2 exists for .com; the only substitutes are other TLDs (.ai, .io, new gTLDs), which do not substitute for .com's default status.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk

Moat: government-sanctioned monopoly with presumptive renewal "in perpetuity unless a material technical breach occurs"; operates 2 of the world's 13 DNS root servers and the authoritative registry; switching costs are existential for a business (losing its .com means losing its identity). No hyperscaler can in-source this — Google, Amazon and Microsoft are registrars/customers, not registries, and the .com concession is awarded by ICANN/NTIA, not won in the market. Substitute threats: new TLDs, social handles / app-based identity, and AI assistants reducing direct domain navigation — all slow, partial, and none removes the need for a .com for a serious business. Regulatory risk is the mirror image: the monopoly exists at the government's pleasure and the pricing freedom (7% steps) was itself a 2018 NTIA policy decision that a future administration could revisit.

**Irreplaceability: 10/10** — there is no second .com and no legal route around the registry.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)

- Q3 2026 (2026-10-22): revenue \$443 million (+5.7%), operating income \$296 million (+4.2%), diluted EPS \$2.35 (vs \$2.27; adjusted \$2.41 vs \$2.42 consensus — a rare slight miss, partly a \$7.5 million pre-tax loss on assets held for sale, −\$0.06 EPS).
- Guidance raised: FY2026 revenue \$1.755–\$1.760 billion, operating income \$1.188–\$1.192 billion; domain-base growth 6.5–7.0%.
- .com price rise to \$10.97 effective 2026-11-01 (announced 2026-04-23); .net rises 10% to \$12.00 on 2027-03-01.
- .web launch on track: Sunrise mid-December 2026, limited registration mid-January 2027, general availability potentially early April 2027; limited beta of Verisign Informed™ high-assurance validation service opened in Q3.

### 6. Risks

Renewal rate slipped to an expected 74.3% in Q3 (vs 75.4% a year ago) on a higher mix of first-time renewals — watch whether the AI-driven registration surge renews at lower rates; regulatory/political risk to the monopoly and to the 7% pricing steps (registrar coalitions protest each rise; a California class action is pending, unproven); growth is only mid-single-digit revenue despite the monopoly; valuation ~34x earnings for ~6% growth prices in the pricing power already.

### Earnings-quality flag — RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN (receivables growing faster than revenue)

**Verdict: benign — not a value-trap signature.** VeriSign's model is structurally prepaid: registrars pay the wholesale fee before the registration term is served, so cash normally precedes revenue and the balance sheet carries large *deferred revenue* (\$1.46 billion at 2026-09-30, +\$79 million YTD), not a receivables build from loose credit. A receivables uptick in this model most plausibly reflects billing timing around the quarter-end registration surge (record 13.1 million new registrations, +3.4 million net adds in Q3) and registrar settlement lags, not revenue recognised ahead of cash — the opposite pattern (deferred revenue growing) is the dominant, cash-first signal. The flag to actually watch is the Q1 2026 disclosure that operating cash flow fell YoY on "payment timing, higher cash taxes and lower customer cash receipts" even as earnings rose — timing, not fabrication, but worth confirming in the Q3 10-Q cash-flow statement before dismissing outright.

## UNP — Union Pacific Corp.

### 1. Shortage thesis

No shortage — freight capacity is ample and trucking is the swing substitute. The demand story is cyclical volume recovery, not scarcity: revenue carloads +6% in Q3 2026, record intermodal and industrial freight revenue. The binding constraint in rail is service reliability and crew/locomotive availability in surges, not structural undersupply; rail is only ~27% of US freight ton-miles, so shippers can and do switch to truck. Diesel is the swing cost: average fuel cost rose 64% to \$4.21/gallon in Q3, a 140-bps operating-ratio headwind — evidence of input-cost exposure, not pricing power from shortage.

### 2. Backlog / order book

Not applicable — no order book; railroads sell capacity trip-by-trip under contracts and tariffs. Visibility proxy: management reaffirmed high-single-digit reported EPS growth for 2026 (vs 2025 EPS \$11.98) and a 3-year CAGR target of high-single to low-double digit through 2027; capex raised to \$3.5 billion for 2026 (pulling forward 2027 infrastructure/locomotive work).

### 3. Category position

Co-#1 in the West. Union Pacific and BNSF form a near-duopoly west of the Mississippi; UP is the largest public railroad in North America, ~32,200 route miles across 23 states, FY2024 revenue \$24.25 billion (2025 ~\$24.5 billion) vs BNSF ~\$23.4 billion. Combined UP + Norfolk Southern would be ~40% of US rail revenue (BNSF ~38%) per UP's merger site (2025 data). UP has led the industry for years in revenue per unit, with a double-digit premium over the industry average (per BNSF's own STB filing critique).

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk

Moat: the physical network itself — 32,200 miles of track, terminals, and rights-of-way that cannot be replicated (no one can build a second transcontinental railroad); captive shippers in many corridors have no rail alternative. Disintermediation: shippers cannot in-source rail, but they can and do shift volume to trucking (the merger case itself argues the combined railroad would win 2.1 million truckloads/year *to* rail — i.e. the contest is rail vs truck, not rail vs rail). Hyperscalers are irrelevant here except as intermodal customers. The real bypass risk is regulatory: the STB could impose conditions or block the NS merger, and captive-shipper pricing power is exactly what regulators police.

**Irreplaceability: 7/10** — the track cannot be replicated, but trucking caps pricing power and the customer can always choose the highway.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)

- Q3 2026 (2026-10-22): record quarter — operating revenue \$7.2 billion (+15%, boosted ~9pts by fuel surcharge), net income \$2.1 billion (+16%), reported EPS \$3.49 (+16%), adjusted EPS \$3.53 (+15%, beat ~\$3.47 consensus); adjusted operating ratio 58.8% (core ratio improved 110 bps ex-fuel and merger costs); record freight revenue ex-fuel surcharge (+6%).
- Dividend raised 3% — 20th straight year of raises; shares +11.6% in the 30 days through late Oct 2026 (\~\$272 → ~\$304).
- Norfolk Southern merger (\$85 billion, announced 2025-07-29): STB procedural schedule published; comment period ends 2026-11-18; management expects first merged-company earnings ~a year out, close H2 2027; CEO Jim Vena said he is "99.999% sure" of approval. BNSF and the National Farmers Union oppose, arguing the combination would control ~50% of US rail volume.
- CFO Jennifer Hamann announced retirement in early 2027.

### 6. Risks

Merger is the whole thesis and it is binary: STB rejection or onerous conditions (trackage rights, rate constraints) would unwind the premium; fuel-cost volatility (diesel +64% YoY in Q3) directly hits the operating ratio; freight cyclicality (industrial, ag, intermodal volumes track the economy); integration risk on a \$85 billion deal; captive-shipper political backlash is already organised.

### 7. Shortage score and verdict

| Ticker | Shortage score (0-10) | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| UNP | 3 | A duopoly railroad with a record quarter, not a shortage — the explosive case is entirely the binary NS merger, and trucking caps any scarcity pricing. |

## WAB — Wabtec Corp.

### 1. Shortage thesis

The strongest genuine shortage in this batch. Locomotives are long-lead, capacity-constrained capital goods: management said order coverage is at "its strongest level to date, particularly for long-lead-time products such as new locomotives," with 24–36 months of coverage on locomotive programs (Q3 2026 call, 2026-10-21). Demand drivers: North American carload traffic +3.5% in Q3 with a larger active locomotive fleet than a year ago, more parts demand as parked locomotives return to service at higher utilisation, plus international fleet renewal (Africa, Brazil). Supply cannot respond quickly — locomotive production slots are booked years out, which is precisely the shortage signature (backlog, not spot price, is the evidence).

### 2. Backlog / order book

Total (multi-year) backlog \$31.44 billion at 2026-09-30, +22.9% YoY (from \$25.58 billion); 12-month backlog \$8.99 billion, +8.8% YoY. Freight multi-year backlog \$25.77 billion (+23.2%), Transit \$5.67 billion (+21.4%). Q3 orders: \$1 billion international locomotive/service awards (Africa, Brazil) and >\$600 million North American locomotive/modernisation orders from two Class I customers, plus \$159 million transit brake orders. At ~\$12.3–12.6 billion guided FY2026 revenue, the multi-year backlog is ~2.5x annual sales. About 30% of revenue is not in backlog (railcar-related, certain parts).

### 3. Category position

Yes — #1 in North American freight locomotives. Wabtec (ex-GE Transportation) holds ~70% of the North American diesel-electric locomotive market, vs ~30% for Caterpillar's Progress Rail / Electro-Motive Diesel — the only other significant competitor (industry sources cited in GE Transportation history; the duopoly structure persists post-GE/Wabtec merger). Globally it is a top-4 locomotive player alongside CRRC, Alstom and Siemens, but North America — the profit pool — is a two-player market it dominates.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk

Moat: installed base + proprietary platforms (the Evolution Series prime mover, Trip Optimizer, digital intelligence), certification/safety approvals, and a service/modernisation annuity on a fleet only Wabtec can fully support; railroads standardise fleets around one builder, making switching extremely costly. Can the big customers in-source? The customers *are* the railroads (UP, BNSF, CSX, NS) — none builds locomotives, and the capital, engineering and EPA certification barriers make entry prohibitive; Caterpillar/Progress Rail is the only credible alternative and it is the #2, not an in-sourcer. Substitute technology (battery/hydrogen locomotives) is a long-dated transition Wabtec itself leads rather than a bypass — it sells the new technology too. The realistic threat is demand-side: railroads deferring fleet renewal in a freight downturn.

**Irreplaceability: 8/10** — a 70% duopoly leader with a 2.5x-sales backlog; docked because the customer base is a handful of Class I railroads who can delay orders.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)

- Q3 2026 (2026-10-21): sales \$3.12 billion (+8.1%), adjusted EPS \$2.83 (+22%, beat \$2.72 consensus; GAAP EPS \$2.42, +33.7%), adjusted operating margin 22.9% (+1.9 pts; Freight +190 bps, Transit +260 bps), operating cash flow \$455 million (vs \$367 million).
- FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance raised and tightened to \$10.85–\$11.05 (from \$10.60–\$10.90), ~22% growth at the midpoint; revenue guidance unchanged at \$12.30–\$12.60 billion.
- Acquisitions performing well (Dellner in Transit); Integration 3.0 savings flowing through margins.
- Analyst consensus Moderate Buy, ~\$320 price target vs ~\$284 (MarketBeat, Nov 2026).

### 6. Risks

Customer concentration (a handful of Class I railroads; two accounted for the >\$600 million Q3 North American orders); North American railcar build forecast cut to ~23,000 units for 2026 (from 25,000, −26% YoY) — a soft spot in the portfolio; locomotive delivery timing slipped from Q3 into Q4 on customer acceptance; tariffs and currency headwinds; freight cyclicality can turn a 24–36-month backlog into deferrals; revenue missed consensus (\$3.12 billion vs \$3.18 billion) even as EPS beat.

### 7. Shortage score and verdict
