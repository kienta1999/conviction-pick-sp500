## CBOE — Cboe Global Markets

### 1. Shortage thesis

Not a physical-product shortage — a demand surge in a capacity-unconstrained but legally exclusive market. The driver is 0DTE SPX options: index options ADV rose 24% YoY to 6.1 million contracts in Q3 2026, with 0DTE a record 65% of overall SPX activity; retail SPX 0DTE volume jumped 46% YoY (institutional +20%), retail now an estimated 58% of record SPX 0DTE volume vs 53% a year ago (CBOE Q3 2026 earnings call, Oct 30, 2026). XSP volume more than doubled YoY after the June 2026 repeal of the pattern day trader rule; Global Trading Hours volume jumped over 130%, Asia now over 30% of GTH volume vs under 20% a year ago. Derivatives net revenue +22% YoY to \$402 million; total net revenue +18% to \$712 million. The "shortage" is of the only legal venue for SPX options — demand growth is not supply-constrained, it is monetised per contract, so the surge flows straight to revenue.

### 2. Backlog / order book

No backlog concept applies to an exchange (no order book of future deliveries). Closest equivalents: SPX options set a record annual volume of 970.6 million contracts in 2025, ADV 3.9 million (+25% YoY, 4th consecutive annual record); open interest recently surpassed 22 million contracts (Cboe / S&P DJI PR, Sep 29, 2026). Q3 2026 index options ADV of 6.1 million is running well above the 2025 daily average — demand is booked in flow, not backlog.

### 3. Category position

\#1 and effectively sole player in its core category. Cboe is the largest U.S. options exchange and holds the exclusive license to list options on the S&P 500 Index (SPX); it also administers the VIX. No other exchange may list SPX options. Multi-listed options (where it competes with Nasdaq/NYSE) grew a solid 10% YoY, but the proprietary index complex is a legal monopoly.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk

Moat: a 25-year exclusive licensing agreement with S&P Dow Jones Indices, signed Sep 29, 2026, extending exclusive SPX options rights through 2051 (prior term ran to 2032/33). Exclusivity also covers Mini-SPX, VIX and S&P binary options, and carries over to tokenized options contracts. Customers cannot in-source this: no bank, broker or hyperscaler can list SPX options without Cboe's license, and the S&P 500 is "the most widely tracked index in the world" (Cboe PR). Substitute threat is real but indirect: traders could use SPY/QQQ ETF options, CME S&P 500 futures options, or OTC — but none replicates SPX's cash settlement, tax treatment and liquidity, and 0DTE flow is concentrated on Cboe. The KPI binary-options franchise (see catalysts) adds a new regulated prediction-market lane. **Irreplaceability: 10/10** — a contractually exclusive franchise through 2051.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)

- Sep 29, 2026: 25-year S&P DJI exclusive license extension through 2051; shares +5.17% to ~\$275.29 on Sep 30 on 2.5x volume.
- Sep 30, 2026 (HOOD Summit): planned launch of SEC-regulated KPI binary options with Robinhood as exclusive initial launch broker, covering 23 U.S.-listed equities, fees waived through year-end 2026.
- Oct 13, 2026: SEC approved listing of KPI binary options (CoinDesk, Oct 16) — cash-settled, European-style, settling on SEC-filing figures; launch planned in October, pending Cboe Clear U.S. provisional registration.
- Oct 30, 2026: Q3 beat — net revenue \$712 million (+18%), adjusted diluted EPS \$3.63 (+36%) vs \$3.43 consensus; raised 2026 organic net revenue guidance to high-teens from mid-to-high teens; \$71.5 million gain on Cboe Australia sale to TMX (closed Jul 31); adjusted cash balance \$2.7 billion at Sep 30.
- Analysts: Morgan Stanley raised price target to \$450 from \$358, Overweight, named Top Pick (late Oct, per Tickeron); JPMorgan raised target to \$332 from \$288 but kept Underweight — shares +6.3% to \$334.02 on Oct 30. Shares +31.9% in the 30 days to ~Oct 26 (\$258 → \$340).

### 6. Risks

- Valuation already pricing it in: +32% in a month, ~\$340–355 vs JPMorgan's \$332 fair-value view; the KPI revenue stream is not yet in consensus and unproven.
- Cyclicality: options volume is volatility- and sentiment-driven; VIX options ADV rose only 3% YoY in a stable-volatility quarter and futures net revenue fell 5% — a calm market caps the VIX leg.
- Regulatory/execution: KPI launch still needs Cboe Clear U.S. provisional registration; prediction-market competition from CFTC-regulated Kalshi and others.
- Royalty step-up in 2027 (guided de minimis, to be offset by pricing) and strategic realignment costs (~20% workforce reduction announced earlier).

## ISRG — Intuitive Surgical

### 1. Shortage thesis

Demand surge, not a supply shortage: hospitals cannot get enough robotic surgery capacity fast enough, and ISRG is the bottleneck supplier. Q3 2026: record 546 da Vinci placements (+28% YoY), including 315 da Vinci 5 (58% of placements), 42 SP, 65 XiR, 56 Ion; installed base ~13,200 systems (da Vinci 12,094, Ion 1,152), +13% YoY. Procedures +15% YoY (da Vinci +14%, Ion +31%); da Vinci 5 now over one-quarter of U.S. procedures vs 13% a year ago. Revenue +22% to \$3.05 billion; recurring revenue 84% of total. The constraint is hospital capital budgets and surgeon training, not ISRG supply — but placement growth of 28% with a richer da Vinci 5 mix shows demand outrunning the installed base's replacement cycle. No lead-time or sold-out capacity quote was found.

### 2. Backlog / order book

ISRG does not disclose a dollar backlog. Placement flow is the proxy: 546 systems in Q3 2026 vs 427 a year earlier (+28%); U.S. net placements +41% YoY; 18 systems to U.S. ambulatory surgery centers (15 XiR). Installed base 12,094 da Vinci systems, +12% YoY. No "booked out to" horizon disclosed — not found.

### 3. Category position

Clear \#1 in soft-tissue surgical robotics, effectively unchallenged for two decades: ~3.15 million da Vinci procedures in 2025 (+18%), 15 million cumulative procedures approaching, 20 million patients treated milestone (Jan 2026). \#2 is now Medtronic Hugo (FDA-cleared Dec 3, 2025 for urology; U.S. rollout began Feb 2026) — no published procedure count, so no share figure exists; J&J's Ottava received FDA De Novo authorisation Jul 22, 2026, CMR Versius is the cost-sensitive-market challenger. ISRG's installed-base lead is an order of magnitude: 12,094 da Vinci systems vs Hugo/Versius in the low hundreds in the U.S.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk

Moat: 4,500+ granted patents (4,200+ active per Lexology), 76,000+ surgeons trained on da Vinci, 25 years of procedural data, FDA clearances across specialties, and a razor-and-blades model (instruments/services 84% recurring). Hospitals cannot in-source a surgical robot; the threat is rival platforms, not customer self-build. That threat has just become real: Hugo and Ottava are now FDA-authorised in 75.6% of U.S. robotic-surgery volume specialties (Axis Intelligence, Aug 2026), and Stryker invested ~\$400 million in CMR Surgical. But hospital capital cycles run 12–24 months, so "no visible impact yet" (Lexology) — ISRG revenue growth actually widened after Hugo's clearance. The \$450 million antitrust settlement over third-party instrument remanufacturing/system servicing (Q3 2026) cracks the aftermarket moat slightly. **Irreplaceability: 8/10** — unmatched installed base and training ecosystem, but the two-decade monopoly has just ended.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)

- Oct 20, 2026: Q3 beat — revenue \$3.05 billion (+22%, consensus ~\$2.91 billion), non-GAAP EPS \$3.00 (+25%, consensus ~\$2.65); GAAP EPS \$1.49 after the \$450 million antitrust accrual (~\$0.99/share). Shares fell ~7–13% after the print despite the beat (see risks).
- da Vinci 5 cardiac clearances: FDA early 2026, CE mark in Q3; now cleared for cardiac in U.S., Europe, India, Japan, Korea; cardiac procedures +40% in Q3, breast +42%; line-of-sight cardiac opportunity 300,000–400,000 procedures/year in cleared regions. Force Feedback used in ~8% of da Vinci 5 cases; SP transvaginal CE mark; Ion reimbursement in Korea.
- Capacity: new instruments-and-accessories manufacturing facility in Penang, Malaysia, operations starting 2028, to support Asia growth (India procedures +42%, 200,000+ cumulative).
- Buybacks: \$2.94 billion in Q3 (\$4.45 billion YTD), 7.8 million shares at ~\$377 average.
- Guidance: FY26 da Vinci procedure growth narrowed to 14–15% (from 13.5–15.5%), non-GAAP gross margin raised to 68.7–69.3%; telesurgery clearances expected H1 2028.

### 6. Risks

- The market's verdict on Q3: U.S. da Vinci procedure growth slowed to 10% — lowest since COVID (Wells Fargo) — for a third straight quarter of deceleration, and management will cut instrument revenue per procedure by 3–5% from H1 2027 (extended-use instruments, volume pricing, China price cut from Nov 2026) to buy penetration in benign procedures — a direct hit to the razor-blade annuity.
- Competition is now real and funded (Hugo, Ottava, Versius/Stryker); price competition in capital sales is coming.
- China remains difficult (few placements, policy-driven price cut); tariffs ~0.9pp gross-margin drag in FY26; ACA/Medicaid/340B policy watch.
- Valuation: ~37x trailing EPS at the post-earnings price (~\$405 after-hours Oct 20), still expensive for mid-teens procedure growth.

## WST — West Pharmaceutical Services

### 1. Shortage thesis

Structural shortage of high-value injectable containment capacity relative to GLP-1/biologics demand. HVP components grew 20% organically in Q3 2026 to \$467 million (53% of company revenue); GLP-1 HVP revenue grew "well into the 30% range" organically and is just under 12% of total revenue; non-GLP-1 HVP grew high-teens; biologics market +23.2% organically; Asia-Pacific +41.9% led by India/China GLP-1 introductions. West added 200+ HVP upgrade projects in 2026, driven by stricter sterile-manufacturing regulation. Management is starting "another round of HVP finishing-capacity investments in Europe and the U.S." taking 18–24 months to make a meaningful contribution — i.e. demand today exceeds qualified finishing capacity, and the fix is two years out. No explicit "sold out" or lead-time quote was found, but the capacity-investment timing is the evidence.

### 2. Backlog / order book

No dollar backlog disclosed. Proxies: 200+ HVP upgrade projects added YTD 2026; HVP delivery devices \$99 million (+26% organically ex-FX/SmartDose); the Dublin drug-handling programme ramping toward a targeted \$60 million+ revenue at 30%+ gross margin. Contract-manufacturing (West Vantage) is the weak leg — see risks. No "booked out to" horizon disclosed — not found.

### 3. Category position

\#1 in injectable-drug elastomer containment by scale and breadth (FY2025 net sales ~\$3.3 billion; ~41 billion components/devices delivered annually; 26 manufacturing facilities). Named rivals in its FY2025 10-K: Datwyler and Aptar in elastomer components; Stevanato Group in glass/containment and devices (Stevanato TTM revenue ~\$1.34 billion vs West ~\$3.4 billion — West is roughly 2.5x its nearest listed pure-play rival). No published market-share percentage was found; West's edge is portfolio breadth, coatings, inspection and regulatory support, not a single-product monopoly.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk

Moat: regulatory qualification lock-in. A stopper/plunger is specified in a drug's FDA filing; switching supplier means re-qualification, stability studies and regulatory risk for a component worth cents on a drug worth thousands — no pharma customer will risk a \$10 billion GLP-1 franchise to save on closures. Customers (Lilly, Novo) cannot realistically in-source elastomer compounding, coating and sterile finishing at West's scale and quality record, and would not want the regulatory liability. Substitute threat: oral GLP-1s (Novo Wegovy pill, Lilly Foundayo) — KeyBanc estimates orals add only ~200bps to total GLP-1 share by 2030 and injectables remain the primary driver; West and Stevanato both assume orals reach ~30% of the GLP-1 market eventually, which would slow but not kill HVP growth. **Irreplaceability: 8/10** — qualification moat is very strong; the bypass route is therapeutic (oral), not a rival component.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)

- Oct 29, 2026: Q3 beat — revenue \$874.5 million (+8.7% reported, +11.9% organic; consensus \$827.1 million), adjusted EPS \$2.66 (+36%; consensus \$2.20), adjusted operating margin 25.3% (+420bps), gross margin 39.7% vs 36.6%.
- Guidance raised: FY2026 revenue to \$3.41–3.42 billion (from \$3.345–3.38 billion), adjusted EPS to \$9.38–9.48. New CEO Michel Lagarde (joined ~Aug 2026) presiding over first print.
- HVP finishing-capacity expansion announced (Europe + U.S., 18–24 months to contribution); CapEx guided \$215–220 million (low end of 6–8% of revenue).
- Buybacks: \$231 million in Q3, \$685 million YTD (2.4 million+ shares); revolver upsized to \$750 million, renewed 5 years.

### 6. Risks

- West Vantage (contract manufacturing) organic revenue −6.6% as a legacy CGM programme rolls off faster than new programmes ramp; trough in Q3; SmartDose 3.5 divestiture to AbbVie creates a ~290bps headwind to reported growth in 2027 (Q3 SmartDose revenue only \$12 million vs \$30 million a year ago, no further revenue expected in 2026).
- Earnings-quality flag — see the RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN brief below; free cash flow down ~5% YTD despite +36% EPS.
- GLP-1 concentration: ~12% of revenue is GLP-1 HVP and rising; an oral shift, GLP-1 price war, or Lilly/Novo demand air-pocket would hit the growth engine. Capacity investments take 18–24 months — mistimed if demand cools.
- Valuation: shares ~\$412 after the print (down ~1.1% on Oct 30 despite the beat) and one DCF-based note flagged ~30% overvaluation; the beat may already be priced after a strong run into earnings.

### Earnings-quality flag brief — WST: RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN

**Flag verdict: mostly benign timing, watch Q4 collection — not a value-trap signature yet.**

The numbers (10-Q for the quarter ended Sep 30, 2026, filed Oct 29, 2026): accounts receivable, net \$748.2 million at Sep 30, 2026 vs \$574.4 million at Dec 31, 2025 (+30.3% in nine months), while nine-month net sales grew roughly 12% organically — receivables are clearly outrunning revenue. Operating cash flow fell \$100.3 million YoY to \$403.4 million (from \$503.7 million) and nine-month free cash flow fell ~5% to \$278 million; the 10-Q attributes the decline "primarily to increases in customer accounts receivable and timing of collections as well as increased inventory levels to support our operations" (inventories \$464.7 million vs \$443.9 million at year-end). Management on the call said the same, added it was "an improvement from Q2's results" and expects "continued improvement in Q4."

Benign case: West sells to large, investment-grade pharma customers (Lilly, Novo and other biologics makers) on normal trade terms — these are not distressed payers, and a surge in GLP-1/biologics shipments late in the quarter mechanically lifts receivables before cash lands; inventory build is explicitly to support operations into the capacity-constrained HVP ramp. No bad-debt, channel-stuffing or revenue-recognition concern was raised on the call or in the 10-Q, and margins expanded 420bps, which is not the profile of a company stuffing low-quality sales.

Value-trap check: earnings are running ahead of cash — that is factually true this year (net income \$506.8 million for nine months vs operating cash flow \$403.4 million). But the gap is working capital (receivables + inventory), not non-cash earnings fabrication, and management has named Q4 as the collection quarter. Verdict: **flag is explained by shipment timing into large-pharma customers plus inventory build for the HVP ramp; downgrade to watch-item, not a trap — re-test on Q4: if receivables keep outrunning revenue while FCF stays flat, it becomes a real earnings-quality problem.**

## LLY — Eli Lilly

### 1. Shortage thesis

The shortage era is over; the thesis is now a demand surge Lilly can finally supply. FDA declared the tirzepatide shortage ended Dec 19, 2024 and semaglutide resolved Feb 2025 (both shortages cleared by May 2025). Q3 2026 volume rose 53% while realised prices fell 15% — Lilly is now selling into demand with capacity, not rationing it. Mounjaro \$10.7 billion (+65% YoY) and Zepbound \$5.35 billion (+49%); together ~\$16.1 billion, two-thirds of Lilly's \$24.4 billion quarterly revenue. The structural driver remains: tirzepatide is the efficacy leader (SURMOUNT-5: 21.6% mean weight loss at 72 weeks vs semaglutide 15.4%), and Lilly holds over 60% of the U.S. incretin market (up from ~40% a year ago), with Mounjaro over 55% of new T2D incretin prescriptions and Zepbound nearly 70% of new branded obesity prescriptions (Q1 2026 data). Manufacturing is the constraint that matters now: Lilly halved its planned €2.3 billion Alzey, Germany injectable plant to ~€1.15 billion (headcount 1,000 → 500) in June 2026 citing German rebate reform, redirecting capital to U.S. sites including the \$4.5 billion Indiana "Medicine Foundry" — new plants take years to qualify, so any quality problem at a key injectable site would show up quickly in sales.

### 2. Backlog / order book

No backlog disclosed (pharma sells through wholesalers/pharmacies, not an order book). Demand proxy: Q3 volume +53% YoY; full-year 2026 revenue guidance raised by \$5.5 billion at the midpoint to \$91–92 billion (from \$85–87 billion). No "booked out" figure — not found.

### 3. Category position

\#1 in incretins, having overtaken Novo Nordisk in early 2025: ~60.9% of the U.S. incretin market vs Novo ~38.8% (BioNixus, Oct 2026); tirzepatide 2025 sales \$36.5 billion vs Novo's three semaglutide brands ~\$34.5 billion — the first year tirzepatide outsold semaglutide. Novo is the only rival with scale and it is losing ground: Novo cut 2025 guidance four times, guided 2026 adjusted sales −5% to −13%, and its CagriSema failed to prove at least as effective as tirzepatide (REDEFINE 4, Feb 2026). Global GLP-1 market ~\$70 billion in 2025, forecast \$87–101 billion in 2026; Morgan Stanley sees \$190 billion by 2035.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk

Moat: patents (U.S. tirzepatide patent to ~2036), superior efficacy data, manufacturing scale, and the pipeline (retatrutide filing Q1 2027). Customers are patients/payers, not hyperscalers — nobody "in-sources" a drug; the bypass routes are (a) rival molecules — retatrutide's head-to-head win over Ozempic (see catalysts) actually widens Lilly's lead; (b) oral GLP-1s — Novo's Wegovy pill leads orals (~90% of U.S. oral obesity scripts, 5 million scripts in 7 months) vs Lilly's Foundayo, which missed in its first full quarter (\$184 million vs ~\$217–229 million expected); (c) compounding — now restricted since the shortage ended; (d) generics — semaglutide composition-of-matter patent expired in China Mar 20, 2026, but tirzepatide is protected to ~2036 in the U.S. **Irreplaceability: 9/10** — the molecule, the data and the capacity are not replicable by any customer; the threat is the next molecule, and Lilly owns that too (retatrutide).

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)

- Oct 28, 2026: retatrutide TRANSCEND-T2D-2 head-to-head vs Ozempic 2 mg — A1C −2.2pts vs −1.8; weight loss 19.5% (44.3 lbs) at 9 mg and 20.9% (47.4 lbs) at 12 mg vs 9.4% (20.9 lbs) on Ozempic at 80 weeks (n=1,298); six Phase 3 wins now; FDA filing planned Q1 2027 (early 2027); Leerink models ~\$15 billion retatrutide sales in 2032 (vs ~\$10 billion consensus). Discontinuations higher (4.8%/7.9% vs 2.7% Ozempic).
- Sep 30, 2026: TRIUMPH-2 published in The Lancet — up to 20.8% weight loss at 80 weeks in obesity + T2D.
- Oct 29, 2026: Q3 beat — revenue \$24.4 billion (+39%, consensus ~\$22.6 billion), non-GAAP EPS \$7.48 (consensus ~\$6.26–6.61) even after \$4.57/share of acquired IPR&D charges; reported EPS \$7.22 (+16%). Guidance: revenue \$91–92 billion (+ \$5.5 billion midpoint), performance margin raised to 52.0–52.5% (from 49.0–50.5%); underlying EPS raised \$4.07 at midpoint, more than offset by the \$4.57 IPR&D charges, so headline EPS guidance dipped to \$35–36 (from \$35.50–36.50). Note ~\$900 million of Q3 revenue was non-recurring (\$410 million final Jardiance milestones — no further milestones remain — plus favourable rebate/discount adjustments).
- Regulatory: FDA approvals of Mounjaro for cardiovascular risk reduction, Onswik once-weekly basal insulin (approved Sep 24, 2026), Inluriyo + Verzenio, expanded Jaypirca; Foundayo oral GLP-1 approved Apr 1, 2026 (first non-peptide oral GLP-1, no food/water restrictions).
- Mounjaro added to China's National Reimbursement Drug List in Q1 2026; international Mounjaro revenue nearly doubled in Q3.

### 6. Risks

- Concentration: one molecule (tirzepatide) is ~two-thirds of revenue and 56% of 2025 revenue — the Novo trap in reverse; retatrutide will partly cannibalise Zepbound/Mounjaro, so the gain is the difference, not the total.
- Price: realised prices fell 15% in Q3 (Zepbound discounting, China NRDL inclusion); U.S. prices \$499–1,086 per 28-day Zepbound fill; IRA negotiation cut semaglutide's 2027 sticker ~70% — pricing pressure is structural.
- Oral lag: Foundayo missed expectations in its first full quarter while Novo's Wegovy pill took ~90% of oral scripts; orals could reach ~30% of the GLP-1 market.
- Pipeline/competition: CagriSema FDA decision Q4 2026, Amgen MariTide, Roche, AstraZeneca, Pfizer and Chinese incretins in motion; retatrutide's higher GI discontinuation rate is a real tolerability overhang.
- Valuation: the market already pays up for the next five years on retatrutide; any Phase 3 stumble or FDA delay hits a premium multiple.
