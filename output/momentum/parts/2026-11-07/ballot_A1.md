# Panelist A — Supply-chain analyst — Single-pick ballot — 2026-11-07

- **Top pick:** WAB
- **Runner-up:** MSI
- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** Wabtec is the only name in the field where the shortage is measured in years of booked production, not in a spot price or a quarterly demand surge. Its multi-year backlog of \$31.44bn is about 2.5x guided FY2026 revenue, with 24–36 months of coverage on long-lead locomotive programmes, so revenue visibility extends well beyond the 12–18-month horizon. It holds about 70% of the North American diesel-electric locomotive market in a two-player duopoly, and railroads standardise fleets around one builder, locking in a service and modernisation annuity on the installed base. Q3 showed the backlog converting profitably, with adjusted EPS up 22% and adjusted operating margin up 1.9 points, and FY2026 EPS guidance was raised to \$10.85–\$11.05. That combination — booked demand, a dominant share, and expanding margins — is the explosive-upside setup this lens is built to find.
- **Key shortage/backlog evidence:** Total multi-year backlog \$31.44bn at 30 September 2026, up 22.9% year-on-year, with management describing order coverage as at its strongest level to date and 24–36 months of coverage on new locomotives.
- **Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk:** Low — the customers are the Class I railroads, none of which builds locomotives, and entry requires capital, engineering and EPA certification that make a new entrant prohibitive; Caterpillar/Progress Rail is the only credible alternative and it is the number-two incumbent, not an in-sourcer. Battery and hydrogen locomotives are a long-dated transition that Wabtec itself leads rather than a bypass.
- **12-18 month return scenario:** Base case roughly +19% to +25%, in line with the analyst target of \$332.08 (+19.2% from \$278.48) as the 12-month backlog of \$8.99bn converts and margins hold; bull case roughly +35% to +45% if locomotive deliveries accelerate, international orders keep compounding the backlog, and the market re-rates a 2.5x-sales backlog toward a higher multiple.
- **Top risk:** Freight cyclicality — a downturn could turn the 24–36-month backlog into deferrals, and customer concentration means a handful of Class I railroads drive the order flow.
- **Conviction (1-10):** 8
