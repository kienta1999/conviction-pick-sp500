## ANET — Arista Networks

**1. Shortage thesis.** Yes — AI-fabric networking is the binding constraint on GPU-cluster build-outs, and Arista is supply-constrained, not demand-constrained. On the Q3 2025 call management said "demand far exceeds supply, with shipments constrained by component availability (38–52 weeks lead time)". The constraint persists into 2026: purchase commitments rose to $11.4B in Q3 2026 (from $9.7B in Q2) "mainly reflecting chips for new products and AI deployments", and management cited "agreements covering silicon, PCB capacity and memory" as the basis for executing the FY2027 plan. Driver: hyperscalers wiring 100k+ GPU clusters at 800G moving to 1.6T; Ethernet has displaced InfiniBand in AI back-end networks (over two-thirds of data-center switch sales in AI back-end by late 2025, up from under half a year earlier).

**2. Backlog / order book.** Arista does not disclose a formal backlog figure. Proxies: purchase commitments $11.4B (Q3 2026, +$1.7B QoQ); deferred revenue $4.7B (Q3 2025, volatile due to acceptance clauses in AI contracts); inventories $3.21B (Q3 2026, up from $2.25B end-2025) built ahead of shipments. Visibility: FY2026 revenue raised to $13.3B (+48%) and an initial FY2027 guide of ~$18.5B (+38–40%) — effectively a booked-out growth path, with AI fabrics guided to ~$7B in 2027 (from ≥$3.5B in 2026).

**3. Category position.** #1 in data-center switching — overtook Cisco in 2024 and "hasn't looked back" (Arista, via Fierce Network, Jul 2026). IDC Q1 2026: Nvidia took the data-center Ethernet lead at 21.5% vs Arista 20.7% vs Cisco 17.8% — so the #1 spot is now contested quarter-to-quarter; a later Evercore note put Nvidia 20.4%, Arista 18.7%, Cisco 18.2%. Arista is the clear #1 in cloud/high-speed (100/200/400G ports) and the leading independent (non-GPU-bundled) vendor.

**4. Irreplaceability.** Moat: EOS — one operating system/management stack across every box from 1G campus to 1.6T AI spines — plus CloudVision telemetry and deep hyperscaler co-design relationships. Hyperscalers cannot easily in-source: Meta and Microsoft each exceed 10% of revenue and buy Arista precisely because building a carrier-grade NOS + switch OS at scale is a multi-year software effort; Arista's bet is open Ethernet (co-founder of Ultra Ethernet Consortium and ESUN at OCP) as the alternative to Nvidia lock-in. Threat is real, not hypothetical: Nvidia's Spectrum-X (Ethernet + BlueField + NVLink scale-up) is the direct substitute, and Nvidia already leads data-center Ethernet share; Arista has no scale-up (intra-rack) answer until trials in 2027/production 2028. **Irreplaceability: 7/10.**

**5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months).** Q3 2026 (Nov 3, 2026): revenue $3.66B, +58.5% YoY — fastest since IPO — beat $3.335B consensus; non-GAAP EPS $1.20 vs $1.08 expected; Q4 guide $3.9–4.0B revenue, EPS $1.26–1.30; FY2026 outlook raised for the 4th time in 2026 to $13.3B; initial FY2027 guide +38–40% to ~$18.5B. Etherlink customers >100 in 2026 (from 4–5 at start of AI journey). June 2026: next-gen 1.6T portfolio launched; open rack-scale architectures with AMD, Broadcom, Meta, Microsoft. Analysts Nov 4: UBS raised target $259→$291 (Buy), JPMorgan $250→$270 (Overweight), Piper Sandler $240→$261 (top pick), Morgan Stanley $220→$230, Truist $234→$240; Rosenblatt reiterated Buy/$280 on Nov 22 after the selloff.

**6. Risks.** Customer concentration: Microsoft and Meta each >10% of revenue; a capex pause at either hits hard. Nvidia Spectrum-X is taking share and owns scale-up networking. Supply: 38–52-week component lead times and $11.4B purchase commitments mean any demand air-pocket leaves inventory/margin pain (inventory already $3.21B, turns down to 1.6). Valuation: shares fell 11.6% on Nov 20, 2026 in a broad AI selloff (S&P −3.7%) despite the blowout quarter — the market is pricing AI-capex sustainability risk, not Arista execution. Gross margin guided down to 62–64% on mix/investment.

**7. Shortage score: 9/10. Verdict:** The purest AI-networking shortage play in the batch — demand visibly exceeds supply, guidance was raised four times in 2026, and FY2027 is pre-guided at +38–40% — but Nvidia's Spectrum-X is a genuine share threat and concentration + the Nov 20 selloff show the tape can punish it anyway.

## APH — Amphenol

**1. Shortage thesis.** Yes — AI data-center interconnect (high-speed copper, fiber, power) is capacity-constrained. Evidence: data-center fibre demand grew ~76% last year and is projected to rise from <5% of global fibre demand in 2024 to ~30% by 2027; "cable lead times have stretched toward a full year, at least one major manufacturer has sold its entire output through 2026" (Digital Infrastructure Institute, 2026); Tier-1 connector brands quote 16+ week lead times and are adding material surcharges, with factory capacity "increasingly allocated to 224G/448G high-speed production lines" (Airoadcon, Aug 2026). Copper ~$13,300/ton (+28%) and a 150k–330k-ton copper deficit in 2026 add cost pressure. Amphenol demonstrated 448 Gbps-per-channel XtremePass co-packaged copper at DesignCon 2026; its Paladin connectors are designed into Nvidia's Vera Rubin VR200 NVL144 midplane architecture.

**2. Backlog / order book.** Record Q3 2026 orders of $12.17B, +99% YoY and +13% sequentially — 1.2x quarterly sales of $10.23B (book-to-bill ~1.19). Orders exceeded the midpoint of Q4 sales guidance by 17%, i.e. more orders in one quarter than next quarter's expected shipments. IT Datacom backlog is "stretching further out than usual" (BofA question on Q3 call); management said longer-dated orders are normal and most demand comes from highly capitalized customers, not neoclouds. Q4 2026 guidance: sales $10.3–10.5B, adj. EPS $0.81–0.83; FY2026 sales $36.9–37.1B (~+60%), adj. EPS $2.82–2.84 (~+70%).

**3. Category position.** #2 globally in connectors overall — TE Connectivity #1 (~15.8–18.2% share), Amphenol #2 (~10.1–12.4%), Molex #3 (~8.0–8.7%) per 2026 industry surveys. But #1 where it matters for this thesis: military connectors, 36.19% share in 2025 (vs TE 14.42%), and the dominant AI-datacom interconnect supplier by growth — IT Datacom is 48% of sales and ~3/4 of it is AI. The Jan 2026 $10.5B acquisition of CommScope's connectivity/cable business (ANDREW brand) materially widened its fibre/cable portfolio.

**4. Irreplaceability.** Moat: breadth (top-10 in 10 of 11 connector sectors), qualification/validation cycles in harsh-environment and mil-spec products, and co-design with every accelerator vendor — "every accelerator, every rack and every data center needs interconnect. Amphenol sells it to all of them" (Zacks, Nov 2026). Hyperscalers cannot realistically in-source connectors: it is a fragmented, precision-manufacturing, standards-driven business with low unit economics per part but extreme qualification barriers; the risk is substitution at the architecture level (optics replacing copper inside the rack, co-packaged optics) rather than in-sourcing. TTI's Jun 2026 supply-chain work: "copper when possible, optics when necessary", hybrid copper/optics persisting through at least the next two AI rack generations, full optical scale-up not before 2029. **Irreplaceability: 6/10** — dominant and diversified, but connectors are ultimately a multi-sourced component, not a platform.

**5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months).** Q3 2026 (Oct 28, 2026): record sales $10.23B (+65% YoY, +37% organic), first $10B quarter; adj. EPS $0.80 (+74%) beat $0.73 consensus; IT Datacom +100% (+77% organic), AI-related datacom revenue +117%, AI ~3/4 of segment; FY2026 IT Datacom sales expected to more than double. Acquisitions: closed Empire Electronics (~$320M 2026 revenue); signed definitive deals for Advanced PCB, DexScreen, Humanetics. Q2 2026 (Aug): sales $8.76B (+55%). Note: shares fell ~5.7% Nov 10–16, 2026 (closed $75.43 Nov 16) on valuation/AI-sustainability concerns despite the record orders — the market is questioning 2027 profit visibility.

**6. Risks.** Cyclicality: connectors are historically cyclical; the current 99% order growth embeds AI-capex continuation — if hyperscaler spending pauses, orders (1.2x sales) can reverse fast. Neocloud funding risk was raised on the call (management dismissed it, citing highly capitalized customers). Input costs: copper/gold/silver at multi-year highs squeeze margins or force price increases. Q4 IT Datacom guided to only mid-single-digit sequential growth after +30% QoQ in Q3 — a near-term moderation. Debt: $17.9B total debt / $12.6B net debt after the CommScope deal. Valuation already reflects much of the AI story (stock +19.6% over 12m despite the November pullback; analysts' 2027 forecast $45.7B revenue / $3.64 EPS implies slower growth).

**7. Shortage score: 8/10. Verdict:** The broadest, most diversified AI-shortage exposure — record $12.17B quarterly orders at 1.2x sales with fibre/copper lead times stretching toward a year — but it is a component supplier, not a platform, so the explosive upside is capped by multi-sourcing and cyclicality.

## MSFT — Microsoft

**1. Shortage thesis.** Yes — and stated in management's own words. CFO Amy Hood on the Q1 FY2027 call (Oct 28, 2026): "Customer demand continues to exceed our available supply" / "we still have demand that exceeds supply." Azure grew 48% despite delayed third-party capacity deliveries; capex was $45.2B in the quarter (75% short-lived AI assets) and would have been higher if deliveries had arrived on time. Microsoft added 24 data centers in the quarter across five continents and still could not meet demand. Driver: AI inference/training demand (Foundry revenue more than tripled YoY) plus enterprise Copilot/M365 migration; AI is now ~30% of the cloud backlog.

**2. Backlog / order book.** Commercial remaining performance obligation (RPO) $677B at Sep 30, 2026, +73% YoY (10-Q; ~$678B at Jun 30, 2026 per 8-K recast), weighted-average duration ~2.3 years, ~30% expected as revenue within 12 months. Commercial bookings +112% YoY in the quarter, driven by Azure commitments from OpenAI plus more $100M+ Azure and M365 contracts. Post-quarter: OpenAI contracted an incremental $250B of Azure services (announced Oct 2026, excluded from the $677B); Anthropic pledged up to $49B to Azure (Nov 2026) while building its own data centers.

**3. Category position.** #2 in cloud infrastructure: Synergy Q3 2026 — AWS 26%, Microsoft 19%, Google Cloud 16% (Big Three ~61%). But #1 in growth among the two leaders (Azure +48% vs AWS +39%) and #1 in backlog ($677B vs AWS $578B, Google $561B — note scopes differ). Azure revenue $33.2B in the quarter, ~72% of AWS's size vs 58% two years ago. In enterprise software (M365/Office, Windows) Microsoft is the undisputed #1.

**4. Irreplaceability.** Moat: the enterprise stack — M365 + Azure + Copilot + Foundry + exclusive OpenAI API/IP rights (rev share and API exclusivity until AGI or through 2030; model/product IP rights extended through 2032). Customers cannot in-source a hyperscale cloud; the realistic disintermediation is the reverse — Microsoft's biggest AI customers (OpenAI, Anthropic) are multi-sourcing (OpenAI now buys from five clouds, expanded AWS by $100B/8 years) and building their own data centers to reduce dependence. OpenAI alone booked ~$10B of Microsoft's revenue in the quarter (~16% of Microsoft Cloud, ~11% of total revenue) — concentration in a single customer whose funding is unproven at this scale. Own silicon (Cobalt 200 CPU, Maia 200 accelerator) reduces Nvidia dependence. **Irreplaceability: 8/10** — the enterprise lock-in is near-total; the deduction is entirely the OpenAI concentration.

**5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months).** Q1 FY2027 (Oct 28, 2026): revenue $92.3B (+19%), adj. EPS $4.89 vs ~$4.73 expected; Azure $33.2B (+48%) disclosed in dollars for the first time, beating 44–45% guidance (5th straight quarter beating Azure guidance); Q2 Azure guidance raised to 49–50% constant currency; total revenue guide $96.0–97.1B. Capex guide >$50B in Q2, ~$170B calendar 2026. OpenAI +$250B Azure commitment. Cantor Fitzgerald and Piper Sandler raised price targets after the print. Stock rose ~5% after hours on the print (then faded with the Nov AI selloff; 12m return only +3.8% per screen — the market has not rewarded the acceleration yet).

**6. Risks.** Capex: ~$170B in calendar 2026 with accelerating H2 spend; FCF-positive guidance for FY2027 is a promise, not a fact, and returns on this spend are unproven. OpenAI concentration: ~30% of the backlog is AI, heavily OpenAI; if OpenAI's fundraising falters, the $677B backlog's collectability is the question (Microsoft notes RPO estimates involve judgment including renegotiation). Google Cloud grew 103% to $30.7B in the same quarter — the #3 player is closing fast. Neoclouds (CoreWeave, Nebius, etc.) are eroding Big Three share (61% vs 63% in Q2). Valuation: ~28x trailing earnings, ~$3.9T market cap — explosive-return math is hard at this size; the screen's +29.2% analyst upside is the lowest-ranked of the four on that metric only because the base is enormous.

**7. Shortage score: 8/10. Verdict:** The highest-quality shortage in the batch — a $677B backlog (+73%) against supply-constrained Azure growing 48% — but at $3.9T, "explosive" upside is structurally limited; this is a compounder with a shortage kicker, not a multi-bagger setup.

## VEEV — Veeva Systems

**1. Shortage thesis.** No — there is no structural shortage of Veeva's product. It sells life-sciences cloud software (CRM, clinical, regulatory, quality, data); supply is code, not capacity. What exists instead is a demand/competitive inflection: Vault CRM is winning back the top-20 pharma CRM decisions, and the Falcon AI platform has five early adopters with initial go-lives expected in FY2027. The "scarcity" is regulatory validation — pharma cannot swap validated systems quickly — not a supply/demand imbalance. No capacity sold out, no lead-time extension, no price-increase evidence found.

**2. Backlog / order book.** Veeva does not disclose a headline RPO figure — **not found** in earnings materials. Proxies: normalized billings $768M in Q2 FY2027, +19% YoY; billings $760.9M (per StockStory, +17.1% YoY — definitional difference vs company-reported normalized billings); subscription revenue $767M (+16%). Multi-year enterprise subscriptions give high revenue visibility, but there is no quantified multi-year backlog comparable to the other three names. FY2027 revenue guidance $3.682–3.687B (+15%).

**3. Category position.** #1 in life-sciences CRM with ~80% market share (per industry write-ups), serving 1,500+ customers including virtually all top-20 pharma. Vault CRM: 12 of top 20 biopharma committed (from 10 signed at Q4 FY2026), >180 customers live, two remaining top-20 decisions expected by end of 2026; ambition to hold >70% life-sciences CRM share. #2 is effectively the Salesforce + IQVIA partnership (Apr 2024) competing for the same migrating customers. In R&D cloud, subscription revenue ($1.675B guided FY2027) now exceeds commercial ($1.405B) for the first time — the business is tilting toward the stickier, regulated side.

**4. Irreplaceability.** Moat: the validation layer. Every Vault deployment must be validated against pharma regulatory requirements; switching costs are extreme (re-validation, re-training, data migration in GxP environments). Customers have no contractual renewal obligation (10-K), so the moat is switching cost, not contract. In-sourcing threat: essentially nil — no pharma company builds its own CRM/clinical suite. Substitution threat: Salesforce (horizontal platform, $37B, limited incentive to go deep in one vertical) + IQVIA (domain expertise, direct motive) are the real contenders; some customers that chose Salesforce are now win-back opportunities, with "project delays at competitors" cited by management as a tailwind. The migration itself is the risk: Veeva is ripping out the Salesforce platform under its flagship CRM and rebuilding on Vault (GA Apr 2024; ~140 customers live at Q4 FY2026; end-of-support pulled forward to Dec 2029 from Sep 2030). **Irreplaceability: 8/10** — the highest switching costs in the batch, docked for the ongoing platform migration and the Salesforce/IQVIA alternative.

**5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months).** Q2 FY2027 (Aug 26, 2026): revenue $928M (+18% YoY) beat $905.3M consensus; non-GAAP EPS $2.35 vs $2.22; non-GAAP operating income $416M (45% margin); beat-and-raise — FY2027 revenue guidance lifted to $3.682–3.687B (from $3.64B) and adj. EPS to $9.21 (from $9.06); Q3 guide $932–935M. Strongest CRM quarter ever: two new top-20 Vault CRM wins (Biogen, Regeneron among announced selections). Falcon: five early adopters, management: "AI is enabling the next big chapter for Veeva and the industry". 8-K Nov 6, 2026: expects to meet or exceed Q3 FY2027 guidance. Investor Day Nov 5, 2026. $2B buyback initiated Jan 2026; $1.4B remaining at Jul 31, 2026; $7.2B cash + short-term investments, zero debt. Stock +20.3% YTD vs S&P 500 −0.1% (Zacks, Nov 24, 2026).

**6. Risks.** The thesis is not a shortage thesis at all — growth is 15–18%, a steady compounder's pace, and the stock's 12m return is −0.7% per screen despite the beat-and-raise, i.e. the market is not paying up for it. Migration execution risk: if Vault CRM stumbles, Salesforce/IQVIA take share permanently. AI monetization: "no material AI revenue expected before FY2028" (Falcon is upside to the $6B 2030 revenue target, not a dependency). Pharma IT budgets are cyclical with drug-pricing and R&D-spending cycles. Fwd PE 26.2 on ~15% growth is not cheap for a non-AI-shortage name.

**7. Shortage score: 3/10. Verdict:** A high-quality #1 vertical-software franchise with brutal switching costs and a clean beat-and-raise — but it fails the panel's shortage test outright: no capacity constraint, no backlog surge, ~15% growth, and no explosive-return mechanism in the next 12 months.
