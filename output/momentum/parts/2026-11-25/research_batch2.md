## LLY — Eli Lilly and Company ($1,347.11, Nov 20 close; ~$1.27T market cap)

**1. Shortage thesis — STALE. Demand surge is real; the shortage is over.**
- FDA removed tirzepatide injection (Mounjaro/Zepbound) from the drug shortage list Oct 2, 2024 and reaffirmed the shortage resolved in a declaratory order Dec 19, 2024, finding Lilly's "supply is currently meeting or exceeding demand" with finished-product reserves plus semi-finished stock and scheduled production (FDA, Dec 19, 2024). The Fifth Circuit later upheld the delisting.
- Demand is still surging: total weekly US GLP-1 prescriptions hit 2.88M in the week of Sep 18, 2026, +37.9% YoY (GLP-1 Global Research, data cut Oct 22, 2026). CEO David Ricks expects global GLP-1 use to rise from ~20M patients at end-2025 to ~30M by end-2026.
- But the constraint has flipped from supply to price/access: Q3 2026 volume +53% while realized price fell 15% (company release, Oct 29, 2026). Lilly is now deliberately cutting price (Zepbound cash-pay reductions, LillyDirect $399/mo) to widen the market. Capacity is no longer the bottleneck — Lilly has committed >$50B in US manufacturing since 2020, including the Lebanon, Indiana site coming online and a $3.5B Pennsylvania injectable/device facility (operational ~2031, for retatrutide).

**2. Backlog / order book — not applicable / not found.** Pharma does not report an order backlog. Closest proxy: prescription momentum (see above) and the Q3 revenue cadence ($19.8B Q1 → $23.0B Q2 → $24.4B Q3 2026).

**3. Category position — #1, and pulling away.**
- Lilly held 62.8% of US branded GLP-1 prescriptions vs Novo Nordisk 37.2% (week of Sep 18, 2026); by product: Mounjaro 32.3%, Zepbound 28.5%, Ozempic 18.3%, injectable Wegovy 12.3% (GLP-1 Global Research).
- Revenue share: Lilly held 61.8% of combined Lilly–Novo incretin revenue in Q2 2026, up from 36.0% in 2024 (Axis Intelligence, from company filings). FY2025: tirzepatide $36.5B vs Novo semaglutide franchise ~$34.5B.
- Zepbound beat Wegovy head-to-head (SURMOUNT-5, ~20.2% weight loss). The one segment where Lilly trails: orals — Novo's Wegovy pill holds ~90% of US oral obesity scripts; Lilly's Foundayo is catching up from behind.

**4. Irreplaceability — 9/10.** No customer can in-source this: tirzepatide is a patented dual GIP/GLP-1 molecule with superior efficacy, protected by patents plus >$50B of purpose-built manufacturing that takes years to replicate, plus a pipeline (retatrutide triple agonist, oral orforglipron) that extends the franchise. The "customers" are patients/payers, not manufacturers. Real bypass threats are (a) Novo's semaglutide/oral franchise, (b) future competitors (Pfizer, Roche, Amgen investing billions), and (c) price regulation — IRA-negotiated 2027 prices are $276.78 per Ozempic pen and $385.63 per Wegovy pack. None of these is in-sourcing; the moat is IP + scale + efficacy.

**5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)**
- Q3 2026 (Oct 29, 2026): revenue $24.4B, +39% YoY, beating ~$22.6B consensus by ~$1.8B; non-GAAP EPS $7.48 vs ~$6.26 expected. Mounjaro $10.72B (+65%), Zepbound $5.35B (+49%). Third guidance raise of 2026: FY revenue $91–92B (from $85–87B).
- Retatrutide: Phase 3 TRIUMPH data — up to 22.6% weight loss at 80 weeks, beat Ozempic 2 mg head-to-head; six Phase 3 wins; US BLA planned Q1 2027.
- Foundayo (oral orforglipron): FDA-approved Apr 1, 2026 (first daily GLP-1 pill without food/water restrictions); Q3 sales $184M — below ~$217–229M expected, new-Rx share nearing 40%. Positive CHMP opinion (EU) Oct 2026.
- Medicare began covering obesity drugs Jul 1, 2026 (pilot); Mounjaro added to China's National Reimbursement Drug List Q1 2026 (ex-US volume +93% in Q3, price −29%); new Mounjaro cardiovascular-risk-reduction label in Q3.

**6. Risks**
- Price deflation is the story now: realized price −15% in Q3 (−7% US, −29% ex-US); if volume growth decelerates, revenue follows.
- Concentration: incretins (Mounjaro/Zepbound/Foundayo) were 66.6% of Q3 2026 revenue.
- Oral race: Foundayo missed estimates while Novo's Wegovy pill leads orals (>5M US scripts in 7 months).
- Valuation: ~40x FY2026 non-GAAP EPS guidance midpoint at the Oct 30 price (sec-api.io); screen fwd PE 26.8 looks stale vs that — verify before citing either. ~$900M of Q3 revenue was non-recurring (Jardiance milestones $410M + rebate adjustments).
- Compounded-tirzepatide overhang is fading (FDA 503B bulks-list proposal, Apr 30) but not fully resolved.

**7. Shortage score: 4/10. Verdict: The shortage premium is gone — this is now a price-for-volume execution story, not a scarcity bet; explosive upside needs retatrutide/oral wins, not a supply squeeze.**

## WST — West Pharmaceutical Services ($390.30, Nov 20 close; ~$27.7B market cap)

**1. Shortage thesis — DEMAND SURGE, not a hard shortage.**
- Driver is GLP-1 + biologics injectables: elastomers for GLP-1s are now 12% of total company sales (Q3 2026 deck, up from 9% in Q3 2025), with "strong growth in both non-GLP-1 and GLP-1" and collaboration "with customers who are launching a pipeline of new GLP-1 molecules and generics" (Q3 2025 call; trend continued into Q3 2026).
- Second driver is regulatory: EU Annex 1 is forcing customers to upgrade to high-value components — 375 ongoing Annex 1 upgrade projects (Q3 2025 call), 211 new upgrade projects added in 2026; Annex 1/HVP upgrades expected to deliver ~200 bps of growth in 2025 (raised from 150 bps).
- Capacity is tight, not sold out: management is "working through our constraint at our HVP manufacturing site in Germany," hiring/training and installing new equipment (Q3 2025 call). No lead-time or allocation quotes found — demand exceeds comfortable capacity in HVP, but West is expanding into it rather than rationing.

**2. Backlog / order book — not found (not disclosed).** West reports no backlog figure. Proxies: HVP Components $466.7M in Q3 2026, +19.7% YoY, 53% of total company sales (from 43% a year earlier); biologics participation rate ">90%" and "trending above historical levels"; 375+ Annex 1 projects in flight. No "booked X quarters out" number exists in filings or calls.

**3. Category position — #1 in elastomer closures.**
- Market leader with an estimated ~18.5% global elastomer-stoppers share (Dataintelo, 2025); top five suppliers hold ~62% collectively. In the broader pharma-packaging market West is #3 (12–16%) behind Schott and Gerresheimer, but in its core elastomer-closure niche it is the recognized leader; Datwyler and Aptar Pharma are the named competitors (10-K). West's biologics participation rate >90% is the more telling share metric — it is in on over 9 in 10 biologic/biosimilar launches.

**4. Irreplaceability — 8/10.** West's components sit inside the drug's regulatory filing (drug master files, container-closure data in the NDA/BLA); switching a stopper/plunger supplier forces re-validation and regulatory submissions — a multi-year, high-risk exercise no pharma company undertakes lightly. Moat: proprietary elastomer formulations (FluroTec coatings, licensed Daikyo Crystal Zenith), validated sterile ready-to-use supply, and biocompatibility dossiers built over decades. In-sourcing threat is low — Lilly/Novo would need cleanroom elastomer molding, formulation IP and regulatory dossiers for a low-cost component that is mission-critical but a tiny share of drug cost; no evidence either is trying. Residual risk: customers dual-source (Datwyler/Aptar exist) and the top 10 customers are 47.6% of 2025 net sales, so a single large-customer loss matters.

**5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)**
- Q3 2026 (Oct 29, 2026): sales $874.5M, +8.7% (+11.9% organic); adjusted EPS $2.66, +35.7% YoY (vs $1.96); adjusted operating margin 25.3%, +420 bps; gross margin 39.7%, +310 bps. Guidance raised: FY2026 sales $3.41–3.42B (from $3.345–3.38B), adjusted EPS $9.38–9.48.
- Dividend raised 18% to $0.26/share (Oct 19, 2026); $231M buybacks in Q3, $685M YTD.
- SmartDose 3.5mL on-body system sold to AbbVie Jul 1, 2026 for $126.9M cash ($55.7M pre-tax gain) — portfolio pruning.
- Synchrony prefillable syringe system launched Jan 2026; Dublin drug-handling site ramping (initial ~$20M revenue).

**6. Risks**
- Customer concentration: top 10 = 47.6% of 2025 sales; a CGM contract is transitioning in 2026 and must be backfilled with higher-margin business (management: late-stage discussions ongoing).
- Contract-manufactured products (West Vantage) declined 7.3% in Q3 2026 — the non-HVP side is soft.
- May 2026 cybersecurity attack temporarily disrupted global operations; a securities class action remains at an early stage (10-Q, Oct 29, 2026).
- HVP capacity constraint in Germany — execution risk on the growth engine.
- Valuation: screen fwd PE 38.4; TTM PE ~43x (Finnhub). The stock is +41% YTD and fell ~7% in the 5 sessions to Nov 24 — momentum is choppy.
- See earnings-quality flag below (receivables).

**Earnings-quality flag — RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN: mostly benign timing, watch the trend.**
- Accounts receivable, net: $748.2M at Sep 30, 2026 vs $574.4M at Dec 31, 2025 (+30.3%) and $625.0M at Sep 30, 2025 (+19.7% YoY) — while 9M 2026 sales grew 14.2% ($2,591.7M vs $2,269.1M) and Q3 sales +8.7% (10-Q, Oct 29, 2026). Receivables are growing roughly 2x sales.
- Benign read: West sells to large pharma on long payment terms; a strong Q3 (esp. September-weighted HVP shipments) mechanically lifts quarter-end AR, and the mix shift toward big GLP-1 customers lengthens the book. No bad-debt or collectability warning in the filing.
- Caution read: operating cash flow YTD was $403M vs net income of $506.8M (9M 2026) — cash is lagging earnings, consistent with the AR build. **One-line verdict: timing/mix, not channel stuffing — but if DSO keeps rising into Q4, it stops being benign.**

**7. Shortage score: 6/10. Verdict: The best picks-and-shovels GLP-1 play in this batch — real demand surge plus margin expansion — but there is no hard shortage or disclosed backlog, and the receivables build needs watching.**

## VRTX — Vertex Pharmaceuticals ($515.02, Nov 20 close; ~$130.6B market cap)

**1. Shortage thesis — NONE. This is a monopoly, not a shortage.**
- Cystic fibrosis is a fixed rare-disease population (~88,000 patients globally eligible for CFTR modulators). There is no supply constraint and no demand surge supply can't meet — Vertex manufactures to a known, slow-growing patient pool. Growth comes from label expansion (younger ages), geographic reimbursement, and new franchises (pain, gene editing, kidney, endocrine), not scarcity.
- The "explosive" angle here is a binary regulatory event, not shortage: povetacicept PDUFA date Nov 30, 2026 — five days after this run date.

**2. Backlog / order book — not applicable / not found.** Pharma, no order book. Proxies: CASGEVY — 160+ patients have initiated cell collection since launch, 39 infusions done (Q3 2025 call; revenue $99M in Q3 2026, +30% QoQ, ~6x YoY); JOURNAVX — 750,000+ prescriptions, now "the most prescribed branded oral pain medicine in the US" (Q3 2026 call).

**3. Category position — #1 by effective monopoly.**
- Vertex is the only maker of CFTR modulators; Trikafta/Kaftrio treats ~90% of CF patients (F508del mutation) and there is "no generic substitute, no competing branded therapy." ~75% of the addressable patient population across 60+ countries is on Vertex therapy. CF revenue $3.3B in Q3 2026 (+8% YoY); Trikafta alone did $10.3B in FY2025. Alyftrek (approved Dec 2024, once-daily successor) did $706M in Q3 2026 as patients switch from Trikafta ($2.44B, −8% as conversion proceeds).

**4. Irreplaceability — 10/10 (for CF).** Nothing can route around it: no alternative disease-modifying therapy exists, patients cannot in-source a triple-combination small molecule, and switching is medically risky. Moat: 20+ years of CFTR science, proprietary clinical data on virtually every CF patient treated globally, specialist-physician system of record, patents into the 2030s with Alyftrek resetting the clock to the late 2030s. Threats are political, not competitive: pricing pressure (US list >$370k/yr) and an Oct 2025 advocacy buyer's-club distributing a Beximco generic Trikafta at $6,375–$12,750/yr in countries without reimbursement — a reputational/access issue, not yet a US revenue threat. No late-stage competing modulator exists.

**5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)**
- Q3 2026 (Nov 2, 2026): revenue $3.44B, +12% YoY, beating ~$3.40B consensus; non-GAAP EPS $4.76 — a miss vs $4.92 consensus and flat YoY (spending absorbed all growth). Guidance raised slightly to $13.15–13.25B (from $13.1–13.2B).
- Povetacicept (dual BAFF/APRIL inhibitor, IgAN): BLA under priority review, PDUFA Nov 30, 2026; renal field force fully hired; at-home once-every-4-weeks auto-injector; RAINIER Phase 3 fully enrolled, interim data in NEJM. Would be the fifth commercial franchise.
- Crinetics acquisition closed Sep 2026 ($9.0B net cash; $4.5B term loan taken) — fourth pillar (rare endocrine); PALSONIFY contributed ~$24M in Q3 incl. pre-close, >40% share among new/switching patients.
- JOURNAVX: $58M in Q3 (+18% QoQ); 150 additional field reps being added early 2026 (per Q3 2025 call). VX-670 (myotonic dystrophy) interim data due by year-end.

**6. Risks**
- CF growth is cooling: global CF +8% (from +11% in Q2), US CF +5% (from +9%); the core is a maturing, capped market.
- Earnings are flat while spending explodes: combined non-GAAP R&D/AIPR&D/SG&A $1.53B in Q3 2026 vs $1.28B a year earlier; GAAP EPS fell to $2.56 from $4.20 on ~$500M Crinetics charges. Cash fell to $9.8B from $13.6B (deal + buybacks).
- PDUFA binary: a Nov 30 rejection or delay hits a stock that has already hired the launch force.
- ~90% of revenue still CF; diversification (Casgevy $99M, Journavx $58M) is real but small. CFO transition effective Jan 1, 2027.
- See earnings-quality flag below (inventory).

**Earnings-quality flag — INVENTORY_BUILD: largely explained by the acquisition, not overproduction.**
- Inventories: $2,245.3M at Sep 30, 2026 vs $1,686.8M at Dec 31, 2025 (+33.1%) and $1,627M at Sep 30, 2025 (+38.0% YoY) — ~3x the ~12% sales growth. Breakdown (10-Q, Nov 3, 2026): raw materials $218.2M (down from $259.8M), work-in-process $1,632.7M (up from $1,196.9M), finished goods $394.4M (up from $230.1M).
- The key reconciling item is disclosed in the same 10-Q Note I: Vertex "acquired a total fair value of $461.8 million PALSONIFY inventory as part of the Crinetics Acquisition in Q3 2026." Strip that out and inventory is ~$1,783.5M, +5.7% vs Dec 31, 2025 — roughly in line with sales. The remainder is plausibly launch stock for povetacicept (PDUFA Nov 30) and the Alyftrek transition.
- **One-line verdict: benign — the build is acquisition accounting plus pre-launch stocking, not channel stuffing; finished goods +71% only becomes a problem if the Nov 30 launch disappoints.**

**7. Shortage score: 2/10. Verdict: No shortage at all — the explosive bet here is the Nov 30 povetacicept PDUFA binary on top of an unassailable CF monopoly; earnings are flat while the company spends to diversify.**

## EW — Edwards Lifesciences ($101.85, Nov 20 close; ~$58.7B market cap)

**1. Shortage thesis — NO. Demand expansion, not supply shortage.**
- TAVR is not supply-constrained; the constraint is diagnosis and procedure capacity. Management: growth is driven by "procedure volumes," ASPs "stable globally," and the company explicitly guides that future growth comes "mainly by continued increases in procedure volumes and less by share gains" (Q3 2026 release, Nov 4, 2026). Real-world median time to intervention is ~157 days vs 32 days in the PROGRESS trial — the bottleneck is referral/diagnosis, not valves.
- The demand-expansion story is label/coverage: PROGRESS trial (moderate aortic stenosis) presented and published in NEJM in Q3 2026 — "a turning point in the management of moderate AS" — and CMS's final updated TAVR national coverage determination (NCD) in Q3 2026 expanding coverage to asymptomatic severe AS and easing hospital volume requirements for site expansion.

**2. Backlog / order book — not found (not disclosed).** Medtech does not report backlog. Proxies: TAVR $1.24B/quarter run-rate (+8.8% cc); TMTT $207M (+45.6% cc) with a stated path to $2B by 2030; the moderate-AS population is a multi-year procedure pipeline if the indication is approved (management: "gradual, progressive impact year-over-year," not a step-change).

**3. Category position — #1, decisively.**
- ~66% global TAVR share vs Medtronic ~28%, Abbott 2.9% (GlobalData, 2025 market $7.0B → $13.8B by 2035, 7.1% CAGR); some estimates put Edwards near 75% of the US market. TAVR is ~74% of Edwards revenue (FY2025 TAVR ~$4.49B). Medtronic's Evolut is the only serious rival; recent Evolut trial data showing superior outcomes is the competitive watch item (GlobalData, 2026).

**4. Irreplaceability — 7/10.** The moat is clinical evidence + physician training + hospital workflow: SAPIEN 3 Ultra RESILIA is balloon-expandable with precise placement, backed by the deepest long-term TAVR evidence base, and embedded in heart-team practice at hundreds of centers. Hospitals cannot in-source valve manufacturing (regulated Class III devices, precision engineering). But this is not a 9–10: Medtronic's self-expanding Evolut is a genuine clinical alternative with recent favorable trial data, Abbott's Navitor is gaining, and hospitals do switch platforms — Edwards itself says share gains are moderating and future growth is volume-led. The moat defends share; it does not lock it.

**5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)**
- Q3 2026 (Nov 4, 2026): sales $1.72B, +10.5% (+11.2% cc), ahead of expectations; TAVR $1.24B +8.0%; TMTT $207M +44.0%; adjusted EPS $0.80, +19% YoY (vs ~$0.74 expected). Guidance raised across the board: total sales ~+11.5% (from 10–11%), TAVR 9–9.5% (from 8–9%), TMTT $780–790M (from $760–780M), adjusted EPS $2.97–3.05 (+18% at midpoint). Q4 guide: sales $1.67–1.75B, EPS $0.61–0.69.
- PROGRESS trial (moderate AS) presented + NEJM publication — potential label expansion in 2027; FDA submission status to be updated at the December investor conference.
- CMS final TAVR NCD (asymptomatic severe AS coverage + site expansion) — Stifel called it "clearly constructive for the overall TAVR market and for Edwards in particular" given its leadership.
- TMTT: PASCAL/EVOQUE/SAPIEN M3 portfolio now fully available in US and Europe; path to $2B TMTT revenue by 2030.
- Balance sheet: ~$3.1B cash; ~$700M buybacks YTD, ~$1.3B authorization remaining.

**6. Risks**
- Share gains are moderating by management's own account; growth is now volume-dependent, and volume depends on diagnosis/referral rates and cath-lab staffing — slow-moving variables.
- Medtronic Evolut competitive pressure (recent trial data favoring Evolut); Abbott Navitor.
- Concentration: ~74% of revenue is TAVR; a TAVR slowdown is an Edwards slowdown.
- Valuation: screen fwd PE 31.0, but TTM PE ~57x (Finnhub) — expensive for ~11% sales growth with mid-single-digit long-term TAVR expectations; the December investor conference is the next guidance event and a disappointment would be punished.
- Moderate-AS benefit is explicitly "gradual" — not an explosive near-term catalyst.

**7. Shortage score: 3/10. Verdict: A quality structural-heart compounder with genuine label-expansion optionality (moderate AS, CMS NCD), but no shortage and no near-term explosive trigger — the upside case is steady compounding, not a squeeze.**
