## 1. Single-pick ballot

- **Top pick:** APH
- **Runner-up:** ANET
- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** Amphenol is the broadest, most diversified play on the one shortage in this batch that is documented in hard order-book terms rather than guidance: AI data-center interconnect is capacity-constrained, with cable lead times stretching toward a year and at least one major manufacturer sold out through 2026. Amphenol sells into every accelerator vendor and every hyperscaler, so no single customer can starve it, and its IT Datacom business — ~3/4 AI, +117% in Q3 — is growing faster than the AI build-out itself. Unlike a platform vendor, it is a component supplier, but that is precisely what makes it hard to disintermediate: hyperscalers cannot realistically in-source precision connectors, and qualification cycles plus co-design lock it into each new accelerator generation. At ~$73.60 (Nov 24 close) against a $106.97 analyst mean target, with 21.4x forward earnings for 48% TTM revenue growth, the selloff has widened the gap between the order book and the price.
- **Key shortage/backlog evidence:** Record Q3 2026 orders of $12.17B, +99% YoY — 1.2x quarterly sales of $10.23B (book-to-bill ~1.19) — with IT Datacom backlog "stretching further out than usual." That is the single strongest backlog print in the dossier: customers are ordering more than Amphenol can ship, and the queue is lengthening.
- **Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk:** Medium — and the lowest-risk form of medium in this batch. Hyperscalers cannot in-source connectors (no scale, no qualification infrastructure, no breadth across copper/fibre/power/sensors), and Amphenol co-designs with every accelerator vendor rather than depending on one. The real threat is architectural substitution — optics replacing copper interconnect — but the dossier puts full optical scale-up no earlier than 2029, which is beyond the 12–18 month horizon of this pick. The offsetting weakness is multi-sourcing: Amphenol is #2 in connectors globally (TE is #1), so it shares the shortage rather than owning it outright, which caps pricing power versus a true monopoly.
- **12-18 month return scenario:** Base +35–45% (toward ~$100–107, i.e. the analyst mean target) as the $12.17B order book converts and IT Datacom sustains high-double-digit growth. Bull +60–70% (toward ~$118–125) if AI datacom growth stays triple-digit into 2027, the CommScope assets integrate cleanly, and the market re-rates a 21x forward multiple toward the growth rate.
- **Top risk:** Cyclicality and concentration in the AI build-out itself — Q4 IT Datacom was guided to only mid-single-digit sequential growth, and if hyperscaler capex pauses, a connector order book can de-book faster than a platform backlog. Net debt of $12.6B after the $10.5B CommScope deal also leaves less cushion than the debt-free ANET.
- **Conviction (1-10):** 8

### Top 3 — most compelling shortage/backlog or moat data point, and 12–18mo scenario

**1. APH — Amphenol.** The single most compelling data point is the Q3 order book: $12.17B of orders, +99% YoY, against $10.23B of sales — a book-to-bill of ~1.19 with IT Datacom backlog stretching further out than usual, in a market where data-center fibre demand rose 76% last year and cable lead times approach a year. That is demand visibly exceeding supply, measured in signed orders rather than management commentary, and it is diversified across every hyperscaler and accelerator vendor. Base +35–45% (to ~$100–107); bull +60–70% (to ~$118–125) if AI datacom stays triple-digit into 2027.

**2. ANET — Arista Networks.** The most compelling data point is the combination of purchase commitments jumping to $11.4B in Q3 (from $9.7B in Q2) with component lead times of 38–52 weeks, plus an initial FY2027 revenue guide of ~$18.5B (+38–40%) that management pre-committed before the year even started — customers are effectively pre-paying for capacity that does not yet exist. The moat is the EOS single-OS stack plus hyperscaler co-design, but the disintermediation risk is the live one in this batch: Nvidia already leads data-center Ethernet (21.5% vs Arista's 20.7% per IDC Q1 2026) and bundles Spectrum-X into the GPU sale. Base +30–40% from the Nov 24 close of $168.43 (to ~$220–235, toward the $249.65 target); bull +60% (to ~$270) if the Spectrum-X threat proves overblown and FY27 guidance is beaten. ANET ranks behind APH on this lens because its backlog is a purchase-commitment proxy, not disclosed orders, and because the Nvidia threat is exactly the in-sourcing trap the doctrine warns about.

**3. MSFT — Microsoft.** The most compelling data point is the commercial RPO of $677B at Sep 30, 2026, +73% YoY with ~2.3-year weighted duration, plus the CFO's own words that "customer demand continues to exceed our available supply" for Azure — and that excludes the incremental $250B OpenAI commitment and up to $49B from Anthropic added after the quarter. No other name in the batch has multi-year revenue visibility on this scale. But at ~$3.9T, explosive upside is structurally limited: base +15–20% (to ~$570–600 from the Nov 24 close of $496.13); bull +30% (to ~$645, near the $636.12 target) if Azure supply catches up with demand and the RPO converts at the guided 49–50% growth rate.
