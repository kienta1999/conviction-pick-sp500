## APH (Amphenol)

### 1. Q3 2026 orders $12.17B, +99% YoY, ~1.2x quarterly sales of $10.23B (book-to-bill ~1.19)
**Verdict: CONFIRMED**
- Amphenol Q3 2026 earnings call (Oct 28, 2026), CEO R. Adam Norwitt: "Orders in the quarter were a record $12,170,000,000, up a strong 99% compared to the 3rd quarter of 2025 and up 13% sequentially, resulting in another very strong book-to-bill ratio of 1.19 to 1." Sales were a record $10.23B (+65% YoY). 12.17 / 10.23 = 1.19x. Sources: AlphaStreet transcript, Oct 28, 2026; Motley Fool transcript, Oct 28, 2026; Finnhub earnings data (Q3 FY2026 revenue $10.230B, reported Oct 28, 2026).

### 2. IT Datacom sales +100% YoY in Q3 2026 (+77% organic), AI-related datacom revenue +117%, AI ~3/4 of the segment
**Verdict: CONTRADICTED (figures misattributed / swapped)**
- The earnings-call transcript states the opposite assignment of the two growth rates: "Sales in this important market grew by a very strong 117% in US dollars and 77% organically" — i.e. **total IT datacom grew +117% YoY (+77% organic), not +100%**. Zacks (two articles, Oct–Nov 2026) and TradingKey likewise report IT datacom +117% YoY / +77% organic. The +100% figure appears only in two secondary recaps (The Buildout, Vestick) and is not supported by the transcript or Zacks.
- The +117% figure therefore belongs to the whole IT datacom market, not to "AI-related datacom revenue" specifically — no source found breaking out a separate +117% growth rate for the AI sub-portion.
- The AI-share element IS confirmed: the transcript says AI "now does represent approximately 3 quarters of our IT datacom sales," corroborated by Zacks ("AI accounting for roughly three-fourths of segment sales"). IT datacom was 48% of Q3 sales.
- Net: the +77% organic and the ~3/4 AI share are correct; the +100% total and the +117%-for-AI-only attributions are wrong as stated. Source: AlphaStreet Amphenol Q3 2026 earnings call transcript, Oct 28, 2026; Zacks, "Astera Labs vs. Amphenol," Nov 2026.

### 3. Analyst mean target ~$106.97 and forward PE ~21.4
**Verdict: CONFIRMED (exact match at one aggregator; other aggregators differ slightly)**
- Oppstock (data as of Nov 24, 2026): "Consensus target: 106.97 USD… Based on 19 analysts" and "Forward P/E, on next year's expected earnings: 21.4." Both figures match the claim exactly.
- Caveat: aggregators disagree modestly — MarketBeat consensus target $106.21 with forward P/E 20.22 (19 analysts, updated Nov 24, 2026); Zacks via Kalshi mean target $107.61, forward P/E 20.42. The dossier's figures are a legitimate snapshot (oppstock, Nov 24, 2026), not a fabrication, but the "mean target" and "forward PE" are source-dependent to within ~$1.40 / ~1.2x. Source: app.oppstock.com/stocks/APH, data as of Nov 22–24, 2026.

### 4. Cable/connector lead times stretching toward a year and at least one major manufacturer sold out through 2026
**Verdict: UNVERIFIED (no supporting evidence found for connectors/cables; the "sold out through 2026" quotes found belong to other components)**
- Best connector-specific evidence found points the other way on magnitude: Future Electronics (Oct 19, 2026, via OurPCB shortage tracker reviewed Nov 14, 2026) lists Amphenol Communications Solutions PCB connectors at **8–10 weeks** lead time, prices increasing; MarketsNXT (Nov 2026) says high-speed assembly lead times "exceed twelve weeks." A Novus blog (Nov 2026) puts imported connector brands (JST, TE, Amphenol, Molex, Hirose) at 3–8 weeks. Nothing near ~52 weeks for connectors.
- The "sold out through 2026" language found in search is for **hard drives, not connectors**: Seagate CEO (Jan 28, 2026 call) — "nearline capacity is fully allocated through calendar year 2026"; Western Digital CEO (Jan 2026) — "We're pretty much sold out for calendar 2026." TrendForce lead times "over 52 weeks" (Sep 2025) also refer to HDDs. Separately, Samsung's 2027 HBM supply is reported sold out (memory, not connectors). Edgewater Research (May 2026) notes capacitor lead times extending to 52 weeks in some cases — again, not connectors.
- Semibuffer (Nov 2026) explicitly states for connectors: "The two releases leave each part's capacity priority and lead time unknown" and "I have no lead-time figure for these families this week." The claim may conflate the HDD/HBM shortage with the connector market. Recommend dropping or re-sourcing this claim.

## MSFT (Microsoft)

### 9. Commercial RPO $677B at Sep 30, 2026, +73% YoY
**Verdict: CONFIRMED**
- Microsoft FY27 Q1 earnings call (Oct 28, 2026), CFO Amy Hood: "Commercial remaining performance obligation grew 73% to $677 billion, with a smaller sequential decline relative to historical seasonality due to the large Azure commitment this quarter." Weighted-average duration 2.3 years; ~30% to be recognized in the next 12 months. Quarter ended Sep 30, 2026. Source: Microsoft Investor Relations FY27 Q1 earnings call transcript, Oct 28, 2026; corroborated by Zacks and Six Sigma Research, Oct 2026.

### 10. CFO Amy Hood on the Q1 FY2027 call (Oct 28, 2026) said customer demand "continues to exceed our available supply" for Azure; Azure grew +48% in the quarter
**Verdict: CONFIRMED (substance; wording is a close paraphrase as reported in call coverage)**
- Azure revenue +48% YoY in Q1 FY2027 is confirmed verbatim in Microsoft's own transcript: "Azure revenue was ahead of expectations and increased 48%."
- The demand-exceeds-supply statement is reported across independent call coverage: AlphaSense — "customer demand for Azure continuing to exceed available supply"; Six Sigma Research — "Customer demand continues to exceed available supply" (in the Q2 guidance discussion); MarketBeat — Hood said "we still have demand that exceeds supply." The exact sentence did not appear in the excerpted portion of the Microsoft transcript retrieved, but the substance and the attribution to Hood on the Oct 28, 2026 call are consistently reported. Azure was guided to 49–50% constant-currency growth in Q2 on the same call. Sources: Microsoft IR transcript, Oct 28, 2026; AlphaSense MSFT Q1 2027 analysis; Six Sigma Research, Oct 2026; MarketBeat, Oct 2026.

### 11. OpenAI contracted an incremental $250B of Azure services (announced Oct 2026)
**Verdict: CONFIRMED**
- Microsoft's Form 10-Q for the quarter ended Sep 30, 2026 (filed/disclosed with the Oct 28, 2026 results) states: "OpenAI has contracted to purchase an incremental $250 billion of Azure services." Reported the same week by Data Center Dynamics (Oct 28, 2026, on the OpenAI for-profit conversion/Microsoft 27% stake announcement) and by AInvest ("after the quarter closed OpenAI signed an incremental $250 billion commitment for Azure services"). The $250B is incremental to prior commitments, over an undisclosed multi-year period. Note: some secondary write-ups date elements of the restructured partnership to an April 27, 2026 amendment and the Oct 28, 2025 PBC conversion; the $250B commitment itself was disclosed in the Oct 2026 10-Q, matching the claim's "announced Oct 2026." Sources: Microsoft Form 10-Q, Q1 FY2027 (Oct 2026); Data Center Dynamics, Oct 2026; AInvest, Oct 2026.
