# Panelist A — Supply-chain analyst ballot — 2026-11-22

## SINGLE-PICK BALLOT

- Top pick: MSFT
- Runner-up: MSI
- Thesis (3-5 sentences): Microsoft is the only name in the field with a genuine, management-confirmed capacity shortage: Azure demand exceeds available supply, Azure grew 48% YoY in Q1 FY27 to \$33.17bn, and Microsoft is adding roughly 1 GW of datacentre capacity per quarter — yet capacity remains the binding constraint, with Q2 FY27 Azure guidance of 49–50% constant-currency growth. That unmet demand is already contracted: commercial RPO stands at \$677bn, up 73% YoY, with the near-term slice (recognised in the next 12 months) up 37% YoY. The shortage is structural, not cyclical — hyperscale AI compute cannot be built fast enough — and every quarter of capacity added converts directly into revenue at 66% cloud gross margin. No other name pairs a shortage this clean with a backlog this large.
- Key shortage/backlog evidence: Commercial RPO of \$677bn at 30 Sep 2026, +73% YoY, against Azure demand that management says "continues to exceed available capacity" — with ~1 GW of capacity added per quarter still not enough to close the gap.
- Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk: low — enterprises cannot in-source hyperscale Azure capacity, and the Microsoft 365 + Azure + OpenAI bundle plus OpenAI API exclusivity through 2030 (IP rights through 2032) lock customers in; the real risk runs the other way, OpenAI building its own compute and diversifying, and hyperscalers self-building — but neither lets an enterprise customer bypass Azure.
- 12-18 month return scenario: rough base / bull case: Base +20–25% (Azure holds ~45–50% growth, RPO converts on schedule, analyst upside of +31.3% partly realised). Bull +40–50% (capacity additions accelerate conversion, Copilot's 30m+ paid seats compound, and the market re-rates MSFT off a flat 12 months, +0.5%).
- Top risk: OpenAI concentration — roughly 45% of the \$625bn RPO at Dec 2025 was OpenAI, whose revenues are far below its commitments; if OpenAI stumbles, the backlog, the \$45bn+/quarter capex and Microsoft's ~27% equity stake all hit at once.

### Top 3 detail

**1. MSFT** — The single most compelling data point is the \$677bn commercial RPO, +73% YoY, sitting on top of a stated capacity shortage: Microsoft added 31 datacentres in Q4 FY26 and ~1 GW per quarter, yet demand still exceeds supply, and Copilot passed 30 million paid seats (from ~20m three months earlier). Rough 12–18mo scenario: base +20–25% on steady RPO conversion; bull +40–50% if capacity additions accelerate and the flat 12-month return (+0.5%) re-rates toward the +31.3% analyst upside.

**2. MSI** — The single most compelling data point is the record \$16.6bn ending backlog at Q3 2026, +14% YoY and +\$1bn sequentially, built on record Q3 orders up over 20% YoY against only 12% revenue growth — the order book is outrunning deliveries, with ~\$12.4bn of it multi-year Software & Services. Rough 12–18mo scenario: base +10–15% as the backlog converts at ~13% guided revenue growth; bull +25–30% if Silvus defence demand and D-Fend counter-drone (expected +40–50% next year) accelerate orders further — though the +41.7% 12-month run and only +5.4% analyst upside left make entry the binding constraint.

**3. LLY** — The single most compelling data point is the demand surge itself: Q3 2026 revenue \$24.4bn, +39% YoY on +53% volume, with Mounjaro + Zepbound over \$16bn in the quarter and only ~1 in 10 eligible US patients on an incretin — plus a \$50bn US manufacturing programme (\$6.5bn Houston and \$5bn Richmond plants broken ground in the quarter) chasing that demand. Rough 12–18mo scenario: base +10–15% as volume growth offsets the −15% realised-price erosion; bull +25–30% if retatrutide (BLA planned Q1 2027, up to 25.0% weight loss in TRIUMPH-1) extends the franchise — though the shortage is maturing into a price war and analyst upside is only +9.1%.

Left out of my top 10: ABBV, EOG, COP — ABBV has no shortage or backlog and its growth engine (Skyrizi) is decelerating every quarter; EOG and COP are commodity price-takers in an oil market the IEA says is moving into surplus (~1.7m bpd in Q4 2026, >3m bpd in 2027), with no order book and no supply constraint to price in.
