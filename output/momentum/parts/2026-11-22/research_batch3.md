## MA — Mastercard Inc.

### 1. Shortage thesis

Weak fit for a classic shortage thesis — Mastercard sells network capacity, not a physical product, and there is no capacity constraint on card transactions. The demand-surge analogue is real but softer: payment volumes keep growing through the network regardless of cycle, and pricing power is the binding evidence. In Q3 2026 (reported 2026-10-29) "Domestic Assessments were up 13%, while worldwide GDV grew 7%. The 6 ppt difference is primarily driven by pricing and mix" and "Cross-Border Assessments increased 16%, while cross-border volumes increased 11%" — i.e. Mastercard raised effective prices ~5–6pp above volume growth with no volume loss. Value-added services and solutions revenue grew 19% YoY in Q3 2026, outpacing the 11% payment-network growth. Demand is not supply-constrained; it is a toll-road volume + pricing story, not a shortage story.

### 2. Backlog / order book

Not applicable in the order-book sense — Mastercard does not report a backlog or RPO. The closest forward indicator is the volume trend through 2026-10-21: cross-border volume +10% MTD in October, switched transactions +9% in Q3 2026, and 3.8 billion Mastercard/Maestro cards issued as of 2026-09-30. No backlog figure exists to quote — not found (no such disclosure).

### 3. Category position

No. 2, clearly behind Visa. Calendar-aligned to the twelve months ended 2026-06-30, Visa processed 62.27% of combined Visa+Mastercard purchase volume (\$15.34tn vs \$9.29tn); in the US Visa's share was 70.96% in Q2 2026 (Axis Intelligence Research, updated 2026-10-27, built from both companies' filings). By US credit-card purchase volume, Visa ~52%, Mastercard ~25%, Amex ~19.5% (Nilson Report via WalletHub, 2025 data). Offset: Mastercard earns more per dollar — 37.75bp of net revenue per dollar of volume vs Visa's 29.01bp, a 30.1% yield premium — and it is gaining share outside the US (Visa's non-US share slipped 56.37% → 56.02%).

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk

The moat is the two-sided network: 3.8bn cards, acceptance in 210+ countries, dispute resolution, fraud infrastructure and compliance scaffolding that no single merchant or bank can replicate cheaply. The concrete bypass threat is real and named by management's own actions: stablecoins, real-time account-to-account rails (Pix, UPI, FedNow) and government payment systems can route around cards. Mastercard's answer has been to buy the rail rather than fight it: it acquired BVNK, a stablecoin payments company, closing 2026-08-03 (reported ~\$30bn annualised stablecoin volume), and launched Agent Pay for Machines in June 2026 with 30+ backers. A single large customer cannot realistically in-source the network, but issuers can and do steer volume between Visa and Mastercard at renewal, and rebates/incentives — over half of payment-network gross revenue in Q3 2026 — are the price of keeping them. **Irreplaceability: 8/10.**

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)

- Q3 2026 earnings beat (2026-10-29): net revenue \$9.81bn, +14% YoY, vs \$9.65bn consensus; adjusted EPS \$5.31 vs \$5.16 consensus (+21% YoY); GDV +7% to \$3.0tn; cross-border +11%; switched transactions +9%.
- FY2026 guidance reaffirmed at the high end of low-double-digit currency-neutral net revenue growth.
- BVNK acquisition closed 2026-08-03, five months ahead of schedule.
- Capital returns: \$11.9bn of buybacks in the first nine months of 2026 (22.9m shares at ~\$520.64 avg), including \$3.0bn in Q3 alone; \$4.9bn of authorisation remained at 2026-10-26; \$763m of dividends paid in Q3.

### 6. Risks

- Interchange regulation and merchant litigation: the US merchant "rules relief" settlement (final-approval hearing November 2026) cuts the average US credit interchange rate ~10bp with a five-year cap and lets merchants refuse some cards; Block and Intuit seek >\$5bn in damages; UK/Netherlands/Portugal suits pending.
- Structural bypass: stablecoins and A2A rails could take volume if Mastercard's BVNK bet fails to convert.
- Cross-border volume growth is decelerating (+15% in Q3 2025 → +11% in Q3 2026); US debit grew only ~1% in Q3 after the Capital One debit migration.
- Valuation: ~33x TTM diluted EPS at \$638.02 on 2026-10-30; buybacks in 9M 2026 (\$11.9bn) equalled ~100% of operating cash flow, partly debt-funded (\$5.0bn bond issue June 2026).

### 7. Shortage score and verdict

**Shortage score: 3/10.** Verdict: a superb compounding toll road with pricing power and a 19%-growing services layer, but no shortage — volume growth is mid-single-digit and the explosive-return case rests on multiple expansion or a services re-rating, not on demand overwhelming supply.

## VEEV — Veeva Systems Inc.

### 1. Shortage thesis

No physical shortage; the demand-surge analogue is the forced Vault CRM migration. Every legacy Veeva CRM customer must re-implement by the time support ends — the end-of-support date was pulled forward to December 2029 (from September 2030) — creating a multi-year, non-deferrable wave of migration projects and new Vault CRM selections. On the Q2 FY2027 call (2026-08-26) management called it "the best CRM quarter ever"; 12 of the top 20 biopharma companies have now selected Vault CRM (new wins from Lilly, Biogen and Regeneron in the quarter), with only two top-20 decisions remaining, expected to resolve by year-end. Demand is also visible in pricing: the 10-Q attributes subscription growth in part to "annual inflation-related price increases". Falcon, the agentic-AI product, has five early adopters live (including a top-20 pharma) with "very high customer interest" — but no revenue is currently modelled from it.

### 2. Backlog / order book

Veeva does not disclose a headline RPO/backlog number. Its 10-Q for the quarter ended 2026-07-31 (filed 2026-08-27) states: "the amount of the transaction price allocated to remaining performance obligations for noncancellable subscription services contracts greater than one year was not significant with the substantial majority of such allocated transaction price included in deferred revenue and expected to be recognized over the next 12 months." The visible backlog proxy is deferred revenue: \$1,310.5m at 2026-07-31, up 18.3% YoY (per SaaSValuation.io analysis of the filing), down seasonally from \$1,488.8m at 2026-01-31 because customers pay annual subscriptions mostly in Q1. Subscription revenue is 84% of total revenue (FY2026: \$2,684.2m of \$3,195.3m). So: a ~\$1.3bn one-year prepaid book, growing high-teens, but no multi-year backlog to quote — RPO total not found (disclosed as not significant beyond one year).

### 3. Category position

Yes — No. 1 in life-sciences CRM by a wide margin. Veeva holds roughly 80% share of life-sciences CRM (multiple 2026 comparisons; Rankite, 2026-10) and serves 1,500+ customers including virtually all of the top 20 pharmas. On the broader pharma/biotech CRM software market definition, Research and Markets (Nov 2025) puts Veeva at 26.81% vs IQVIA 17.73% and Salesforce 16.40%. Veeva targets ≥70% share of biopharma CRM subscription revenue through 2030.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk

The moat is the validation layer, not just software: Veeva's Vault platform is embedded in regulated commercial, clinical, regulatory, safety and quality workflows, with deep workflow integration and high switching costs (75% gross margin, 45% non-GAAP operating margin in FY2026). A pharma company cannot realistically build this itself — the cost is re-validating regulated workflows and rebuilding integrations across R&D and commercial. The concrete threat is the migration itself: because every customer must re-implement by 2029/2030, Salesforce (Life Sciences Cloud / Agentforce, built with IQVIA) has a once-in-a-generation window to steal accounts at the decision point — 140+ organisations had selected Agentforce by mid-2026, including AstraZeneca, Novartis, Pfizer, Takeda, Moderna and AbbVie, vs 125+ Vault CRM customers and 10–12 of the top 20 committed to Veeva. Management itself concedes some customers have chosen Salesforce and that Veeva is "positioning itself as a backup option" to win some back in 2027–2028. **Irreplaceability: 7/10** — dominant and sticky, but the forced migration temporarily lowers switching friction for the first time in 15 years.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)

- Q2 FY2027 beat-and-raise (2026-08-26): revenue \$928.0m, +17.6% YoY, vs ~\$904–905m consensus; non-GAAP EPS \$2.35 vs \$2.22; subscription revenue +16%; operating margin 29.6% (GAAP) vs 24.8% a year earlier. Stock rose ~13.8% that week.
- FY2027 guidance raised again: revenue \$3.682–3.687bn and non-GAAP EPS ~\$9.21, both above consensus (\$3.65bn / \$9.06).
- Vault CRM wins: Lilly, Biogen (global commitment) and Regeneron in Q2; EDC now at 9 of the top 20 accounts.
- Falcon AI: five early adopters live; spending scaled within guidance.
- Balance sheet: \$7.2bn cash + short-term investments, zero debt at 2026-07-31; \$2bn buyback authorised Jan 2026, ~\$467m spent in H1 FY2027.
- Note the reversal: shares fell ~12% in the five sessions to 2026-11-20 (52-week high \$325.99 on 2026-10-22 → \$266.22 close 2026-11-20), so the Q2 pop has partly unwound into the Q3 print on 2026-12-03.

### 6. Risks

- The Vault CRM migration is the single biggest execution risk in company history: a botched migration or a Salesforce win at one of the two remaining top-20 decisions would be read as a share-loss signal.
- R&D segment transition: legacy products (eTMF, KTMF, QDocs, regulatory suite) are fading while newer products (EDC, Safety, LIMS) are early-stage; timing of the handover is uncertain even to management.
- Customer concentration in top-20 pharma decisions; IQVIA partnership is improved but IQVIA remains a competitor via OCE.
- Valuation: ~46x TTM earnings (Finnhub, 2026-11-20) — a growth multiple that punishes any guidance disappointment; stock already −17% in a month into earnings.

### 7. Shortage score and verdict

**Shortage score: 5/10.** Verdict: the closest thing to a forced-demand wave in this batch — a non-deferrable, industry-wide re-implementation cycle with Veeva winning most top-20 decisions — but it is a migration cycle, not a supply shortage, and the same cycle hands Salesforce its best-ever opening; the Dec 3 earnings print is the near-term binary.

## EW — Edwards Lifesciences Corp.

### 1. Shortage thesis

The strongest genuine demand-surge thesis in this batch, driven by eligibility expansion rather than factory capacity. Severe aortic stenosis affects ~3% of adults over 75, and the eligible pool has widened ~30% in major markets as guidelines moved TAVR into lower-risk and asymptomatic patients. Edwards is the only company with FDA and CE Mark approvals for asymptomatic TAVR (FDA expanded indication for SAPIEN 3, April 2025, on the EARLY TAVR trial), and in Q3 2026 CMS finalised the updated national coverage determination extending Medicare coverage to asymptomatic severe-AS patients — removing the reimbursement barrier. The newest leg: the PROGRESS trial (presented at TCT 2026, published in the New England Journal of Medicine) showed SAPIEN 3 superior to clinical surveillance in moderate AS with at-risk features — a potential further doubling of the addressable pool if guidelines follow. Management: "procedure growth continued to benefit from a heightened focus on TAVR therapy" and Q3 TAVR growth was driven by "global procedural growth and stable average selling prices" — i.e. volume-led, price-stable demand growth, not a capacity-constrained shortage. No sold-out capacity or lead-time evidence found; the constraint is patient identification and referral, not Edwards' supply.

### 2. Backlog / order book

Not applicable — Edwards sells implantable devices into procedures, not a booked order backlog; no backlog or RPO figure is disclosed. The forward indicator is procedure volume and guidance: TAVR sales \$1.24bn in Q3 2026 (+8.8% constant currency), TMTT \$207m (+45.6% constant currency). Backlog figure: not found (no such disclosure).

### 3. Category position

Yes — No. 1 in TAVR. GlobalData: Edwards ~66% of the TAVR market vs Medtronic ~28% and Abbott ~2.9%; other estimates put Edwards at 55–56% vs Medtronic ~29% (Global Growth Insights / GMI, 2025). The global TAVR market was ~\$7.0bn in 2025, projected to reach \$13.8bn by 2035 (~7.1% CAGR, GlobalData) — or \$6.6bn → \$16.5bn by 2035 at 9.8% CAGR (GMI). Either way Edwards holds a roughly 2:1 lead over No. 2.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk

The moat is clinical evidence plus the implant ecosystem: decades of trial data, trained heart teams, hospital credentialing and device-specific procedural expertise. A hospital cannot in-source a heart valve; the substitute is surgical aortic valve replacement (SAVR), which is losing share as TAVR expands to younger/lower-risk patients, or a rival valve. The concrete threat is Medtronic's Evolut: the SMART trial (3-year head-to-head) showed Evolut superior on hemodynamics in small-annulus patients (~40% of the TAVR population), with significantly lower thrombosis and bioprosthetic valve dysfunction — GlobalData expects Medtronic to take share, and Edwards management itself now says future TAVR growth will come "primarily from procedure volumes rather than competitive share gains" and that "benefits from prior competitive changes have been absorbed". Abbott (Navitor) and Boston Scientific are smaller challengers. **Irreplaceability: 8/10** — the product cannot be in-sourced or bypassed, but share within the duopoly is contestable on trial data.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)

- Q3 2026 beat-and-raise (2026-11-04): sales \$1.72bn, +11.2% constant currency, vs \$1.68bn consensus; adjusted EPS \$0.80 vs \$0.74 consensus (+19% YoY); TAVR \$1.24bn (+8.8% cc); TMTT \$207m (+45.6% cc).
- Guidance raised for the third time in 2026: FY2026 total sales growth ~11.5% (from 10–11%), TAVR 9–9.5% (from 8–9%), TMTT \$780–790m (from \$760–780m), adjusted EPS \$2.97–3.05.
- PROGRESS trial win (TCT 2026, NEJM): TAVR superior to surveillance in moderate AS — opens the moderate-AS indication.
- CMS final TAVR NCD (Q3 2026): Medicare coverage extended to asymptomatic severe AS.
- FDA approvals: PASCAL Precision for tricuspid repair (Oct 2026), PASCAL Ace with Capture Clarity for mitral TEER; European approval of SAPIEN M3 RESILIA; FDA approval of AUTUS paediatric surgical valve.
- TMTT path to \$2bn revenue by 2030 reiterated; more detail on 2027 at the December investor conference.

### 6. Risks

- Medtronic share pressure in small-annulus patients after SMART; management has already conceded share gains are moderating.
- Growth is procedure-volume dependent: hospital capacity, staffing and referral bottlenecks slowed TAVR in 2023–2025 and could recur; the asymptomatic/moderate-AS benefit is "gradual" per management, not a step-change.
- Reimbursement/policy risk (CMS NCD giveth; future coverage or pricing changes could take away); GAAP EPS was only \$0.53 in Q3 on impairment charges.
- TMTT is still sub-scale (\$207m/quarter) and competitive (Abbott MitraClip/TriClip).

### 7. Shortage score and verdict

**Shortage score: 6/10.** Verdict: the best shortage-adjacent thesis here — a structurally undertreated disease pool that keeps widening by guideline and coverage decisions, with the No. 1 player holding ~2x the share of No. 2 — but it is a slow demographic/eligibility grind (high-single-digit growth), not an explosive supply crunch; the PROGRESS moderate-AS indication is the one potential step-change.

## PM — Philip Morris International Inc.

### 1. Shortage thesis

The clearest literal shortage in this batch, now largely resolved. ZYN nicotine pouches exploded from 2023 onward; "384.8 million Zyn cans were sold worldwide in 2023, up 62% from 237 million cans in 2022. This rapid increase in demand contributed to a shortage in May 2024, with Philip Morris International stating that it was experiencing supply chain tensions" (Wikipedia, citing PMI). PMI answered with a \$600m dedicated ZYN plant in Aurora, Colorado (ground broken Dec 2024; limited production began Sep 2025; commercial production began July 2026; 780,000 sq ft campus) plus a \$232m Owensboro, Kentucky expansion. The shortage is over: in April 2026 PMI scaled back Owensboro from a 24/7 to a 24/5 schedule because "production currently exceeding demand following a period of rapid expansion" (Tobacco Insider). The demand surge itself persists at a lower rate — global nicotine-pouch market ~\$6.9bn in 2025 projected to ~\$42.4bn by 2033 (~25% CAGR, Grand View Research) — but the supply-constraint phase has ended, and capacity now leads demand.

### 2. Backlog / order book

Not applicable — consumer packaged goods sold through distributors; no backlog or RPO disclosed. The forward indicators are shipment volumes: total Q3 2026 shipments 206.2bn equivalent units (+0.6%); IQOS heated tobacco units 41.5bn (+1.5%, at the high end of expectations); ZYN US 3.2bn pouches (+4.2% reported, +13% ex prior-year promo); e-vapor 1.4bn units (+61.6%, led by VEEV). Backlog figure: not found (no such disclosure).

### 3. Category position

Yes — No. 1 in both of its growth categories. IQOS: PMI holds ~76% of global heat-not-burn HTU volume ("around three-quarters of HTU volume share", Q3 2026 release) and ~9.9% of combined cigarette+HTU industry volumes in markets where present. ZYN: US nicotine-pouch share exceeded 70% in 2023 and was ~60% by 2025 (Wikipedia) — still "well above half by both dollar and volume measures in early 2026" — with No. 2 far behind (Altria's on! and BAT's Velo). Smoke-free products are now >40% of PMI net revenue (~42% YTD) and available in 110 markets, with >30 markets above 50% smoke-free revenue.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk

The moat is regulatory plus brand/scale: ZYN was the first nicotine pouch authorised for US sale by FDA (Jan 2025) and in June 2026 received MRTP orders for 20 products — the only pouch line authorised to make reduced-risk claims; IQOS received renewed FDA modified-risk granted orders in April 2026 (effective five years). A competitor cannot simply copy the product — it must clear FDA PMTA/MRTP review, which takes years. Customers (adult nicotine users) cannot in-source anything; the bypass threat is illicit/grey-market pouches and vapes, and rival pouches (on!, Velo) that have now also won FDA clearances under a streamlined path (May 2026 FDA guidance loosened pouch/vape rules). Manufacturing is replicable — BAT, Altria and Swisher are all adding US pouch capacity (> \$1bn industry-wide) — so the moat is regulatory timing and brand, not production know-how. **Irreplaceability: 7/10.**

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)

- Q3 2026 results (2026-10-21): revenue \$11.44bn, +5.5% reported / +7.1% organic, vs \$11.32bn consensus; adjusted EPS \$2.36, a quarterly record, vs \$2.32 consensus; adjusted operating income crossed \$5bn in a quarter for the first time (+10.5% organic); adjusted operating margin near 45%.
- ZYN Ultra: FDA issued marketing granted orders in Aug 2026 for 11 ZYN Ultra products (9mg/11mg); US rollout of ZYN Ultra plus 20-pouch cans and new 1.5mg/8mg strengths in Q4 2026; ZYN US category share +1.4pp Jun→Sep 2026.
- FDA MRTP orders for 20 ZYN products (June 2026) and renewed IQOS modified-risk orders (April 2026).
- Aurora, Colorado plant in commercial production since July 2026, supporting the expanded lineup.
- FY2026 guidance maintained: 5–7% organic revenue growth (upper half likely), 7–9% organic operating income growth, 7.5–9.5% currency-neutral adjusted EPS growth; medium-term (2026–2028) targets 6–8% organic revenue and 9–11% adjusted EPS growth.
- Pending swing factor: FDA authorisation for IQOS ILUMA PMTA — the US is PMI's "largest unaddressed growth market"; analyst targets ~\$205–212 (Barclays, Citi, Needham raised; Morgan Stanley Overweight). Note the stock has pulled back: \$184.09 close 2026-11-20 vs 52-week high \$214.35 on 2026-10-30 (−6.4% in five sessions).

### 6. Risks

- The shortage is over and ZYN US growth has decelerated hard: +4.2% shipment growth in Q3 2026 (vs +32% offtake growth in Jul–Aug 2025 and +40% YoY volumes in early 2024) — the explosive phase is behind it; share is drifting down from 76% (2023) toward ~60%.
- Heavy US commercial investment: management says the Q3 2026 cost level is "a good proxy" for next year's spend — margin drag while defending share.
- IQOS US launch still pending FDA authorisation — timing unknown; Japan (largest HTU market) faces excise-driven pricing headwinds and Poland a characterising-flavour ban (IQOS adjusted IMS +7.1% headline but +10% ex Japan/Poland).
- Regulation/litigation overhang is permanent for tobacco/nicotine; valuation at ~28x TTM earnings (Finnhub) is rich for a 6–8% grower, and the 12m return (+22.5%) already reflects much of the smoke-free re-rating.

### 7. Shortage score and verdict

**Shortage score: 4/10.** Verdict: a genuine shortage existed in 2024 and PMI built the capacity to end it — which is exactly the problem for an explosive-return bet: the constraint is gone, ZYN growth has normalised to single digits, and the remaining upside is a binary FDA decision (IQOS ILUMA in the US), not a supply crunch.
