# Agent A ballot — supply-chain lens (RUN_DATE 2026-10-08)

**Lens doctrine:** shortage/backlog evidence and multi-year revenue visibility rank above all else; a name with contracted, cash-backed offtake through 2028+ beats a name with a demand narrative but no book.

---

## Ballot 1 — single-pick nomination (Agent A, supply-chain lens)

- **Top pick: MU**
- **Runner-up: AVGO**

**Thesis (3–5 sentences):** Micron is the one name in the field where the shortage has been converted into enforceable, multi-year contracts rather than a forecast. HBM supply for 2026 is fully sold out under binding agreements with demand running an estimated 50–67% above available supply, and no fresh cleanroom capacity is expected online until late 2028 — a structural two-year window where buyers pay rather than negotiate. The revenue visibility extends well beyond the cycle peak: 26 strategic customer agreements cover more than 35% of revenue through 2030, some running into 2031, and Micron is pushing substantial 2027 HBM price surcharges because fixed contracts locked it out of this year's price surge. In an industry where prices spike and then collapse, Micron's book is the closest thing to an annuity with commodity upside still attached.

**Key shortage/backlog evidence:** ~\$150B in remaining performance obligations disclosed at fiscal Q4 2026 earnings (Sept 30, 2026) — every agreement with take-or-pay volumes, counting only agreements with set pricing frameworks, which CFO Mark Murphy called "inherently conservative" — backed by \$32B in customer financial commitments, the vast majority of which are cash deposits.

**Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk: LOW.** Only 3 companies on earth produce HBM at AI-grade scale, and leading DRAM process nodes (1γ/1β) require tens of billions in capex plus years of process know-how. The hyperscalers build their own AI chips (TPUs, Trainium, MTIA) but sign multi-year *memory* supply agreements with Micron rather than build memory fabs — that is the concrete evidence they cannot in-source this layer. The real threat is oligopoly overbuild once 2028 cleanrooms land, not customer disintermediation (irreplaceability score in dossier: 8/10).

**12–18 month return scenario:** At ~\$1,064 with forward P/E of only ~5.0x against an analyst mean target of ~\$1,536 (~+44% implied), the base case is a valuation re-rating as the take-or-pay book converts into delivered quarters and 2027 HBM price surcharges land: **base ~+45–65%**. The bull case — shortage consensus holding through 2028, SK Hynix's past-2030 warning proving right, and contracted pricing repricing the whole oligopoly's earnings — supports **bull ~+90–130%**, because the market still prices memory with a cyclical discount the contracts no longer justify.

**Top risk:** The classic memory-cycle trap: only ~a third of revenue is covered by the long-term agreements, and 2028 cleanroom capacity gets built by *all three* players, not just Micron — if all three expand into a softening AI demand backdrop, pricing rolls over and the contracted portion can't cover the spot exposure.

**Conviction (1-10): 9**

**Top 3 elaboration:**

**1. MU.** The single most compelling datapoint is the \$32B in customer financial commitments sitting behind the ~\$150B take-or-pay book — customers aren't just signing, they're putting cash deposits down years ahead, which is the strongest proof of a genuine capacity shortage in the entire dossier. The moat layer: Micron is one of only three AI-grade HBM producers and the only US-based one with CHIPS Act backing, while customers moved from quarterly spot negotiation to 3–5 year agreements. Base ~+45–65% on backlog conversion and multiple re-rating; bull ~+90–130% if the shortage runs past 2028 and 2027 price surcharges reprice the cycle.

**2. AVGO.** The most compelling evidence is the ~\$73B AI backlog "expected to ship over the next 18 months" combined with six hyperscalers holding multi-year agreements through 2028 — including OpenAI's 10-gigawatt multi-year pact and Anthropic's 1 GW (2026) scaling to 3 GW (2027), which is power-bound, contracted demand rather than forecast. The moat is implementation: hyperscalers design their own silicon but cannot tape out, validate, and rack a 10-GW deployment without Broadcom's platform (13-year Google TPU lineage), and Broadcom holds an estimated 60–80% share of the custom-silicon market. Base ~+30–50% toward the \$531 analyst mean target as the backlog ships through FY2027's "line of sight to over \$100B" AI revenue; bull ~+70–100% if the >\$50B private financing for OpenAI deployment closes before year-end and de-risks the demand chain.

**3. NVDA.** The most compelling datapoint is supply so tight that Nvidia is cannibalizing its own product lines: GDDR7 memory and TSMC capacity redirected from gaming (RTX 50-series production cut up to 40% in H1 2026; RTX 60 pushed to 2028) into Blackwell Ultra and Rubin, with supply commitments nearly doubling in one quarter to \$279B "primarily related to the procurement of memory." The CEO-stated \$1T backlog through 2027 and \$500B in 2025–2026 bookings is the largest absolute demand figure in the field, and CUDA remains the unassailable software moat (Amazon's own internal documents cite CUDA as the factor preventing migration off Nvidia). Base ~+35–55% toward analyst targets as Rubin ramps; bull ~+70–100% if backlog converts cleanly — tempered by the two genuine cracks (inference ASIC share loss, customer-financed demand) that keep it below MU and AVGO on this lens.

**Names deliberately left out of my top 10:** LLY (shortage resolved — the FDA removed Mounjaro/Zepbound from the shortage list; this is a growth story, not a backlog story, and the lens punishes both), NEM (no backlog at all; 3/10 irreplaceability — a gold-price lever, not a supply-constraint name), EOG (no order backlog; 5/10 shortage is cyclical price torque on a fungible barrel), CF (genuine 8/10 nitrogen supply deficit and 98–99% utilization, but no disclosed backlog — a commodity lever, not contracted visibility), AMAT (only a stale ~\$15B backlog from Oct 2025 with ~two quarters of visibility, and its own tools are not the scarce good — LRCX has stronger current order-book evidence).
