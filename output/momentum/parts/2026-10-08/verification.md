# Independent verification — momentum panel, 2026-10-08

Independent check of the tentative pick (MU) and the \#2/\#3 ranks (AVGO, GOOGL) against public sources. Verdicts: **CONFIRMED** / **CONTRADICTED** / **UNVERIFIED**. Sources are summarized, not copied; dollar figures escaped as `\$`.

## MU (Micron) — tentative single pick

### Claim 1 — Backlog: ~\$150B RPO, all take-or-pay, ~\$32B customer commitments (26 agreements), "more than sold out"
**CONFIRMED.** Micron's fiscal Q4 2026 earnings (reported Sept 30, 2026) disclosed 26 strategic customer agreements — multi-year take-or-pay contracts the company estimates represent >35% of revenue through 2030. CFO Mark Murphy put remaining performance obligations at ~\$150B, calculated on committed volumes at minimum pricing (described as "inherently conservative") and covering only agreements with defined pricing frameworks. Customer financial commitments stand at \$32B, "the vast majority of which are cash deposits" (CEO Sanjay Mehrotra), with \$12.3B in customer cash deposits taken in Q4 alone. The phrase "more than sold out" was used by Mehrotra (reported again July 2026 in connection with supply tightness continuing into 2027, and in the Dec 2025 call: "more than sold out for high-bandwidth memory through 2026"). Sources: earnings-call reporting via aistockwire (Oct 1, 2026), unite.ai, htx.com, thestreet.com, techtimes.com.

### Claim 2 — Shortage driver: HBM demand 50-67% above supply; sold out through 2026; shortage into 2028+; no new cleanroom capacity until late 2028
**CONFIRMED, with one timing nuance.** Mehrotra said Micron can supply key customers only "about 50% to two-thirds of their requirements" in the medium term (post-FQ2 call, March 2026, via CNBC). Analyst estimates put the 2026 HBM supply-demand gap at 50-60%. HBM capacity is sold out through all of 2026 (one August 2026 report says DRAM and HBM are sold out through all of 2027). Shortage-duration consensus: Gartner, Counterpoint, and TrendForce converge on a shortage running to at least 2028; SK Hynix publicly warned it could persist past 2030; Mehrotra expects 2027 and 2028 markets to be tighter than 2026; "no line of sight" to supply/demand rebalance. Micron plans cleanroom acceleration for "late calendar 2028 and beyond." Nuance: Idaho ID1 fab initial wafer output is expected mid-2027, so the claim "no new cleanroom capacity until late 2028" overstates slightly — meaningful new capacity, however, is still a late-2028 event. Sources: marketwise.com, ainvest.com, simplywall.st, unite.ai, techtimes.com (July 2026).

### Claim 3 — Catalyst: record FQ4 results and next-quarter guidance
**CONFIRMED.** Fiscal Q4 2026 (quarter ended Sept 3, 2026), reported Sept 30, 2026 after the close: revenue \$54.23B, +379% YoY (consensus ~\$51.07B per LSEG), non-GAAP EPS \$33.42 vs \$3.03 a year ago (consensus \$31.61), record non-GAAP gross margin 87.0%, up 31% sequentially — a record quarter. Full fiscal 2026 revenue \$133.19B; FY2026 non-GAAP EPS \$75.52. Fiscal Q1 2027 guidance: revenue \$61.5B ± \$1.5B, non-GAAP EPS \$38.15 ± \$1.00, non-GAAP gross margin ~86.25% (framed as the floor for FY2027). Sources: Micron via Yonhap Infomax, RTTNews/dpa-AFX (TradingView), vtmarkets.com, thecerbatgem.com, ad-hoc-news.de.

### Claim 4 — Moat: only ~3 firms on earth can manufacture AI-grade HBM (Micron, SK Hynix, Samsung)
**CONFIRMED.** Repeated across sources: "only three companies on the planet manufacture [HBM] at scale: SK Hynix, Samsung, and Micron." Market shares vary by source/date (SK Hynix ~56-61%, Samsung ~17-27%, Micron ~16-25%; IDC data cited in SK Hynix's F-1 filing puts SK Hynix at 56.4% as of Q1 2026). Sources: indmoney.com, nasdaq.com, ainvest.com, cryptobriefing.com, en.sedaily.com.

## AVGO (Broadcom) — ranked \#2

### Claim 1 — Backlog: ~\$73B AI backlog shipping over ~18 months; Q3 AI revenue ~\$16.7B (+221%), Q4 guide ~\$21.7B
**CONFIRMED.** CEO Hock Tan stated on the fiscal Q4 2025 post-earnings call (Dec 11, 2025) that Broadcom has a backlog of \$73B it anticipates shipping over the next 18 months (Reuters). Q3 FY2026 (reported Sept 2, 2026): AI semiconductor revenue \$16.7B, +221% YoY and +54% sequentially, ~56% of total revenue; Q4 FY2026 guidance: AI semiconductor revenue ~\$21.7B (+236% YoY) on consolidated revenue ~\$34.8B; full-FY2026 AI revenue target ~\$58B (+186%). Note: the \$73B figure dates to Dec 2025; Sept 2026 reporting describes disclosed AI backlog as "exceeding \$73B." Management's FY2027 target is "significantly in excess of \$100B" (~\$115B) in AI revenue. Sources: Reuters, techtimes.com, barchart.com, news.alphastreet.com, fool.com, ainvest.com.

### Claim 2 — Shortage driver: hyperscaler custom-AI-accelerator demand; multi-year agreements through 2028 with six hyperscalers (OpenAI 10GW pact; Anthropic 1GW→3GW)
**CONFIRMED in substance; one figure needs correction.** Six confirmed XPU co-development customers: Google, Meta, OpenAI, Anthropic, ByteDance, Fujitsu (techtimes.com, Sept 3, 2026). OpenAI 10GW pact: 2025 partnership to jointly develop 10 gigawatts of OpenAI custom chips on Broadcom's networking technology, deployment from H2 2026 through end 2029 (WSJ context, investinglive.com, Oct 7, 2026). Anthropic: recent sources say 1GW in 2026 scaling to **10GW by 2028** (ainvest.com, Sept 2026) — the panel's "1GW→3GW" figure is **not supported** by current reporting; it may reflect older figures. Google TPU partnership runs through 2031; Anthropic has signed a five-year \$125.2B TPU lease. Related financing: Broadcom agreed to lend Anthropic up to \$42B (convertible facility disclosed in Anthropic's IPO prospectus, reported by Reuters Oct 1, 2026); a ~\$60B syndication process began Oct 2, 2026 (Bloomberg via temperature2.com).

### Claim 3 — Catalyst: reported Oct 7, 2026 (WSJ) that Broadcom is financing >\$50B of OpenAI chip deployment
**CONFIRMED, with a caveat on wording.** The Wall Street Journal reported Oct 7, 2026 ("Oracle, Broadcom and SpaceX Seek Blockbuster Debt Deals to Pay for AI Chips"): Broadcom "has been working in recent weeks to arrange more than \$50 billion in financing for OpenAI's custom artificial intelligence chip, which the firms are developing together," with Apollo and Blackstone among lenders approached; talks are early and the deal size could change, possibly covering several gigawatts. Caveat: the WSJ says Broadcom is working to *arrange* the financing (not a closed deal, borrower/terms unconfirmed) — the panel's "is financing" overstates the certainty. Sources: wsj.com (primary), runtimewire.com, investinglive.com, stocktwits.com.

## GOOGL (Alphabet) — ranked \#3

### Claim 1 — Backlog: ~\$514B Cloud RPO/backlog
**CONFIRMED.** Google Cloud backlog was \$514B as of June 30, 2026 (reported at Q2 2026 earnings, July 2026), up from ~\$460-462B at end of Q1 2026 and \$106B in Q2 2025; just over half expected to convert to revenue within ~24 months. CFO Anat Ashkenazi confirmed the majority of the \$514B is GCP contracts, not TPU sales. Sources: fierce-network.com, webpronews.com, ad-hoc-news.de (Q2 transcript), tickeron.com, nova.kapualabs.com, inferentialinvestor.com (Q2 call notes).

### Claim 2 — Shortage driver: cloud capacity sold out ("demand is not the problem, capacity is"); TPU utilization >90%
**Substance CONFIRMED; both quoted specifics UNVERIFIED.** Compute constraint is well documented: CEO Sundar Pichai (Q1 2026 call, April 2026): "we are compute constrained in the near-term" and cloud revenue "would have been higher if we were able to meet that demand"; CFO Ashkenazi (Q2 2026): "continued supply constraints" with "demand still outpacing its investments"; Google's own researchers reportedly queue for TPU time behind paying customers (ainvest.com, Sept 2026). However: (a) the exact quote "demand is not the problem, capacity is" was **not found verbatim in any source** — UNVERIFIED as a quote; (b) **no source states a TPU utilization >90% figure** — the closest number is "TPUs already powering 90% of Google Search AI models," which is a workload-share statistic, not utilization. Both details should be restated or dropped.

### Claim 3 — Catalyst: 2026 capex plan \$195-205B and market selloff (~17% off highs) on capex fears
**CONFIRMED, with one conflicting report noted.** Alphabet raised 2026 capex guidance to \$195-205B (Q2 2026 earnings, July 2026; Q2 capex was \$44.9B, pushing FCF negative ~\$5.8-5.9B), principally for servers, data centers, and networking. One July 2026 report (fierce-network.com) cited "\$180-190B" — likely stale (that was the Q1 raise) or misreported; the weight of sources supports \$195-205B. Selloff: 52-week high \$408.61 (mid-May 2026); traded near \$338 in early October 2026 (tickeron.com, ~Oct 1) = 17.3% below the high — the "~17%" claim is arithmetically accurate at early-October levels. Coverage ties the overhang to capex: "AI Capex Weighs on Sentiment" (tickeron, Oct 2026); ad-hoc-news: record AI infra spend "raises the execution bar." (Per Finnhub data, ~\$350.5 on Oct 8 = ~14% off the high; the ~17% figure matches the early-October \$338 prints.)
