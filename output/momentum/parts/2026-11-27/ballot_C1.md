Lens: category dominance, margins, returns on capital, balance-sheet durability, and above all irreplaceability. ## Single-pick ballot

- **Top pick:** CBOE
- **Runner-up:** EMR
- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** CBOE is the only name in the dossier whose shortage is a true supply constraint rather than a demand surge: it is the sole legal venue for SPX options, and on 28–29 Sep 2026 it signed a 25-year extension of the exclusive S&P Dow Jones Indices licence through 2051, replacing rights that previously ran only to 2032. Demand into that monopoly is at records — index options ADV +24% YoY to 6.1 million contracts in Q3 2026, SPX 0DTE ADV a record 3.2 million contracts (+34% YoY), and SPX options set a record 970.6 million contracts in 2025. The moat is a signed contract, not a network effect that can erode, and management notes the SPX complex has not undergone a meaningful pricing adjustment for several years, leaving unexercised pricing power. At 22.7x forward PE it is also the cheapest name in the batch on that measure, despite Q3 2026 net revenue +18% and adjusted EPS +36%.
- **Key shortage/backlog evidence:** SPX 0DTE ADV hit a record 3.2 million contracts in Q3 2026, +34% YoY, with 0DTE at a record 65% of overall SPX activity — demand only CBOE can legally supply, now locked in through 2051.
- **Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk:** low — no customer can build its own SPX options market, because the index is S&P DJI's IP and the licence is exclusive; substitutes at the edges (SPY/QQQ ETF options, Nasdaq-100 options, OTC variance swaps, futures) do not replicate SPX's cash-settled, European-style, Section 1256 tax treatment and deep 0DTE liquidity. The live bypass threat is regulatory rather than commercial (the CFTC perpetual-futures fight), and it threatens future adjacent products, not the SPX core.
- **12-18 month return scenario:** base roughly +15–25% if index-options ADV growth stays in the double digits and the 2027 royalty reset is indeed de minimis as management describes; bull roughly +35–45% if volatility stays elevated, retail 0DTE demand keeps compounding, and CBOE exercises its unadjusted SPX pricing power.
- **Top risk:** The engine runs on retail speculation and volatility — retail is an estimated 58% of SPX 0DTE volume and retail 0DTE grew 46% YoY, so a volatility collapse, a retail drawdown, or a regulatory clampdown on 0DTE would hit growth directly; the stock also rose 10.03% in the first 12 days of November to \$369.19 as the VIX spiked above 28, so entry after a vol spike is the wrong side of the trade if vol mean-reverts.
- **Conviction (1-10):** 8

**2. **3. UNP.** The most compelling moat data point is the network itself: UP leads US freight rail on revenue (about \$24.5B in 2025 vs BNSF about \$23.4B) with the industry's best operating ratio among Class Is (59.8% in 2025; Q3 2026 adjusted OR 58.8%), and the UP–NS merger would create the first single-line coast-to-coast railroad controlling about 50% of US Class I-originated shipments. Base roughly +10–15% over 12–18 months on operating leverage and high-single-digit EPS growth; bull roughly +30–40% if the STB approves the merger cleanly in 2027 and the \$3.5B annual shipper savings / modal-shift story reprices the network.
