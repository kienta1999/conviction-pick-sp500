## LLY — Eli Lilly

**1. Shortage thesis — RE-VERIFIED, NOT a live shortage.** Tirzepatide (Mounjaro/Zepbound) is NOT in the FDA Drug Shortages database: FDA declared the shortage resolved 2 Oct 2024 and reaffirmed it 19 Dec 2024, and independent FDA-database trackers re-queried 8 Oct, 18 Nov and 19 Nov 2026 all show zero tirzepatide records (glpchart.com; fitrxapp.com; getamrx.com). This confirms, rather than copies, yesterday's finding. What remains is a **demand surge, not a supply shortage**: Q3 2026 volume +53% YoY, Mounjaro +65% to \$10.7bn, Zepbound +49% to \$5.35bn (Lilly Q3 release, 29 Oct 2026, SEC filing). One soft claim persists — that injector-pen device supply runs tight at higher doses while drug substance is ample (peptidedeck.com, Jun 2026) — but I could not verify pen-level tightness from a primary source, so treat it as anecdotal, not evidence. Lilly is now *adding* supply faster than demand: 10th new US site since 2020 broke ground 12 Nov 2026 (Lehigh Valley, PA, \$3.5bn, operational by 2031, for retatrutide), part of a \$50bn US manufacturing push.

**2. Backlog / order book — not applicable / not found.** Pharma does not report an order backlog. Closest proxy: guidance raised — FY2026 revenue guidance midpoint raised \$5.5bn to \$91–92bn (from \$85–87bn) on 29 Oct 2026, the third raise this year.

**3. Category position — #1 and widening.** Lilly holds ~63% of US incretin prescriptions (IQVIA, 4-week rolling to 25 Sep 2026) and ~58.4% of international incretin revenue vs Novo Nordisk's 41.6%; Mounjaro extended its US TRx share lead to 56%, Zepbound exited Q3 at 58% (Lilly Q3 slides via Valuaize). Q3 incretin sales ~\$16.3bn vs Novo's ~\$9.4bn — a ~1.7x lead. Foundayo (oral orforglipron) took ~40% of new-to-brand prescriptions in its US market in Q3.

**4. Irreplaceability — 7/10.** Moat: tirzepatide's dual GIP/GLP-1 efficacy, manufacturing scale (\$50bn build-out, 10 new US sites), and the oral small-molecule Foundayo (no food/water restrictions, unlike oral semaglutide). Threats are real and named: Novo's oral Wegovy (approved Dec 2025), semaglutide patent expiries ex-US, and compounding pharmacies. Hospitals/payers cannot in-source a patented biologic; the bypass route is a rival molecule, not self-manufacture.

**5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months).** Q3 2026 (29 Oct): revenue \$24.4bn +39% YoY, beat consensus by ~10%; non-GAAP EPS \$7.48 vs \$5.38 expected (+39% beat); FY2026 revenue guidance raised \$5.5bn at midpoint. FDA approvals in Q3: Mounjaro for CV-risk reduction in T2D, Onswik once-weekly basal insulin, Inluriyo + Verzenio in breast cancer. Foundayo FDA-approved 1 Apr 2026 (50-day review under the National Priority Voucher pilot) and launched 6 Apr 2026; Q3 Foundayo sales \$184m. Lehigh Valley \$3.5bn groundbreaking 12 Nov 2026. Medicare GLP-1 Bridge programme (1 Jul 2026–31 Dec 2027) expanding access; Medicare GLP-1 prescriptions topped 500,000. Retatrutide US submission planned Q1 2027.

**6. Risks.** Realised prices fell 15% worldwide in Q3 (−29% ex-US); CFO said net price stays "a headwind" in 2027. Non-GAAP EPS guidance was *cut* to \$35–36 (from \$35.50–36.50) because \$4.57/share of acquired IPR&D charges offset the \$4.07 underlying raise. ~\$900m of Q3 revenue was one-off (Jardiance milestone, rebate-estimate changes); ~\$2bn of 2026 items won't repeat in 2027. Concentration: Mounjaro+Zepbound+Foundayo = 66% of 9M revenue. Novo price cut to \$675/month from 1 Jan 2027 pressures the whole class.

**7. Shortage score: 3/10. Verdict:** Demand surge is real and Lilly is winning the duopoly, but the FDA-declared shortage is over and supply is catching up — this is a share-gain growth story, not a shortage squeeze.

## ISRG — Intuitive Surgical

**1. Shortage thesis — demand-led, not supply-constrained.** No capacity-sold-out or lead-time evidence found; the "shortage" here is hospitals' unmet demand for robotic capacity, met by record placements. Q3 2026 (20 Oct 2026): 546 da Vinci placements, +28% YoY (427 a year earlier), including 315 da Vinci 5 (58% of placements); Ion placements 56 vs 50. Procedures: da Vinci +14%, Ion +31%, total +15%; >10,000 procedures/day across ~7,000 customers in 70+ countries. US da Vinci procedures only +10% (vs +16% a year earlier) — the constraint is procedure growth moderating, not system supply.

**2. Backlog / order book — not found.** ISRG does not disclose a dollar backlog. Closest proxies: installed base 12,094 da Vinci systems (+12% YoY) plus 1,152 Ion (+21%), total 13,246 systems (+13%); 291 of Q3 placements were operating leases, which pre-fund recurring revenue. 84% of Q3 revenue was recurring.

**3. Category position — #1 by a wide margin.** ~60%+ of surgical robotics (third-party estimates; one source puts revenue share at ~83.5% of a \$9.2bn 2025 market — estimates vary by definition, treat as approximate) and ~83% of US robotic urology procedures. Installed base >11,700 da Vinci systems; nearest rivals are only now arriving.

**4. Irreplaceability — 9/10.** Moat: 20+ years of clinical evidence, surgeon training infrastructure, >2,000 da Vinci 5 systems installed, 18,000+ surgeons trained on da Vinci 5, and a razor/blade model (instruments + service per procedure). Hospitals cannot build a surgical robot themselves. Substitute threat is now concrete, not hypothetical: Medtronic Hugo FDA-cleared (urology, Dec 2025; first US commercial case Feb 2026), J&J Ottava approved mid-2026, CMR Versius live ex-US with US clearance pending, plus low-cost rivals at ~1/3 the price. None has yet dented ISRG's installed-base growth (+12% YoY), but the monopoly era is ending.

**5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months).** Q3 2026 (20 Oct): revenue \$3.05bn +22% (beat \$2.91bn consensus); non-GAAP EPS \$3.00 vs \$2.65 consensus (+25% YoY); instruments & accessories +18% to \$1.80bn. New Penang, Malaysia manufacturing facility to begin operations in 2028. SP procedure growth +55% globally (record 42 SP placements); da Vinci 5 >25% of US procedures (vs 13% a year ago). \$2.94bn buyback in Q3 (7.8m shares at ~\$377 avg). FY2026 da Vinci procedure growth guidance narrowed to 14–15% (from 13.5–15.5%), midpoint expected.

**6. Risks.** The stock fell ~7% after hours on the Q3 print despite the beat — the market focused on two items: (a) management will *cut* da Vinci instrument/accessory revenue per procedure by ~3–5% from H1 2027 via extended instrument usable life + volume-based pricing — a deliberate give-back on the most valuable revenue line; (b) a \$450m charge to settle a US class action over third-party remanufacturing/servicing (GAAP EPS \$1.49 vs \$1.95 a year earlier). US procedure growth decelerating (10% vs 20% ex-US); six straight quarters of GLP-1-driven bariatric decline; tariffs ~0.9% of revenue. First real competition arriving (see §4).

**7. Shortage score: 4/10. Verdict:** A dominant installed-base compounder with record placements, not a shortage play — and management is voluntarily cutting per-procedure pricing, which caps the explosive-return case near-term.

## AMGN — Amgen

**1. Shortage thesis — none; volume-led growth in specific drugs.** No product in structural shortage. The demand-surge pockets are Repatha (+37% to \$1.085bn in Q3, volume-driven) and Uplizna (+191% to \$451m), Evenity (+39% to \$751m), Tezspire (+43% to \$538m), Imdelltra (more than doubled to \$406m). These are share gains in competitive categories, not supply-constrained markets. Q3 2026 total revenue \$10.4bn, +8.5% YoY, all volume-driven (US volume +8%, ROW +16%).

**2. Backlog / order book — not found.** Pharma does not report backlog. Closest proxy: FY2026 guidance raised for the third time, to revenue \$38.9–39.7bn (Feb guide was \$37.0–38.4bn) and non-GAAP EPS \$22.90–23.70.

**3. Category position — #1 in several niches, challenger in obesity.** Repatha is a leading PCSK9 inhibitor (3m patients targeted in FY2026 per company guidance disclosure); Prolia was the former top seller but is collapsing under biosimilars. In obesity, Amgen is a *challenger*: MariTide is Phase 3, unapproved, behind Lilly/Novo by years.

**4. Irreplaceability — 6/10.** Moat: complex biologics manufacturing, 17 products growing double digits, patent-protected molecules (Repatha, Evenity, Tezspire). But the moat is visibly eroding where patents lapse: Prolia sales fell 45% to \$632m in Q3 on biosimilar competition. Customers (payers/PBMs) *can* and do substitute biosimilars the moment exclusivity ends. MariTide's differentiation is dosing convenience (monthly, possibly quarterly), not efficacy — Phase 2 weight loss 12.3–16.2% at 52 weeks vs Zepbound's higher figures and retatrutide's ~28% in Phase 3 reports.

**5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months).** Q3 2026 (3 Nov): revenue \$10.4bn +8%, beat \$9.95bn consensus; non-GAAP EPS \$6.45 vs \$5.74 consensus (+14% YoY); GAAP EPS \$7.08 (+19%). Guidance raised (revenue, GAAP and non-GAAP EPS) for the third time in 2026. Three positive Phase 3 readouts in the quarter. MariTide: MARITIME-1/2 primary completion expected Jan 2027; MARITIME-CV enrolment complete (primary completion Jun 2028); MARITIME-T2D-3 head-to-head vs semaglutide initiated and enrolling; MARITIME-SWITCH and extensions enrolling. Dividend \$2.52/share (+6%). Capex \$600m in Q3, ~\$2.6bn FY2026, scaling capacity in NC, OH and Puerto Rico for a potential MariTide launch. New CFO Thomas Dittrich from 1 Sep 2026. Note: a material cybersecurity incident in third-party cloud environments was disclosed 31 Jul 2026 (10-Q Item 1.05).

**6. Risks.** Biosimilar erosion is the live bleed (Prolia −45%; Enbrel net price −14% on IRA Medicare Part D price-setting, volume down, top line flattered by rebate-estimate changes and +8% wholesaler inventory). \$54.0bn debt vs \$13.2bn cash (Horizon deal legacy). MariTide Phase 3 readout (Jan 2027) is binary and efficacy trails the leaders. FCF fell to \$3.7bn from \$4.2bn YoY. Q4 opex guided up sharply.

**7. Shortage score: 2/10. Verdict:** A solid volume-growth quarter with a real pipeline option in MariTide, but no shortage, no pricing power in its challenged franchises, and the explosive case rests entirely on a Jan 2027 Phase 3 readout.

### Earnings-quality flag — AMGN: RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN

Trade receivables \$10,353m at 30 Sep 2026 vs \$8,490m at 30 Sep 2025 (+21.9%) against Q3 revenue +8.5% and product sales +8% — receivables are outrunning sales by ~13 points YoY, and the average receivable collection period lengthened to ~102 days (Sep 2026) from ~90 days (Sep 2025). Counter-evidence: vs 31 Dec 2025 (\$9,570m) receivables are up only 8.2%, tracking 2026 sales growth; Q2 DSO actually *improved* to ~98 from ~100 days; Q3 FCF was still \$3.7bn; management attributes the FCF dip to rebate/collections timing; growth is volume-driven across 17 products, not price or channel loading; and ex-US mix (ROW volume +16%) structurally carries longer payment terms, plus disclosed wholesaler inventory builds (e.g. Enbrel +8%). **One-line verdict: mostly benign — timing, international mix and wholesaler inventory, not channel stuffing — but amber, not cleared: DSO lengthened 12 days YoY, so Q4 collections are the test.**

## EW — Edwards Lifesciences

**1. Shortage thesis — demand expansion, not a supply shortage.** The driver is *indication expansion enlarging the treatable pool*, not capacity sold out. In Q3 2026 the PROGRESS trial (moderate aortic stenosis) was presented and published in the *New England Journal of Medicine*, and CMS released the final updated TAVR national coverage determination (NCD). TAVR sales grew 8.0% to \$1.24bn; TMTT (mitral/tricuspid) surged 44.0% to \$207m. No sold-out capacity, lead-time or price-increase evidence found.

**2. Backlog / order book — not found.** EW does not disclose a backlog. Closest proxies: TMTT guidance raised to \$780–790m for FY2026 (from \$760–780m) with a stated path to \$2bn TMTT revenue by 2030; TAVR growth guidance raised to 9.0–9.5% (from 8–9%); total sales growth guidance raised to ~11.5% (from 10–11%).

**3. Category position — #1 in TAVR.** ~66% global TAVR/TAVI share vs Medtronic ~28% and Abbott ~2.9% (GlobalData, 2025 market \$7.0bn, projected \$13.8bn by 2035 at 7.1% CAGR); ~75% of the US TAVI market per industry estimates. SAPIEN is the leading balloon-expandable valve family; Edwards pioneered bioprosthetic valves (1958) and TAVR.

**4. Irreplaceability — 8/10.** Moat: RESILIA tissue technology (anti-calcification, dry storage), the deepest TAVR clinical-evidence base (EARLY TAVR long-term economics, PROGRESS), and entrenched cath-lab training/relationships. Hospitals cannot in-source a transcatheter valve; the bypass is a rival valve, and Medtronic's Evolut just posted superior 3-year SMART-trial haemodynamics (lower thrombosis and valve dysfunction) in small-annulus patients — GlobalData expects Medtronic to gain share from this. Management itself said its competitive position has plateaued, expects "a slight loss over time as competition increases," and that future growth will come from procedure volume, "less by share gains."

**5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months).** Q3 2026 (4 Nov): sales \$1.72bn +10.5% (+11.2% constant currency), adjusted EPS \$0.80 (+19% YoY), beating \$0.73 consensus; raised FY2026 sales, TAVR, TMTT and EPS guidance (adj. EPS \$2.97–3.05, +18% at midpoint). PROGRESS trial in NEJM; final CMS TAVR NCD; EARLY TAVR long-term economic data; SAPIEN M3 CE Mark (mitral). Q4 sales guided \$1.67–1.75bn, adj. EPS \$0.61–0.69. Stock +17.1% over the past month to 25 Nov, near its 52-week high (\$108 on 16 Nov).

**6. Risks.** Concentration: TAVR is ~74% of sales, so growth is hostage to cath-lab staffing, imaging bottlenecks and referral rates. Share gains are done — management guides to volume-led growth with slight share loss. Medtronic SMART-trial threat (see §4). GAAP EPS only \$0.53 (impairment); Q4 adj. EPS guidance was cut vs Q3's range; FX headwind ~\$15m in Q4; adjusted gross margin at the low end of 78–79% guidance.

**7. Shortage score: 4/10. Verdict:** The clearest #1 in this batch with a genuine indication-expansion tailwind (moderate AS), but growth is now volume-led at high-single digits with share peaking — durable, not explosive.

## SYK — Stryker

**1. Shortage thesis — inverted: SYK has the supply problem, not the demand surplus.** Peripheral-vascular supply constraints are *hurting* SYK (management: supply problem runs into Q4; Inari written down \$293m goodwill impairment on lower forecast cash flows). The demand side is weak, not surging: US Knees +1.3%, US Hips −0.6% in Q3; management cut estimated hip/knee market growth to ~2.5% (higher insurance premiums/deductibles delaying patients, more pre-authorisation denials). Capital demand is strong (Mako placements), but "Mako capital itself sold strongly, so the robot is placing but the implant share is not following" (Aardvark Labs recap).

**2. Backlog / order book — not found as a dollar figure.** Management flagged "elevated capital equipment orders" and "continued capital demand," but also "increased caution about fulfilling certain equipment orders due to execution constraints" — i.e. a capital backlog that is *not converting on schedule* (Aardvark: "if this quarter did not show the capital backlog converting on schedule, the rating would go to Hold. It did not"). Q3 organic growth 7.7% vs the ~11.5% H2 rate the July guide midpoint required.

**3. Category position — #1 in orthopaedic robotics and reconstructive.** Mako is the market leader by installed base (1,800+ systems, 2.5m+ procedures in 47 countries) and procedure volume; third-party estimates put Stryker at ~38.5% of robot-assisted joint replacement (Dataintelo) or ~22% of the orthopaedics robotics sub-sector (VMR) vs Zimmer Biomet Rosa ~23.6% in hip robotics; ~19.4% share of the reconstructive segment vs DePuy Synthes 17.2% and Zimmer Biomet 15.1% (VMR, 2026 est.). Estimates vary by definition — directionally #1 in its niche.

**4. Irreplaceability — 6/10.** Moat: Mako's haptic-feedback/CT-planning workflow, surgeon training base, and implant lock-in (Triathlon/Accolade implants are Mako-compatible; the robot pulls implant sales). Hospitals cannot build this in-house. But the moat is *not* converting right now: implants are the profit engine and implant share is lagging robot placements; Zimmer Rosa and Smith & Nephew CORI are credible substitutes, and CORI's lower cost/smaller footprint suits hospitals Mako doesn't. A July SAP go-live in Orthopaedics "cost visibility and selling time."

**5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months) — mostly negative.** Q3 2026 (29 Oct): sales \$6.52bn +7.7%, *missed* consensus (\$6.62bn); adj. EPS \$3.52 (+10.3%) missed \$3.55. FY2026 organic guidance *cut* 205bp to 6.5–7.0% (from 8.3–9.3%, ~\$515m of revenue) and adj. EPS cut to \$14.80–14.90 (from \$14.95–15.10). Shares fell 11.8% after hours. Offsets: MedSurg/Neurotech +9.1% organic; Endoscopy US +13%; Ortho Tech US +18% on Mako/power tools; adj. operating margin +60bp to 26.2%; \$1.0bn buyback in Q3 plus a new \$3.0bn authorisation; Spencer Stiles named to succeed Kevin Lobo as CEO on 1 Jan 2027; Mako Shoulder early success; Trauma & Extremities +5.3%.

**6. Risks.** The quarter's story is execution, not demand: Q1 cybersecurity incident + July SAP implementation disrupted commercial execution; hip/knee market slowed to ~2.5%; peripheral-vascular supply constraints into Q4; Inari \$293m impairment; new-CEO first guide in January carries estimate risk; Mako RPS and Triathlon Gold behind plan.

**7. Shortage score: 3/10. Verdict:** The anti-thesis of this batch — a supply-constrained seller into a slowing procedure market that just cut guidance; the Mako installed base is a real asset, but nothing here is scarce in SYK's favour right now.
