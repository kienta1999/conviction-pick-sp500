## V — Visa Inc. (\$424.39, rev +12.2% TTM, fwd PE 24.8, next earnings 2027-01-26)

### 1. Shortage thesis

No structural shortage. Visa sells network capacity that is effectively infinite — it processed 74.2 billion transactions in fiscal Q4 2026, up 10% YoY, with no capacity constraint cited anywhere. What exists is steady demand growth, not demand supply cannot meet: global payments volume +9% in constant dollars, cross-border volume ex-intra-Europe +14%, US payments volume +10% (Visa Q4 FY2026 earnings release, 27 Oct 2026). Travel-related cross-border volume accelerated to +13% and cross-border e-commerce +17% (Nasdaq/Zacks coverage of the call, Nov 2026). The nearest thing to a surge driver is value-added services (fraud, issuing, data, consulting), which grew 31% in constant dollars to \$4 billion in the quarter and 30% to \$14 billion for FY2026 — but that is a product-mix shift, not a scarce good. There is no sold-out capacity, no extending lead time, no price increase forced by scarcity. Shortage thesis: **fails on its own terms**.

### 2. Backlog / order book

Not applicable in the conventional sense — Visa has no order book; revenue is a toll on volume that has not happened yet. The closest forward indicator is the FY2027 contract-renewal cycle: roughly 25% of Visa's payments volume is up for renewal in fiscal 2027 (Finn/Tickeron summaries of Q4 FY2026 commentary, Nov 2026). That is a double-edged pipeline — renewal is an opportunity to attach more value-added services, but it is also when client incentives spike. Evidence: client incentives surged 21% to \$5.1 billion in Q4 FY2026, outpacing 13% net revenue growth (Nasdaq, Nov 2026). No backlog figure is disclosed; "not found" for any contracted future revenue number.

### 3. Category position

Clear #1. Visa held 62.27% of combined Visa + Mastercard purchase volume worldwide in the twelve months to 30 Jun 2026 (\$15.34 trillion vs Mastercard's \$9.29 trillion), and 70.96% in the US in calendar Q2 2026 (Axis Intelligence Research, updated 27 Oct 2026, built from both companies' filings). On the broader Nilson measure, Visa holds ~52% of US card purchase volume vs Mastercard ~25% and Amex ~19.5%. Visa is ~1.65x Mastercard on volume and ~1.27x on net revenue. Note the offset: Mastercard earns 30.1% more net revenue per dollar of volume (37.75bp vs 29.01bp).

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk

**Score: 8/10.** The moat is the two-sided network: ~4.5 billion cards in force, acceptance in 200+ countries, and VisaNet's authorization/clearing/settlement with fraud and chargeback infrastructure no single merchant or bank can replicate cheaply. But the in-sourcing/bypass threat is concrete and live, not theoretical: (a) stablecoins — Visa itself reports stablecoin settlement at a \$25 billion+ annualized run rate with 225+ linked card programs, and outside the US stablecoins are already overtaking fiat in some markets (Tickeron, Nov 2026; Cointelegraph, Jul 2026); (b) real-time domestic rails and account-to-account payments that bypass card networks entirely; (c) Big Tech wallets (Apple Pay, Google Pay) that sit between Visa and the consumer. Visa's answer is to integrate rather than fight — stablecoin settlement pilots, an OpenAI agentic-commerce partnership — which preserves relevance but concedes that the toll is contestable at the margin. No single customer can replace Visa; the system as a whole slowly can.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)

- Q4 FY2026 beat, 27 Oct 2026: net revenue \$12.1 billion (+13%), non-GAAP EPS \$3.48 (+17%, vs \$3.44 expected); FY2026 net revenue \$45.9 billion (+15%), described by management as the strongest growth in 15+ years ex-pandemic/acquisitions (company earnings release; Barron's, 27 Oct 2026).
- Dividend raised 16% to \$0.780/share quarterly; \$5.2 billion returned via buybacks + dividends in the quarter.
- FY2027 guidance: adjusted net revenue and EPS growth both low double digits to low teens (StockRow summary of the call).
- Value-added services guided to low-to-mid-20% growth in FY2027 (Tickeron).
- Analyst sentiment constructive: recent upgrades from Bernstein and Citi noted, consensus target in the mid-\$400s (Tickeron, Nov 2026).

### 6. Risks

- DOJ antitrust suit (filed 24 Sep 2024, SDNY 24-cv-7214) alleging Visa monopolized US debit network services; motion to dismiss denied 23 Jun 2025 — case proceeds. Interchange multidistrict litigation accruals continue (\$1.1 billion added in Q3 FY2026; \$707 million in Q1).
- Credit Card Competition Act and Regulation II / Illinois interchange restrictions could force routing to rival networks and cap fees.
- The FY2027 renewal cycle (25% of volume) pressures margins via client incentives, already growing faster than revenue.
- Stablecoin / real-time-rail disintermediation of the highest-margin cross-border flows over time.
- Valuation: fwd PE 24.8 with low-double-digit growth leaves little room for a miss.

### 7. Shortage score and verdict

**Shortage score: 2/10. Verdict: a durable toll-road compounder, not a shortage bet — steady 9–13% volume growth with no scarcity premium to re-rate it explosively.**

## ABNB — Airbnb Inc. (\$172.15, rev +14.9% TTM, fwd PE 27.1, next earnings 2027-02-11)

### 1. Shortage thesis

A genuine but soft supply/demand tension, not a hard shortage. US short-term-rental supply growth has stalled: AirDNA reported US listings grew just 1.6% YoY in 2026, "the slowest pace since the 2021 reopening", with demand still growing 1.4–2.1% and occupancy floored near 57% for two years (VRMA 2026 / AirDNA, Nov 2026). AirDNA had already cut its 2026 US available-listings growth forecast from +4.6% (Dec 2025) to +2.7% (Jul 2026). Against that flat supply, Airbnb's Q3 2026 demand surged: Nights and Seats Booked +16% to 155 million, accelerating from +10% in Q2, with lead times extending "across major travel corridors" (earnings call transcript, 5 Nov 2026). ADR rose 4% to \$179. So the mechanism is real — demand is outrunning new supply — but supply is host-driven and can respond (hosts list in weeks), and regulation caps (Paris/London 90–120 nights/year) constrain supply by law rather than by Airbnb's capacity. This is a demand-surge-into-flat-supply story, worth a mid-range score, not a capacity-sold-out story.

### 2. Backlog / order book

Airbnb has no backlog in the industrial sense; the closest proxy is unearned fees — service fees guests have already paid for trips not yet taken — which were \$1,536 million at 30 Sep 2026, down 16% YoY even as bookings grew 21% (Beancount analysis of the Q3 filing, 8 Nov 2026). The decline is explained by Reserve Now, Pay Later shifting payment timing, not by weak forward demand. Forward guidance is the better gauge: Q4 2026 revenue guided to \$3.22–3.28 billion (+16–18%), GBV growth mid-teens, Nights and Seats growth at least low double digits; full-year 2026 revenue growth raised to the high teens with adjusted EBITDA margin ~36% (company Q3 release, 5 Nov 2026; Zacks, 6 Nov 2026). No contracted backlog figure is disclosed; "not found".

### 3. Category position

Clear #1 in short-term rentals, with share still rising. Airbnb accounts for ~52% of US nights booked vs Vrbo 14% and direct/other channels 33% (AirDNA via VRMA 2026). In professionally managed US rentals, Airbnb hit 51% of reservations and 43% of revenue in Q2 2026, up from 47%/36% a year earlier, while direct bookings fell to 21%/29% (KeyData via RedAwning, Q2 2026). Globally Airbnb has 9 million+ listings in 220+ countries vs Vrbo's ~2 million in 190 countries, and ~44% of global STR market share (Lodgify, Nov 2026). The #2 (Vrbo/Expedia) is roughly a quarter of Airbnb's listing count. Booking.com is the broader-travel rival (~21% of online travel by one estimate) but is hotel-led.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk

**Score: 6/10.** The moat is demand aggregation: 66% of nights are now booked through the app (up from 62%), app nights +29% YoY, first-time bookers +13% (fastest since early 2022). Hosts cannot replicate that demand pool themselves — but they can and do multi-home: only 28% of listings worldwide are on two or more channels, yet Vrbo, Booking.com and direct bookings remain live alternatives, and Airbnb itself is piloting direct-booking links at a reduced 6–10% host fee (vs standard 15.5%), effectively conceding that hosts want to bypass it (RedAwning/Skift, Aug–Sep 2026). The April 2026 fee restructure (host-only 15.5%) also gives hosts a reason to steer repeat guests direct. Guests can substitute hotels trivially — and Airbnb's own hotel push (thousands of boutique hotels added, hotel nights growing ~3x faster than home nights, Lark Hotels deal for 75+ properties) shows it knows the boundary is porous. Irreplaceable as the demand aggregator; replaceable as a listing pipe.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)

- Q3 2026 blowout, 5 Nov 2026: revenue \$4.87 billion (+19%, fastest in 3+ years, vs \$4.75 billion expected), EPS \$3.16 (+43%, vs \$2.85 expected), net income \$1.9 billion (39% margin), GBV \$27.7 billion (+21%), adjusted EBITDA \$2.5 billion (+22%, 52% margin). Shares rose ~12% on the print (Barron's; Zacks; XTB, Nov 2026).
- Guidance raised: full-year revenue growth to high teens, adjusted EBITDA margin to ~36%.
- \$12 billion new buyback authorization (on top of \$2.7 billion remaining), after \$700 million repurchased in Q3 and \$4 billion over the past year (company release, 5 Nov 2026).
- Seventeen desks raised targets on 6 Nov 2026 alone (Citi \$193→\$227, Mizuho \$200→\$245 and named top pick, Evercore \$200→\$230, Wedbush \$200→\$237); Bernstein raised to \$234 on ~20 Nov 2026; Redburn upgraded to Neutral from Sell (\$190) on 10 Nov; PhillipCapital upgraded to Neutral (\$184) on 16 Nov (AnaChart; TipRanks).
- AI: support assistant now resolves ~40% of customer issues without a human (up from a third in Q2), support cost per booking down 17%+; AI search launched in the US 30 Sep 2026; a full AI travel agent is planned for H1 2027 with sponsored listings to follow (earnings call; Oppenheimer note).
- Hotels expansion accelerating; Reserve Now, Pay Later now covers ~40% of GBV.

### 6. Risks

- The Q3 beat was flattered: Reserve Now, Pay Later added a 3-point net lift to nights; excluding it, bookings grew ~13%, not 16% — and cancellation rates rose to 19% from 16% as pay-later adoption widened (PhillipCapital, Nov 2026). The benefit is expected to peak in Q3 and fade in Q4.
- New businesses (Experiences, Services, hotels) are "unlikely to be material over the next 3 to 5 years" (PhillipCapital).
- Regulation: city-level caps and registration regimes (New York, Paris, London, Barcelona) can remove supply overnight; US international inbound demand is soft (AirDNA: international traveller demand 12% below last spring, Canada −32% vs 2024).
- Take rate fell to 17.6% in Q3; stock-based compensation \$462 million in one quarter; FCF margin slipped to 29% from 33%.
- Valuation after the run: shares +53.6% YTD through 16 Nov 2026; ~43x trailing P/E vs a ~5-year average near 26x (Trefis/Instagram breakdown, Nov 2026).

### 7. Shortage score and verdict

**Shortage score: 5/10. Verdict: the strongest demand-acceleration print in the batch, with real supply tightness behind it — but a third of the bookings beat was pay-later timing, so the explosive case needs Q4 to prove the acceleration is organic.**

## VEEV — Veeva Systems Inc. (\$271.90, rev +11.1% TTM, fwd PE 26.5, next earnings 2026-12-03)

### 1. Shortage thesis

Not a capacity shortage — a forced-demand wave. Veeva's legacy Veeva CRM runs on Salesforce; the Salesforce agreement ended 1 Sep 2025 and Veeva supports legacy CRM only until 31 Dec 2029, forcing every life-sciences customer to choose Vault CRM or a rival inside a roughly four-year window (company filings; Utilarium summary, Nov 2026). That is a genuine structural demand surge with a hard deadline: Q2 FY2027 was Veeva's best CRM quarter ever. In August 2026 two more top-20 biopharmas plus one large enterprise biopharma committed to Vault CRM, taking top-20 commitments to 12 of 20, with two decisions remaining and management "optimistic about our chances in the remaining two", expecting decisions announced this year (Q2 FY2027 prepared remarks, 26 Aug 2026). More than 180 customers are live on Vault CRM, including five of the top 20, up from 140 live at end-FY2026. One top-20 customer deployed Vault CRM plus Agentic Call Report across its entire US field force. The driver is regulatory-grade switching cost plus a contractual deadline — demand that cannot be deferred past 2029. Re-verified fresh: the migration wave is real and still the core thesis.

### 2. Backlog / order book

No formal backlog — and this matters. Veeva's 10-Q for the quarter ended 31 Jul 2026 states the transaction price allocated to remaining performance obligations on non-cancellable subscription contracts longer than one year "was not significant", and the majority of subscription orders have one-year terms (10-Q via DK Street Journal, 24 Nov 2026). The company recognized \$992 million of revenue out of beginning deferred-revenue balances in the six months to 31 Jul 2026. The honest proxy for forward demand is normalized billings: \$768 million in Q2 FY2027, up 19% YoY (Q2 prepared remarks). The migration pipeline (12 committed top-20 accounts, most not yet live) is the de facto backlog — large enterprise deployments convert over quarters — but there is no contracted multi-year book to point at, and the market has noticed: the stock fell 5.2% on 19 Nov 2026, its eighth consecutive down session, worth ~\$7.1 billion of market value (DK Street Journal). "Not found" for any disclosed RPO/backlog figure beyond the 10-Q statement above.

### 3. Category position

#1 in life-sciences industry cloud, by a wide margin in its niches. Vault is the standard for clinical, regulatory, quality and safety content; Commercial (CRM + data + content) is the pharma sales stack. The relevant head-to-head is Vault CRM vs Salesforce's Life Sciences Cloud: Veeva has won 12 of the top 20 biopharma CRM commitments, with two decisions outstanding — i.e. it is winning the migration contest it had to win. No credible third CRM rival exists at enterprise pharma scale; competition is effectively a duopoly for the migration decision. Market-share percentages for the niche are not disclosed; "not found" for a precise share figure.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk

**Score: 8/10.** The moat is validation and switching cost: Vault holds the regulated documents (clinical trials, submissions, safety, quality) that drugmakers cannot move without re-validation, retraining thousands of reps, and regulatory risk. A top-20 migration is a multi-year implementation with data migration and change management (Pulse2, Aug 2026). That said, the threat is the most concrete of the four names: Salesforce is now a direct competitor for the very customers being forced to move, and it owns the platform Veeva is leaving. One independent analysis notes Veeva retained only ~70% of its biopharma CRM subscription base through the transition (DeepValue, Aug 2026) — the other 30% is the leakage to watch. The emerging second threat is AI agents: the 24 Nov 2026 DK Street Journal piece explicitly frames Q3 (3 Dec) as the first quarter in which customers "had reason to ask whether an agent could do the monitoring instead" — Veeva's answer is Veeva AI / Falcon (five early adopters, first go-lives expected in 2026; first top-20 go-live H1 next year). Customers cannot realistically build this themselves; they can, however, choose Salesforce or an AI-native rival at the migration moment.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)

- Q2 FY2027 beat and raise, 26 Aug 2026: total revenue \$928 million (+18%, accelerating from +16% in Q1), subscription revenue \$766.8 million (+16%), GAAP operating income +40% to \$275 million, non-GAAP EPS \$2.35 (vs \$2.22 expected). FY2027 guidance raised to revenue \$3,682–3,687 million and non-GAAP EPS ~\$9.21 (company release; Pulse2).
- Vault CRM best quarter ever; top-20 commitments 10 → 12 in the quarter.
- Veeva AI / Falcon agentic platform: five early adopters, first go-lives in 2026; Vault AI agents added in August.
- Analysts still constructive despite the sell-off: Needham raised target to \$360 (Buy) 6 Nov; Truist raised to \$360 (Buy) 9 Nov; Wells Fargo raised to \$400 (Overweight) 11 Nov; Canaccord raised to \$315 (Hold) 9 Nov; median target ~\$315, ~16% above the close (FundSpec, 25 Nov 2026). Consensus "Moderate Buy", 19 Buy / 8 Hold / 1 Sell (MarketBeat via American Banking News, 26 Nov 2026).
- Q3 FY2027 guidance given in August: revenue \$932–935 million, non-GAAP EPS \$2.33–2.34; consensus now ~\$935 million revenue / \$2.34 EPS.

### 6. Risks

- **Timing risk — the print is 6 days away (3 Dec 2026, after close).** The stock is down 13.3% in one month and 16.6% below its 52-week high of \$325.99 (22 Oct 2026); it has fallen eight straight sessions into 19 Nov. A Q3 miss or soft FY2028 signal would extend the drawdown before any panel pick could be acted on.
- No multi-year backlog: revenue is re-underwritten every annual renewal; consensus already expects growth to ease to ~12.3% in FY2028.
- Salesforce competition for the two remaining top-20 decisions and for the ~30% of CRM base not retained.
- AI-agent substitution risk to Veeva's monitoring/workflow revenue — unproven but now explicitly priced into the November sell-off narrative.
- Concentration: a single industry (life sciences); biopharma R&D budget cuts or pricing pressure flow straight through.
- Q3 is the first quarter where the "AI does the monitoring" question meets actual customer behaviour — guidance language on 3 Dec matters more than the headline beat (Veeva has beaten EPS in 8 of its last 8 quarters, so a beat alone is not the catalyst).

### 7. Shortage score and verdict

**Shortage score: 6/10. Verdict: the best forced-demand wave in the batch, with 12-of-20 top pharma committed and a hard 2029 deadline — but the 3 Dec earnings print is a coin-flip event after a 13% monthly slide, so this is a post-print name, not a pre-print chase.**

## CBOE — Cboe Global Markets Inc. (\$351.80, rev +13.4% TTM, fwd PE 22.7, next earnings 2027-02-05)

### 1. Shortage thesis

The closest thing in the batch to a true shortage: demand for SPX options is surging and only one venue on earth can legally supply them. Index options ADV rose 24% YoY to 6.1 million contracts in Q3 2026, with 0DTE SPX options at a record 65% of overall SPX activity; SPX 0DTE ADV hit a record 3.2 million contracts, +34% YoY (Q3 2026 earnings call, 30 Oct 2026). Retail SPX 0DTE volume jumped 46% YoY (vs +20% institutional) and retail is now an estimated 58% of SPX 0DTE volume, up from 53% a year ago. SPX options set a record 970.6 million contracts in 2025, ADV 3.9 million (+25%, fourth straight record year) (Cboe/S&P DJI release, 29 Sep 2026). XSP volume more than doubled YoY after the June 2026 repeal of the pattern-day-trader rule; Mini-SPX averaged 251,000 contracts/day; overnight SPX volume +117%. This is demand growth for a product whose supply is contractually capped at one supplier — the textbook shape of this panel's shortage thesis, even though the "shortage" is legal exclusivity rather than physical capacity. Management also noted the SPX complex "has not undergone a meaningful pricing adjustment for several years" — i.e. pricing power is unexercised headroom (earnings call via TradingKey).

### 2. Backlog / order book

Not applicable — an exchange has no order book; volume is the backlog, and it is reported daily. The forward book is the license itself: on 28–29 Sep 2026 Cboe and S&P Dow Jones Indices signed a 25-year extension of the exclusive licensing agreement, keeping Cboe as the sole venue for SPX options (plus Mini-SPX, VIX and related S&P options) through 2051, replacing rights that previously ran only to 2032 (company press release, 29 Sep 2026; 8-K). Royalty terms are unchanged for 2026, with new per-contract fees from 1 Jan 2027, described by management as a de minimis impact on 2027 growth. The 25-year runway is the durable forward revenue visibility no other name in this batch can claim.

### 3. Category position

#1 where it counts, #2 where it does not. Cboe operates the largest options exchange in the US (Cboe Options Exchange) and held 30.5% combined US options market share in Q3 2026 (vs 30.9% a year earlier) — #1 among exchange operators, ahead of Nasdaq and NYSE/ICE venues. In US equities it is #3 (on-exchange share 8.9%, down from 9.8%; off-exchange 21.3%, up from 17.9%). But the strategically decisive position is absolute: 100% of SPX options and VIX options — the two most systemically important index-derivative franchises — trade only on Cboe. No #2 exists for SPX.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk

**Score: 10/10.** The moat is a signed contract, not a network effect that can erode: exclusive SPX/VIX rights through 31 Dec 2051, a 40+ year partnership dating to the 1983 SPX launch. No customer can build its own SPX options market — the index is S&P DJI's IP and the license is exclusive. Substitutes exist at the edges (SPY/QQQ ETF options, Nasdaq-100 options, OTC variance swaps, futures), but none replicates SPX's cash-settled, European-style, Section 1256 tax treatment and deep 0DTE liquidity, which is exactly why 0DTE volume concentrates here. The live bypass threat is regulatory rather than commercial: Cboe and allies sued the CFTC over its approval of perpetual futures on single stocks, arguing they are securities that belong on regulated exchanges; Coinbase, Kalshi and Kraken's parent are backing CFTC oversight, and several already have approvals (AVA Fund news summary, 2–12 Nov 2026). If stock-linked perpetuals trade on crypto-style venues instead of Cboe, Cboe loses a growth avenue — but that threatens future products, not the SPX core. Nothing can route around SPX itself before 2051.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)

- Q3 2026 beat, 30 Oct 2026: net revenue \$712.2 million (+18%), adjusted diluted EPS \$3.63 (+36%, vs \$3.43 expected), GAAP EPS a record \$4.08 (helped by the Cboe Australia sale gain). Options net revenue +23% to \$469.5 million; derivatives net revenue +22% to \$402 million; Data Vantage +13%.
- 25-year S&P DJI exclusive license extension through 2051 announced 29 Sep 2026; shares rose ~5.8% to \$268.01 that day (TheStreet; Weekly Observer).
- 2026 guidance raised: organic net revenue growth to high teens (from mid-to-high teens), Data Vantage to mid-teens organic growth; adjusted OpEx narrowed to \$830–845 million.
- KPI binary options (prediction-market contracts on reported corporate metrics — EPS, revenue, Nvidia data-centre revenue, Tesla Model 3/Y production, 100+ indicators across 23 companies) received SEC approval mid-October, listed 9 Nov 2026, with Robinhood as first retail distributor (fees waived through year-end), Interactive Brokers and Schwab cited as incoming; mini-Russell 2000 binaries also launched 9 Nov (Cboe notices C2026110607; CoinPost, 24 Nov 2026). Miami International, NYSE Arca and NYSE American have filed to list the same contracts — first-mover window is short.
- Capital return: buyback authorization increased \$550 million on 22 Oct 2026 (capacity ~\$1 billion); plan to repay \$650 million of senior notes in Q1 2027; \$2.7 billion adjusted cash, 0.7x leverage.
- Volatility tailwind: shares rose 10.03% in the first 12 days of November to \$369.19 as the VIX spiked above 28 (AVA Fund).
- Cboe Clear Europe cleared 1.5 billion+ client equity trades in 2026 to date, beating its 2022 full-year record of 1.49 billion.

### 6. Risks

- 0DTE is retail-froth dependent: retail is 58% of SPX 0DTE volume and retail 0DTE grew 46% YoY. A volatility collapse, a retail drawdown, or a regulatory clampdown on 0DTE would hit the growth engine directly. VIX options ADV rose only 3% in Q3 amid a narrow 14–20 VIX range — the franchise needs volatility, not calm.
- The 2027 royalty reset (new per-contract S&P DJI fees from 1 Jan 2027) is described as de minimis, but the exact economics are not detailed until February — a small unknown on the core margin.
- Prediction-market competition: rival exchanges are already filing to list KPI binaries; the SEC approval order notes standardized terms, so Cboe's first-mover edge may compress to distribution (Robinhood/IBKR/Schwab) rather than product.
- CFTC perpetual-futures fight: if crypto venues win the right to list stock-linked perpetuals, a whole adjacent growth market bypasses regulated exchanges.
- Multi-listed options share is under pressure (combined options share 30.5% vs 30.9% YoY; US equities on-exchange share fell to 8.9%); the growth is concentrated in the proprietary index complex, which is also the concentration risk.
- Fwd PE 22.7 is the cheapest in the batch, but the stock has already run hard in November (VIX spike) — entry after a vol spike is the wrong side of the trade if vol mean-reverts.

### 7. Shortage score and verdict

**Shortage score: 7/10. Verdict: the purest shortage-monopoly in the batch — record 0DTE demand for a product only Cboe may sell, now contracted to 2051 — but the engine runs on retail speculation and volatility, so size it as a momentum trade, not a buy-and-forget compounder.**
