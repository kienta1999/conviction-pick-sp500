## Doctrine honesty note

This field is largely fully priced, and I say so plainly. The two names with the strongest shortage evidence — LLY (8/10) and CBOE (8/10) — are the two worst buys on valuation: LLY trades at $1,392 with only +5.6% to the mean target, realized prices falling −15% YoY, and ~66% of revenue concentrated in two molecules; CBOE trades ~3% below a $363 mean target, near its 52-week high, with the 25-year SPX license extension already rallied into the price and its growth engine a retail 0DTE flow that can reverse in a calm market. Neither is a bargain, however excellent the business. EMR is my pick precisely because it is the least-priced-in name with a real shortage and a real backlog — not because it is the best business in the batch.

**Strict-doctrine vs valuation-adjusted order:** Under strict doctrine (best shortage + moat, ignoring price), the order would be CBOE #1 (10/10 irreplaceability, 8/10 shortage, a true monopoly through 2051), LLY #2 (8/10 shortage, 8/10 irreplaceability, $16.1B of GLP-1 sales in one quarter), then EMR #3 and VEEV #4. My valuation-adjusted order demotes CBOE to #5 and LLY to #6 because both headline catalysts are already priced in — the license extension (+6% on the news) and the GLP-1 demand story (27.4x forward earnings, +5.6% target upside) leave little explosive upside for the next 12–18 months. The one place valuation does not change my mind: ISRG, EW and SYK stay out of the top 10 under either ordering, because their problem is not price alone but decelerating growth and deteriorating competitive position.

**LLY shortage adjudication (yesterday 4/10 vs today 8/10):** Both readings have evidence. The FDA did declare the tirzepatide shortage resolved in Dec 2024, and the binding constraint has rotated from supply to price (−15% realized prices, MFN caps). But the 2026 marginal-gap evidence is real — high-dose pens still seeing 1–4-week regional gaps, the device (not drug substance) as bottleneck, and a $50bn capacity build that only makes sense if demand still outruns marginal supply. I split the difference in spirit: the shortage is real but no longer the scarce-good story it was, and the market has already paid for it. That is why LLY ranks #6 for this lens despite the 8/10 shortage score.
