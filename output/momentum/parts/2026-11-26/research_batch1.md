## 1. LLY — Eli Lilly and Company ($1,392.22; rev +36.7% TTM; fwd PE 27.4; next earnings 2027-02-03)

### 1. Shortage thesis
Yes — a demand surge that supply is still chasing, though the acute national shortage is over. The FDA declared the tirzepatide shortage resolved on 19 Dec 2024 (reaffirmed after an Oct 2024 determination), but the driver persists: GLP-1 demand keeps outrunning capacity at the margin. Evidence: (a) Q3 2026 worldwide revenue +39% YoY to $24.4bn was "driven by a 53% increase in volume, partially offset by a 15% decrease in realized prices" (Lilly Q3 2026 release, 29 Oct 2026) — volume, not price, is the growth engine; (b) high-dose pens (12.5/15 mg) still see "intermittent regional gaps lasting 1 to 4 weeks in some markets" in 2026, with the autoinjector/KwikPen device — not drug substance — the bottleneck (TrimRx/Medfinder shortage trackers, 2026); (c) Lilly's own capacity build is the largest in its history: >$50bn US manufacturing commitments since 2020, ten US sites announced since 2020, including a $6.5bn Houston API plant (broke ground 21 Sep 2026) for orforglipron/Foundayo, a $5bn Virginia plant, $6.5bn Huntsville plant, $3.5bn Lehigh Valley PA device plant (ops from 2031, for retatrutide), and $21bn in Indiana alone; Lebanon API site opening 2027 is billed as the largest API site in US history. A company does not spend $50bn+ because supply is comfortable. The Medicare GLP-1 Bridge program (launched 1 Jul 2026, $50/month out-of-pocket) expanded US coverage ~35% and drove >500,000 Medicare GLP-1 prescriptions in Q3.

### 2. Backlog / order book
Not reported as a backlog (pharma sells through wholesalers, no order book). Closest proxies: Mounjaro Q3 $10.72bn (+65% YoY), Zepbound $5.35bn (+49%); combined $16.1bn in one quarter, +88% YoY tirzepatide in H1 2026; 9M 2026 Mounjaro $29.3bn (+89%), Zepbound $14.4bn (+56%). Total weekly US GLP-1 prescriptions reached 2.88m in the week of 18 Sep 2026, +37.9% YoY. Demand visibility is effectively "sold out at the margin on high doses" rather than a dated book — no "booked through X" figure exists.

### 3. Category position
#1, and pulling away. Lilly held ~62.8% of US branded GLP-1 prescriptions vs Novo Nordisk 37.2% (week of 18 Sep 2026; GLP-1 Global Research, data cut-off 22 Oct 2026); ~63% of the US obesity/diabetes drug market in Q3 2026 per Lilly's earnings presentation (CNBC). By revenue, Lilly held 61.8% of combined Lilly–Novo incretin revenue in Q2 2026, up from 36.0% in 2024 (Axis Intelligence GDSI). Mounjaro was the world's best-selling drug in 2025 ($36.5bn tirzepatide franchise). In orals Lilly trails: Novo's Wegovy pill ~90% of US oral obesity scripts (~5m+ prescriptions in 7 months) vs Foundayo 1.9% of US branded GLP-1 Rx in Sep 2026 — the one segment where Lilly is #2.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk
Customers are patients/payers, not OEMs — nobody "in-sources" a patented molecule. The moat is: (a) tirzepatide's dual GIP/GLP-1 mechanism with head-to-head efficacy benchmark status (SURMOUNT-5: −20.2% at 72 wks); (b) manufacturing scale — $50bn+ of purpose-built capacity and the Lebanon/Houston API plants are a multi-year, multi-billion barrier no generic or compounder can replicate at scale; (c) label depth (diabetes, obesity, OSA; FDA approval Oct 2026 for Mounjaro to reduce CV risk in T2D); (d) the oral pipeline (Foundayo approved 1 Apr 2026 in the US, first small-molecule oral GLP-1; China approval 12 Nov 2026 — China's first oral GLP-1 obesity approval). The bypass threat is real but different: oral semaglutide (Novo), CagriSema (FDA decision Q4 2026), Amgen's MariTide, and Chinese incretins; plus semaglutide generics launched in India Mar 2026 and Canada/Brazil. Compounding was the cheap bypass — now largely closed (FDA Apr 2026 proposal to exclude tirzepatide from bulk compounding list). **Irreplaceability: 8/10** — the molecule + scale moat is formidable; docked 2 points because the category itself is contestable by rival mechanisms and price competition is intensifying.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- Q3 2026 (29 Oct 2026): revenue $24.4bn +39%, beat consensus ($22.1–22.6bn) by ~10%; non-GAAP EPS $7.48 vs $5.38 Zacks consensus (+39% beat); FY2026 revenue guidance raised $5.5bn at midpoint to $91–92bn (from $85–87bn); non-GAAP performance margin outlook raised to 52–52.5%.
- FDA approval of Mounjaro for CV-risk reduction in T2D (Oct 2026); Foundayo global rollout to 40+ countries targeted for 2027; China orforglipron approval 12 Nov 2026.
- Retatrutide: TRIUMPH-1 published in NEJM Nov 2026 (−25.0% body weight at 80 wks on top dose vs −3.9% placebo); BLA filing planned Q1 2027 for obesity, knee-OA pain and OSA.
- Houston $6.5bn plant groundbreaking 21 Sep 2026; $3.5bn Lehigh Valley PA plant announced (retatrutide devices).
- Medicare GLP-1 Bridge: >500k prescriptions in Q3; coverage +35%.

### 6. Risks
- Price is the story now, not volume: Q3 realized prices −15% YoY (−9% underlying US price ex-rebate adjustments, Q2 call); Zepbound list ~$1,086/mo vs $299–449 self-pay; White House Nov 2025 MFN pricing agreements with Lilly/Novo cap future pricing power; IRA 2027 Ozempic price also pressures the class.
- Novo is not dead: Wegovy pill is winning orals (8m+ US prescriptions by end-Sep 2026, DKK 4.2bn in Q3); CagriSema decision due Q4 2026.
- Valuation: at $1,392 and fwd PE 27.4 the market already prices years of GLP-1 dominance; any retatrutide delay (FDA drug-vs-biologic classification dispute — Lilly counts 41 amino acids, FDA says ≤40; district court sided with FDA Sep 2025, Lilly appealed) or a safety signal hits hard.
- Concentration: Mounjaro+Zepbound = ~66% of Q3 revenue; the $900m Q3 benefit included $410m of Jardiance milestones that will not repeat.

### 7. Shortage score & verdict
**Shortage score: 8/10.** Demand still outruns the marginal unit of supply (high-dose pens, oral ramp) even after the formal shortage ended; the $50bn capacity build is the tell.
**Verdict:** The strongest shortage-plus-scale story in the batch — a demand surge with a multi-year capacity moat — but priced as such, and the risk has rotated from "can't make enough" to "can't charge enough."

## 2. ISRG — Intuitive Surgical ($427.18; rev +17.0% TTM; fwd PE 34.3; next earnings 2027-01-21)

### 1. Shortage thesis
No — this is not a shortage story; it is an installed-base/razor-and-blade demand story. There is no evidence of da Vinci systems being sold out or of hospital lead times extending; placements are constrained by hospital capital budgets, not by Intuitive's production. The demand surge is real (procedures +15% YoY in Q3) but it is a penetration story: robotic penetration of eligible procedures is still only low-to-mid teens (~15%) globally, so the "shortage" is of robotic capacity in hospitals, not of Intuitive's supply. Q3 placements hit a record pace (546 systems, +28% YoY) precisely because supply is available.

### 2. Backlog / order book
Intuitive does not report a dollar backlog. Proxies: 546 da Vinci placements in Q3 2026 (vs 427 year-ago), of which 315 were da Vinci 5 (58% of placements, vs 240 a year ago); Ion placements 56 (vs 50). Installed base: 12,094 da Vinci (+12% YoY) and 1,152 Ion (+21%); 13,246 total systems (+13%). US net placements +41% YoY; 18 systems placed in US ambulatory surgery centers (incl. 15 XiR). The recurring engine: instruments & accessories $1.80bn in Q3 (+18% YoY); I&A revenue per procedure ~$1,860. No "booked through" horizon is disclosed.

### 3. Category position
#1 by a wide margin, but the monopoly era just ended. Estimates vary by definition: ~60%+ of surgical robotics (The Teardown, Jun 2026), ~75% of the global robotic-surgery market on ~$8bn robotics revenue (Lobby Network, Nov 2026), ~83.5% share in one 2025 market sizing (ravikant.dev). ~83% of US robotic urology procedures. #2 is Medtronic's Hugo — FDA-cleared in urology late 2025 (first US commercial case Feb 2026), procedure volume more than doubling YoY but from a tiny base; J&J's Ottava won approval mid-2026; CMR's Versius is live in Europe/Asia with US clearance pending. None has yet dented Intuitive's installed-base growth (+12% YoY).

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk
Very high, with a new crack. Moat: 12,094-system installed base, ~86% recurring revenue, two decades of clinical evidence, surgeon training ecosystem, and proprietary instruments/service per procedure — a hospital that buys da Vinci is locked into Intuitive consumables for the system's life. Hospitals cannot realistically build their own surgical robot (FDA clearance, precision engineering, clinical validation). The bypass threat is competitive substitution, not in-sourcing: Hugo at ~$1.5–2m (vs da Vinci 5 higher ASP) targets cost-conscious mid-size hospitals; low-cost rivals (CMR Versius per-procedure leasing, SS Innovations SSi Mantra at ~1/3 the price) attack from below; and Intuitive itself is diluting its own moat — it announced extended-use instruments and volume-based pricing from H1 2027 that will cut I&A revenue per procedure by ~3–5% on a constant-mix basis, plus a China policy-driven price cut from Nov 2026. **Irreplaceability: 8/10** — nobody can replicate the installed base and evidence overnight, but the first credible substitutes now exist and pricing power is being voluntarily surrendered.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- Q3 2026 (20 Oct 2026): revenue $3.05bn +22% YoY, beat $2.91bn consensus; non-GAAP EPS $3.00 vs $2.65 consensus (+25% YoY); procedures +15% (da Vinci +14%, Ion +31%); non-GAAP gross margin 69.9%.
- da Vinci 5: FDA 510(k) for cardiac procedures 26 Jan 2026; CE mark for cardiac (incl. CABG) in Europe Sep 2026 — a 300k–400k annual cardiac-procedure opportunity per management; Real-Time Surgical Insights for da Vinci 5 launched.
- Europe expansion: direct operations begun in Italy, Spain, Portugal, Malta, San Marino (announced Nov 2026).
- $2.94bn of buybacks in Q3 (7.8m shares at ~$377 avg); cash/investments $6.8bn.
- Board: Aaron Martin (President of Insurance, Humana) appointed effective 1 Jan 2027 (announced 2 Nov 2026).

### 6. Risks
- The market punished the print anyway: shares fell ~8% on 21 Oct 2026 ($438 → ~$402) on the narrowed 2026 procedure-growth guide (14–15%, "closer to the midpoint") and the self-imposed I&A price cuts — the recurring-revenue line is being deliberately repriced down.
- Competition is now real: Hugo, Ottava, Versius; China tender volumes and competitive intensity cited on the call; ACA premium-subsidy changes affecting US patient behaviour.
- GLP-1 headwind: six straight quarters of bariatric-procedure decline as weight-loss drugs shrink the surgery pool.
- Valuation: fwd PE 34.3 (trailing ~52x) with procedure growth decelerating from ~17% to ~14–15%; $450m antitrust settlement charge in Q3 (GAAP EPS $1.49 vs $1.95 year-ago) over third-party instrument remanufacturing/servicing — a reminder the closed ecosystem is a legal target.

### 7. Shortage score & verdict
**Shortage score: 4/10.** Strong demand growth, but no supply constraint — hospitals buy when budgets allow; Intuitive can meet demand.
**Verdict:** The best moat in the batch (installed base + recurring consumables) at the worst moment to pay up for it — growth is decelerating, pricing power is being given back, and real competition has arrived for the first time in 20 years.

## 3. AMGN — Amgen ($469.24; rev +6.8% TTM; fwd PE 18.7; next earnings 2027-02-02)

### 1. Shortage thesis
No. There is no structural shortage of Amgen's products. Amgen is a diversified biopharma whose growth is volume-led across many products, not a single scarce product. No capacity-sold-out, lead-time or allocation evidence was found for any Amgen product in 2026. The closest thing to a demand surge is Repatha (first $1bn+ quarter) and IMDELLTRA's launch ramp — both are market-share/adoption stories, not supply-constrained ones. The real "shortage-adjacent" story is prospective: MariTide is being built for the GLP-1 market where demand structurally exceeds supply, but it is still in Phase III with no approval or launch.

### 2. Backlog / order book
Not reported and not applicable (biopharma, no order book). Proxies: Q3 2026 total revenue $10.37bn (+8% YoY), product sales $9.88bn (+8%), "driven entirely by volume gains"; six key growth drivers +27% YoY and >70% of product sales; 17 products with double-digit volume growth. No booked-through horizon exists.

### 3. Category position
Not #1 in any single category that matters for the explosive-return thesis. Amgen is a top-10 global biopharma with leadership in niches: Repatha is a leading PCSK9 inhibitor (vs Sanofi/Regeneron's Praluent); Prolia/Xgeva lead bone health but face biosimilars; Enbrel is a mature, declining TNF brand. In the obesity category that would drive an explosive re-rating, Amgen is a challenger: MariTide is in Phase III behind Lilly's tirzepatide and Novo's semaglutide/CagriSema, with differentiation only on dosing frequency (monthly, potentially 4–6 doses/year maintenance).

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk
Moderate-low for the current business, higher for MariTide if it works. Moat: biologics manufacturing complexity, patent portfolios, and the antibody-peptide conjugate platform behind MariTide. But the in-sourcing/substitution threat is concrete and already biting: biosimilars are eroding mature brands (the Q3 call flagged biosimilars as a "woe"); Prolia/Xgeva biosimilar pressure, Enbrel decline, and government price regulation (IRA) are structural. Customers (payers/PBMs) can and do substitute biosimilars and rival mechanisms — Amgen itself is a biosimilar seller ($977m biosimilar sales in Q3, +26%), so it profits from the same disintermediation it suffers. No customer can "build" a biologic, but payers can route around it. **Irreplaceability: 5/10** — manufacturing and IP protect individual products, but the portfolio is a collection of substitutable biologics facing biosimilar erosion, not a single irreplaceable platform.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- Q3 2026 (3–4 Nov 2026): revenue $10.37bn (+8%), beat $9.95bn consensus; non-GAAP EPS $6.45 vs $5.74 consensus; GAAP EPS $7.08 (+19%); FY2026 guidance raised to revenue $38.9–39.7bn (from $38.2–39.4bn) and non-GAAP EPS $22.90–23.70 (from $22.30–23.50). FCF $3.7bn in Q3.
- Repatha $1,085m in Q3 (+37%) — first $1bn+ quarter; EVENITY +39%; UPLIZNA +191%; IMDELLTRA $406m (more than doubled YoY) with Phase III first-line maintenance SCLC data: median OS 21.1 months vs 12.7 months for IMFINZI alone.
- MariTide: 10 named Phase III studies running; enrollment completed in MARITIME sleep-apnea studies and the CV outcomes study; first of 3 T2D Phase III studies initiated; 2 more T2D studies to start in Q4 2026; MARITIME-T2D-3 head-to-head vs semaglutide enrolling.
- Daxdilimab to Phase III in primary discoid lupus after positive Phase II (both doses met primary endpoint, benefit from week 4).
- Capex ~$2.6bn in 2026 incl. US manufacturing in NC, Ohio, Puerto Rico ahead of a potential MariTide launch.

### 6. Risks
- The obesity option is years away and unproven on tolerability; MariTide Phase III readouts will not arrive until well into 2027+; by then Lilly's retatrutide and Novo's CagriSema may have reset the bar.
- Biosimilar erosion of the mature base and IRA price regulation cap the core; Q4 opex steps up materially (sales mix, profit share, SG&A timing).
- Balance sheet: $54.0bn total debt (vs $13.2bn cash) from the Horizon acquisition; net interest ~$2.6bn annualized drag.
- Valuation is the bull case (fwd PE 18.7, cheapest in the batch) — but cheap reflects a 6.8% TTM revenue grower with a binary pipeline kicker, not a shortage.

### 7. Shortage score & verdict
**Shortage score: 2/10.** No supply constraint on any current product; the only shortage-adjacent angle (MariTide into GLP-1 demand) is pre-revenue.
**Verdict:** A cheap, well-executing biopharma with a genuine but distant obesity lottery ticket — not a shortage bet; the explosive case rests entirely on MariTide data that is at least a year out.

#### Earnings-quality flags (mandatory)
- **RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN — Verdict: LARGELY BENIGN, monitor.** Trade receivables were $10,353m at 30 Sep 2026 vs $8,490m at 30 Sep 2025 (+21.9% YoY) while Q3 revenue grew +8.5% YoY — receivables are indeed outrunning revenue on a point-in-time YoY comparison. ## 4. EW — Edwards Lifesciences ($106.36; rev +11.1% TTM; fwd PE 31.4; next earnings 2027-02-09)

### 1. Shortage thesis
No — a steady procedure-growth story, not a shortage. TAVR demand grows with demographics and expanding indications (earlier intervention, moderate AS), and Edwards' constraint is procedure throughput (cath-lab staffing, imaging, diagnostic referral rates), not valve supply. No evidence of valves being sold out, allocated, or back-ordered; average selling prices were "stable globally" in Q3 (Edwards Q3 release, 4 Nov 2026) — the opposite of a shortage pricing signal. The demand surge is in the mitral/tricuspid segment (TMTT +44%), but again adoption-led, not supply-constrained.

### 2. Backlog / order book
Not reported (medical devices, no order book). Proxies: Q3 sales $1.72bn (+10.5%, +11.2% constant currency); TAVR $1.24bn (+8.0%, +8.8% cc); TMTT $207m (+44.0%, +45.6% cc); Surgical $264m (+3%). No booked-through horizon disclosed. The forward indicator is the installed/treated-patient funnel: >800,000 cumulative SAPIEN implants (per industry reports), and TMTT's $2bn-by-2030 revenue target (from ~$780–790m guided for 2026).

### 3. Category position
#1 in TAVR, decisively. GlobalData: Edwards ~66% of the TAVR market vs Medtronic ~28%, Abbott ~2.9%; other estimates put Edwards at 56–66% (range reflects definitions) and ~75% of the US TAVI market. TAVR is ~74% of Edwards' revenue (FY2025: $4.49bn). #2 Medtronic (Evolut) is a genuine threat: a 3-year head-to-head Evolut-vs-SAPIEN study favoured Evolut, and GlobalData expects Medtronic to gain share from it. Edwards itself said on the Q3 call that its competitive position has "reached a plateau after recent share gains," was "stable sequentially" in the US, and that management expects "a slight loss [of share] over time as competition increases."

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk
High for the valve itself, low for the procedure pathway. Moat: the SAPIEN platform's clinical-evidence base (decades of trial data incl. PARTNER), RESILIA tissue technology, physician training/relationships, and regulatory labels — a hospital cannot in-source a transcatheter valve, and switching to Medtronic requires retraining and different anatomy suitability (balloon-expandable vs self-expanding). The bypass threat is substitution by the #2 (Medtronic Evolut) and, longer-term, by surgical valves or emerging competitors (Abbott Navitor, Boston Scientific) — plus the PROGRESS trial opening moderate-AS treatment, which expands the market for everyone, not just Edwards. Also: TAVR depends on hospital cath-lab capacity, so the "customer" (health system) can throttle Edwards by throttling procedures. **Irreplaceability: 7/10** — the valve is effectively irreplaceable per-patient, but share is contestable by one strong rival and management itself guides to slight share loss.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)

- SAPIEN M3 with RESILIA tissue launched in Europe (Q3); strong SAPIEN M3 demand for patients unsuitable for mitral TEER.
- TCT 2026 (Oct): new evidence on SAPIEN, PASCAL, EVOQUE and SAPIEN M3; PROGRESS trial results advanced the moderate-AS case (SAPIEN 3 use in moderate AS still investigational).
- US approvals: PASCAL Precision (tricuspid repair) and PASCAL Ace with Capture Clarity (mitral repair); $700m buybacks YTD, $1.3bn authorization remaining; $3.10bn cash at 30 Sep 2026.

### 6. Risks
- Share plateau is management's own word: future TAVR growth must come from procedure volume, not share gains, and Medtronic's head-to-head data is a live threat.
- Concentration: ~74% of revenue is TAVR; growth is hostage to cath-lab staffing, imaging bottlenecks and referral rates.
- TMTT is exciting (+44%) but small ($207m/quarter) and will not move the needle until closer to the $2bn-by-2030 target.
- Valuation: fwd PE 31.4 for ~11% growth with guided share loss is full; Q4 adjusted EPS guidance ($0.61–0.69) implies a sequential step-down.

### 7. Shortage score & verdict
**Shortage score: 3/10.** Solid demand growth with no supply constraint and flat pricing — the opposite of a shortage signature.
**Verdict:** The #1 player in a growing structural-heart market, but a share-plateau, volume-only growth story at a premium multiple — steady compounder, not an explosive shortage bet.
