# Ballot C — Quality / moat & irreplaceability — stock-pick-momentum, 2026-11-26

## (A) Single-pick ballot

- **Top pick:** LLY
- **Runner-up:** CBOE
- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** LLY is the only name in this batch that clears both halves of my lens at once: a genuine demand-outruns-supply shortage (8/10) behind a patented, purpose-built moat (8/10). It is pulling away as the #1 GLP-1 franchise — 62.8% of US branded GLP-1 prescriptions, 61.8% of combined Lilly–Novo incretin revenue, up from 36.0% in 2024 — with a $50bn+ manufacturing build that no rival can replicate quickly. Q3 revenue grew 39% YoY on +53% volume, guidance was raised $5.5bn, and retatrutide (−25.0% at 80 weeks, NEJM Nov 2026) extends the pipeline into the next generation. The constraint has rotated from "can't make enough" to "can't charge enough," but that is a pricing problem on a franchise growing ~37% — a far better problem than a moat with no growth.
- **Key shortage/backlog evidence:** Tirzepatide did $16.1bn in a single quarter (Mounjaro $10.72bn + Zepbound $5.35bn), and high-dose pens (12.5/15 mg) still see intermittent 1–4 week regional gaps in 2026 because the autoinjector/KwikPen device — not drug substance — is the bottleneck.
- **Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk:** medium — the dual GIP/GLP-1 molecule, label depth (diabetes, obesity, OSA, Mounjaro CV-risk approval Oct 2026) and $50bn of purpose-built capacity are not copyable, and no customer can in-source a patented biologic. But substitution is real and live: Novo's Wegovy pill already holds ~90% of US oral obesity scripts, CagriSema gets an FDA decision in Q4 2026, MariTide and Chinese incretins are coming, and compounding was only recently closed. This is a patent-and-scale moat, not a legal monopoly — it defends by staying ahead, not by being unassailable.
- **12-18 month return scenario:** Base +10–15% — volume growth continues but realized-price erosion (−15% YoY) and MFN pricing caps keep the multiple from expanding; the analyst mean target implies only +5.6%, so the base case is mostly earnings compounding. Bull +30–40% — retatrutide BLA (planned Q1 2027) lands cleanly, high-dose pen supply normalizes into full demand capture, and pricing stabilizes as the Medicare GLP-1 Bridge ($50/month, >500k prescriptions in Q3) broadens the covered base.
- **Top risk:** Price, not supply — realized prices already fell 15% YoY, White House MFN pricing agreements cap pricing power, and Mounjaro + Zepbound are ~66% of Q3 revenue, so any further pricing concession hits the whole thesis.
- **Conviction (1-10):** 7

### Note on the doctrine

No name in this batch fully satisfies my lens — there is no AI-hardware-style shortage name, and the two 8/10-shortage names are both compromised on upside (LLY by pricing, CBOE by a priced-in license extension and ~3% analyst upside). LLY is the closest: it is the only name with shortage 8/10 *and* irreplaceability 8/10 *and* explosive growth (+36.7% TTM revenue). CBOE has the perfect moat (10/10) but its explosive leg is a retail 0DTE flow that can reverse, and the market has already paid for the 2051 license. Per the doctrine, a moat without explosive upside ranks below a name with both — hence LLY over CBOE, and CBOE over the pure compounders (V, ISRG, EW) whose moats are real but whose shortage scores are 3–4/10.

On the LLY shortage disagreement: yesterday's panel scored LLY shortage 4/10 ("shortage over") on the FDA's Dec 2024 resolution declaration. I side with today's 8/10 — the FDA declaration resolved the drug-substance shortage, but the dossier's 2026 evidence (1–4 week high-dose pen gaps, device as bottleneck, 2.88m weekly US GLP-1 scripts +37.9% YoY) shows demand still outruns *marginal* supply at the dose/device level. It is a narrower shortage than 2024, not a resolved one.

### Top 3 — most compelling shortage/backlog or moat data point + scenario

**1. LLY.** The single most compelling data point is the $16.1bn of tirzepatide revenue in one quarter with high-dose pens still gapping 1–4 weeks regionally — demand is still rationing itself at the margin while Lilly spends $50bn+ on capacity no competitor can match on this timeline. Base +10–15% (volume growth offset by price erosion); bull +30–40% (clean retatrutide BLA, pricing stabilizes, Medicare Bridge broadens coverage).

**2. CBOE.** The moat data point is the 25-year exclusive SPX license extension signed Sep 29, 2026, running through 2051, at ~$0.95 index revenue per contract versus ~$0.05 for multi-listed options — a legal monopoly printing 71.4% adjusted operating margins on record SPX 0DTE volumes (3.2m contracts/day, +34%, 65% of all SPX activity). Base +5–10% (volumes hold, margins absorb the higher S&P DJI royalties from Jan 2027); bull +20–25% (volatility stays elevated, retail 0DTE keeps compounding, XSP keeps doubling post-PDT-rule repeal).

**3. EMR.** The only quantified backlog in the batch: $8.2bn, +11% YoY, with underlying orders +14% (accelerating from +7%), Ovation orders +26%, and a $13bn project funnel +17% — real forward visibility tied to a documented multi-year equipment shortage (large power transformers ~128-week lead times, +77% prices since 2019). The honest caveat: EMR is a second-order beneficiary — it sells the control systems, not the scarce transformers — and only 70% of backlog ships within 12 months. Base +8–12% (FY27 sales ~+5%, EPS ~$7.25, +10%); bull +25–30% (power greenfield activity +45% converts into a multi-year automation supercycle as data-centre capacity goes 24 GW → 110 GW by 2030).
