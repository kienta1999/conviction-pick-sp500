## LLY

### 1. Q3 2026 tirzepatide revenue $16.1bn — CONFIRMED

- Lilly Q3 2026 results (8-K, reported 29 Oct 2026): total revenue **$24.4bn, +39% YoY**, "supported by a 53% increase in volume that more than offset a 15% reduction in realized prices."
- **Mounjaro $10.72bn (+65% YoY)**; **Zepbound $5.35bn (+49% YoY)**; combined **$16.08bn ≈ $16.1bn**, ~66% of total revenue.
- Sources: Lilly Q3 2026 8-K via SECFilings.com (29 Oct 2026); Pharmaceutical Technology (30 Oct 2026); Zacks (29 Oct 2026); TheStreet. All four figures in the claim match to the decimal.

### 2. High-dose pen gaps of 1–4 weeks in 2026, device bottleneck — UNVERIFIED

- The **FDA resolution date is correct**: FDA declared the tirzepatide shortage resolved 19 Dec 2024 (reaffirmed after an initial 2 Oct 2024 resolution).
- **No primary source found** for the "intermittent regional gaps of 1–4 weeks" figure or for the autoinjector/KwikPen bottleneck. The only sources stating the 1–4-week figure and the device-bottleneck claim are low-authority telehealth marketing sites (TrimRx; Practical Telehealth; PeptideDeck), which cite IQVIA dispensing data and patient reports without linking them. No Lilly press release, earnings-call remark, or FDA statement corroborates the specific duration or the device bottleneck.
- **Counter-evidence:** a live FDA shortage-list tracker (getamrx.com, loaded from the FDA 26 Nov 2026, 01:41) shows **no current shortage record for tirzepatide at any strength** — Mounjaro and Zepbound, all six doses, pen and KwikPen formats, all "No shortage listed." The FDA's own position is that only "intermittent, localized supply disruptions" may still occur.
- **Corrected framing:** the claim should be downgraded from a factual shortage to "anecdotal/secondary reports of pharmacy-level tightness at 12.5/15 mg doses in 2026, with no FDA-listed shortage and no company confirmation." This is the weakest leg of the LLY thesis and directly bears on the panel's 8/10 vs yesterday's 4/10 shortage disagreement.

### 3. Retatrutide BLA Q1 2027; TRIUMPH-1 NEJM Nov 2026, −25.0% — PARTLY CONTRADICTED

- **BLA filing planned Q1 2027: CONFIRMED.** Lilly announced (July 2026, with TRIUMPH-2/-3 topline) it plans to submit a Biologics License Application to the FDA in Q1 2027, for obesity, knee-osteoarthritis pain and obstructive sleep apnoea. Repeated in Lilly's 29 Sep 2026 releases and the Q3 earnings call.
- **TRIUMPH-1 published in NEJM "Nov 2026": CONTRADICTED.** TRIUMPH-1 was published online in the *New England Journal of Medicine* on **29 September 2026** (Jastreboff et al., DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa2604169), alongside TRIUMPH-2 in *The Lancet*. The November 2026 NEJM retatrutide publications were TRIUMPH-4 and TRIUMPH-1-OA (knee osteoarthritis), presented at ACR Convergence 8–10 Nov 2026. The dossier appears to have conflated the two publication waves.
- **−25.0% at 80 weeks: CONFIRMED, with an estimand caveat.** −25.0% is the NEJM primary-analysis (treatment-regimen / intention-to-treat) figure for the 12 mg arm at 80 weeks. Lilly's own headline figure using the efficacy estimand is **−28.3%**. Both are correct under different estimands; the dossier's −25.0% matches the NEJM paper.
- **Correction:** "TRIUMPH-1 published in NEJM 29 Sep 2026 (−25.0% ITT / −28.3% efficacy estimand at 80 wks on 12 mg); BLA filing planned Q1 2027."

### 4. >$50bn US manufacturing since 2020; $6.5bn Houston API plant, broke ground 21 Sep 2026 — CONFIRMED

- Lilly press release, **21 Sep 2026** (via PR Newswire / Morningstar): Lilly "broke ground today in Houston, Texas, one of ten U.S. manufacturing sites … announced since 2020," as part of "the company's $50 billion push to reshore medicine production"; "the $6.5 billion Houston site will manufacture Foundayo (orforglipron) … along with other small-molecule medicines." Site expected online ~2030, 600+ jobs, 4,000 construction jobs.
- Corroborated by Construction Dive (24 Sep 2026) and ConstructConnect.
- Minor note (not part of the claim as written): Construction Dive describes the Huntsville, Alabama plant as a **$6bn** project, whereas the dossier says $6.5bn — worth a one-line check if that figure is used elsewhere.
