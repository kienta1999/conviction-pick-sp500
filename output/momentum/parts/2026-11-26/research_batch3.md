## 1. V — Visa Inc. ($424.39; rev +12.2% TTM; fwd PE 24.8; next earnings 2027-01-26)

### 1. Shortage thesis
No structural shortage in the classic sense — Visa sells network capacity, not a scarce physical good, and its network has effectively infinite headroom (it processed ~9,000 transactions/second in Q4 FY2026). The closest analogue is a demand surge it cannot be cut out of: cross-border volume is accelerating while supply of alternatives is thin. Q4 FY2026 (reported Oct 27, 2026): payments volume +9% constant dollars, cross-border volume ex-intra-Europe +14% (up from +12% in Q3), travel-related cross-border +13% (3 pts acceleration), cross-border e-commerce +17%. Through Oct 21, cross-border was still running +15%. Commercial payments volume grew 12% for FY2026, "the fastest annual growth rate in eight years, excluding the post-pandemic recovery" (Q4 FY2026 earnings call, Oct 27, 2026). Visa Direct topped 15 billion transactions (~$1.5 trillion volume) in FY2026. Stablecoin settlement passed a $25bn annualized run rate, 10x a year earlier, with 225+ stablecoin-linked card programs. This is demand-pull growth, not capacity-constrained shortage — no sold-out capacity or extending lead times exist to quote.

### 2. Backlog / order book
Not applicable in the order-book sense; Visa does not report a backlog. The nearest forward indicator is its contracted volume pipeline: ~25% of payments volume comes up for client renewal in FY2027 (vs ~20% in FY2026), per CFO Chris Suh on the Q4 call — a renewal wave, not a booked backlog. Value-added services (VAS) revenue reached $14bn in FY2026, +30% constant dollars, with FY2027 VAS growth guided low-to-mid-20s.

### 3. Category position
Clear #1. Visa processed 62.27% of combined Visa+Mastercard purchase volume in the 12 months to June 30, 2026 ($15.34tn vs Mastercard's $9.29tn), per Axis Intelligence Research's calendar-aligned analysis of both companies' filings (Oct 27, 2026). In the US its share was 70.96% in Q2 2026 (vs 70.28% a year earlier). Nilson Report (Apr 2026): Visa handled ~31% of US debit transactions and ~30% of US credit transactions vs Mastercard's ~12–14%. Mastercard earns more per dollar (37.75bp vs Visa's 29.01bp net revenue per dollar of volume), but Visa is ~1.65x on volume.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk
Moat: a two-sided network with ~80% global processing penetration, 281bn transactions in FY2026 across 200+ countries, and issuer/acquirer relationships that no single merchant or bank can replicate. The concrete in-sourcing/bypass threats are real and named in Visa's own risk disclosures: (a) account-to-account / open-banking rails that bypass card interchange entirely; (b) stablecoins as an alternative settlement layer — Visa's answer is to co-opt them (settlement on 10 blockchains, validator seats on Tempo and Canton, invitation to Circle's Arc); (c) large merchants/banks building direct rails, and (d) the FY2027 renewal wave — 25% of volume up for renewal, with client incentives already up 21% to $5.12bn in FY2026, growing faster than revenue. No customer can realistically in-source the global acceptance network; the threat is margin erosion via incentives and slow disintermediation at the edges, not replacement.
**Irreplaceability score: 8/10.**

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- Q4 FY2026 earnings (Oct 27, 2026): adj EPS $3.48 (+17% YoY, beat $3.43 consensus); net revenue $12.14bn (+13%, beat $12.08bn); FY2026 revenue a record $45.9bn (+15%), "strongest underlying growth in more than 15 years".
- FY2027 guidance: low-double-digit adjusted net revenue growth, low-double-digit to low-teens adj EPS growth; VAS low-to-mid-20s growth.
- Stablecoin settlement $25bn annualized run rate (10x YoY); ~50 AI/agentic-commerce partners in production.
- Pending $2.4bn BioCatch acquisition (fraud protection) announced around the Q4 call.
- FIFA World Cup demand: US host-city inbound card-present spend +20%+ YoY July 1–19, 2026.

### 6. Risks
- Renewal/incentive squeeze: 25% of volume renews in FY2027; incentives +21% to $5.12bn already outpacing revenue.
- International transaction revenue grew only +8% in Q4 despite +14% cross-border volume (FX volatility, mix) — monetization lags volume.
- Stablecoin/A2A disintermediation is a slow-burn structural threat; management concedes stablecoin/agentic monetization is "in the early stages" with limited near-term profit impact.
- Macro/cyclical: consumer spending and cross-border travel (Middle East conflict flagged) drive volume; FY2027 guide steps down from FY2026's 15% to low-double-digit growth.
- Valuation already prices a quality compounder: fwd PE 24.8 at $424.39, near highs; explosive-return upside is limited without a re-rating.

### 7. Shortage score: 3/10
**Verdict:** A best-in-class tollbooth with accelerating cross-border demand, but no shortage and no explosive-return setup — a compounder, not a momentum breakout bet.

## 2. ABNB — Airbnb Inc. ($172.15; rev +14.9% TTM; fwd PE 27.1; next earnings 2027-02-11)

### 1. Shortage thesis
Partial, demand-side: travel demand is re-accelerating faster than supply in key markets, and Airbnb is capturing the flow. Q3 2026 (Nov 5, 2026): Nights and Seats Booked +16% YoY to 155m (acceleration from +10% in Q2), GBV +21% to $27.7bn, revenue +19% to $4.87bn — fastest revenue growth in 3+ years. Management cited "lead times extended across major travel corridors" and North America nights growing at "their fastest pace in four years". First-time bookers +13% YoY, fastest since early 2022, with Gen Z fastest. The supply side is not short in aggregate (~9m listings globally), but regulatory caps are shrinking legal supply in major cities (see Risks), which pushes prices up for remaining listings — ADR +4% YoY to ~$179. The shortage is city-level and regulation-driven, not company-level capacity.

### 2. Backlog / order book
No formal backlog; the forward book is visible in unearned fees and lead times. Unearned fees (guest service fees paid for trips not yet taken) were $1,536m at Sep 30, 2026 — down 16% YoY even as bookings grew 21%, because Reserve Now, Pay Later (RNPL) lets guests book further ahead without prepaying; RNPL added a ~3% net lift to Q3 nights. Q4 2026 guidance: revenue $3.22–3.28bn (+16–18% YoY), mid-teens GBV growth, at least low-double-digit Nights and Seats growth. FY2026 revenue growth outlook raised to "high teens" (third upward revision in 2026) with ~36% adjusted EBITDA margin.

### 3. Category position
Clear #1 in short-term rentals. Skift 2026: Airbnb ~44% of the global STR market (up from 28% in 2019) vs Booking.com ~18% and Vrbo ~9%. US professionally managed STRs (KeyData Q2 2026): Airbnb 51% of reservations (up from 47% a year earlier) and 43% of revenue, vs Vrbo 20% of reservations and direct bookings 21%. ~9m listings worldwide vs Vrbo's ~2m. In North America Airbnb captures 70%+ of STR bookings (AirDNA). Booking.com is the #2 threat globally (~40% of global bookings per AirDNA's review-count measure, dominant in Europe) — the two sources disagree on Booking.com's share, so treat the exact #2 figure as approximate.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk
Moat: two-sided network effects — 9m listings and the default search destination for guests; hosts multi-list but Airbnb delivers the majority of reservations, so hosts cannot leave. Disintermediation threats: (a) direct bookings — hosts increasingly steer repeat guests off-platform (direct fell to 21% of US professionally managed reservations in Q2 2026, but that is still a large leakage channel; Airbnb is piloting direct-booking links at a reduced 6–10% host fee vs standard ~15.5% to keep those bookings on-platform); (b) Booking.com/Vrbo multi-listing means hosts are not locked in; (c) hotels and Google Travel as substitutes. No guest or host can in-source the demand aggregation, but the take rate (~13–14% implied) is constantly arbitraged by hosts.
**Irreplaceability score: 6/10.**

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- Q3 2026 (Nov 5, 2026): revenue $4.87bn (+19%), EPS $3.16 (+43% YoY, beat $2.85 expected); GBV +21%; net income $1.9bn (39% margin); adjusted EBITDA $2.5bn (+22%, 52% margin). Shares surged ~12% on the print (to ~$188.61 intraday Nov 6).
- FY2026 outlook raised to high-teens revenue growth; Q4 guide +16–18%.
- New $12bn share buyback authorization (Nov 3, 2026) on top of $2.7bn remaining; $712m repurchased in Q3.
- App nights +29% YoY, 66% of nights booked (from 62%); RNPL expansion lifting bookings and lead times.
- Balance sheet: $12.6bn cash/investments/restricted cash at Sep 30, 2026.

### 6. Risks
- Regulation is the structural overhang: NYC Local Law 18 (2023) cut listings ~90% (~22,000 → <3,000 registered); Barcelona will not renew any of its 10,101 tourist-flat licenses, ending licensed short stays by Nov 2028; Budapest District 6 banned STRs Jan 1, 2026; Athens froze new registrations; Paris capped at 90–120 nights/yr; Maui County Bill 9 (signed Dec 15, 2025) phases out apartment-zone vacation rentals. Each city that caps supply caps Airbnb's TAM there.
- Cyclical travel demand; ADR growth (+4%) is modest and take rate fell slightly as RNPL shifts payment timing.
- Insider selling: founders sold ~$1.12bn of ~$1.14bn insider sales in the last 12 months (under trading plans).
- Competition from Booking.com in Europe and hotels; direct-booking leakage pressures take rate.
- Valuation: fwd PE 27.1 with a ~$172 price well below the post-earnings ~$189 spike — momentum has already partially faded.

### 7. Shortage score: 5/10
**Verdict:** Real demand re-acceleration and a Q3 beat-and-raise with a fresh $12bn buyback, but the "shortage" is regulation-created and city-specific, and regulatory creep is the same force that caps the upside — a momentum candidate on the earnings inflection, not a structural-shortage explosive bet.

## 3. VEEV — Veeva Systems Inc. ($271.90; rev +11.1% TTM; fwd PE 26.5; next earnings 2026-12-03 — 7 days, timing risk)

### 1. Shortage thesis
No physical shortage. The demand-surge analogue is a forced, industry-wide migration wave: every Veeva CRM customer must move off the Salesforce platform — Veeva CRM support ends December 31, 2029, the Salesforce agreement expired September 1, 2025, and Salesforce-based application sales are prohibited after September 1, 2030. This creates a multi-year, non-deferrable re-platforming demand surge across the whole life-sciences industry, with Veeva's Vault CRM as the default destination. The migration is a genuine re-implementation project for each customer (custom Apex code and workflows do not carry over), so services demand is also strong. But it is a one-time migration wave, not a recurring capacity shortage, and Salesforce is contesting every deal.

### 2. Backlog / order book
Veeva does not headline a backlog figure; remaining performance obligations (RPO) figure not retrieved from the Q2 FY2027 10-Q — **not found**. Forward indicators: FY2027 revenue guidance $3,682–3,687m (raised at Q2); Q3 FY2027 guidance $932–935m total revenue, non-GAAP EPS $2.33–2.34. Subscription revenue $766.8m in Q2 FY2027 (+16% YoY), ~83% of revenue. Vault CRM: 180+ customers live at Q2 (200+ by the Nov 5 Investor Day), including 5 top-20 biopharmas live; 12 of top 20 committed at Q2, 14 of top 20 committed by Investor Day (Nov 5, 2026), with only 2 top-20 decisions still open, expected to resolve by year-end. Management targets a $6bn revenue run rate by 2030 (reiterated Nov 5, 2026) and ~70% share of biopharma CRM subscription revenue through 2030.

### 3. Category position
Dominant #1 in life-sciences CRM. Veeva controls "more than 80% of market share in biopharma CRM" (Barron's, Oct 2026) / ~70–80% among large pharma (industry estimates); on a broader pharma/biotech CRM software market definition, Veeva ~26.8% vs IQVIA ~17.7% and Salesforce ~16.4% (Research and Markets, Nov 2025). #2 is now Salesforce Life Sciences Cloud (built with IQVIA's OCE), which claims 100+ life-sciences customers have selected it and 6 of the top 10 pharma. In R&D Vault (clinical/regulatory/quality/safety), Veeva is also the category standard. The CRM contest is the closest thing to a share fight in this batch.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk
Moat: purpose-built, validated workflows for regulated pharma (sample distribution, HCP engagement, compliance), 20 years of compounding, and deep integration across Commercial + R&D Vault on one platform; switching costs are high because the system is woven into reps' daily work and regulatory audit trails. In-sourcing threat: effectively nil — no pharma builds its own CRM. Substitution threat: real and live — Salesforce + IQVIA Life Sciences Cloud is a credible, well-funded alternative, and Salesforce claims it is "gaining market share from Veeva like there is no tomorrow" (CRO Miguel Milano, post-Dreamforce 2025). Veeva's own 10-Q warns some customers "have chosen, or may choose, to move to Salesforce". Scorecard so far favors Veeva: 12–14 of top 20 committed to Vault CRM vs 6 for Salesforce (per earlier 2026 tallies), an ~80% win rate in decided top-20 deals. But each lost top-20 account is a multi-year revenue hole, and the migration window (2026–2029) is exactly when customers are most movable.
**Irreplaceability score: 7/10** (high for the product, docked for the live Salesforce contest during the forced migration window).

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- Q2 FY2027 (Aug 26, 2026): revenue $928.0m (+18% YoY, beat $905m consensus), non-GAAP EPS $2.35 (beat $2.22); subscription +16%; non-GAAP operating margin ~45%; FY2027 guidance raised to $3,682–3,687m revenue and ~$9.21 non-GAAP EPS. Shares rose +15.3% the next day.
- Vault CRM wins in Q2 from Lilly, Biogen, Regeneron; 12 of top 20 committed; 14 of top 20 by Nov 5 Investor Day.
- Investor Day (Nov 5, 2026): $6bn run-rate-by-2030 target reiterated; 200+ Vault CRM customers live; Veeva Falcon (agentic AI platform) 5 early adopters with initial go-lives in 2026; new horizontal CRM startup "Aspen" disclosed.
- Nov 6, 2026 8-K: company expects to meet or exceed Q3 FY2027 guidance (revenue $932–935m, non-GAAP EPS $2.33–2.34).
- Acquisitions: Copli (Q2 FY2027) and Ostro/Rise Healthcare Tech ($90m, Mar 2026).
- Balance sheet: $7.24bn cash + short-term investments vs $151.7m debt at Jul 31, 2026; FCF TTM ~$1.64bn.

### 6. Risks
- **Earnings in 7 days (Dec 3, 2026, after market close; call 5:00 PM ET):** consensus EPS $2.33–2.34 on revenue ~$935m; average post-earnings move 8.3%, last print +15.3% — a binary event right at the panel date. Any Vault CRM loss or guidance trim would hit hard.
- Salesforce competition is the central risk: 6 of top 20 already lost or leaning Salesforce; the forced migration gives every customer a free option to switch.
- Customer concentration: top 10 customers = 28% of FY2026 revenue.
- EU Data Act cited as a novel cancellation risk; AWS dependency for Vault.
- Valuation: fwd PE 26.5 (TTM PE ~43.8) for ~15–18% growth; the stock fell ~44% over the 12 months to mid-2026 in the SaaS selloff before recovering — AI-disruption fears overhang the whole vertical SaaS complex.
- The $6bn-by-2030 target implies a CAGR the current ~16–17% trajectory does not quite reach without acceleration.

### 7. Shortage score: 6/10
**Verdict:** The forced Vault CRM migration is the closest thing to a genuine demand surge in this batch, and Veeva is winning most of the contested top-20 decisions — but the Dec 3 earnings print is 7 days away, Salesforce is a real challenger, and this is a migration wave, not a shortage; only for a panel willing to hold through the print.

## 4. CBOE — Cboe Global Markets Inc. ($351.80; rev +13.4% TTM; fwd PE 22.7; next earnings 2027-02-05)

### 1. Shortage thesis
The strongest structural-shortage analogue in the batch — but the scarce good is legal exclusivity, not physical capacity. Cboe is the *only* venue that can list SPX options, and demand for that product is at records: SPX ADV +26% YoY to 4.9m contracts in Q3 2026; SPX 0DTE ADV hit a record 3.2m contracts (+34%), a record 65% of all SPX activity; index options ADV +24% to 6.1m contracts; total options ADV +14% to 21.3m contracts. Retail is driving it: SPX 0DTE retail volume +46% YoY (vs institutional +20%), retail now ~58% of SPX 0DTE volume (from 53%). XSP volume more than doubled YoY after the June 2026 repeal of the pattern day trader rule. SPX overnight-session volume +117%; global trading hours ~250k contracts/day. Nobody else can supply SPX options — demand growth accrues entirely to Cboe's tollbooth, at premium pricing (~$0.95 index revenue per contract vs ~$0.05 for multi-listed options, per Parallax analysis of Q3 2026).

### 2. Backlog / order book
Not applicable — an exchange has no order backlog; volume *is* the book. Forward indicators: Q3 2026 net revenue $712.2m (+18% YoY); options net revenue $469.5m (+23%). Management raised 2026 organic net revenue growth guidance to the high teens (from mid-to-high teens) on Oct 30, 2026. The 25-year license extension (below) effectively books the SPX franchise through 2051.

### 3. Category position
Monopoly in its flagship product, not just #1. Cboe holds the *exclusive* license to list S&P 500 Index (SPX) options — **verified**: Cboe and S&P Dow Jones Indices signed a 25-year extension on Sep 29, 2026 (announced Sep 29–30, 2026; 8-K dated Sep 28), extending exclusivity through 2051, covering SPX options plus VIX-related cross-licenses, with possible future collaboration on tokenized options. There is no #2 for SPX options — competitors (Nasdaq PHLX, NYSE Arca, MIAX) can only offer multi-listed equity options and competing index products (e.g., Nasdaq-100 options), not SPX. In overall US options volume Cboe is one of the largest operators, but the SPX/VIX complex is a legal monopoly. Royalty terms: no impact on 2026; higher per-contract license fees begin Jan 1, 2027 — Cboe expects almost no effect on 2027 net revenue; S&P Global expects a couple of points of index revenue growth from it.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk
Moat: a 25-year exclusive contract (through 2051) on the world's most-traded index option, plus the VIX complex, plus network liquidity that concentrates in the single SPX venue. Customers (market makers, institutions, retail brokerages) cannot build or in-source this: only Cboe may list SPX; a substitute index option (e.g., Nasdaq-100, Russell 2000) is an imperfect hedge, not a replacement for S&P 500 exposure, and SPX's liquidity begets liquidity. Threats: (a) regulatory — a future antitrust or SEC challenge to exclusivity is conceivable but there is no live challenge; (b) demand-side — 0DTE is a retail-driven, volatility-dependent flow that could reverse in a calm market or if regulators curb 0DTE; (c) S&P DJI could in principle not renew in 2051, a 25-year-out risk; (d) tokenized/DeFi alternatives are speculative. This is the highest irreplaceability in the batch.
**Irreplaceability score: 10/10.**

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- **Sep 29, 2026: 25-year S&P DJI exclusive license extension through 2051** — shares rose ~6% on the news; removes the single biggest long-term overhang.
- Q3 2026 (Oct 30, 2026): adj EPS $3.63 (+36% YoY, beat $3.43 consensus by 5.8%); GAAP EPS a record $4.08 (helped by gain on sale of Cboe Australia); net revenue $712.2m (+18%, beat ~$700m); adjusted operating margin expanded 610bp to 71.4%; 2026 organic net revenue growth guidance raised to high teens.
- Record SPX 0DTE (3.2m ADV, 65% of SPX activity); XSP more than doubled; SPX overnight +117%.
- SEC approval for Cboe Predicts (company-KPI event contracts) ahead of a November 2026 launch; KPI binary options launched on Robinhood.
- Analyst action post-earnings (Nov 2, 2026): Barclays raised target $343 → $392 (Overweight); TD Cowen $342 → $417 (Buy); Wells Fargo $309 → $357 (Equal Weight). Goldman Sachs downgraded Strong Buy → Hold on Nov 4, 2026.
- Short interest fell to ~2.55m shares (Nov 2026) from ~4.67m in June 2026.

### 6. Risks
- **Analyst target check — the screen's $360.27 figure does not match the current consensus:** MarketBeat (refreshed Nov 25, 2026) shows a mean target of **$363.00** across 14 analysts (range $320–$450, consensus Hold, 5 Buy / 8 Hold / 1 Sell), implying only ~3.2% upside from $351.80 — and the stock trades **below**, not above, that mean target. The brief's premise ("trades above its analyst mean target ($360.27)") is not supported by the current MarketBeat mean; either the $360.27 figure comes from a different provider/vintage or is stale. Either way, analyst upside is thin.
- 0DTE concentration: 65% of SPX volume is now same-day expiry, heavily retail (58%) — a flow that can evaporate in low-volatility regimes or under regulatory scrutiny; it is also arguably a fad tailwind, not durable growth.
- Higher S&P DJI royalties from Jan 1, 2027 compress index-option margins at the margin.
- Exchange volumes are cyclical with volatility; a calm market cuts the growth engine.
- Fwd PE 22.7 is the cheapest in the batch but the stock is near its 52-week high ($379.76) after the license-extension rally — much of the monopoly-renewal news is priced in.

### 7. Shortage score: 8/10
**Verdict:** The only true monopoly in the batch — exclusive SPX options through 2051 with record 0DTE volumes — but the license news is already priced in, analyst upside is ~3%, and the growth engine is a retail 0DTE flow that can reverse; the strongest moat here, the weakest explosive-return setup on price.
