## LRCX — Lam Research (wafer fab equipment: etch & deposition)

### 1. Shortage thesis
Yes — structural. Lam's CFO Doug Bettinger told investors at the Goldman Sachs Communacopia & Technology conference on **10 Sept 2026** that the chip-equipment industry is **"fundamentally sold out"**, and at Citi's Global TMT conference the day before that it is "fundamentally under-supplying demand" — with **clean-room availability, not customer appetite, as the binding constraint** (Financial News, 10 Sept 2026). Drivers:
- **HBM4/16-Hi stacks**: Lam is the dominant player in high-aspect-ratio etch; HBM-tool revenue grew **>50% YoY** in the last reported quarter.
- **2nm / Gate-All-Around (GAA)**: GAA is ~15–20% more etch/deposition intensive than FinFET; Lam says GAA adds ~**$1B SAM per 100k wafer starts/month** (AInvest, Aug 2026).
- Management raised 2026 WFE outlook **three times this year** ($135B → $140B → low-$150B range) as AI fab spending outpaced its own forecasting (financial-news.co.uk, Sept 2026).
- Lam committed **$3B to new US lab/engineering capacity** (new Oregon site) and is "deeply engaged" with SpaceX's Terafab project.

### 2. Backlog / order book
Lam does not publish a single headline backlog figure in recent releases; demand signal comes via management commentary and outlook: on the July 29, 2026 (FQ4 FY26) call management said **customer discussions now extend to delivery planning into 2027 and beyond** (Motley Fool transcript summary of FQ3; reiterated through FQ4). Q1 FY27 guidance (reported Jul 29, 2026): revenue **$8.10B ±$400M**, EPS $2.15 ±$0.15 — both **well above consensus** — on top of record FQ4 revenue of $6.72B (+30% YoY). Growth rate: FQ4 revenue +30% YoY, EPS +37% YoY.

### 3. Category position
**#1 in dry/plasma etch** globally; Lam's core markets are etch (dominant, ~50%+ share class leadership historically) and deposition where it is #2 behind AMAT. It is the largest or co-largest player in the two most etch/deposition-intensive steps — the steps whose intensity is *increasing* with GAA and HBM.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk
**Irreplaceability score: 9/10.** Etch/deposition tools are 15+ year process-technology assets; every recipe must be qualified by the chipmaker per node. Customers cannot in-source: only a handful of firms on earth can build leading-edge etch tools (Lam, TEL, AMAT); fabs cannot replicate process know-how, service infrastructure, or the 1M+ annual experiments of Lam's R&D network. Moat = process-tech lead + qualification lock-in + capital intensity. Competition risk (TEL in etch, AMAT in deposition) is real but share has been stable; the cycle is the bigger risk, not disintermediation.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- **Jul 29, 2026 — FQ4 FY26 beat**: $6.72B revenue (+30% YoY, beat by 0.7%), non-GAAP EPS $1.82 (+37%, beat 7.7%); raised FQ1 FY27 guidance well above Street.
- **Sept 10, 2026** — CFO "fundamentally sold out" comments at Goldman Sachs conference; WFE forecast raised to low-$150B.
- **Aug 15, 2026** — TD Cowen's Krish Sankar reiterated Buy with **$700 price target**; Street consensus "Strong Buy" (23 of 33 analysts; street-high $500, mean ~$370 at the time).
- $3B US lab/Oregon capacity commitment (announced Aug 2026).

### 6. Risks
- **Cyclicality**: WFE is the most cyclical capex category; the Motley Fool (Sept 13, 2026) notes orders can turn quickly. LRCX YTD +57%, 1-yr +126% — much of the AI buildout may be priced in.
- Customer concentration in memory (Samsung/SK Hynix/Micron) and China (~34% of revenue) exposes it to memory price cycles and China trade restrictions.
- Valuation: forward P/E ~32–52x; beta 1.85–1.95 (high drawdown risk). Insider selling reported in last six months (MarketBeat, Aug 5, 2026).

### 7. Shortage score & verdict
**Shortage score: 9/10.** **Verdict:** The clearest "fundamentally sold out" supplier in semiconductors right now — etch/deposition intensity rises with every node transition, guidance is being raised quarterly, but the stock has tripled in a year so entry timing matters.

## AMAT — Applied Materials (wafer fab equipment: deposition, etch, metrology; AGS services)

### 1. Shortage thesis
Yes — same structural shortage as LRCX, largest beneficiary by breadth. CEO Gary Dickerson on the Aug 13, 2026 FQ3 call: **"the rapid global build-out of AI infrastructure... provides an exceptionally strong foundation for multi-year revenue and profit growth"** and guided that 2027 will be another strong growth year. Demand drivers:
- **Largest customers are giving rolling 8-quarter forecasts** so AMAT can pre-build manufacturing capacity — evidence of supply-constrained planning horizons (Morningstar, May 2026).
- CFO Brice Hill (Aug 13, 2026): AMAT is **expanding capacity to double quarterly semiconductor system output by 2028** and securing options for increases through 2030 — a capacity chase that only happens when supply lags demand.
- **Pricing power**: Hill noted "higher prices for both new and existing products," supporting margin expansion — a classic shortage signal.
- Q3 2026 revenue grew **+25% YoY to record $9.12B** — the highest sequential growth in company history; WFE for 2026 now expected >$150B.
- AI DRAM and advanced-packaging demand: "continued strong revenue growth in the second half... particularly in DRAM as well as leading-edge foundry-logic and advanced packaging" (Aug 13, 2026 PR).

### 2. Backlog / order book
AMAT does not disclose a headline backlog number. Demand visibility evidence: record cash from operations **$3.04B** (FQ3); management explicitly said demand visibility is increasing with rolling 8-quarter customer forecasts. Revenue guidance acceleration: guided quarters have landed **$160–260M above guidance midpoint every quarter** of FY2026; Q4 FY26 guided to adjusted EPS $4.02 ±$0.20, ~85% YoY — the first-ever **~$10B revenue quarter** is expected this fall (Motley Fool, Sept 13, 2026). Growth rate: revenue +25% YoY (FQ3), accelerating from −2% (Q1) → +11% (Q2) → +25% (Q3).

### 3. Category position
**#1 globally in overall wafer fab equipment** (~20–23% WFE share class leader), #1 in deposition, #2 in etch (behind LRCX), #1 in process control/metrology (with KLA competition). Breadth across all three bottleneck steps (deposition, etch, advanced packaging) makes it the broadest beneficiary of the cycle.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk
**Irreplaceability score: 9/10.** Same qualification-lock-in dynamics as LRCX; its EPIC Center R&D partnerships now number 11 engagements with leading chipmakers, deepening co-development lock-in. No customer can in-source deposition/etch/metrology at scale; only 3–4 firms on earth can compete. Applied Global Services (recurring service revenue on the installed base) adds switching-cost moat. **New wrinkle:** export controls remain the real bypass risk — China restrictions push customers toward domestic Chinese toolmakers, a slow disintermediation in the China segment.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- **Aug 13, 2026 — FQ3 beat**: record $9.12B revenue (+25% YoY), record non-GAAP EPS $3.50 (vs $3.40 consensus), 13th consecutive quarter of gross-margin expansion (50.4%).
- Q4 guidance: EPS $4.02 ±$0.20, first ~$10B quarter expected (Motley Fool, Sept 13, 2026).
- Raised Semiconductor Systems growth outlook to **>30%** for calendar 2026 (up from >20%).
- Capacity doubling plan by 2028 announced on the call.
- Pricing power: "higher prices for both new and existing products" (Morningstar, Aug 13, 2026).

### 6. Risks
- **Cyclicality**: same WFE cycle risk; AMAT 1-yr +149%, YTD +62% — stock is pricing in multi-year growth. Fool (Sept 13, 2026): 36x FY26 earnings; "the market is already counting on fiscal 2027 being another strong year."
- China/export controls: US restrictions on advanced equipment to China are a persistent revenue drag and nurture domestic competitors.
- Lumpiness: equipment revenue can swing on shipment timing/trade restrictions in any single quarter.
- No formal backlog disclosure reduces forward visibility precision.

### 7. Shortage score & verdict
**Shortage score: 8/10.** **Verdict:** The broadest, most diversified play on the WFE shortage — capacity doubling plans and 8-quarter customer forecasts are textbook supply-constraint behavior; slightly less pure-play explosive than LRCX's etch leverage, but the record-margin trajectory de-risks execution.

## ANET — Arista Networks (AI datacenter networking switches, EOS software)

### 1. Shortage thesis
Partially — it's a **demand surge** more than a pure supply shortage, with real supply constraints on the margin. Evidence:
- CEO Jayshree Ullal on the Aug 4, 2026 Q2 call cautioned **industry supply constraints could persist until 2028**; purchase commitments surged to **$9.7B** (from $8.9B in Q1 and ~$3.6B a year earlier) — ANET is pre-buying memory/cooling supply through 2027 because components are constrained.
- Gross margin guidance range (62–64%) explicitly embeds "anticipated supply chain cost increases for memory and silicon" (CFO Chantelle Breithaupt, Aug 4, 2026) — inputs are scarce/priced up.
- IDC 2Q26: total Ethernet switch market **+43.4% YoY to $18.9B**, with AI infrastructure the driver. Arista's own 2026 revenue guidance raised to **$12.6B (+40% growth)**; AI fabrics goal **≥$3.5B**; Etherlink AI-switch customers **>100** (up from 4–5 in 2024).
- Shortage is concentrated at the leading edge (800G/1.6T): customer trials ramping into 2027 production.

### 2. Backlog / order book
No formal backlog figure disclosed. Demand evidence: purchase commitments **$9.7B** (↑ ~170% YoY); Q2 revenue **$3.04B (+37.7% YoY)**, first-ever $3B quarter; Q3 2026 guidance $3.3B. Full-year 2026 guidance raised to 40% growth (~$12.6B vs ~$9B in 2025). Growth rate: +37.7% YoY in Q2, accelerating.

### 3. Category position
**#1 in branded datacenter Ethernet switching — but NVIDIA has overtaken it on IDC's latest tracker.** Data points vary by quarter: fierce-network (July 2026): Arista "the number one market leader in global data center," having taken the lead from Cisco in 2024. IDC 2Q26: NVIDIA 20.4% DC share, Arista 18.7% DC share, Cisco 18.2% (DC). So ANET is a **top-3 DC player**, #1 among traditional branded switch vendors alongside a surging NVIDIA. ~91% of revenue is datacenter.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk
**Irreplaceability score: 6/10.** Moat: single software stack (EOS) across all platforms, co-founded leadership with decades of cloud customer relationships, 1.6T and liquid-cooling design wins. **But the in-sourcing/substitution threat is the most concrete of the four:** hyperscalers can and do deploy white-box switches (still "a significant presence" per IDC), Meta/Amazon have in-sourced networking before; NVIDIA's Spectrum-X bundle (switch + DPU + cables) is purpose-built for AI clusters and is already the #1 DC vendor by revenue. Two disclosed 10%+ customers (Microsoft, Meta per fierce-network, July 2026) concentrate buyer power. ANET's Q2 call Q&A itself addressed whether customers will "rethink the build versus buy decision" — Jayshree Ullal's bull case is that rising AI-networking complexity favors an integrated platform; the risk is that it favors NVIDIA's.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- **Aug 4, 2026 — Q2 beat**: EPS $1.02 (+39.7% YoY, beat $0.89 consensus by 14.6%), revenue $3.04B (+37.7%, beat 7.15%).
- Raised FY2026 guidance: 40% revenue growth (~$12.6B), operating margin 48–49%, AI fabrics ≥$3.5B, campus ≥$1.25B.
- Q3 guidance: revenue ~$3.3B, GM ~63%, op margin 48–49%.
- Etherlink >100 customers; 7060XE7 1.6T platform launched with liquid cooling; 1.6T production ramp in 2027.
- Zacks Rank #1 (Strong Buy) as of Aug 19, 2026; 2026 EPS estimates +11.3% in 60 days.

### 6. Risks
- **Hyperscaler concentration + in-sourcing**: Microsoft and Meta each ≥10% of revenue; white-box/NVIDIA alternatives are the existential overhang.
- **NVIDIA competition**: Spectrum-X is growing far faster (+181% YoY in 2Q26) and owns scale-up (NVLink) networking; Arista won't reach scale-up until ~2027.
- Valuation: P/E ~62x trailing, forward price/sales ~16.9x vs industry 8.75x (Zacks, Aug 19, 2026) — momentum is priced in.
- Supply constraints to 2028 could cap upside even as demand runs.

### 7. Shortage score & verdict
**Shortage score: 5/10.** **Verdict:** A demand-surge compounder with the best margins in networking and a genuine AI-fabric tailwind — but the "shortage" is softer here and the NVIDIA/white-box substitution threat caps the irreplaceability case; momentum, not scarcity, is the bet.

### Earnings-quality flags
None.

## APH — Amphenol (connectors, interconnect, sensors; IT datacom focus via CommScope acquisition)

### 1. Shortage thesis
Yes — demand-surge with tight-supply evidence. CEO R. Adam Norwitt on the Jul 29, 2026 Q2 call: AI infrastructure demand is "continuing to accelerate"; IT datacom sales **+89% YoY** (+63% organically) on "more high-speed copper, fiber optic solutions and power interconnect" demand. Evidence of supply/demand tightness:
- **Record orders $10.732B (+94% YoY, +14% QoQ)** with **book-to-bill 1.23:1** — every end market positive (CFO Craig Lampo, Jul 29, 2026).
- Record sales **$8.76B (+55% YoY, +30% organic)** and adjusted EPS $1.35 (beat $1.19 consensus).
- Raised CommScope CCS 2026 outlook to **$4.6B sales / $0.30 EPS accretion** (from $4.1B/$0.15) — acquired fiber capacity is being absorbed faster than expected.
- Connector industry data (Bishop & Associates, 2026): 2025 global connector sales **+14.7%** with telecom/datacom leading growth — the industry is in a broad up-cycle.

### 2. Backlog / order book
Orders $10.732B vs sales $8.76B = **1.23x book-to-bill** (Jul 29, 2026), the second consecutive very strong quarter (Q1: $9.4B orders). "Record" order language two quarters running; backlog growth is compounding — the booked pipeline exceeds shipped revenue by ~23%, providing forward coverage. Growth rate: orders +94% YoY, sales +55% YoY.

### 3. Category position
**#2 globally in connectors** behind TE Connectivity; per Bishop/TTI data, Amphenol is the #2 connector company and is **"challenging TE for the number one ranking"** (TTI, Oct 2024; connectorsupplier.com). In the high-speed IT datacom segment that matters for AI, Amphenol is the share leader via the CommScope CCS fiber acquisition plus its copper franchise.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk
**Irreplaceability score: 5/10.** Moat: scale, 10.4% 20-yr sales CAGR via acquisition integration, design-in relationships with hyperscalers, and breadth across copper/fiber/power architectures — management's core pitch is that Amphenol participates "across multiple evolving architectures" regardless of who wins (Norwitt, Jul 29, 2026). **But**: connectors are a less proprietary, more price-competitive category than etch tools or networking silicon — TE Connectivity matches APH across nearly every line, Luxshare/Bel Fuse compete on price, and architecture shifts (e.g., co-packaged optics, Broadcom shaping next-gen AI cluster standards per Zacks, Apr 2026) can route around copper interconnect. Hyperscalers qualify multiple vendors and can shift share; they don't "need" Amphenol the way a fab needs Lam. Qualification is real but multi-sourcing dilutes it.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- **Jul 29, 2026 — Q2 beat**: record $8.8B sales (+55% YoY), adj. EPS $1.35 (beat $1.19), record $10.7B orders, book-to-bill 1.23x; adj. operating margin +420bps YoY to 29.8%.
- Raised CommScope 2026 outlook ($4.6B sales, $0.30 accretion, doubled from prior).
- FY2026 EPS consensus raised ~11.7% in 30 days to $5.25; Zacks Rank #1 (Strong Buy) (Aug 14, 2026).

### 6. Risks
- **Competition/multi-sourcing**: TE Connectivity, Luxshare, Bel Fuse — limited pricing power vs etch-equipment peers; gross leverage is on volume, not technology monopoly.
- **Architecture risk**: shift toward co-packaged optics / linear-drive pluggables / Broadcom-led standards could reduce copper interconnect content per rack.
- Valuation: forward P/E ~28x vs sector ~21.5x (Zacks, Aug 2026); Zacks Value Score D.
- Cyclical end markets (auto, industrial) still large; AI datacom masks but doesn't remove cycle exposure.
- $8.8B quarter is partly acquisition-driven (CommScope) — organic growth is 30%, still strong but less than headline.

### 7. Shortage score & verdict
**Shortage score: 6/10.** **Verdict:** Record orders and a 1.23x book-to-bill confirm a genuine AI-interconnect demand wave with tight supply, but connectors are the most substitutable link in the AI chain — a strong cyclical compounder, not an irreplaceable choke point.

### Earnings-quality flags
None.
