# Ballot A1 — Panelist A (Supply-chain analyst lens) — run 2026-09-17

Lens weighting: shortage/backlog evidence and contracted multi-year revenue visibility above all else. The best pick is the most undeniable supply-demand imbalance with contracted, multi-year revenue visibility.

---

## BALLOT 1 — Single-Pick Nomination

- **Top pick:** SNDK
- **Runner-up:** LRCX
- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** Sandisk is the only name in the dossier with truly *contracted* multi-year revenue visibility through a shortage: its NBM contracts cover >half of FY27 bits and ~2/3 of FY28 bits at floor pricing, with ≥$93.9B expected revenue and $16.5B in cash-backed guarantees. NAND prices have tripled since January 2026 and the NBM business runs at >80% gross margins — Morgan Stanley's "emblematic of a commodity in a shortage." New fabs take ~3 years, so no line of sight to balance through at least CY2027. As the only pure-play NAND supplier, it converts the imbalance into price power with no DRAM overhang diluting the lever.
- **Key shortage/backlog evidence:** RPO of $59.8B ($91.1B including two NBMs signed after quarter-end), backing >half of FY27 bits and ~2/3 of FY28 bits, with $16.5B in cash deposits guaranteeing customer obligations — bits are pre-sold years out, not hoped for.
- **Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk:** LOW. No hyperscaler or customer fabricates NAND — there is no in-house path; only ~6 firms on earth make merchant NAND and only ~3–4 can ship 300+ layer flash. The honest caveat: Sandisk is the #5/#6 NAND supplier (YMTC just overtook it at 14% vs 11%), so the exposure is commodity *fungibility*, not disintermediation — Sandisk-the-company is replaceable, but NAND-the-product cannot be bypassed and every supplier's bits sell.
- **12-18 month return scenario:** Base ~+40–60% — NAND pricing holds through CY2027, NBM floors deliver, HBF optionality builds (note analyst upside sits at +32%, so this assumes real shortage pricing). Bull ~+100%+ — deficit extends into 2028 (UBS sees DRAM in deficit through at least 2Q28), ASPs keep rising through 2027, Sandisk stays "highly selective" and extracts higher floors.
- **Top risk:** The memory cycle turns — the Big 4 over-invest and flood NAND supply in 2027–28, crashing prices; plus YMTC share gains. The stock is already +1,663% over 12 months and 12x trailing sales, so margin expansion is largely priced in.
- **Conviction (1-10):** 8

**Top 3 deep notes (supply-chain lens):**

1. **SNDK.** The single most compelling data point is the NBM stack: ≥$93.9B expected revenue at floor pricing, $91.1B RPO including post-quarter signings, and $16.5B in cash deposits — contracted multi-year demand visibility no other name approaches (Micron's book is mostly 2026 HBM; equipment makers disclose no formal backlog). Rough 12–18mo: base +40–60% on sustained NAND pricing through CY2027; bull +100%+ if the deficit bleeds into 2028 and HBF ramps as an HBM-adjacent alternative.

2. **LRCX.** CFO said the industry is "fundamentally sold out" (Sept 10, 2026), with clean-room availability — not customer appetite — as the binding constraint, and WFE raised three times this year to the low-$150B range. Irreplaceability 9/10: 15+ year qualification lock-in, #1 in dry/plasma etch, and etch intensity rises ~15–20% with every GAA node. Rough 12–18mo: base +25–40% as FQ1 FY27 guide ($8.1B revenue) lands and HBM4/GAA tool revenue grows >50% YoY; bull +70%+ if WFE overshoots again and delivery planning extends into 2028.

3. **MU.** Entire 2026 HBM supply sold out under multi-year contracts, $22B in customer prepayments collected, and the CEO can fulfill only "half to two-thirds" of demand — DRAM prices up ~3.5x in nine months. It trails SNDK only because contracted visibility concentrates in 2026 HBM and SCAs cover just ~20% of DRAM / ~1/3 of NAND volume, with Micron #3 in HBM allocation. Rough 12–18mo: base +30–50% (Q4 FY26 ~$50B revenue guide vs ~$44B consensus); bull +80%+ if spot > contract persists into 2027 and HBM4 revenue compounds at 2x HBM3E pace.

**Deliberately left out:** VRTX (no shortage — monopoly launch story, not a supply-demand imbalance; scores 3/10), EW (no supply scarcity; reimbursement-driven demand expansion, 5/10), NEM (gold is fungible with no backlog or lock-in; a price wave, not a structural shortage — 3/10 irreplaceability).
