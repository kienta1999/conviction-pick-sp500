## CAT — Caterpillar Inc.

### 1. Shortage thesis
Genuine structural demand surge for large-engine power generation driven by AI datacenter buildout. On the Jan 29, 2026 earnings call, CAT said customers are deploying generator sets and turbines as *primary* power sources (not just backup) to bypass grid constraints and accelerate timelines; Power & Energy sales to users rose 37% in Q4 and power generation sales increased 44% on AI/cloud orders (PYMNTS, Jan 29, 2026). CAT signed its sixth 1-gigawatt prime power deal and raised its 2030 engine capacity target to nearly 3x 2024 levels (Zacks, May 22, 2026). The Monarch Compute Campus deal (announced Jan 2026) orders 2 GW of fast-response gas gensets with deliveries Sep 2026–Aug 2027, scaling toward 8 GW (Energy Digital, Jan 2026). CEO Joe Creed (Q2 call, Aug 4, 2026): "no one is slowing down at the moment. In fact, if we can get more units out, they're asking us to give them more units." Mining side: 827 autonomous haul trucks in operation at end-2025 vs 690 a year earlier; mining capex is recovering (Yellow Table analysis, Sep 16, 2026). This is a real supply-can't-keep-up dynamic on large gensets, with CAT expanding capacity because current capacity is insufficient.

### 2. Backlog / order book
**$72 billion backlog at end of Q2 2026, up 92% year over year** (Q2 2026 earnings presentation, Aug 4, 2026). This was CAT's first-ever quarter above $20B in sales and revenues ($20.5B, +24% YoY). Duration detail not disclosed, but CEO cited "the breadth and duration of our record backlog" and guided mid-to-high-teens full-year 2026 revenue growth with "strong growth" in Q3.

### 3. Category position
Clear #1: Caterpillar leads the 2026 Yellow Table with ~$37.5B in equipment sales (~15.7% share) vs Komatsu #2 at ~$27.1B (~11.0%) — CAT is ~1.4x its nearest rival (KHL Yellow Table, Apr-May 2026 issue; LinkedIn/2026 data analysis, Sep 16, 2026). #1 spot held every year through 2023–2025.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk
CAT does not have a monopoly — Cummins, Generac, Rolls-Royce, Wärtsilä compete in power generation, and Komatsu/XCMG/Sany compete in equipment. But CAT's moat is substantial: the world's largest independent dealer network (parts/service availability within hours, the key buying criterion for heavy equipment), fleet standardization switching costs (mixed fleets raise training/parts costs), and 827 autonomous trucks demonstrating a proprietary autonomy tech lead in mining. In large gensets (up to 10 MW reciprocating, 1–39 MW turbines), the installed base + service network creates stickiness, though substitution is feasible for a determined buyer. **Irreplaceability score: 7/10.**

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- Aug 4, 2026: record Q2 — sales $20.5B (+24% YoY), first $20B+ quarter ever; power-generation product sales to users +72%; construction industries +35%; resources +20%; full-year guidance raised to mid-to-high-teens growth.
- Q2 Power & Energy operating margin hit a record 29.8% (Zacks, Sep 2, 2026); H1 2026 segment revenue $15.3B (+19% YoY).
- Blue Point low-carbon ammonia JV ($4B, with JERA/Mitsui) — permitted construction scheduled to begin August 2026 (for CF cross-read; CAT relevance: none). For CAT: engine capacity expansion to ~3x 2024 levels by 2030 announced.
- Shares up >50% over the past year on the AI power theme (Marketplace, Jan 29, 2026).

### 6. Risks
- Cyclicality: construction/mining equipment is cyclical; the non-power businesses could roll over if infrastructure spend slows.
- Power demand could cool if AI capex pauses; order-book concentration in datacenters is a new, untested cycle.
- Competition in gensets (Cummins etc.) and pricing pressure if capacity expansions industry-wide overshoot.
- Valuation: the stock has already repriced on the AI narrative (+50% in a year); momentum could reverse on any backlog deceleration.
- Execution: delivering 3x engine capacity by 2030 is capital-intensive.

### 7. Shortage score and verdict
**Shortage score: 8/10.** Large-engine datacenter power is the closest thing to a genuine structural shortage in industrials — buyers are asking for more units than CAT can ship, lead times are being absorbed by behind-the-meter campuses, and CAT is building capacity to catch up.
**Verdict: Real structural shortage, dominant #1 — the best shortage-momentum candidate of the three.**

## NEM — Newmont Corporation

### 1. Shortage thesis
Gold mine supply is structurally constrained: the World Gold Council warns global mine supply is "effectively hitting a wall" (Jan 7, 2026 note, cited by Dominion Review), with major discoveries steadily declining and ~10+ years from discovery to first pour. Q2 2026 mine supply was just 966t (+2% YoY) while central banks bought a net 289t in Q2 alone, up 62% YoY, and 45% of central banks surveyed intend to increase reserves over the next 12 months (WGC Q2 2026 Gold Demand Trends). J.P. Morgan (Dec 2025 research) forecasts gold >$5,000/oz through at least Q4 2027, underpinned by ~755t of 2026 central-bank buying — far above pre-2022 averages of 400–500t. BUT: honesty requires saying gold is a fungible commodity with no industrial shortage mechanism — it is a monetary-asset price wave, not a supply-chain bottleneck. Newmont's own Q2 realized price was $4,414/oz (+33% YoY; guidance assumption was $4,500/oz). Nobody can be "short" of gold; they can only be short of *cheap* gold.

### 2. Backlog / order book
**Not applicable** — miners sell into the spot/contract market; there is no order book. The closest analog is the reserve/resource base (Newmont holds one of the industry's largest reserve bases; exact current figure not verified this round). Guidance-based proxy: full-year 2026 production guidance of **5.3 million attributable gold ounces**, on track after 1.3M oz produced in Q2 (Q2 2026 earnings release, Jul 23, 2026).

### 3. Category position
Newmont is the **world's largest gold producer** by output (5.3M oz 2026 guidance, post-Newcrest acquisition), ahead of Barrick and Agnico Eagle. It is also the only gold major in the S&P 500, giving it the index-fund and generalist-investor bid that smaller miners lack.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk
None in product terms: gold is perfectly fungible, and Newmont's bars are substitutable with any miner's output or recycled gold. Its real (weak) advantages are scale, low-quartile costs (Q2 by-product AISC $1,621/oz vs $4,414 realized price = enormous margin), and Tier-1 jurisdictions. No customer is locked in; no network effects. **Irreplaceability score: 3/10** (points only for scale, reserve base, and balance-sheet optionality on acquisitions).

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- Jul 23, 2026: Q2 results — $1.3M attributable oz, record Q2 free cash flow of $2.2B, adjusted EPS $2.10 (beat $2.05 consensus), $1.9B returned to shareholders via buybacks/dividends since prior call; full-year guidance reaffirmed.
- Q1 2026 (Apr): all-time record quarterly FCF of $3.1B; board authorized an additional $6.0B buyback; $4.6B+ in after-tax proceeds from the non-core divestiture program; ended Q1 with $8.8B cash, net cash position $3.2B.
- Gold price environment: realized $4,414/oz in Q2 (+33% YoY); analyst targets clustered above $5,000/oz into 2027.
- Dividend $0.261/share declared for Q2 (Jul 23, 2026).

### 6. Risks
- This is a pure commodity-price bet: a gold reversal (dollar strength, rate surprises, central banks slowing purchases — Q1 2026 central-bank buying was revised sharply down by Metals Focus to 57t) would crush the thesis.
- Production is flat-to-declining (Q2 output down 12.5% YoY on planned mine sequencing); Newmont is harvesting, not growing, ounces.
- Costs rising: co-product AISC up ~21.7% YoY to $1,938/oz.
- Gold equities can lag the metal if investors prefer direct exposure (ETFs) over operating risk.

### 7. Shortage score and verdict
**Shortage score: 5/10.** There is a genuine structural squeeze in *mine supply growth* (plateauing output, no pipeline) colliding with structurally elevated official-sector demand — but gold never "runs out," so this is a price-wave story, not a shortage story.
**Verdict: A leveraged, well-run gold-price proxy with record cash returns — but a commodity momentum trade, not a structural shortage. Honest scoring puts it behind CAT.**

## CF — CF Industries Holdings

### 1. Shortage thesis
The strongest genuine *commodity* shortage case of the three. CF itself says "a tight global nitrogen supply-demand balance" lifted UAN average selling prices (Q1 2026 earnings release, May 6, 2026) and "further tightened by supply disruptions related to the Russia-Ukraine war." The company now expects global nitrogen supplies **to remain constrained through the end of 2026 and into 2027**, citing reduced Middle Eastern exports, continued risks to Russian production, and difficult economics for European producers. Quantified: the Iran conflict has removed ~4–4.5 million metric tons of traded urea and ~1 million metric tons of ammonia from the market; China maintains strict urea export quotas for food security; Europe's gas-cost disadvantage persists (CF's US gas cost advantage vs Yara reached ~$200–400/ton of ammonia, per Wedbush, 2026). Nitrogen demand is structurally inelastic (food production; global grain stocks near decade lows). CF ran at >99% of available ammonia capacity in Q1 2026 to capture pricing. Urea barge prices at New Orleans jumped >46% after the Iran conflict began in late February 2026 (StoneX, via MINING.COM, May 2026). This is a real, quantified supply deficit, not just a price rally.

### 2. Backlog / order book
**Not applicable in the CAT sense** — fertilizer is sold on short-cycle spot/forward contracts, not multi-year backlogs. Evidence of tightness instead: CF prioritized US customers over higher-priced export markets during the disruption, and sold out granular urea at record pricing (Q2 2026: urea revenue $759M vs $547M a year earlier, avg selling price $593/ton vs $460/ton — Fertilizer Daily, Aug 6, 2026).

### 3. Category position
CF is the world's largest pure-play nitrogen fertilizer producer and North America's dominant low-cost producer. In the US, four firms (CF, Nutrien, Koch, Yara-USA) control ~75–84% of nitrogen capacity (USDA summary via PYMNTS, Sep 2026; FarmAction, Apr 2026). CF's structural edge is its Gulf Coast asset base on cheap Henry Hub gas, giving a $200–400/ton cost advantage over European rival Yara, plus a $4B Blue Point low-carbon ammonia JV (FID 2025, with JERA/Mitsui; construction scheduled to begin August 2026) aimed at the Japanese power co-firing market.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk
Nitrogen fertilizer is a commodity — farmers buy the cheapest delivered nutrient ton. CF's "moat" is cost position, not product: North American logistics infrastructure, rail/port access, and the Henry Hub spread. These are durable but not irreplicable; a new plant could be built (just slowly, at high capex). Concentration also invites regulatory risk: the FTC has opened an industry-wide antitrust investigation into fertilizer pricing (reported Sep 2026), and a class-action complaint was filed in Colorado federal court in March 2026. **Irreplaceability score: 4/10** (cost-advantaged scale and logistics, but fungible commodity with zero customer lock-in).

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- Aug 5, 2026: Q2 2026 — net earnings $727M, **+88% YoY**; revenue $2.22B (+18%); adjusted EBITDA $1.19B (vs $761M); first-half net earnings $1.34B (vs $698M), including a ~$170M litigation settlement gain.
- Q1 2026 (May 6): net earnings $615M nearly doubled from $312M; revenue +20% to $1.99B; operated at >99% ammonia capacity.
- Feb 19, 2026: Q4 2025 beat — adjusted EPS $2.99 vs $2.50 consensus; sales +22.8% to $1,872M.
- Blue Point JV construction start (permitted, scheduled August 2026) — a new growth leg in low-carbon ammonia for Japan.
- Yazoo City outage extended to H1 2027 — a partial offset reducing 2026 gross ammonia output to ~9.5Mt from 10.1Mt in 2025 (operational headwind, not a catalyst).

### 6. Risks
- **Geopolitical resolution**: a ceasefire/easing of the Iran conflict or restored Middle East exports would collapse the scarcity premium fast — this is the single biggest binary risk.
- Commodity cyclicality: nitrogen prices are mean-reverting over cycles; new capacity (including CF's own Blue Point) eventually clears tightness.
- Yazoo City outage drags 2026–H1 2027 volumes.
- Regulatory overhang: FTC antitrust investigation + Colorado class-action suit (filed March 2026).
- Natural gas price spikes in the US would compress CF's cost spread.
- Demand elasticity at the margin: sustained high prices can curb application rates.

### 7. Shortage score and verdict
**Shortage score: 7/10.** This is the most honestly quantifiable supply deficit of the three (4–4.5Mt urea + 1Mt ammonia removed, China export curbs, company-guided tightness into 2027) — but it is event-driven (war, export bans) rather than permanent, so it scores below CAT's multi-year capacity-building cycle.
**Verdict: Real, quantified near-term nitrogen shortage with a low-cost pure-play lever — a strong momentum trade, but the thesis dies the day geopolitics normalize.**
