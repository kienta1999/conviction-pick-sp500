# Ballot A1 — Agent A, supply-chain analyst
**Date:** 2026-08-04 · **Universe:** 14 momentum-screen survivors · **Source:** `output/momentum/research_dossier.md` only

**My lens, stated up front:** I rank on the *quality, hardness and duration of the order book*. A dated, quantified, disclosed number beats management characterization; management characterization beats a channel check; a channel check beats a narrative. Withdrawn or absent order-book disclosure is a material downgrade, not a neutral — visibility you cannot audit is visibility you do not have. Where this disagrees with the growth, quality and skeptic panelists, so be it.

## BALLOT 1 — SINGLE PICK

- **Top pick:** **AVGO**
- **Runner-up:** **GE**

**Thesis (3–5 sentences):**
Broadcom is the only name in this field that pairs a hard, dated, disclosed forward order number with a revenue base small enough that converting it doubles the company. On 2026-06-03 it disclosed **$30B of AI semiconductor bookings against $10.8B shipped in the same quarter — a 2.8x book-to-bill on the AI line alone**, and management effectively confirmed an implied **$200B+ 18-month backlog covering 2H FY26 through FY2027** against FY26 AI revenue guided to ~$56B. Critically for a supply-chain analyst, that book has both a *supply* reason and a *funding* reason attached: Hock Tan states customers are queuing because chip availability, power and memory are the constraints, and the **$35B Apollo/Blackstone AI XPV Platform** exists specifically to finance 20GW of the deployments that consume Broadcom silicon — the order book comes with its buyers pre-funded, which is the single rarest feature in this entire dossier. And Broadcom is on the *right* side of the doctrine's trap: it does not get disintermediated by hyperscaler in-sourcing, it *is* the in-sourcing, with multi-year contracted programs at Anthropic (1GW 2026 → 3GW 2027), OpenAI (first-gen XPU 2027 at 1GW+), Google, Meta, ByteDance and Apple.

**Key shortage/backlog evidence (single most compelling data point):**
**$30B of AI semiconductor bookings against $10.8B shipped in Q2 FY26 — a disclosed, dated 2.8x book-to-bill**, corroborated by a **+32% sequential revenue guide to ~$29.4B (+84% y/y)**, the steepest guide in the field. Both the numerator and denominator are company-disclosed for the same period, so the ratio is computable rather than asserted — that is what separates it from Nvidia's "$1 trillion through 2027."

**Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk: MEDIUM.**
Broadcom holds **70%+ of the custom AI accelerator design-services market** against one credible #2 (Marvell), and the moat is SerDes/networking IP plus 2–3 year program-level co-development, not software lock-in — there is no CUDA equivalent here. The classic trap is inverted: the customers cannot in-source *away* from Broadcom without building a full silicon organization, and Broadcom's growth *is* their in-sourcing of Nvidia. But medium, not low, because **Google has already progressively internalized TPU design at Broadcom's own largest program**, and in custom silicon a program loss is binary and abrupt.

**12–18 month return scenario (from $418.16):**
- **Base:** the 18-month book converts roughly on schedule and FY27 lands near consensus EPS of **$17.54**; the stock tracks the **consensus target of $528, +26%**.
- **Bull:** FY27 AI revenue reaches the stated **$100B+** target, FY27 estimates get revised up through the year and the multiple holds in the low-30s on upgraded numbers → **$600–650, roughly +45–55%**.
- **Bear:** the $100B FY27 target slips to ~$80B and the ~36x FY26 multiple compresses to the mid-20s → **−30% or worse**. There is no valuation cushion.

**Top risk:**
The order book is concentrated in a handful of binary programs, and part of it is financed by a vehicle Broadcom helped create. If AI infrastructure credit tightens, the $35B XPV platform, the bookings and the revenue all fail together — the same reflexivity as Nvidia's equity stakes, but in debt form. Second-order: the dossier could not reconcile a separately cited "$73B AI backlog" with the $200B+ implied figure, which is a real, if minor, disclosure-consistency ding on my scorecard.

**Conviction: 8 / 10.**

### Top 3 — the one data point and the scenario

**1. AVGO — $418.16.**
The single most compelling number is **$30B of AI bookings against $10.8B shipped in Q2 FY26**, because unlike every other forward number in this dossier both halves of the ratio are company-disclosed for the identical period — you can check the arithmetic rather than trust the adjective. Behind it sits an implied **$200B+ 18-month backlog** against ~$56B of FY26 AI revenue, with named multi-year programs and a purpose-built $35B financing vehicle attached to the demand. **Base: ~$528 (+26%) as the book converts and FY27 tracks $17.54 EPS. Bull: $600–650 (+45–55%) if the $100B FY27 AI target lands and estimates re-rate with it.**

**2. GE — $377.28.**
The best *auditable* order book in the entire field: **over $210B of backlog (~$170B of it commercial services), roughly 4.4x annual revenue**, with the rare corroboration that **95% of Q3 2026 spare-parts revenue was already in backlog** and **planned engine removals exceed the full-year shop-visit capacity by more than 40%** — that is allocation in all but name, disclosed by a company whose management says outright that supply, not demand, is the constraint. This is doctrine-perfect (shortage 9, irreplaceability 9, sole-source LEAP-1B on every 737 MAX), and it is only the runner-up because the price already reflects it. **Base: consensus $405, +7%. Bull: the street-high $455 (+21%) if 2027E EPS of roughly $9.27 is met and the ~48x multiple holds.** Margins down 130bps y/y with management pushing expansion to "2028 and beyond" is what caps it.

**3. GOOGL — $377.65.**
**$514B of contracted Google Cloud backlog, up ~$50B sequentially, with more than half stated to convert within 24 months** — that is ~5.2 years of current cloud run-rate, the longest hard-contracted visibility in the field, and it is validated by the CFO saying plainly that Alphabet is **supply-constrained** and renting third-party capacity as a bridge. Cloud growing +82% while cloud operating margin expanded from 20.7% to 35.6% is the pricing signature of a sold-out market — you do not expand margin 15 points into a discounting environment. **Base: consensus $427, +13%. Bull: +30–50% over 18 months if backlog conversion plus TPU merchant sales outrun the capex/depreciation drag.** The honest cap is $4.57T of market value: superb visibility, arithmetic ceiling on the percentage return.

### Deliberately left out of the top 10

**VRT, ANET, LLY, CF** — **VRT** is the sharpest exclusion and the most lens-driven: management **switched off backlog and orders disclosure on 2026-02-11**, so the last hard number is $15.0B at 2025-12-31 and the current book is unobservable at exactly the moment Q2 proved supply congestion can turn shortage into a revenue miss; a thesis I cannot audit is not a thesis I can rank. **ANET** has no classic backlog, its $8.9B of purchase commitments are its own obligations rather than customer orders, the shortage is in its *inputs* (220bps of gross-margin compression) rather than its output, and after the ~11% after-hours move it sits at or above freshly-raised $200–210 targets. **LLY** fails the screen's own premise — the FDA formally ended the tirzepatide and semaglutide shortages and Lilly guides to a low-to-mid-teens percent *price* drag; there is no scarcity to underwrite. **CF** has no order book at all by the dossier's own admission, sells a fungible commodity into new Gulf Coast supply it is itself building, and reports an unhedged binary on 2026-08-05.

**Timing caveats carried into these ballots:** LLY (08-05), CF (08-05) and HWM (08-06) are all pre-print, and I have ranked HWM 10th and excluded the other two partly *because* an unreported quarter is another layer of unverifiable visibility on top of already-absent disclosure. ANET reported after the 08-04 close and rose ~11% after hours; my exclusion is made with that beat and the refreshed $200–210 targets fully in view.
