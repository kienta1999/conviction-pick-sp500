### 1. Shortage thesis — STRONG, and explicitly stated by management
This is the cleanest shortage story of the three. GE management's own framing on the Q2 call: **"supply — not demand — is the primary factor limiting how quickly the company can grow"**; the company describes itself as **"supply-constrained rather than demand-constrained."**

Concrete evidence:
- **MRO capacity is "oversubscribed."** Planned engine removals exceed the full-year shop-visit forecast by **more than 40%** — i.e. airlines want 40% more shop visits than GE can physically do this year. That is allocation in all but name, and it hands visibility into 2027.
- **Spare-parts delinquencies rose 20% sequentially** in Q2 on material constraints — GE is late on parts it has already sold.
- **95% of Q3 2026 spare-parts revenue was already in backlog** at the time of the Q2 report.
- Aftermarket demand "remaining stronger than anticipated"; record internal shop-visit output in the quarter and still short.
- Industry backdrop corroborates: castings/forgings/bearings that used to quote 4–6 weeks now quote **20–40+ weeks**, strategic items 24–40 weeks, some specialty parts **>50 weeks**; **titanium lead times ~9 months** (2026 aerospace supply-chain surveys).

Driver is structural, not cyclical: an aging in-service fleet (deferred retirements because OEMs can't deliver new aircraft fast enough) forces more shop visits per airframe, while GE's own MRO and parts capacity was cut in 2020–21 and has not been rebuilt.

### 2. Backlog / order book
- **Total backlog: over $210 billion** (as of Q2 2026, reported 2026-07-16).
- **Commercial services backlog: ~$170B**, up nearly **$30B since end-2024**.
- **Defense backlog: over $30B**, up ~$5B since the start of 2026.
- Orders +17% to $16.5B in Q2; CES orders $12.9B (+18%), DPT orders $4.1B (+12%). Services orders **+34% in H1**.
- Booked-out horizon: $210B backlog vs ~$47–48B of 2026 revenue = **roughly 4.4x annual revenue**. Spare-parts revenue is 95% pre-booked one quarter out; shop-visit demand is 40% oversubscribed, giving hard visibility into 2027.
- LEAP: **deliveries +41% in H1 2026**; total engine deliveries +31%. FY2026 LEAP delivery growth guidance **raised to high-teens % (from 15%)**. LEAP shop turnaround time ~100 days, improved by over two weeks YoY. A ~2,000-unit LEAP delivery target for 2026 has been cited but I could not confirm it in a primary GE source — treat as unconfirmed.
- **Spare parts: +25%+ in Q2; +30%+ in H1 2026.** Commercial services revenue +26% in Q2, **+32% in H1**.

### 3. Category position — clear #1
- **CFM International (GE 50% / Safran 50%) holds >60% of the narrowbody engine market vs ~35% for Pratt & Whitney.**
- **LEAP-1B is the sole-source, exclusive powerplant on every Boeing 737 MAX** — a monopoly on the highest-volume commercial airframe in production.
- LEAP-1A holds ~55% selection rate on the A320neo family against P&W's GTF (the only alternative).
- LEAP-1C is the exclusive powerplant on COMAC's C919.
- P&W GTF installed base >2,800 engines across 60+ operators (early 2026) — meaningful but a fraction of GE's.
- **Installed base: ~50,000 commercial + ~30,000 military engines** (per Q1 2026 filing; was ~45,000/25,000 in the FY2024 10-K). **Services ≈ 70% of revenue.**

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation — score **9/10**
- **Certification lock-in.** Engine type certificates are held by GE/CFM; an airframe-engine pairing is certified as a unit. On the 737 MAX there is literally no alternative engine — Boeing cannot in-source or dual-source without recertifying the airframe, a multi-year, multi-billion-dollar exercise.
- **Parts approval lock-in.** Only GE-approved parts (or narrowly-approved PMA parts) can go into a GE engine under FAA/EASA rules. Qualifying an alternative source for a flight-critical casting or forging requires AS9100D + NADCAP accreditation, material traceability, NDT and heat-treat records; new-aircraft certification timelines have themselves stretched from 1–2 years to **4–5 years**, which slows alternate-part and alternate-supplier qualification correspondingly. Customers historically **single-source** critical components because qualification cost is prohibitive, and **tooling/qualification cost is borne by the customer**, reinforcing incumbency.
- **Aftermarket annuity.** ~70% of revenue is services against an installed base of ~50,000 commercial engines with 25–30 year lives. Engines are typically sold at low or negative margin and monetized over decades of shop visits — the classic razor/blade. Management expects **LEAP dollar-per-shop-visit profitability to reach CFM56 levels by 2030**, which is the explicit bull case: today's LEAP deliveries are tomorrow's high-margin annuity, and GE has already booked ~$170B of it.
- **Capital intensity.** Rebuilding an engine MRO network and a superalloy/casting supply chain takes a decade and billions; nobody is entering.
- Only real leak: independent MRO shops and PMA parts, which nibble at the margin but cannot touch new-engine share. Rolls-Royce is a widebody, not narrowbody, competitor.

**Does the aftermarket annuity justify ~41x forward (49x current-year)?** Partly. The defensible part: ~70% services revenue, $170B contracted services backlog, 40%-oversubscribed shop visits, and a LEAP fleet whose profitability *inflects upward* through 2030 — this is a very high-quality, very long-duration earnings stream that deserves a large premium to an industrial. The uncomfortable part: at 49x FY26 guidance with **margins down 130bps YoY** and orders growth decelerating to +17%, the market is already paying for the 2030 LEAP-profitability inflection today. Analyst mean target $405 is only +7.3% — the sell side, having just raised targets to $400 (JPM), $431 (Citi) and a street-high $455 (Jefferies), still sees limited upside from spot. The multiple is *defensible*, but it is not *cheap*, and the near-term margin trajectory is the wrong direction.

### 6. Risks
- **Margin compression is real and current:** adj. op margin -130bps YoY in Q2 on OE/installation mix and inflation. Management calls it "timing-related" with expansion resuming "in 2028 and beyond" — that is a long time to ask a 49x stock to wait.
- **Supply chain is the binding constraint** — GE cannot convert oversubscribed demand into revenue faster than castings, forgings and titanium allow. Delinquencies already rising.
- **Order growth decelerating** (+17% vs prior quarters); the stock fell 4.6% on the print, showing how little slack the valuation leaves.
- **OEM build-rate slippage:** if Boeing/Airbus stumble again, OE engine volume slows (though this paradoxically *helps* the aftermarket by keeping old jets flying).
- **Valuation:** 49x current-year guidance, +20% above 200d SMA, at 52w high, mean target only +7.3%. Priced for continued flawless execution.
- Tariffs on aerospace inputs; a demand shock to air traffic would hit shop visits with a lag.

### 7. Shortage score: **9/10**
**Verdict:** The purest structural-shortage asset of the three — management itself says supply, not demand, is the constraint, with shop visits 40% oversubscribed against a $210B backlog and a 737 MAX engine monopoly — but at ~49x current-year earnings with margins compressing near-term, you are paying full price for a 2028–2030 inflection.

### The 9%-growth-at-47x interrogation — the screen premise is wrong
The brief asks me to interrogate "only ~9% TTM growth vs 47x forward." **The 9% figure is stale and should not be used.** The actual sequence:

| Period | Revenue | YoY |
|---|---|---|
| Q2 2025 | $2.053B | **+9%** ← this is where the screen's number comes from |
| Q4 2025 | $2.2B | **+15%** |

**Growth is accelerating: 9% → 11% → 15% → 19%.** So the "9% growth, 47x multiple" gap the brief flags does not actually exist as stated.

**But the honest caveat:** part of the 2026 acceleration is inorganic. HWM closed **CAM (Consolidated Aerospace Manufacturing) for ~$1.8B on 2026-04-06** (from Stanley Black & Decker) and **Brunner Manufacturing for ~$120M on 2026-02-06**. CAM contributes **~$485–495M of FY2026 revenue** — roughly **$120–125M per quarter from Q2 onward**, i.e. **~5–6 points of Q2 growth**. So Q2 2026's guided +17% is roughly **+11–12% organic**, versus Q1's **+19% fully organic** (CAM had not closed). Read carefully: **Q1 was a genuinely strong organic quarter; Q2's headline growth is flattered by M&A and the organic rate steps down.** The Q2 print on 2026-08-06 is the key test of whether organic growth holds in the mid-teens.

**Margins/incrementals — this is the real story.** Adjusted EBITDA margin **32.0%, +320bps YoY** in Q1 2026, on top of Q4 2025's 30.1% (+330bps YoY). Adjusted EPS grew **+42% on +19% revenue** — an incremental drop-through far above the corporate average, driven by price on long-term agreements, mix shift into high-margin engine spares, and operating leverage on a capacity-constrained asset base. FY2025 adj EBITDA grew **+26% on +11% revenue**. **Earnings are compounding at roughly 2x the revenue rate.** That, not the revenue line, is what the multiple is capitalizing.

**Is 47x (2027E) / 58x (FY26 guide) defensible?** Marginally, and only on the earnings-leverage argument. If HWM does ~$4.94 in 2026 and the sell side is at ~$6.09 for 2027 (+23%), then a 47x 2027 multiple is ~2x PEG — rich but not absurd for a sole-source, capacity-constrained, 32%-EBITDA-margin monopolist with expanding margins. What kills the defence is the **downside asymmetry**: at 58x current-year, a single quarter of decelerating organic growth or a Boeing/Airbus rate pause takes a large percentage out of the stock. Analyst mean target $313 is **+8.6%** — the sell side sees very little left. HWM is the most expensive name in this batch on current-year earnings and has the least backlog visibility disclosure.

### 1. Shortage thesis — STRONG (structural), but less explicitly quantified than GE
HWM sits at the exact chokepoint of the aerospace supply chain: **castings, forgings and fasteners are the industry's #1 named bottleneck.**
- 2026 aerospace supply-chain surveys: **castings, forgings, aluminum, steel and superalloys "continue to experience supply pressure"**; high-performance steels, nickel alloys, titanium and specialty coatings are "common bottlenecks."
- **Lead times that were 4–6 weeks now quote 20–40+ weeks**; strategic items 24–40 weeks; some specialty parts **>50 weeks**. **Titanium ~9 months.**
- Aerospace-grade conversion steps (forging, precision grinding, NDT, batch documentation) add **4–10 extra weeks** on top.
- Root cause is structural: Tier-1/Tier-2 capacity was **deliberately cut in 2020–21 and never restored**, and skilled labour left and has not returned.
- HWM's own behaviour confirms it is capacity-limited, not demand-limited: it added **235 net headcount in Engine Products in Q1 2026 alone** specifically "to support anticipated revenue growth," and the **majority of capex went to Engine Products** to expand capacity. Companies don't pre-hire into a soft market.
- Pricing power: margin expansion of +320bps YoY on an input-cost-inflating cost base is de facto evidence of price increases sticking.
- I could **not find an explicit "sold out" or "allocation" statement** from HWM management — mark this as inferred from lead times, capex, hiring and margin, not quoted.

### 2. Backlog / order book (revenue visibility proxy)
HWM does not report a single headline backlog number the way GE does. What it does report:
- FY2026 revenue guidance **$9.575–9.725B** with a **midpoint raise of $550M** in a single quarter — a strong visibility signal.
- Management explicitly anchors visibility on customers' books: **"Commercial aerospace OEM customers continue to target production rate increases supported by record backlogs."** HWM's visibility is derivative of Boeing's and Airbus's ~10-year order books.
- Long-term agreements (LTAs) with engine OEMs and airframers are the contractual mechanism — multi-year, program-life, typically sole- or dual-sourced. **I could not find a disclosed aggregate LTA value or a stated years-of-coverage figure** — not found.
- Q1 2026 FCF of $359M (Q1 record) and $300M of buybacks in the quarter indicate management confidence in the forward book.
- CAM adds ~$485–495M FY26 revenue at **>20% adj EBITDA margin pre-synergies**, at a **~13x FY26 adj EBITDA transaction multiple** after synergies and tax benefit.

**Verdict on visibility: good but materially less than GE's.** GE gives you a hard $210B contracted number; HWM gives you a guidance range and a pointer to its customers' backlogs. That is a real quality-of-visibility gap, and it matters when HWM trades at a *higher* current-year multiple than GE.

### 3. Category position — #1, but "leading player" rather than documented monopoly
- HWM is a **leading global player in aircraft engine castings** — airfoils (turbine blades and vanes), structural castings, seamless rolled rings, forgings, titanium extrusions and aerospace fasteners.
- One source states HWM produces **"over 90% of structural and rotating aero engine components"** — I flag this as **poorly sourced and probably a misreading** (likely meaning it supplies parts across >90% of platforms, not 90% market share). **Do not rely on it.** I could not find a clean, citable HWM-vs-#2 market-share split for airfoil castings or aero fasteners — **not found.** The historical #2 in investment castings is Precision Castparts (Berkshire Hathaway-owned), plus Doncasters and regional players; in fasteners, LISI Aerospace and TransDigm units.
- What is solidly established: HWM has **vertical control over aluminium and titanium casting operations**, focuses on **mission-critical components where switching costs are high and program lives are long**, and is content-bearing on the 737 MAX, A320neo and major defence platforms.
- The **CAM acquisition consolidates the fastener market further** — buying a leading precision-fastener/fluid-fitting maker removes a competitor and increases HWM's share of a bottlenecked category.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation — score **8/10**
- **Qualification lock-in is the core moat.** Switching a supplier for a flight-critical casting requires full re-qualification: lengthy qualification plus manufacturing-process development, "resulting in significant cost and operational risks." **Tooling and qualification costs are borne by the customer**, which structurally deters switching. Historically, **customers single-source these components because qualification cost is prohibitive.** With new-aircraft certification timelines having stretched to **4–5 years**, alternate-source qualification has stretched with them. A realistic estimate for qualifying a new source on a rotating flight-critical casting is **multiple years plus customer-funded tooling** — I could not find a single authoritative "X years" number, so treat the 4–5 year certification figure as the best available proxy rather than a direct measure.
- **Can customers in-source?** Practically no. GE, Safran, RTX and Boeing have spent decades *divesting* casting and fastener capacity because it is capital-intensive, metallurgically specialised and low-return at sub-scale. Rebuilding single-crystal airfoil casting capability from scratch would take the better part of a decade — and they need parts *this year*.
- **Substitute technology risk:** additive manufacturing is the theoretical route around investment casting. It is real but slow — AM is qualified for some static and repair parts, not yet for high-volume rotating single-crystal turbine airfoils. Not a 2026–2030 threat; a 2035 question.
- **Weaker than GE on one axis:** HWM sells *components* into someone else's certified system. It does not own a type certificate and has no installed-base aftermarket annuity of its own comparable to GE's ~$170B services backlog. It captures engine spares demand, but as a supplier, not as the OEM of record. It is also one step further down the chain from the end customer, so it is more exposed to OEM inventory and rate decisions.

### 5. Recent catalysts (since ~2026-05)
- **2026-05-07:** Q1 2026 beat (rev $2.31B vs $2.24B; EPS $1.22 vs $1.11), **FY26 guidance raised** ($550M revenue / $0.49 EPS / $300M EBITDA at midpoint). Stock reportedly surged **~12%** on the earnings beat and raised guidance.
- **2026-04-06:** **CAM acquisition closed, ~$1.8B**, expanding Fastening Systems; ~$485–495M FY26 revenue at >20% EBITDA margin, ~13x post-synergy multiple.
- **2026-02-06:** Brunner Manufacturing acquired (~$120M) for larger-size fasteners.
- Q1: **$300M of share repurchases**; 235 net headcount added in Engine Products for capacity.
- **Boeing 737 rate 42 → 47/month** with FAA concurrence during H1 2026; **fourth 737 line began low-rate production July 2026** — direct volume tailwind to HWM content.
- **Upcoming catalyst 2026-08-06: Q2 2026 results** (guide $2.39–2.41B / $1.22–1.24). This is the near-term binary.

### 6. Risks
- **Valuation is the dominant risk.** 58x FY26 guidance / 47x 2027E with a mean target only +8.6% above spot. At 22% above the 200d SMA and at 52w highs after +57% in 12 months, this is priced for perfection.
- **Organic deceleration masked by M&A.** Q2's guided +17% is ~+11–12% organic vs Q1's +19% organic. If the 2026-08-06 print shows organic in the low teens, the "accelerating growth" narrative that justifies the multiple weakens immediately.
- **OEM build-rate slippage is HWM's single biggest exposure.** HWM is one tier removed from the airlines: it ships to Boeing, Airbus, GE, Safran and RTX, so it takes the full force of any rate pause or destocking. Boeing is mid-transition 42→47/month; **Airbus already cut its 2026 A220 target from 14 to 12 per month**, and supply-chain constraints are expected to persist through 2027. Any Boeing quality/regulatory event, strike, or Airbus rate reset flows straight into HWM volume with limited aftermarket cushion.
- **Engineered Structures was -3% in Q1 2026** — one of four segments already shrinking.
- **Integration risk on CAM** ($1.8B, partly debt-funded via a $1.2B notes issue) and dilution of the reported organic growth rate.
- Input-cost inflation (nickel, titanium, cobalt) and tariffs on metals; HWM has passed these through so far, but that is a pricing-power assumption, not a guarantee.
- Net debt/EBITDA 0.94 is comfortable — balance sheet is not the risk here.

### 7. Shortage score: **8/10**
**Verdict:** Howmet owns the aerospace supply chain's tightest chokepoint — castings, forgings and fasteners with 20–50-week lead times and prohibitive re-qualification costs — and its growth is *accelerating* (9%→19%) with EBITDA margins up 320bps, not stuck at the screen's stale 9%; but at 58x current-year earnings, with Q2's growth partly bought via CAM and no disclosed backlog to underwrite it, the multiple leaves no room for the OEM rate slippage that is its most likely failure mode.

### 1. Shortage thesis — **THE SUPPLY SHORTAGE IS RESOLVED. Say it plainly.**
The brief asked me to check this and flag it if supply has caught up. **It has.**
- **FDA removed tirzepatide from the drug shortage list in 2024**, determining "the manufacturer's stated product availability and manufacturing capacity meet the present and projected national demand."
- **FDA removed semaglutide from the shortage list in 2025**, stating supply "meets or exceeds both current and projected US demands."
- **2026-04-30: FDA proposed excluding semaglutide, tirzepatide and liraglutide from the 503B Bulk Drug Substances List**, finding "no clinical need" for outsourcing facilities to compound them **absent a drug shortage** — i.e. the regulator is formally closing the compounding loophole precisely *because* the shortage is over.
- Only dulaglutide and liraglutide (older, non-Lilly-critical GLP-1s) remain on the shortage list.

**Implication: LLY does not belong in a "structural shortage" screen on the same basis as GE and HWM.** There is no allocation, no lead-time extension, no sold-out capacity, no ability to price off scarcity. The opposite is happening: **prices are falling.** Lilly itself guides that **US price will be a drag on top-line growth in the low-to-mid-teens percent in 2026**; Q4 2025 revenue growth of +43% was volume +46% *offset by* realized prices **-5%**.

What LLY has instead is a **demand surge that volume is absorbing at declining price** — a genuinely excellent business, but a *different* thesis. Volume growth (+46% in Q4'25) is doing all the work and more, which is why revenue still compounds at 45–56%. Anyone underwriting LLY as a "shortage" name is mis-specifying it; underwrite it as a category-defining volume ramp with negative pricing.

### 2. Demand-visibility proxy (no backlog exists)
**Manufacturing capacity / capex commitments:**
- **Total US capital expansion commitments since 2020: more than $55 billion** — Lilly has stated a plan to more than double US manufacturing investment since 2020 to exceed $50B, and the running total is now >$55B.
- **Four new US plants, ~$21B in newly announced facilities:**
  - **Houston, TX — $6.5B** API plant (orforglipron + small-molecule cardiometabolic/oncology/immunology/neuro).
  - **Huntsville, AL — $6.0B** API plant (peptides + small molecules incl. orforglipron); construction begins 2026, completion 2032.
  - **Goochland County, VA — $5.0B** API plant (bioconjugates, mAbs).
  - **Lehigh Valley, PA — >$3.5B** injectables and device manufacturing, including obesity drugs and **retatrutide**.
- **2026-07-30: Lilly + Resilience to invest $750M** in additional US drug manufacturing expansion.
- Note the timing asymmetry: the Alabama plant completes in **2032**. Most of this capex supports the 2029–2035 franchise, not 2026–2027 revenue.

**Prescription / demand trends:**
- Tirzepatide franchise run-rate ~$12.8B/quarter and growing 80–125% YoY.
- **Foundayo (orforglipron)**: 5,723 patients with a prescription/dispense between 2026-04-01 and 2026-07-06 (Truveta); **weekly scripts now ~20,000**. Street consensus **~$146M in Q2 2026** and **$1.2–1.6B for FY2026**; some analysts float **5M+ prescriptions in 2026**.
- **Novo's Wegovy pill (oral semaglutide)** approved 2025-12-22, launched January 2026, and had passed **3 million prescriptions by June 2026** — ahead of Foundayo's trajectory. Reporting notes Lilly's pill "appears to lag Novo's" despite Lilly calling the early days "encouraging." This is the one place Lilly is currently *behind*.

### 3. Category position — #1 and extending
- **US combined obesity + diabetes GLP-1 market share: Lilly 60.1% vs Novo Nordisk 39.4%** — Lilly is #1 with a ~21-point lead, and prescriber preference favours Lilly's molecules.
- **Trend is strongly in Lilly's favour.** Q1 2026 pulled the duopoly apart: **Lilly +55.5% revenue with raised guidance; Novo -4% adjusted sales at constant currency and cutting 9,000 jobs.** Novo is bracing for a 2026 sales *decline* while Lilly guides to +28%.
- **Lilly has also overtaken Novo in GLP-1 share outside the US** (reported May 2026) — the last geography where Novo led.
- Efficacy hierarchy underwrites it: tirzepatide (dual GIP/GLP-1) beats semaglutide on weight loss, and **retatrutide (triple agonist) beats both**.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation — score **7/10**
Strong but *qualitatively different* from GE/HWM: Lilly's moat is patent + pipeline + manufacturing scale, not certification lock-in. Assessing the brief's five named threats through 2030:

| Threat | Assessment through 2030 |
|---|---|
| **Patent cliff** | **Well protected.** Tirzepatide's compound patent (US 9,474,780) runs to **January 2036**, with device/formulation/method-of-use patents extending to ~2041. First US generics unlikely before **2036–2038**. **No cliff before 2030 — this is a non-issue in the window.** |
| **Oral GLP-1 competition** | **The live threat, and Lilly is currently behind.** Novo's Wegovy pill launched Jan 2026 and hit **>3M scripts by June 2026**; Lilly's Foundayo launched April 2026 at ~**20,000 weekly scripts**. Novo has a 4-month head start and better early traction. Lilly's counter is convenience (any time of day, no food/water restrictions) and ATTAIN-1 efficacy (12.4% / 27.3 lb weight loss at top dose). Real competitive pressure, but on an expanding pie. |
| **Compounded / generic semaglutide** | **Largely neutralized.** With both shortages resolved, FDA has set transition deadlines to stop compounding and on **2026-04-30 proposed permanently excluding semaglutide/tirzepatide/liraglutide from the 503B bulks list.** Grey-market supply is being closed off — a *positive* for Lilly. |
| **Medicare/CMS + government pricing** | **The most material margin threat, and it is already happening.** Semaglutide products are negotiated at **~$274/month effective January 2027, a 71% discount to list.** Trump-administration MFN deals cut DTC injectable GLP-1 pricing to **~$350/month (Zepbound from >$1,080 to ~$346)**, initiation doses $199. Foundayo lists at **$649/month WAC**, $149 self-pay, $25 with commercial coverage, **$50/month for eligible Medicare Part D from 2026-07-01**. Trulicity is in the 2028 negotiation cycle. Lilly's own guidance of **low-to-mid-teens % price drag** quantifies this. Volume is more than offsetting today; the question is whether it still does at 2028 prices. |
| **New entrants (amylin, Novo pipeline, Chinese biotechs)** | **Real but not imminent.** Novo is developing **UBT251** with a China-based partner, with strong Phase 2 China data. Amylin analogs and Chinese biotech assets are mostly Phase 2 with 2029+ launch horizons. Lilly's answer is **retatrutide**, which sets a bar (up to 28.3–30.3% weight loss) no competitor has matched. |

Net: the franchise is **very well protected through 2030 on IP**, is **winning on efficacy and share**, but is **structurally exposed on price** and faces one genuine competitive gap (oral, where Novo is ahead). It is not "irreplaceable" in the GE sense — a better molecule *can* displace it, and one day will — but nobody has one, and Lilly owns the best next-generation asset too. **7/10.**

### 5. Recent catalysts (since ~2026-05)
- **2026-05:** **TRIUMPH-1 (retatrutide) Phase 3 topline** — **28.3% average body weight reduction at 12mg over 80 weeks (~70 lb)**; **>45% of participants lost ≥30% of body weight**, a bariatric-surgery-equivalent level; all doses (4/9/12mg) met primary and key secondary endpoints in a 2,339-participant trial. (Some coverage cites up to 30.3% on a completer/efficacy-estimand basis.)
- **2026-07-23:** **TRIUMPH-2 and TRIUMPH-3 topline positive** — retatrutide met primary endpoints in adults with obesity + type 2 diabetes and established cardiovascular disease. Seven further Phase 3 trials complete during 2026.
- Retatrutide also delivered **~71.2 lb average weight loss plus substantial osteoarthritis pain relief** in its first successful Phase 3 — opens an OA label.
- **2026-07-30:** Lilly + Resilience **$750M** US manufacturing expansion.
- **2026-07-01:** Foundayo available at **$50/month for eligible Medicare Part D** via CMS' GLP-1 Bridge; PBM coverage at two of three majors secured by mid-May.
- **2026-04-30:** Q1 blowout (rev +56%, EPS $8.55 vs $6.66) and **FY26 guidance raised** on revenue, EPS and margin.
- **2026-04-01 / 04-09:** **FDA approved and Lilly launched Foundayo (orforglipron)** — first-ever oral GLP-1 for obesity with no food/water timing restrictions.
- **2026-07-21 (negative):** **Novo Nordisk sued Lilly** in D.N.J. under the Lanham Act over Zepbound/Mounjaro DTC ads allegedly comparing max tirzepatide doses against sub-maximal Wegovy (1.7/2.4mg, omitting the 7.2mg dose approved March 2026) and Ozempic (1mg vs approved 2mg). Novo seeks withdrawal plus corrective advertising and may seek a preliminary injunction. Low direct financial impact; signals how aggressive the fight has become.

### 6. Risks
- **Pricing/policy is the #1 risk and it is contractual, not hypothetical.** Semaglutide at ~$274/month from Jan 2027 resets the whole category's reference price; MFN deals already took Zepbound DTC from >$1,080 to ~$346. Lilly guides to a **low-to-mid-teens % price headwind in 2026** and prices fell 5% in Q4'25. If volume growth normalizes from +46% while price keeps falling double digits, revenue growth compresses fast.
- **The shortage thesis is gone.** No scarcity pricing power; capacity is now adequate and Lilly is adding >$55B more. Excess capacity in a price-falling market is an operating-leverage risk in reverse if volumes disappoint.
- **Novo is ahead in oral.** 3M+ Wegovy-pill scripts by June 2026 vs Foundayo's ~20k/week. If oral becomes the volume format and Lilly stays second, the share lead erodes at the margin that matters most.
- **Concentration:** two products (Mounjaro + Zepbound) = **~65% of Q1 2026 revenue**. Any safety signal, label change or competitive readout hits ~two-thirds of the P&L.
- **Very high comps.** Lapping +125%/+80% growth rates gets arithmetically harder every quarter; 2027 deceleration is near-certain.
- **Stock is -10% from its 52w high** and was down ~13% YTD at one point in March 2026 — the market is already debating these risks. Note this is the *only* one of the three not at a 52-week high, which is a momentum-screen negative but a valuation positive.
- **Q2 2026 prints 2026-08-05 (tomorrow)** — an immediate binary on Foundayo's first full quarter and on whether the price drag is tracking guidance.
- Litigation noise (Novo's false-advertising suit), and long-dated capex ($21B of plants completing 2029–2032) that consumes cash well before it produces revenue.

### 7. Shortage score: **4/10**
**Verdict:** Lilly is the best *business* in this batch — 60% US incretin share and widening, +56% revenue growth, EPS guided +50%, patent protection to 2036, and in retatrutide the most powerful obesity asset ever tested — and it is by far the cheapest at ~31x current-year guidance with the largest analyst upside (+14.5%); but **its GLP-1 shortage is formally over per FDA, prices are falling by policy mandate, and it therefore fails the specific "structural shortage" test this screen is built around.**
