# Ballot C1 — Agent C, the quality/moat & irreplaceability investor
**Date:** 2026-08-04 | **Universe:** 14 momentum-screen survivors | **Source:** `output/momentum/research_dossier.md` only

**My prior, stated up front:** the single most reliable way to destroy capital in this cycle is to buy a supplier whose largest customers are learning to build the thing themselves. A shortage is a rent you collect only for as long as nobody can route around you. So I sort first on *who the customer is* — if the customer is a hyperscaler, I need proof they cannot become the supplier; if the customer is a fab, an airframer or a farmer, that whole risk class disappears. I will pay up for that, but not infinitely: quality at any price is not quality.

---

## BALLOT 1 — SINGLE PICK

- **Top pick:** LRCX
- **Runner-up:** KLAC

**Thesis (3-5 sentences):**
Lam Research sells the etch and deposition tools that physically build 3D NAND stacks, DRAM capacitors and the HBM through-silicon-via and hybrid-bonding structures the entire AI buildout is currently starved of — and its customer list is Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron, TSMC and Intel, none of whom has ever built its own etcher and none of whom is a hyperscaler. That single fact removes the only failure mode I genuinely fear, and it is why I will take Lam over the two names (NVDA, AVGO) whose customers are actively and successfully in-sourcing. The business behind it is elite on my metrics: 65% ROE, 37% operating margin, a 52% gross margin that is the highest in twenty years, and *net cash* (net debt/EBITDA −0.21) going into the most violent capex cycle in tech. Critically, Lam is the **only** one of these four semis names whose screen forward P/E survived the dossier's own audit — 25.6x was called "roughly defensible," while NVDA, AVGO and KLAC were all found to be quoted on a two-years-forward basis and are materially more expensive than they look. And the stock fell 27% from its 2026-06-30 high *while printing its fourth consecutive record quarter*, which is how a moat asset gets offered at a growth-adjusted discount to its own weaker-momentum peers.

**Key shortage/backlog evidence (single most compelling):**
Lam raised its CY2026 WFE forecast **twice in two months** — to ~$140B on 2026-06-02, then to the "low $150B range" on the 2026-07-29 Q4 call — and then guided the September quarter to **$8.1B ±$400M against $6.72B just delivered, over +20% sequential off a record**. You do not raise your own industry forecast twice and then guide +20% sequentially off a record without a booked order book behind it. The corroborating hard number is the demand signal upstream: conventional DRAM contract prices **+90–95% q/q in Q1 CY2026** with a further +58–63% projected for Q2, and HBM sold out for 2026 — memory makers earning those margins convert them directly into the WFE spend Lam captures.

**Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk: LOW.**
This is the cleanest answer available in the field short of KLAC. There is no plausible path by which Google, Meta, Microsoft or Amazon builds a plasma etch tool — they are not customers, and the ~5 companies that *are* customers have never built their own etchers in fifty years of trying not to. The moat is threefold: process-tech lead (etch/dep recipes are co-developed per-node over years), qualification lock-in (swapping tool vendors mid-node risks yield on a multi-billion-dollar fab, so switching costs are measured in years), and capital/know-how concentration (realistically three firms on earth can supply leading-edge etch/dep at scale). Crucially, there is **no substitute technology that routes around it** — the content story runs the other way: 3D NAND going from 128 to 500+ layers **doubles Lam's served available market per wafer**, and gate-all-around, backside power delivery and hybrid bonding all *raise* etch/dep intensity. The two honest deductions from a perfect score: Applied Materials is a genuine, well-capitalised #2 in deposition, and China export-control policy can remove a chunk of TAM by headline. Neither is disintermediation; both are competition and politics. That is the risk I am willing to own.

**12-18 month return scenario:**
- **Base (+25–40%, ~$400–440):** the memory cycle runs through 2027 as management guides ("extraordinary"), FY27 EPS lands in the $11–12.5 range against FY26's $5.82, and the stock simply reclaims its 2026-06-30 all-time closing high of $433.33. This requires no multiple expansion at all — just the earnings arriving and the 27% drawdown reversing. Morgan Stanley's $404 and the $368 mean target bracket the low end.
- **Bull (+50–57%, ~$475–500):** Susquehanna's $475 or Cantor's $500, on the "generational semiconductor cycle" framing, if the $40B of NAND conversion spending lands before end-2027 as Lam expects and WFE marches toward the $300B long-run path Susquehanna's channel checks describe. In this case you get FY27 earnings *and* a re-rating on the market accepting cycle durability.

**Top risk:**
Cyclicality, full stop — this is a cyclical at peak margins (52% GM, a 20-year high) trading on a growth multiple. A DRAM/NAND price roll-over converts a +20% sequential guide into double-digit sequential declines inside two quarters; the 2022–23 memory downturn cut Lam's revenue by roughly a third. The 27% drawdown from the June high is precisely the market arguing about cycle *durability*, not the current quarter, and that argument is not settled. Sharpening the point: the WFE forecast Lam raised twice is Lam's own number, which means it can be cut twice as fast.

**Conviction: 8 / 10.**

### Top 3 — detail

**1. LRCX — Lam Research.**
The most compelling single moat data point is not a share number but a content number: **Lam expects its served available market per wafer in NAND to double from the 128-layer node to 500+ layer devices**, while WFE itself was revised up twice in two months to the "low $150B range." That is the definition of a moat that gets wider without the company doing anything — every architecture the industry adopts to escape the shortage (more layers, GAA, backside power, hybrid bonding) mechanically consumes more of exactly what Lam sells, and there is no substitute process that removes the need to etch and deposit. Combine that with 65% ROE, net cash, and the only forward P/E in the semis batch the dossier could reproduce from near-term earnings. **Base +25–40% to $400–440 as FY27 earnings arrive and the 27% drawdown reverses; bull +50–57% to $475–500 on the Susquehanna/Cantor cycle-durability case.**

**2. KLAC — KLA Corporation.**
The best *business* in the entire field of fourteen, and the strongest irreplaceability score in it: **58% share of process control, sustained for decades, with no #2 approaching 30%**, on 87% ROE and 42% operating margins. The moat data point I weight most is qualitative and decisive — you cannot route around inspection by choosing a different architecture, because every new architecture (GAA, backside power, 500-layer NAND, hybrid bonding) needs *more* defect-finding, not less, and KLA's defect libraries and installed-base data are a compounding asset a new entrant cannot buy at any price. Advanced-packaging process control guided to ~$1.1B in CY2026, **up more than 70% y/y** and raised from a prior high-50% expectation, is the AI-specific proof. It is the runner-up rather than the pick for three price-discipline reasons the dossier establishes: ~36x FY27 on bottom-up math (not the screen's 27.9x), the slowest growth in the batch (12% TTM, 15% y/y in Q4), and the only name here whose most recent analyst action was a *cut*, with the market punishing a revenue beat on gross-margin mix as advanced packaging dilutes incremental margin. **Base +23–41% to ~$240–275 (mean target $231, Susquehanna $275) as the WFE cycle it just guided to ~$150B delivers; bull +62–66% to $317–325 (BofA, Cantor) if the margin-mix worry proves transient and the −35% drawdown — the deepest in the field — fully unwinds.**

**3. GOOGL — Alphabet.**
The only company in this universe that owns the whole stack — TPU silicon, DeepMind/Gemini models, GCP distribution, and Search/YouTube as a captive demand sink — which inverts the disintermediation question entirely: Alphabet cannot be in-sourced *because it is the in-sourcer*. The data point that settles it for me is that **Anthropic's expansion to 3.5GW of next-gen TPU capacity (up to ~5GW overall) was attributed by Google Cloud CEO Thomas Kurian explicitly to TPU price-performance, not strategic alignment** — TPUs won an open bake-off against Nvidia on unit economics — and Meta has signed a multibillion-dollar TPU lease and is in talks to buy TPUs outright for its own datacenters from 2027. Its two biggest AI-lab competitors now pay it rent. Behind that sits **$514B of contracted Google Cloud backlog, ~5.2 years of current run-rate cloud revenue**, over half converting within 24 months, and a CFO stating plainly that Alphabet is supply-constrained and renting third-party capacity as a bridge. Cloud grew 82% while cloud operating margin expanded from 20.7% to 35.6% — accelerating volume with expanding margin is the signature of allocation, not discounting. It ranks third rather than first only because of arithmetic: at a **$4,568B market cap** a double requires adding $4.6T, and the mean target implies just +13.1%. **Base +13–20%; bull +30–50%** — a superb compounder, not an explosive-return bet, and I will not pretend otherwise to make it rank higher.

### Deliberately left out of my top 10

**ANET, VRT, CF and AVGO** — each fails my central test in a different way: **ANET** is the cleanest reject in the field (irreplaceability 5/10, ~42% of revenue from two customers who already design their own switches and run their own NOS in SONiC and FBOSS, while NVIDIA's Spectrum-X went 4%→21.5% share in two years to take the #1 position and bundles the substitute with the GPUs those same customers must buy — and after the after-hours pop it trades at or above freshly-raised $200–210 targets, so there is no margin of safety to pay me for the risk); **VRT** has Google contributing its own 2MW CDU design to OCP with six vendors delivering prototypes in a quarter — that is hyperscalers commoditizing Vertiv's newest, highest-growth SKUs in public — compounded by management switching off backlog and orders disclosure in February 2026, which removes the only evidence I could have underwritten; **CF** sells a fungible commodity whose world-scale plants cost ~$2,700–2,900 per tonne of annual capacity and take ~4 years, versus copper's $22,359/tonne and ~17 years, so its asset is replicable, its product is bypassable by imports and Chinese export policy, and it is a well-run cyclical at a cycle high rather than a moat; and **AVGO** is the subtlest exclusion but the most important one for this lens — its entire product is helping the world's most sophisticated silicon buyers acquire the capability to not need a merchant vendor, its anchor customer Google has already largely internalised TPU design over successive generations, there is no CUDA-equivalent software switching cost, and program losses in custom silicon are binary and total. A business whose value proposition is teaching customers to build their own is a business whose best customers eventually graduate.
