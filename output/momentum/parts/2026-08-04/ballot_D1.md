**2. That is the single most decision-relevant fact in the dossier for my lens, and it inverts most of the screen's cheapness ranking.

So: this is not a cheap field. But "buy nothing" is not my answer, because one name has taken a *documented* de-rating on a *correctly identified* risk while the demand evidence underneath it accelerated. That gap is my pick.

# BALLOT 1 — SINGLE PICK

- **Top pick:** **MSFT**
- **Runner-up:** **NVDA**

**Thesis (3–5 sentences):**
Microsoft is the only name in this field where the market has already done the de-rating, and did it for a reason that is real but bounded. The stock round-tripped from a ~$555 all-time high to a ~$356 low in April 2026 — a >35% drawdown — and prints a **-5% 12-month return while FY26 revenue grew 18% and EPS grew 32% to $17.95**; that is pure multiple compression to **~21.8x, its lowest valuation in three years**, and 20.8x forward is one of only three screen multiples in the entire dossier that survived bottom-up verification. Meanwhile the actual evidence got *better*: Azure accelerated to **+43% against its own 39–40% guide**, commercial RPO hit **$678B, +84% y/y** — and grew **+25% excluding OpenAI**, with *all* sequential RPO growth coming from customers outside the frontier labs. The market is pricing Microsoft as a capital-intensive infrastructure company whose demand is an OpenAI derivative; the disclosure says **nearly 90% of Microsoft Cloud revenue comes from customers outside the frontier AI labs**, and Copilot crossed **30M paid seats with net adds more than doubling q/q** — the first hard evidence of AI monetizing at *software* margin rather than datacenter margin. I am buying 32% EPS growth at ~21x from a starting point of negative 12-month momentum, which is the only genuinely un-crowded position available here.

**Key shortage/backlog evidence (single most compelling data point):**
**Commercial RPO of $678B, +84% y/y — and +25% ex-OpenAI, with all sequential growth from non-frontier-lab customers.** Against ~$59.3B of quarterly Microsoft Cloud revenue that is **~2.9 years of contracted cloud revenue**, and longer-term contractual commitments rose ~138%. The corroborating quote is CFO Amy Hood verbatim: *"Yes, demand continues to exceed available supply."* Azure beat its guide by squeezing efficiency out of the existing fleet precisely *because* it could not add iron fast enough.

**Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk: LOW.**
This is why MSFT wins under my lens rather than NVDA or AVGO. The doctrine's trap is the supplier whose largest customers build the thing themselves. Microsoft's customers are enterprises, not hyperscalers — and the asset they would have to in-source is not compute, it is **Entra ID, M365, and the compliance perimeter**. An enterprise can move a GPU workload in a weekend; it cannot move its identity graph or 400M seats of Office. The dossier's own two proof points are the RPO growing +25% *ex*-OpenAI and 90% of cloud revenue coming from non-frontier-lab customers — i.e. the "MSFT is an OpenAI SPV" bear case is empirically rebutted by the disclosure. The honest weakness is the opposite of disintermediation: **Maia 200 is a generation behind TPU in external validation** — nobody outside Microsoft rents Maia the way Anthropic and Meta buy TPU capacity — so Microsoft's silicon reduces its own Nvidia bill but never becomes a merchant revenue line. That caps the upside; it does not open a hole in the revenue base. The genuine residual is model-layer commoditization (if enterprises buy models direct, Azure becomes rented real estate), mitigated by royalty-free IP rights through 2032 and in-house MAI models.

**12–18 month return scenario:**
- **Base: +12–18%.** Earnings do the work, the multiple does nothing. FY26 EPS $17.95 (+32%) compounding at a decelerating but still high-teens-plus rate on a static ~21x gets you roughly to the **$563 mean target (+14.3%)**. No re-rating assumed — this is deliberately the assumption I do *not* want to need.
- **Bull: +30–40%.** Copilot seat monetization keeps inflecting (net adds already more than doubling q/q) and the market accepts that AI software margin is arriving, so the multiple re-expands off a three-year low while RPO converts. This requires the depreciation debate to stop being the whole story.
- **Downside I am underwriting: roughly -25 to -30%**, a revisit toward the ~$356 April-2026 area if FY27 margins fall by more than "slightly" and Azure decelerates. Note the stock has already made that exact trip once in the last twelve months while the business compounded — the downside here is a multiple event, not a franchise event, which is the only kind of downside I am willing to pay for.

**Top risk:**
**Company-guided FY27 operating margin decline plus the depreciation wave.** Capex is ~$175B calendar-2026 and the reduction from ~$190B was **optical — a lease reclassification, with "the underlying investment plans remain unchanged."** Worse, the useful-life extension that produced the flattering capex optic also *delays rather than avoids* the depreciation, and extending useful lives on AI servers is an aggressive assumption if hardware cycles compress. If depreciation peaks later and higher than expected, the multiple never re-expands and I collect only the earnings growth — that is the base case, not the disaster case, but it means the bull case is genuinely contingent. Secondary: OpenAI equity-method losses scaling with OpenAI's burn ($3.1B in a single quarter and rising) and non-exclusivity through 2032 allowing OpenAI workloads to migrate.

**Conviction: 7 / 10.**
Seven, not nine, because I am picking the name with the *best expectation gap*, not the best explosive-return odds — the dossier is right that MSFT has the "lowest explosive-return probability" in its batch, and I will not pretend otherwise. But a 7 here is worth more than a 9 on a name where the sell side has already marked the price to the story.

## Top 3 — detail

**1. MSFT.** The single number: **commercial RPO $678B, up 84% y/y, and up 25% excluding OpenAI, with every dollar of sequential growth coming from customers outside the frontier labs** — roughly 2.9 years of contracted cloud revenue, against a CFO saying in plain words that demand exceeds available supply. The reason this is underappreciated is that the market spent 2026 re-rating Microsoft from asset-light software compounder to capital-intensive infrastructure, and it did so on the capex line while the contract line was compounding at 84%. *Base 12–18mo: +12–18%, earnings growth only, roughly to the $563 consensus target on a static ~21x. Bull: +30–40% if Copilot (30M paid seats, net adds doubling q/q) forces the market to re-rate the software layer separately from the datacenter layer.*

**2. NVDA.** The single number: **$119.0B of supply commitments disclosed in the 10-Q, $95B payable within FY2027** — an audited, hard, cash-backed figure sitting underneath Huang's $1T stated Blackwell/Rubin visibility through 2027. My contrarian read is that the bear case is now the *consensus* case: the multiple has compressed from ~35x in July to **~22–23x** and the 12-month return is only **+22% while earnings nearly doubled**, which is the market pricing in ASIC in-sourcing before it has happened. Nvidia also carries the field's **widest consensus headroom at +42.9%** to a $303 mean target. What keeps it at #2 rather than #1 is that this is the doctrine's trap in its purest form — **~40% of revenue from four hyperscalers all shipping competing silicon, custom-ASIC shipments growing 44.6% y/y vs 16.1% for merchant GPUs** — plus circular financing (the $15.9B of equity gains are on customers it also funds) and a $5T cap that makes explosive percentage returns arithmetically hard. *Base: +15–25%. Bull: +40%+, roughly the analyst mean target, if Rubin ships at the guided pace and the inference-share erosion stays a 2028 story rather than a 2027 one.*

**3. AVGO.** The single number: **over $30B of AI semiconductor bookings against $10.8B shipped in Q2 FY26 — a ~2.8x book-to-bill on the AI line alone**, with management effectively confirming a **$200B+ implied 18-month backlog**, and the Apollo/Blackstone **$35B AI XPV platform** purpose-built to fund 20GW of that demand through 2028. That is the hardest order-book evidence in the entire dossier, and +26.2% consensus headroom is the second-widest. My reservations are valuation and reflexivity: **~36x FY26** (the screen's 20.1x is not reproducible on any near-term basis), which means the **$100B FY2027 AI revenue target is already embedded in the price** — delivering "only" $80B would be a severe de-rating — and the XPV vehicle is Broadcom financing its own customers' purchases, the same circularity as Nvidia's equity stakes but in debt form. *Base: +15–25%. Bull: +35–45% toward the $528 target if the FY27 doubling tracks and no XPU socket is lost; the losses in custom silicon are binary and abrupt, which is the shape of the downside.*
