## MU (Micron)

### Claim 1 — ~$150B RPO across 26 take-or-pay SCAs; ~$32B customer financial commitments; ~$12.7B cash deposits held at fiscal Q4-end
**Verdict: CONFIRMED (as management-stated on the call) — with a material filing caveat: the 10-K RPO figure is ~$134B, not $150B.**

- On the fiscal Q4 2026 earnings call (30 Sep 2026), Micron said it had signed **26 strategic customer agreements (SCAs)**, up from 16 in June, all with take-or-pay volumes, estimated to cover >35% of revenue through 2030 (some extending into 2031); **remaining performance obligations of about $150B**, counting only agreements with a defined pricing framework, based on committed volumes and minimum prices — CFO Mark Murphy called the figure "inherently conservative"; **customer financial commitments increased to $32B, "the vast majority of which are cash deposits"** (CEO Sanjay Mehrotra). Sources: AIStockWire, "Micron Q4 FY2026 earnings: $54.2B revenue, $150B RPO" (30 Sep / 1 Oct 2026); TIKR recap of the Q4 call (Oct 2026); MarketWatch live coverage of Micron earnings (30 Sep 2026, noting Bernstein's observation that only ~20% of the RPO is secured by deposits).
- **Filing caveat (important):** Micron's Form 10-K (filed 9 Oct 2026, fiscal year ended 3 Sep 2026) states the transaction price allocated to remaining performance obligations was **approximately $134 billion**, of which $13B has been recognised as contract liabilities, ~one-fourth expected to be recognised as revenue over the next 12 months, fully within five years, and explicitly warns RPO "are not expected to be indicative of future revenue under these contracts." So the $150B is the call/prepared-remarks number; $134B is the audited filing number. The dossier should not cite $150B as the filing figure.
- **Deposits ~$12.7B: CONFIRMED from the filing.** The 10-K states Micron **received cash deposits of $12.75B in fiscal 2026** in connection with the SCAs, nearly all scheduled to be repaid between 2029 and 2031 (plus $7B of letters of credit issued/committed). The Q4 balance sheet shows **noncurrent customer contract liabilities of $12,895M** at 3 Sep 2026 (vs $568M at 28 May 2026), and the FY2026 cash-flow statement shows **"proceeds from customer contract liability deposits" of $12,747M**, classified in financing (not operating) cash flow. Sources: Micron Form 10-K filed 9 Oct 2026 (via micron.gcs-web.com); MomoView FQ4 2026 earnings analysis (Oct 2026) reconciling the $12.7B deposits / $12.895B liability.

### Claim 2 — HBM sold out through calendar 2027; Micron can meet only ~50–66% of key customers' medium-term HBM demand (and when was that figure stated?)
**Verdict: CONFIRMED in substance, with two corrections — (a) the latest call says "vast majority," not 100%; (b) the 50–66% figure was first stated in December 2025 and was originally about demand from several key customers generally, not an HBM-only statistic.**

- Sold-out / contracted through 2027: On the **30 Sep 2026** FQ4 call, prepared remarks state: "We have **completed agreements for the vast majority of our calendar 2027 HBM bit supply** with significant price increases year over year." Micron also said >75% of total fiscal/calendar 2027 output is already committed. Earlier reporting (Particle News, 21–23 Sep 2026, summarising the June/Q3 position) headlined "HBM sold out through 2027." So "sold out through 2027" is a fair shorthand for the pre-Q4 position, but the precise Q4 language is "vast majority" under agreement — not literally every bit. Sources: Micron FQ4 2026 call transcript (Longbridge / MarketBeat, 30 Sep 2026); SignalStack, "Micron Fiscal Q4 2026 Results: What the 8-K Shows" (Oct 2026); Particle News (Sep 2026).
- 50–66% figure — **when stated: first on the fiscal Q1 2026 earnings call, 17 Dec 2025.** TrendForce (18 Dec 2025) reported Mehrotra saying Micron can currently meet only about 50% to 66% of demand from its core customers. The actual transcript wording (Motley Fool transcript of the Q1 FY26 call) was broader: *"in the medium term, we are only able to meet about 50% to two-thirds of our demand from several key customers"* — i.e., a medium-term, multi-product statement about several key customers, which later coverage (e.g., StockMoguls, May 2026; NewsCase, Jun 2026) re-framed as specifically HBM demand. The figure was still being cited through the FQ2 (Mar 2026) and FQ3 (Jun 2026) cycles. The dossier's use is directionally right, but it should be dated Dec 2025 and not presented as a fresh, HBM-specific FQ4 2026 disclosure.

## NVDA (Nvidia)

### Claim 5 — ~$1T Blackwell + Vera Rubin purchase-order / demand visibility through 2027: formal backlog/RPO or management visibility? Filing RPO ~$2.6–3.2B?
**Verdict: CONFIRMED — it is management visibility / stated purchase orders, NOT formal backlog or ASC 606 RPO. The filing RPO range is also CONFIRMED ($2.6B in Q1 FY27; $3.2B latest).**

- At **GTC, 16 Mar 2026**, CEO Jensen Huang said: last year Nvidia saw ~$500B in demand for Blackwell and upcoming Rubin chips through 2026; "right here where I stand, **I see through 2027, at least $1 trillion**" in orders for Blackwell and Vera Rubin. This was a keynote projection of purchase orders / high-confidence demand — not a filed backlog number. Sources: TechCrunch, 16 Mar 2026 ("Jensen Huang just put Nvidia's Blackwell and Vera Rubin sales projections into the $1 trillion stratosphere"); InsiderFinance GTC recap; Acade Research GTC 2026 report (describing it as "$1 trillion in high-confidence demand and purchase orders").
- **Filing RPO:** Nvidia's Q2 FY2027 Form 10-Q (period ended 26 Jul 2026) states: "As of July 26, 2026, revenue related to **remaining performance obligations from contracts greater than one year in length was $3.2 billion**, which included $3.0 billion from deferred revenue and $244 million that has not yet been billed or recognised as revenue" (~39% to be recognised over the next 12 months). YCharts' series from the filings shows **$2.6B at 26 Apr 2026 (Q1 FY27) rising to $3.2B at 26 Jul 2026 (Q2 FY27)** — exactly the dossier's ~$2.6–3.2B range. Sources: NVDA Q2 FY2027 10-Q (filed 26/27 Aug 2026); BTW Media analysis of the 10-Q RPO disclosure (Sep 2026); YCharts RPO history.
- Interpretation confirmed: the ~300x gap between the $1T keynote figure and the $3.2B filing RPO is real. The $1T must be labelled management demand visibility/purchase orders, never backlog or RPO.

### Claim 7 — Receivables rising to ~$63.1B with DSO expansion, and large non-cash equity gains in GAAP income (HIGH_ACCRUALS flag)
**Verdict: CONFIRMED. The HIGH_ACCRUALS flag is supported by the filing evidence.**

- **Receivables/DSO:** Accounts receivable reached **$63.1B** at 26 Jul 2026 (from $38.5B at the Jan 2026 fiscal year-end; ~$22.3B of the Q2 net-income-to-cash gap was receivables build). **DSO rose from 45 days to 60 days in one quarter**, which management attributed to "extended payment terms on large, multi-quarter agreements with certain investment-grade customers"; the 10-Q describes extended terms of **90 days up to one year** for investment-grade customers on large data-center builds. Five direct customers accounted for ~70% of the receivables balance. Q2 operating cash flow was $24.1B vs GAAP net income of $59.7B.
- **Non-cash equity gains:** The Q2 FY2027 10-Q income statement shows **"Gains from equity securities, net" of $7,771M in Q2** (and $23,707M for the first half), and the 10-Q states these gains "were primarily driven by **unrealized** gains in equity securities." This is why GAAP net income ($59,688M; EPS $2.46) **exceeds** non-GAAP net income ($53,954M; EPS $2.22): the non-GAAP reconciliation removes the $7,771M gain. The gains are subtracted again in the cash-flow reconciliation — they are paper gains, not cash.
- Sources: NVDA Q2 FY2027 10-Q (Other Income note and cash-flow statement, Aug 2026); Nvidia Q2 FY2027 earnings release GAAP/non-GAAP reconciliation (26 Aug 2026); Beancount earnings analysis (26 Aug 2026); Zacks (Sep 2026); Motley Fool (23 Sep 2026); AInvest cash-flow analysis (Aug 2026).

### Claim 11 — Amphenol is #2 in connectors behind TE Connectivity; hyperscalers do not in-source connectors but do license designs to second sources
**Verdict: PARTIALLY CONFIRMED — the #2 ranking and the second-source licensing pattern are confirmed; the blanket "hyperscalers do not in-source connectors" is UNVERIFIED (no public source proves a negative; nothing found contradicts it).**

- #2 ranking: Multiple industry rankings place **TE Connectivity #1 and Amphenol #2** among global connector manufacturers by sales (e.g., top-10 connector ranking as of 2024 cited by PortfolioParrot's Amphenol deep dive; Dataintelo electronic/wire connector market reports, 2026, describing TE as largest and Amphenol as second-largest). This confirms the mechanism in the dossier — the customer (here Nvidia) pushes the design holder to license to a second source rather than the customer manufacturing connectors itself.
- In-sourcing: searches found no instance of a hyperscaler manufacturing connectors in-house, and hyperscaler practice in the sources is to qualify/specify and dual-source (Edgewater also notes hyperscalers "generally qualify fiber to a performance spec rather than requiring a specific supplier"). That is consistent with the claim but does not prove it; treat "do not in-source" as UNVERIFIED-but-uncontradicted.
