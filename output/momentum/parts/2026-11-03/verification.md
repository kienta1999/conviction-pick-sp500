## MU — Micron Technology

### MU-1. HBM sold out through 2026; most of 2027 output committed; shortages persisting well beyond 2028 (CEO, fiscal Q4 2026 call, 30 Sep 2026)
**Verdict: CONFIRMED (substance), with one wording qualification.**
- HBM sold out in 2026: confirmed. Trendintech (Oct 2026 webinar coverage) reports Micron's 2026 HBM supply, including HBM4, was sold out before the webinar, with the first such statement traced to the February 2026 earnings call.
- Most of 2027 committed: confirmed, precisely. Zacks, 1 Oct 2026, reporting the 30 Sep 2026 fiscal Q4 call: CEO Sanjay Mehrotra clarified that "more than 75% of Micron's 2027 output is already committed across SCA and non-SCA customers, while discussions increasingly center on 2028 demand"; the "vast majority" of calendar-2027 HBM bit supply is covered by agreements at prices significantly above 2026.
- Shortage duration — qualification: the reporting found says supply constrained "through calendar 2028" (Zacks, 1 Oct 2026), "much tighter in calendar 2027 and 2028 than in 2026" with "no line of sight to when supply and demand will return to balance" (Morningstar / Dow Jones, 30 Sep 2026, 18:27 ET), and "into 2028 and possibly beyond" (Technobezz, 5 Oct 2026). The exact phrase "well beyond 2028" as a direct CEO quote was NOT found in the sources retrieved; "through 2028 + no line of sight to balance" is the verified formulation. That is a softening of the dossier's adverb, not a reversal — the direction (shortage extends at least through 2028, negotiations already into 2028) is confirmed.
- Sources: Zacks 2026-10-01; Morningstar/Dow Jones 2026-09-30; Technobezz 2026-10-05.

### MU-2. ~$100bn minimum-price revenue under multi-year strategic customer agreements with price floors, plus $12.75bn customer contract-liability deposits in fiscal 2026
**Verdict: CONFIRMED, but the $100bn figure is a superseded (June) snapshot — the current audited total is larger.**
- $12.75bn deposits: confirmed exactly from the primary filing. Micron FY2026 Form 10-K (fiscal year ended 3 Sep 2026, filed 9 Oct 2026, accession 0000723125-26-000023): cash-flow statement "Proceeds from customer contract liability deposits 12,747" ($m); MD&A: "we received cash deposits of $12.75 billion in 2026. Nearly all of the deposits are scheduled to be repaid between 2029 and 2031." Balance sheet: noncurrent customer contract liabilities $12,895m at 3 Sep 2026 vs $142m a year earlier. Deposits are refundable as minimum purchases are met — a liability, not revenue, until delivery.
- ~$100bn / 16 agreements / price floors: confirmed as the June 2026 position. Webull's 30 Sep 2026 earnings summary repeats the dossier figure verbatim: "16 multi-year strategic customer agreements, 14 of which carry approximately $100 billion in minimum-price revenue with contractual price floors." Independent 10-K analysis (n-ledger) traces the sequence: 16 signed agreements detailed 24 Jun 2026 at ~$100bn, then 26 agreements by 30 Sep 2026.
- Update the panel should carry: by the 30 Sep 2026 call Micron reported 26 strategic customer agreements (up from 16 in June), >35% of estimated revenue through 2030, and the audited 3 Sep 2026 remaining performance obligation was ~$134bn at minimum prices (10-K revenue note; ~$150bn "to date" at 30 Sep per management snapshot, unaudited and on a different date/population). The 10-K itself cautions the $134bn is built on minimum pricing, excludes agreements without fixed pricing/price bands and contracts of a year or less, and "is not expected to be indicative of future revenue." The dossier's $100bn therefore understates the current contracted book; it does not overstate it. No ranking impact — if anything it strengthens MU.
- Sources: Micron FY2026 10-K filed 2026-10-09 (via evidinvest / hotmolts / techworld2 10-K reads, Oct 2026); Morningstar 2026-09-30 ($32bn financial commitments / 26 agreements); Zacks 2026-10-01 (26 agreements, >35% of revenue through 2030).

- Sources: SiliconANGLE 2026-10-23; TrendForce via datacenterdisk 2026-10-11 and zandigital Oct 2026; sim-networks Oct 2026 (Q2 58–63%, Q3 13–18%).

### CDNS-2. EDA duopoly position with Synopsys — Cadence as one of two dominant EDA vendors that customers cannot realistically replace in-house
**Verdict: CONFIRMED with a definitional qualification: it is a Big-3 market with a clear top two, not a literal two-firm market.**
- Top-two dominance confirmed: SemiAnalysis EDA Primer (May 2026, via dossier and shashi.co Jul 2026 summary): Synopsys "close to a third" of the global EDA market (leads synthesis/simulation), Cadence "a similar share" (leads custom/analog layout), Siemens EDA ~10–15%; together 70–80% of global EDA revenue, a concentration held for over a decade. TalkMarkets (Oct 2026): the two firms "each with about 30% market share… the only other firm even close is Siemens EDA, at about 13% share," calling them "the 'Coke' and 'Pepsi' of the EDA industry" with an "effective duopoly."
- "Cannot realistically replace in-house" — supported as an inference, not a directly sourced quotation: the barriers cited across sources are full-flow integration, foundry-PDK embedding, switching a full flow mid-program being a multi-year high-risk undertaking, and the absence of any credible full in-house EDA flow from a hyperscaler (dossier §4; TalkMarkets on end-to-end flows as a "unique asset" that discourages entrants; ainvest on EDA as "winner-takes-all" infrastructure). No source was found claiming a customer has built a full in-house replacement flow.
- Qualification the panel should keep: Siemens EDA is not negligible — its Calibre tool runs physical verification on nearly every tape-out regardless of which vendor handled earlier stages, and major customers (Apple, Nvidia) routinely use all three vendors for different stages rather than a single vendor. "Duopoly" is therefore fair shorthand for the top two, but the precise structure is Big-3 with a top-two lead. This softens the label, not the moat claim, and does not change CDNS's ranking.
- Sources: SemiAnalysis May 2026 via shashi.co Jul 2026; TalkMarkets Oct 2026; ainvest 2025.
