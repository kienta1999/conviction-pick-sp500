## 1. SINGLE-PICK ballot

- Top pick: MU
- Runner-up: MSFT
- Thesis (3-5 sentences): Micron is the only name in the 14 where scarcity itself is the product: HBM consumes roughly 3x the wafer capacity of standard DRAM, AI servers/HBM now consume close to 70% of total DRAM output, and conventional DRAM was starved as a result, so price — not volume — is doing the work. That scarcity is contracted, not just spot: 2026 HBM capacity is sold out, more than 75% of total 2027 output was already committed at the 30 Sep 2026 call, and negotiations have moved into 2028, giving multi-year revenue visibility no other name matches on a price-setting basis. At the screen price of $884.74, forward PE of 4.3x and analyst mean target of $1,561.91 (+76.5% upside, the highest in the 14), the market is still pricing a memory cycle rather than a contracted shortage. The explosive case is therefore duration: every quarter the shortage persists beyond 2028 reprices the contracted book upward.
- Key shortage/backlog evidence: $12.75bn in customer contract-liability deposits received in fiscal 2026, backing 16 multi-year strategic agreements, 14 of which carry ~$100bn in minimum-price revenue with contractual price floors — customers prepaying to secure supply, with CEO Sanjay Mehrotra saying shortages will persist "well beyond 2028" and Micron meeting only about half to two-thirds of core customer demand.
- Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk: low — hyperscalers cannot realistically in-source DRAM: it requires leading-edge fabs, EUV and TSV stacking at tens of billions in capex, and only three firms in the world (Samsung, SK hynix, Micron, ~90–95% of DRAM revenue) do it at scale; no hyperscaler DRAM fab programme was found, and HBM is architecturally required beside every Nvidia/AMD accelerator, with no drop-in substitute at scale.
- 12-18 month return scenario: rough base / bull case — base: shortage persists through 2027 as contracted, with steady upward repricing toward part of the +76.5% analyst mean upside; bull: shortage extends into 2028 negotiations at higher contracted prices and the stock approaches or exceeds the $1,561.91 mean target. Both cases are anchored to the dossier's analyst upside figure, not a new price target.
- Top risk: Classic memory cyclicality — if AI capex pauses or the Idaho One (mid-2027) / Tongluo (late 2027 into 2028) capacity lands into softer demand, DRAM prices can fall as violently as they rose; Samsung HBM4 ramp / re-qualification at Nvidia and China's CXMT (10% DRAM share) are the share threats, and Micron is only #3 in DRAM (23.3%) and #3 in HBM (~18%).
- Conviction (1-10): 8

**2. MSFT.** The single most compelling data point is commercial remaining performance obligation of $677B at 30 Sep 2026, +73% year-on-year and ~2.04x FY2026 total revenue, paired with CFO Amy Hood's repeated statement that demand still exceeds Azure supply — capacity, not customers, is the constraint, even as Q1 FY2027 capex of $45.2B came in below the >$50B guide because third-party capacity deliveries slipped. **3. NVDA.** The single most compelling data point is ~3.6 million Blackwell units in backlog from the largest cloud providers alone (Morgan Stanley research cited in the dossier), against production approaching ~1mn units/month — roughly a 12-month visibility window — with CoWoS-L packaging and HBM as the gating constraints through fiscal 2028. Rough 12–18mo scenario: base, progress toward part of the +60.7% analyst mean upside ($328.95 target on the $204.64 screen price) as Blackwell/Rubin convert; bull, approaching or exceeding that target if Rubin ramps on schedule and packaging constraints stay binding.
