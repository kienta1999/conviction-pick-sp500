## 1. SINGLE-PICK ballot

- Top pick: CDNS
- Runner-up: NVDA
- Thesis (3-5 sentences): Cadence sits in a two-player EDA duopoly at the one step no AI chip can skip: verification before tape-out. As chips grow larger and multi-die, Palladium/Protium emulation becomes the gating bottleneck, and CEO Anirudh Devgan cited chip sizes projected to increase 48-fold over the next five years. That demand is converting into a record backlog and a third consecutive beat-and-raise in FY2026, with recurring revenue up 18% and all product groups in double-digit growth. Unlike a GPU or switch, the product is not a socket a hyperscaler can swap or build itself — it is a full flow embedded in foundry PDKs and customer design flows.
- Key shortage/backlog evidence: Record backlog of $8.2 billion at Q3 2026, equal to about 1.28x guided FY2026 revenue, with $4.2 billion of remaining performance obligations expected to be recognised within the next 12 months.
- Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk: low — and why: Hyperscalers design their own chips but buy EDA tools, and none has credibly built a full in-house EDA flow; switching a full flow mid-program is a multi-year, high-risk undertaking, and Cadence leads custom/analog layout and hardware emulation within the duopoly. The realistic threats are narrower — Synopsys as the ever-present alternative in every deal, and export controls pushing Chinese customers toward domestic EDA over a decade — not customer in-sourcing or a drop-in substitute.
- 12-18 month return scenario: rough base / bull case: Base — roughly toward the analyst mean target of $411.76 versus the $327.89 screen price (+25.6% in the dossier metrics table), as the $4.2 billion of backlog due within 12 months converts and recurring revenue keeps compounding. Bull — above that mean target if emulation demand from larger AI/HPC designs and the Maestro / AI Super Agent engagements (over 100 active engagements cited in the dossier) accelerate full-flow wins beyond the current beat-and-raise cadence. These are scenario estimates anchored to the dossier's mean target, not dossier forecasts.
- Top risk: Valuation and duopoly competition — the stock already reflects three beat-and-raises and a record backlog, Synopsys (now with Ansys) contests every deal, and guidance assumes export controls remain substantially similar.

**2. **3. MSFT:** The single most compelling data point is the $677 billion commercial remaining performance obligation at 30 September 2026 (+73% year on year), alongside CFO Amy Hood's repeated statement that demand still exceeds Azure supply — Azure itself grew 48% to $33.2 billion in Q1 FY2027 despite capacity deliveries slipping. That is a sold-out capacity moat fused to an enterprise distribution funnel (M365 / Copilot) no rival can copy wholesale. Rough 12-18 month scenario: base roughly toward the $631.09 mean target (+20.9% from $521.91); bull above it if Azure sustains ~48-50% growth as new power and buildings arrive — tempered by the flat sequential RPO, OpenAI concentration (~11% of quarterly revenue, roughly a third of backlog by one analysis), and ~$170 billion planned FY2027 capex.
