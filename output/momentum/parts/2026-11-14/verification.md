## CF INDUSTRIES (CF)

### Claim 1 — 97% ammonia capacity utilisation in 9M 2026; fill-programme order book into Q1 2027
**Verdict: CONFIRMED**
- Source: CF Industries Q3 2026 earnings call, Nov 5, 2026 (transcript via Motley Fool / TradingKey). CEO Chris Bohn: "Since the beginning of the year, we have operated our available ammonia capacity at 97% utilization." The Q3 2026 results presentation (8-K exhibit, Nov 4, 2026) shows "97% 9M 2026 Available Ammonia Capacity Utilization".

- No correction needed. Note the 97% is of *available* capacity — the presentation defines available capacity as average annual gross capacity less the Yazoo City complex, which is idled pending a H1 2027 restart.

### Claim 2 — Middle East hostilities since late Feb 2026 removed ~9 Mt of urea supply; exports ~800,000 t/month vs normal 1.5–1.7 Mt/month; normalisation 4–6 months even if hostilities ended immediately
**Verdict: CONFIRMED**
- Source: CF Industries Q3 2026 earnings call, Nov 5, 2026 (Motley Fool / Nasdaq / TradingKey transcripts). CCO Bert Frost, prepared remarks: "Since hostilities began in the Middle East in late February, we estimate that the global market has lost approximately nine million metric tons of urea supply." In Q&A: "Outside of Oman, we're probably considering 500,000-550,000 tons per month has been exiting the Gulf, and then throwing Oman on top of that because it sits outside the Gulf, you're probably around 800,000 tons out of 1.5 million-1.7 million tons normally per month." And: "if regional hostilities in the Middle East ended immediately, it would likely take four to six months for global shipping volumes and plant operations to return to normal."
- Independent corroboration of the disruption's scale: Kpler data reported by The National (Oct 31, 2026) — Middle East fertiliser exports fell ~70–75% since the war began Feb 28, 2026, to 994,000 t in September 2026 from 3.41 Mt in September 2025; ITC data shows urea exports from Hormuz-dependent economies fell 83% YoY in April 2026.
- Correction/nuance: all three figures are CF management's own estimates, not independently audited market data — but they are the company's stated numbers, accurately quoted, and third-party ship-tracking data is directionally consistent. The 800,000 t/month figure *includes* Oman (ex-Oman Gulf exports are 500,000–550,000 t/month); the dossier's phrasing does not state that, but the number itself is as quoted.

### Claim 7 — Pratt & Whitney's GTF crisis has ~650 A320neo-family aircraft grounded, with shop visits stretched to 300–360 days
**Verdict: CONFIRMED (with a sourcing nuance)**
- Sources: SafeFly (2026 update, figures current as of Aug 2026): "Pratt & Whitney has cited a peak of roughly 650 grounded GTF-powered aircraft... shop-visit turnaround times stretched to 300–360 days against an original 60-day estimate." DWU Consulting (citing RTX's Sep 11, 2023 disclosure and Reuters): "as many as 650 jets idle in the first half of 2024 at the global peak"; "Shop visit turnaround times of 300–360 days per engine — versus the 60 days initially projected (JetBlue CFO, Feb 22, 2024)". RTX's original disclosure: an average of ~350 aircraft parked per year through 2026, peaking at ~650.
- Nuance: **~650 is Pratt & Whitney/RTX's own cited peak figure.** Independent fleet trackers have reported higher peaks at various points (Simple Flying, Jun 5, 2026: "At its peak, the crisis grounded around 835 aircraft"; ActiveFlights: ~720 jets / ~636 grounded-or-stored). The 300–360-day turnaround is also a peak figure — current (2026) averages are improving to ~120 days per SafeFly. The dossier presents both figures as the crisis at its worst, which is accurate; they should not be read as the current (Nov 2026) grounding count or current turnaround time. No thesis impact — the direction (rival grounded, CFM gaining share) is undisputed.

### Claim 10 — High-speed interconnect lead times of 40+ weeks vs 8–16 weeks for standard connectors
**Verdict: CONFIRMED (with a category nuance)**
- Source: Utmel, "High-speed Connectors Shortage Outlook 2026" — executive summary: "standard connectors maintain 8–16 week lead times, while high-speed interconnects, RF modules, and transceivers extend to 40+ weeks", citing LINK-PP industry analysis and the IBS Electronics Market Report.
- Nuance: the same source's detailed table breaks the category down — **800G optical transceivers 40+ weeks**, while **high-speed (backplane/IO) connectors are 16–26+ weeks** and high-current power connectors 20–40 weeks. So "high-speed interconnect lead times of 40+ weeks" is accurate for the high-speed interconnect *category as a whole* (transceivers/optics/RF) as the source's summary states it, but for connectors specifically the granular figure is 16–26+ weeks — still roughly double the standard 8–16 weeks, and still a real constraint. Also note Utmel is a component-distributor blog citing industry analyses, not a primary source (no Amphenol- or TE-published lead-time figure exists, as the dossier itself notes). The shortage direction is corroborated independently by Amphenol's own order flow (claim 9) and by Semibuffer (Nov 2, 2026) advising buyers to confirm allocation per part number. No thesis impact.
