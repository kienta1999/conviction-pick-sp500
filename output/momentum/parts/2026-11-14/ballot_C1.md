# Ballot C1 — Quality / Moat & Irreplaceability — momentum 2026-11-14

Lens: category dominance, margins, returns durability, and above all irreplaceability — reject any name whose customers could realistically in-source the product or that a substitute technology could route around. The trap is disintermediation / in-sourcing, applied as the primary filter. All figures are from the shared research dossier (2026-11-14); no fresh research.

## Single-pick ballot

- Top pick: GE
- Runner-up: CF
- Thesis (3-5 sentences): GE Aerospace is the closest thing in this set to the doctrine's ideal — the biggest in its niche, riding a structural shortage, owning technology nobody can replace or in-source. CFM is the #1 commercial engine franchise, the LEAP-1B is the exclusive powerplant on every Boeing 737 MAX ever built or ordered (100%, no alternative), and the LEAP installed base is set to triple by 2030, locking in a 20–30-year high-margin services annuity. The shortage is on both sides of the business: airframes are sold out through the end of the decade, rival Pratt & Whitney's GTF crisis has ~650 A320neo-family aircraft grounded at peak times, and GE's own spare-parts delinquency rose again in Q3 despite 10 straight quarters of material-input growth — so strong that GE is buying its casting bottleneck outright (CPP, $11.75B). Q3 was a beat and the second consecutive guidance raise (adjusted EPS guide $8.00–$8.10, FCF $9.1–$9.4B), and the backlog converts over decades, not quarters.
- Key shortage/backlog evidence: Total backlog >$170B (RPO $180.4B at Sep 30, 2026, including $170.2B services, up 10% from end-2025); Q3 orders $19.0B, +49% YoY; GE enters Q4 with >95% of spare parts in backlog and >100% of Q4 internal shop-visit engines already in the shop (Q3 2026 call, Oct 20, 2026).
- Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk: low — FAA/EASA certification locks an engine to a specific airframe, re-certifying a new engine takes a decade and billions, airlines do not build engines and Boeing/Airbus do not build engines. The only live threat is a Pratt & Whitney GTF durability fix restoring Airbus's ramp, which would stop CFM's share gains but not reverse orders already won; Rolls-Royce holds widebody (A350) where GE has no engine.
- 12-18 month return scenario: base — backlog converts steadily, spare-parts delinquency slowly clears, services grow low-double-digit as guided into 2027; roughly +15–25% as the $378.43 analyst target (+14.0% from $331.81) plus services compounding implies. Bull — GE9X certifies by end-2026, CPP closes cleanly, spare-parts queue clears faster than expected and the market re-rates the services annuity; +35–45%.
- Top risk: The shortage is partly GE's own failure to deliver — spare-parts delinquency caps the highest-margin revenue line (spare parts ~40% of services revenue) and the CPP fix does not close until H2 2027; plus GE9X certification risk and a ~35.5x forward P/E that already assumes the backlog converts.
- Conviction (1-10): 8

### Top 3 detail

**1. GE.** The single most compelling data point is the combination of a >$170B backlog (RPO $180.4B at Sep 30, 2026) with GE's own spare-parts queue so long it is spending $11.75B to buy Consolidated Precision Products, the casting supplier behind its airfoil bottleneck — a company buying the constraint that caps its own deliveries. Add the LEAP-1B's 100% exclusivity on the 737 MAX and a grounded rival fleet (~650 A320neos at peak, shop visits stretched from a targeted 60 days to 300–360 days), and the moat is certification plus installed-base annuity, not a marketing claim. Base case 12–18mo: +15–25% as services grow low-double-digit and the $9.1–$9.4B FCF guide is delivered. Bull case: +35–45% if GE9X certifies by end-2026 and the spare-parts delinquency clears faster than the market expects.

**2. CF.** The cleanest shortage in the set: 97% ammonia utilisation in the first nine months of 2026, a sold-out fill-programme order book extending into Q1 2027, ~9 Mt of urea supply removed by Middle East hostilities (exports ~800,000 t/month vs a normal 1.5–1.7 Mt/month), and >35% of European ammonia capacity curtailed — with CF holding ~39% of US ammonia capacity and the lowest-cost North American gas ($3.31/MMBtu realised in 2025). Nitrogen is biologically irreplaceable and no customer can build a world-scale ammonia plant. Base case 12–18mo: +15–25% if the disruption lasts into 2027 as management guides ("constructive into 2027"). Bull case: +40–50% if Middle East exports stay disrupted and India tenders return, pushing urea back toward its 12-month highs.

**3. PH.** The most tangible backlog after GE: a record $13.7B at Sep 30, 2026 (up from $12.5B at Mar 31), with rolling 12-month order rates +13% overall and aerospace +14%, on flight-critical components certified to specific airframes under FAA/EASA — re-qualifying an alternative takes years and the airframer bears the risk, designed in for the 20–30-year life of a programme. Aerospace adjusted margin hit a record 30.7% in Q1 FY27 and FY27 guidance was raised (adjusted EPS $36.45–$37.25). Base case 12–18mo: +15–20% as the backlog converts (~75% typically within 12 months) toward the $1,137.60 analyst target (+19.0%). Bull case: +30–40% if Boeing/Airbus production rates break higher and the Filtration Group/CIRCOR integrations land cleanly.

Left out of my top 10: NEM — a price-taking gold miner (irreplaceability 4/10); gold is irreplaceable, the company is not, and spot gold has broken below $4,000 to a 13-month low (~$3,885, −31% from January's record), compressing the Q4 margin as AISC is guided to ~$1,900/oz.
