## SINGLE-PICK BALLOT

- **Top pick:** MU
- **Runner-up:** NVDA
- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** Micron is the most concrete pre-sold capacity story in the dossier: all 2026 HBM supply is sold out, management says it can fill only half to two-thirds of customer demand with no line of sight on supply catching up and no meaningful new fab output until FY2028, and 16 strategic agreements carry ~$100B of minimum contracted revenue with ~$22B already collected as upfront customer deposits — capacity literally sold before it exists. The HBM "die penalty" (~3x the wafer area of equal DDR5) structurally drains ordinary DRAM supply, which is why Gartner/Counterpoint/TrendForce and even SK Hynix (past 2030) all put the shortage's end in 2028 or beyond. Only three firms on earth can make leading-edge DRAM and none of the buyers can in-source memory, so this backlog is both the longest visibility and the least disintermediable. With FQ4 earnings in 5 days guided at ~$50B revenue and ~86% gross margin, the next print is the immediate referendum on repricing it from cyclical to utility.
- **Key shortage/backlog evidence:** CEO Mehrotra, FQ3 2026 (Jun 24, 2026): all 2026 HBM supply sold out; Micron can fill only half to two-thirds of customer demand, with "no line of sight on when supply catches up" and no meaningful new fab output until FY2028 — corroborated by ~$22B of upfront customer deposits already collected against ~$100B of contracted revenue.
- **Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk:** LOW — one of only three scaled global DRAM producers (Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron); 2-year $10B+ fab build cycles; memory is the single input hyperscalers cannot build themselves, so the oligopoly is the moat. Watch-items: China's CXMT DRAM share rose to ~9.5% in Q2 2026 and the memory flywheel (tight → capex → glut) has never been switched off.
- **12-18 month return scenario:** Base +30–50% (shortage persists through 2028, FQ4/FY2027 beats carry the stock toward the $1,500–1,600 analyst PTs). Bull +70–100% if HBM/NAND pricing holds at super-cycle levels through 2027 and FY2027 EPS prints far above current estimates.
- **Top risk:** The classic memory bust — $25B+ annual capex is planting the 2027–28 oversupply; stock is +589% in 12 months and already embeds peak assumptions; DeepSeek-style lower-memory AI models could shorten demand duration.
- **Conviction (1-10):** 8

### Top-3 deep dives

**1. MU:** The single most compelling data point is the combination of physical sold-out (only ½–⅔ of HBM demand fillable, "no line of sight" on catch-up, no new fab output until FY2028) with cash in hand ($22B of upfront deposits against $100B contracted) — customers are prepaying for capacity that does not exist yet, which is the purest revenue-visibility signal a semiconductor can give. In a 3-firm oligopoly with 2-year fab build cycles, that contract structure converts the AI memory squeeze into multi-year revenue that the cycle cannot easily reprice away. 12–18mo: base +30–50% toward $1,500–1,600 PTs on FQ4/FY2027 beats; bull +70–100% if super-cycle pricing persists into 2028.

**2. NVDA:** Colette Kress confirmed ≈$500B of Blackwell + Rubin revenue visibility through FY2027, cloud GPUs are "completely sold out for the foreseeable future," and FQ3 guide of $108B excludes China entirely — sovereign AI and neoclouds are filling the hole, which widens the demand base beyond the big-four hyperscalers. That is the largest absolute order pipeline in the dossier, and Vera Rubin doubled TAM per gigawatt from $18–25B to ~$40B. 12–18mo: base +20–35% as the pipeline converts; bull +60–80% if Jensen's "chip sales could double next year" (Scotland, Sept 2026) plays out. The disintermediation tax: Google TPU v7, AWS Trainium 3, Meta MTIA, and Microsoft Maia are the most serious CUDA attack ever, so this pipeline is secure but not permanent.

**3. GOOGL:** The $514B Cloud backlog grew $50B in a single quarter to ~4x the annual Cloud run-rate, ~50% expected to convert within 24 months, with Pichai flatly stating "we are compute constrained in the near-term" — a signed backlog larger than its own build capacity, funded by a $195–205B/yr capex program. Vertical integration (custom TPU, Gemini, private fiber) gives Google a ~10% cost edge and makes the shortage self-correcting: it cannot be in-sourced against. 12–18mo: base +15–25% as capacity catches up and backlog converts; bull +40%+ if TPU Ironwood pricing/share gains compound. The bear case is the capex bill (negative FCF quarter, "scary" $270–280B 2027 capex modeled by Truist), not demand.
