No customer can build its own hyperscale cloud (hundreds of billions in capex); the real in-sourcing vector here is Google's own TPU stack, which is a cost advantage, not a threat — hyperscalers are the in-sourcers and Google is one of them. Realistic competition is oligopoly share-shift to AWS/Azure, not displacement; multi-cloud spreads spend but does not bypass the toll booth.

If utilization lags the build, margins compress and the backlog narrative collapses into overbuild.

### Top-3 write-ups (most compelling shortage/moat data point + 12–18mo scenario)

**1. GOOGL.** The single most compelling data point is the $514B Cloud backlog: it grew $50B in one quarter — backlog expanding at roughly 2x the pace of revenue (+82% Cloud growth), with management stating Cloud revenue would have been higher "if we were able to meet that demand." That is a signed, quantified, physical capacity shortage, and the stock is down on the capex needed to serve it — the classic setup where the fear is priced and the asset isn't. 12–18mo: base ~$430 (+27%) on backlog conversion; bull ~$510 (+50%) if Gemini 4 ships before year-end and FCF turns positive.

**2. CF.** The most compelling point is physical: CF ran its available ammonia network at ~98% utilization in 1H 2026 while global nitrogen supply-demand is "tight before geopolitical disruptions are considered," with the Iran conflict knocking out an estimated 4–4.5M tons of urea and capacity additions lagging demand growth for the next four years. **3. MU.** The most compelling point is the ~$100B of contracted revenue across 16 strategic customer agreements, with ~$22B already collected as upfront customer deposits — take-or-pay floors that are the only mechanism in memory history that can break the tight→capex→glut flywheel. But my lens demands honesty: this is +589% in 12 months, $25B+ of annual capex is planting the 2027–28 oversupply, and FQ4 earnings land in 5 days — the immediate referendum. 12–18mo: base ~$1,350 (+25%) if HBM4 ramp and deposits validate duration (toward UBS/Cantor $1,500–1,600); bull ~$1,600+ (+50%) if the contracted backlog proves the cycle is structurally muted; bear case is a classic memory bust if supply catches up.
