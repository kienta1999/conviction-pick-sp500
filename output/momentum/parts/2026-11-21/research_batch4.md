## VEEV — Veeva Systems (life-sciences industry cloud software)

### 1. Shortage thesis
Weak-to-moderate as a *shortage* story; strong as a *forced-migration / demand-surge* story. There is no physical product sold out or capacity-constrained. The demand driver is structural and regulatory, not cyclical: every large pharma running legacy Veeva CRM on Salesforce must decide by 2029–2030 whether to migrate to Vault CRM or switch platforms. Veeva's Salesforce agreement expired Sept 2025; legacy CRM is in "stability mode" (security patches, no new features) with support ending Dec 2029 (moved from Sept 2030 in March 2026). Migrations are concentrated 2026–2029 and a global pharma CRM migration typically takes 18–36 months, so the decision window is now. A second driver is industry-specific AI: at its Nov 5, 2026 Investor Day, CEO Peter Gassner raised Veeva's TAM to $30bn from $20bn a year earlier, "largely because of opportunities in industry-specific AI." No evidence of sold-out capacity, extending lead times, or price increases was found — this is software, priced per subscription, not a scarce physical good.

### 2. Backlog / order book
No RPO/backlog dollar figure found in sources checked. Closest proxy: deferred revenue of $1,476.5m at April 30, 2026 (Form 10-Q for the quarter ended April 30, 2026). Veeva reports annual billings as the better indicator (per Q2 FY26 call commentary, annual billings were raised by $35m), but no current RPO total was retrieved. Subscription revenue is highly recurring: FY27 subscription guidance ~$3.08bn of ~$3.685bn total. Vault CRM momentum is the forward indicator instead of a backlog: ~140 customers live on Vault CRM as of the Q4 FY26 call (Mar 4, 2026); 13 of the top 20 pharma companies committed to Vault CRM vs 6 for Salesforce (Truist, via Aug 27, 2026 coverage); new global wins at Biogen and Regeneron in Q2 FY27.

### 3. Category position
#1 in life-sciences CRM by a wide margin, though the monopoly is now contested. Multiple secondary sources put Veeva at ~80%+ share of life-sciences CRM (e.g. Substack/Right Click Research, 2026; AInvest, Sept 2025). One analyst dataset (cited by Diginomica) puts Veeva at 26.8% of the broader pharma CRM market vs Salesforce 16.4%, Oracle 11.2% — definitions differ, so treat the 80% figure as the narrow life-sciences-CRM definition. In Vault (R&D/quality content & data), Veeva is also the leader. The contest: Salesforce Life Sciences Cloud (co-developed with IQVIA) had 140+ organisations selected by mid-2026 including AstraZeneca, Novartis, Pfizer, Takeda, Moderna, AbbVie (AInvest, Aug 2026) — the market has moved "from monopoly to contest."

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk
Customers cannot realistically in-source this. The moat is validation + regulatory workflow lock-in, not raw technology: Veeva's software is the system of record for validated, GxP-regulated processes (clinical, regulatory, safety, quality, commercial compliance, sample accountability, adverse-event reporting). Rebuilding it in-house would require re-validating every workflow with regulators, retraining global field forces, and re-doing 18–36-month migrations — for a non-core capability. Switching costs are high once live, and the platform spans the whole product lifecycle (Development, Quality, Commercial, Data clouds). The real threat is not in-sourcing but *switching to Salesforce*: the forced migration gives every customer a once-in-15-years re-evaluation point, and Salesforce + IQVIA is a credible, well-funded alternative. AI is a second-order risk/opportunity: if generic AI agents commoditise the workflow layer, seat-based pricing could erode — Veeva's answer is Falcon agentic labour and Vault AI, but neither has booked revenue yet (see §5).
**Irreplaceability score: 8/10** (very hard to replicate or bypass; docked 2 points because the forced migration window gives Salesforce a genuine opening).

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months, i.e. ~Aug 21 – Nov 21, 2026)
- **Q2 FY27 beat + raise, Aug 26, 2026:** total revenue $928m, +18% YoY (subscription $767m, +16%); non-GAAP EPS $2.35 vs $2.22 consensus; FY27 guidance raised to $3.682–$3.687bn revenue and ~$9.21 non-GAAP EPS. Stock rose ~17% on Aug 27, 2026 (Motley Fool).
- **Vault CRM wins:** Biogen committed globally to Vault CRM (announced ~Aug 2026); Regeneron also a new win in Q2 FY27; 13 of top 20 pharma now committed vs 6 for Salesforce.
- **Investor Day, Nov 5, 2026:** $6bn revenue run-rate target for calendar 2030 reaffirmed (built from existing products only — explicitly excludes Falcon/Vault AI/Aspen); TAM raised to $30bn from $20bn on industry AI; Vault AI priced at 10% of applicable Vault subscription fee; Falcon agents carry separate annual licence fees, with six- and seven-figure deals in pipeline and revenue expected next year (2027). Roche is an early Falcon adopter.
- **Nov 6, 2026 8-K:** Veeva said Q3 FY27 (quarter ended Oct 31, 2026) would meet or exceed guidance of $932–935m revenue / $417–420m non-GAAP operating income. Full results due Dec 3, 2026 (after run date).
- **Analyst reaction:** six banks raised price targets after Investor Day (The Inference, Nov 2026); Morgan Stanley kept Hold, $275 target, citing early-stage AI commercialisation (TipRanks, Nov 2026). Multiple firms raised targets after the Aug 27 beat (Oppenheimer, Piper Sandler, Guggenheim, Stifel, Barclays, Truist).

### 6. Risks
- **Salesforce contest:** the migration window is a genuine share-loss risk; Salesforce has already won 140+ selections and 6 of the top 20. A few large defections would dent the $6bn/2030 path.
- **AI monetisation unproven:** no Falcon or Vault AI revenue in any filing yet; if agents cannibalise seats faster than they add licence fees, growth could decelerate. Morgan Stanley's Hold reflects exactly this.
- **Valuation already pricing it in:** ~44x earnings per a Nov 19, 2026 third-party analysis (vs 10-yr median ~77x — so cheaper than its own history, but still a premium multiple); ~15–18% growth at that multiple leaves little room for a miss.
- **Pharma spending cyclicality:** biotech funding droughts / big-pharma cost-cutting can delay migrations and new-module purchases; services revenue is the most exposed line.
- **Execution:** the Vault CRM migration itself is the riskiest strategic move in company history (ripping out the Salesforce platform underneath the flagship product); any large-scale migration failure would be reputationally severe.
- **Concentration:** depends on the top-20 pharma cohort; losing even 2–3 of them to Salesforce matters.

### 7. Shortage score & verdict
**Shortage score: 4/10. Verdict:** Not a shortage play at all — a high-quality, #1-franchise compounder with a forced-migration tailwind and an unproven AI option, growing mid-teens at a premium multiple; explosive upside requires Falcon to monetise, which has zero booked revenue so far.

## IDXX — IDEXX Laboratories (veterinary diagnostics)

### 1. Shortage thesis
No structural shortage. The driver is a *secular utilisation* story, not supply-constrained demand: pet owners test more per visit, pets age, and diagnostics move earlier in the care pathway. There is no sold-out capacity, no extending lead times, no price-increase-driven scarcity — in fact IDEXX takes ~4% annual net price realisation as a matter of course (2026 guidance: ~4% global net price, 3.5% in the US). The nearest thing to a "surge supply cannot meet" is instrument demand: inVue Dx placements are running at a record pace (see §2/§5), but IDEXX manufactures and places these itself — placement pace is a go-to-market success, not a capacity constraint. The demand backdrop is actually *adverse* on volume: US same-store clinical visits fell ~1.8% in Q3 2026 (worse than the ~1.5% assumed) and ~1.3% in Q2 2026, with wellness visits down 3.4% in Q2 — growth is coming from more testing per visit and price, not more visits.

### 2. Backlog / order book
No order backlog is reported — IDEXX is a recurring-revenue razor-and-blades model, not an order-book business. The relevant forward indicators: (a) premium instrument installed base, which expanded 12% in 2025 and ~11% YoY in Q3 2026; (b) CAG Diagnostics recurring revenue, $967m in Q3 2026, +11% organic, ~80% of total revenue; (c) customer retention in the high-90% range globally in CAG diagnostics. Instrument placements are the backlog proxy: 1,904 inVue Dx analyzers placed in Q3 2026 (record pace), installed base ~11,000; full-year 2026 goal ~5,500 placements, which management said it will exceed, as well as the five-year goal of 20,000 placements. Total premium placements in Q3 2026 were 5,780, including ~1,200 competitive/greenfield Catalyst placements. No "booked out until" date applies.

### 3. Category position
Clear #1 in veterinary diagnostics. Estimates vary by market definition: Global Market Insights puts IDEXX at >30% share in 2025 (top 5 players = 70%); Verified Market Research puts it at 31.4% global share in 2026; Douglas Insights estimates ~45% of pet diagnostic-test value, with the top three (IDEXX, Antech/Mars, Zoetis) holding ~75%. #2 is Antech Diagnostics (Mars, private — no disclosed revenue; ~22.8% of North American lab volume per VMR) and #3 is Zoetis (diagnostics revenue $434m in 2025 per its 10-K, vs IDEXX CAG diagnostics recurring revenue $3,407m in 2025). IDEXX is roughly 8x Zoetis in diagnostics revenue. In reference labs and in-clinic analysers specifically, its lead is wider than the blended share suggests.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk
Customers (vet clinics) cannot realistically in-source this. Moats: (i) closed-cartridge analyser ecosystem — Catalyst/inVue Dx consumables only work on IDEXX instruments, and instruments are placed largely in exchange for multi-year consumables commitments; (ii) reference-lab network scale and turnaround; (iii) proprietary data/AI corpus — inVue Dx cell-morphology AI is trained on IDEXX's own pathologist-labelled image corpus, which a rival would need years to replicate; (iv) practice-management software integration (Cornerstone, ezyVet, Neo, Vetspire) plus VetConnect PLUS ties diagnostics into clinic workflow. Substitution threats are real but partial: Zoetis bundles diagnostics with its pharma portfolio (Vetscan Imagyst AI imaging) and Antech/Mars bundles reference labs with Heska point-of-care instruments; generic AI scribes/imaging could nibble at the edges. But no customer can build a reference-lab network, analyser menu, and validated assay library itself. The moat is commercial/technical, not regulatory certification in the human-medtech sense.
**Irreplaceability score: 8/10** (razor-and-blades lock-in + proprietary AI data corpus; docked for credible Zoetis/Antech bundling competition).

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- **Q3 2026 beat + raise, Nov 2, 2026:** revenue $1,214m, +10% reported and organic (beat ~$1,191m consensus); CAG Diagnostics recurring revenue $967m, +11% organic (US +9.6%, international +14%); EPS $4.01, +18% reported / +22% comparable (vs ~$3.72 consensus); operating margin 33.1%, +100bps reported. FY26 guidance raised: revenue $4,741–4,767m (from $4,700–4,745m), EPS $14.96–15.16 (from $14.69–14.94, +$0.25 at midpoint). Stock +4.5% on the day (Motley Fool).
- **Record inVue Dx placements:** 1,904 in Q3 2026, above the ~1,400/quarter needed for the 5,500 full-year goal; VetLab consumables $443m, +14.6% organic — growing faster than the ~11% installed-base growth, i.e. more testing per instrument.
- **CoVet acquisition, Sept 16, 2026:** acquired CoVetAI, AI-native ambient listening / clinical workflow software for vet practices (terms not disclosed; ~$20m annualised revenue run rate, growing strong double-digits; ~$5m added to 2026 revenue outlook; ~$0.13/share dilution and ~30bps margin drag in 2026). Folds into IDEXX software ecosystem / VetConnect PLUS; IDEXX will keep supporting third-party scribes. Consolidation context: ScribbleVet was acquired by Instinct Science in Jan 2026.
- **Innovation:** inVue Dx with FNA and IDEXX Cancer Dx becoming a multi-cancer panel (CEO Mike Erickson, Q3 call); ImageVue DR50 Plus driving imaging installations (+11% software/imaging revenue in Q3).
- **Q2 2026 (Aug 4, 2026):** revenue $1,217m, +9% organic; CAG recurring +10.3% organic despite visits −1.3%.

### 6. Risks
- **Visit-volume headwind is the core risk:** US same-store visits have fallen for multiple quarters (wellness −3.4% in Q2 2026); if the decline deepens (consumer pressure from inflation/housing costs), price + utilisation may not offset it forever.
- **Price-driven growth limits:** ~4% annual price realisation is doing heavy lifting; clinics/pet owners may push back, and rapid-assay revenue already fell 3% organically in Q4 2025 on wellness pressure and mix shift.
- **Competition/bundling:** Zoetis (pharma bundling + Imagyst AI) and Mars/Antech+Heska (lab network + point-of-care) can attack on price/bundle; IDEXX's premium pricing is the vulnerability.
- **Instrument-revenue lumpiness:** capital instrument revenue fell 20% organically in Q2 2026 as IDEXX lapped the inVue Dx launch; the placement boom will normalise.
- **Margin:** CoVet dilutes 2026 margins (~30bps) and EPS (~$0.13); stepped-up H2 2026 R&D/commercial investment means margin expansion in 2027 is "unproven" (Drillr, Nov 2026).
- **Valuation:** a premium compounder multiple on ~10% growth; not a cheap stock, and growth is steady rather than explosive — the opposite of an explosive-return setup if visits keep falling.

### 7. Shortage score & verdict
**Shortage score: 3/10. Verdict:** A superb #1 razor-and-blades franchise executing brilliantly against a falling visit backdrop (11% recurring growth with visits −1.8%), but there is no shortage, no backlog, and no explosive catalyst — a steady compounder, not a momentum explosion candidate.

## EW — Edwards Lifesciences (structural heart / TAVR)

### 1. Shortage thesis
Moderate — the closest thing to a genuine demand-supply imbalance in this batch, but it is *patient-pool expansion*, not a supply shortage. The core product (SAPIEN TAVR valves) treats aortic stenosis, a disease of ageing; demand is driven by (a) demographic growth, (b) guideline/indication expansion into ever-earlier disease, and (c) a large untreated pool. The Nov 2, 2026 PROGRESS trial (TCT 2026, simultaneously in *NEJM*) showed TAVR superior to clinical surveillance in moderate AS with at-risk features: 33% relative reduction in death/HF events at 2 years (16.7% vs 23.9%), HF hospitalisations halved (7.4% vs 14.9%), with no mortality/disabling stroke at 30 days — and 57% of surveillance patients needed a valve within 2 years anyway. That opens a large new patient population (moderate AS) that is currently only watched. No evidence of valves being sold out, lead times extending, or price increases — Edwards manufactures to demand and ASPs were described as "stable" on the Q3 2026 call. The "shortage" is untreated patients, not Edwards' ability to supply. TMTT (mitral/tricuspid) is the faster-growing adjacent pool: $207m in Q3 2026, +44–46%, on its way to a $2bn-by-2030 target.

### 2. Backlog / order book
No backlog/order-book figure is reported — valves are implanted from hospital inventory as procedures are scheduled; there is no multi-quarter order book to quote. Forward indicators instead: TAVR sales $1.24bn in Q3 2026 (+8.8% constant currency); FY26 TAVR guidance raised to 9–9.5% growth; TMTT guidance $780–790m for 2026; and the indication pipeline (moderate AS via PROGRESS, asymptomatic AS via EARLY TAVR, US guideline updates and updated TAVR National Coverage Determination both expected Q4 2026 per the Dec 2025 investor conference). **Backlog figure: not found (not a backlog business).**

### 3. Category position
Dominant #1 in TAVR. GlobalData: Edwards ~66% of the global TAVR market in 2025 vs Medtronic ~28%, Abbott ~2.9%; US TAVR share 65.7% in 2025 vs Medtronic 25.8%, Abbott 5.4%. Global Market Insights: Edwards >55% in 2025 (top 5 = 80%). TAVR market ~$6.6–7.0bn in 2025, projected $13.8–16.5bn by 2035 (~7–10% CAGR). In TMTT (mitral/tricuspid) Edwards trails Abbott — Abbott leads TMTT via MitraClip — but Edwards is closing fast with PASCAL (repair) + EVOQUE (tricuspid replacement) + SAPIEN M3 (mitral replacement). Surgical valves are a smaller, more fragmented business where Medtronic is strong.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk
Hospitals cannot in-source a transcatheter heart valve. Moats: (i) PMA/FDA approval + randomised-trial evidence base — SAPIEN has the broadest RCT record in the field (PARTNER, EARLY TAVR, PROGRESS), and guidelines/coverage follow the evidence; (ii) RESILIA tissue technology (anti-calcification) and 70+ country physician-training footprint; (iii) manufacturing/quality scale for a Class III implantable — only a handful of firms on earth can make a transcatheter valve at scale (Edwards, Medtronic, Abbott, Boston Scientific, Meril/JenaValve at smaller scale); (iv) heart-team workflow lock-in — implanting centres are trained, proctored, and credentialed per platform, and switching platforms means retraining the whole team. Substitution: surgical AVR remains an alternative for some patients (especially younger/low-risk), and Medtronic's self-expanding Evolut is a genuine head-to-head competitor with trial wins of its own; Boston Scientific's ACURATE and Abbott's Navitor are credible #3/#4 entrants. But there is no technology that "routes around" a valve replacement for severe AS, and no customer (hospital system) can build its own valve.
**Irreplaceability score: 9/10** (Class III device + evidence/guideline lock-in + only ~4 credible makers on earth; docked 1 point for real Medtronic head-to-head competition).

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- **Q3 2026 beat + raise, Nov 4, 2026:** sales $1.72bn, +10.5% reported / +11.2% constant currency (beat consensus); TAVR $1.24bn, +8.0% reported / +8.8% cc; TMTT $207m, +44.0% / +45.6% cc (PASCAL + EVOQUE + SAPIEN M3); adjusted EPS $0.80, +19% YoY, beating $0.73 consensus; gross margin 78.5% (from 77.8%). FY26 guidance raised: total sales ~11.5% (from 10–11%), TAVR 9–9.5% (from 8–9%), TMTT $780–790m (from $760–780m), adjusted EPS $2.97–3.05. Q4 guide: sales $1.67–1.75bn, adjusted EPS $0.61–0.69. Cash ~$3.10bn at Sept 30, 2026.
- **PROGRESS trial, Nov 2, 2026 (TCT 2026 + NEJM):** TAVR superior to surveillance in moderate AS with at-risk features (see §1) — a potential indication expansion into a much larger patient pool; use in moderate AS is still investigational, so revenue impact depends on future guideline/coverage decisions.
- **SAPIEN M3 FDA approval (Dec 2025, BusinessWire release 20251223):** first FDA-approved transseptal transcatheter mitral valve replacement, for symptomatic moderate-to-severe/severe MR in patients unsuitable for surgery or TEER; ENCIRCLE 1-yr data showed 95.7% elimination of significant MR. 2-yr ENCIRCLE data presented at TCT 2026 (Nov 2026): 97% of patients MR ≤1+ at 2 years; death/HF hospitalisation 35.6% at 2 years vs 45% 1-yr performance goal.
- **Guideline/coverage tailwinds pending:** US TAVR guideline updates and updated National Coverage Determination both expected Q4 2026 (per Dec 2025 investor conference); Q3 call noted updated US coverage policy would expand coverage to asymptomatic patients and give heart teams more flexibility.
- **Other:** PASCAL Precision FDA approval for tricuspid repair expected Q4 2026 (per Dec 2025 investor conference); AUTUS size-adjustable surgical pulmonary valve FDA-approved (paediatric, announced 2026); investor event at TCT, Nov 2, 2026; annual investor conference Dec 4, 2026 (after run date — not a catalyst yet).

### 6. Risks
- **TAVR growth is decelerating to high-single-digits:** 9–9.5% guided for 2026 vs the double-digit era; the core market is maturing in the US/EU and share gains have flattened (competitive position "stable sequentially" in the US in Q3 2026; the benefit from a competitor's exit outside the US has fully annualised).
- **Medtronic competition:** Evolut platform wins (e.g. positive Evolut trial data per GlobalData) and aggressive pricing; Medtronic ~28% and not standing still. Boston Scientific/ACURATE and Abbott/Navitor add pressure at the margin.
- **Moderate-AS expansion is not yet revenue:** PROGRESS is a paradigm shift clinically, but use in moderate AS is investigational; guidelines, coverage, and heart-team adoption will take years, and all-cause mortality at 2 years was similar between arms (8.4% TAVR vs 7.3% surveillance, per MedTech Dive) — payers will scrutinise cost-effectiveness.
- **TMTT execution:** TMTT is growing 44%+ but from a small base ($207m/qtr); scaling PASCAL/EVOQUE/M3 to $2bn by 2030 requires flawless launch execution and reimbursement wins.
- **Margins/earnings quality:** GAAP EPS was only $0.53 in Q3 2026 (vs $0.80 adjusted) on one-off costs; operating margin fell to 17.4% from 19.8% YoY in Q3 2026 (per StockRow) even as gross margin rose — strategic-investment phasing and legal/impairment charges recur.
- **Procedure-volume sensitivity:** TAVR depends on hospital capacity, referral pathways, and screening; any slowdown in procedure growth (or a shift toward surgery in younger patients given durability questions) hits the core.
- **Valuation:** the stock has historically traded at a premium for the TAVR growth story; at high-single-digit core growth, a premium multiple needs TMTT + moderate-AS optionality to be believed.

### 7. Shortage score & verdict
**Shortage score: 5/10. Verdict:** The strongest "shortage-adjacent" name in the batch — a dominant #1 (66% TAVR share) with a genuine new patient pool opening via PROGRESS and a 44%-growing TMTT franchise — but core TAVR growth is high-single-digits, there is no supply shortage or backlog, and the explosive case rests on moderate-AS coverage decisions that are years away, not a near-term squeeze.
