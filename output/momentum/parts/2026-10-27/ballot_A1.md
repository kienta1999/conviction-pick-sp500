# Panelist A — Supply-chain analyst ballot — 2026-10-27

## 1) Single-pick ballot

- Top pick: MU
- Runner-up: SNDK
- Thesis (3-5 sentences): Micron has converted the HBM shortage into the longest-dated contracted visibility in the dossier: its entire 2026 HBM output is sold out, ~75% of calendar-2027 capacity is already booked, and signed Strategic Customer Agreements run through 2030. Unlike a spot-priced commodity spike, that book is at minimum pricing with customers prepaying cash, so revenue visibility extends well beyond the current cycle print. Crucially, the custom-ASIC trend that threatens GPU vendors does not remove Micron — a home-grown accelerator still needs HBM from one of only three makers. With rev growth TTM of 154.5%, an 80.7% operating margin on the screen, and analyst upside of +71.2% to $1,561.91, it offers explosive torque with a contract book underneath it.
- Key shortage/backlog evidence: ~$150B of remaining performance obligations across 26 Strategic Customer Agreements (up from 16 in one quarter), backed by ~$32B in customer financial commitments, mostly cash deposits — while Micron can currently satisfy only 50–66% of key customers' HBM demand.
- Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk: low — hyperscalers cannot fabricate HBM (advanced stacking, TSVs and multi-quarter qualification limit supply to Micron, SK hynix and Samsung), and in-sourcing an accelerator via a custom ASIC still requires buying HBM from that closed three-firm oligopoly; the real risk is share loss to SK hynix/Samsung at each generation, not customers bypassing Micron altogether. Irreplaceability 7/10 in the dossier.
- 12-18 month return scenario: base +30–45% as 2027 contracted volumes ship at the significantly higher prices Micron says are already contracted for most of calendar-2027 HBM; bull ~+70% toward the $1,561.91 analyst target if supply stays tight into 2028 and fiscal Q1 2027 guidance ($61.5B revenue) is beaten/raised.
- Top risk: Memory cyclicality — all major makers are adding capacity simultaneously, so oversupply landing in 2028+ could collapse earnings as in prior cycles (historically -70–90% from peak), and the SCA floor pricing/deposits have never been stress-tested through a capex downturn.
- Conviction (1-10): 9

### Top 3 — shortage/backlog detail and scenarios

**1. MU:** The single most compelling point is the combination of physical rationing and cash backing — only 50–66% of key customers' HBM demand can be met, 2026 HBM is entirely committed, ~75% of 2027 capacity is booked and ~35% is booked through 2030, yet customers still posted ~$32B in commitments against a ~$150B minimum-price RPO. That is multi-year visibility no other name here matches in both duration and enforceability. 12-18mo scenario: base +30–45%; bull ~+70% toward the $1,561.91 analyst target.

**2. SNDK:** SanDisk turned a 4–5% industry NAND supply deficit, with virtually no new wafer capacity in 2026, into 10 New Business Model agreements totalling a minimum $93.9B of contracted revenue at floor pricing, backed by $16.5B in enforceable guarantees, covering over half of expected FY2027 bits and about two-thirds of FY2028 bits over an average duration exceeding four years. The discount to MU is moat, not visibility: at #5 in NAND with 11.4% share and irreplaceability of only 4/10, customers can multi-source equivalent bits once supply loosens in H2 2027. 12-18mo scenario: base +25–40% on scarcity pricing and the contracted book; bull ~+70% toward the $2,173.00 analyst target if December-quarter contract prices rise the expected 15–20% and tightness persists.

**3. LLY:** Lilly has no formal backlog, but its capacity behaviour is the evidence — >$50B committed to U.S. manufacturing since 2020, including the $6.5B Houston API plant broken ground in September 2026, while Q2 volume grew +60% and Mounjaro plus Zepbound reached ~$14.9B in one quarter. With ~61% U.S. GLP-1 share, a Medicare Bridge expansion adding 35% more covered U.S. obesity patients, and FY2026 guidance already raised twice to $85–87B, demand is demonstrably outrunning installed supply for years, not quarters. 12-18mo scenario: base +15–25% on volume growth and a further guidance raise; bull +35–45% if the 2026-10-29 print delivers a third raise and Foundayo's oral rollout (expected ~$261M in Q3, ~$553M in Q4) scales faster than peptide capacity constraints imply.

Deliberately left out of top 10: CF, FANG, META, VRTX, ISRG — CF and FANG are real Hormuz-driven shortages (shortage 8/10 each) but spot-price rents with no order book or contracted backlog and a printed 2027 normalisation path; META reports no RPO and is the buyer/in-sourcer creating the shortage rather than a supplier selling into it; VRTX (shortage 3/10) is a CF monopoly with no scarcity; ISRG (shortage 2/10) just cut instrument pricing 3–5% and has no supply constraint or near-term catalyst.
