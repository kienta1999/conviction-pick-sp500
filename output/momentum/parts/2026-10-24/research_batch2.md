## LLY — Eli Lilly

**1. Shortage thesis — GLP-1 / incretin demand surge, formerly supply-capped, now volume-driven**
- Driver is structural, not cyclical: diabetes + obesity prevalence plus expanding indications/coverage. Mounjaro + Zepbound did $27.7B in H1 2026, up ~88% YoY, ~65% of total revenue (Zacks, 2026-10-19, citing H1 results); in Q2 alone $9.94B Mounjaro (+91%) + $4.93B Zepbound (+46%).
- The 2024–25 shortage was real and revenue-capping: "demand exists but we can't make it" — the bottleneck was sterile fill-finish for pre-filled pens, which takes 3–5 years and billions to build (industry analysis, 2026). Lilly has committed >$50B to US manufacturing since 2020 / ~$55B total overhaul, incl. $27B for four new US sites announced Feb 2025.
- Important change: at JPM Healthcare Jan 2026 Lilly said it had scaled production and effectively ended the chronic Mounjaro/Zepbound shortages. So the shortage phase is resolving — growth is now demand/coverage-led, not sold-out-led. New demand step-up: Medicare GLP-1 Bridge programme effective 1 July 2026, cited as expanding access to ~20M eligible beneficiaries.
- Pricing is the offset: Q2 2026 volume +60% with realised price -13% (ex-US price -36% on China NRDL listing for Mounjaro); FY26 guidance assumes low-to-mid-teens price decline more than offset by volume.

**2. Backlog / order book**
- Not found / not applicable — pharma does not disclose an order book. Best proxies: (i) FY26 revenue guidance raised 2026-08-05 to $85–87B (from $82–85B), adj. EPS guidance $35.50–36.50; (ii) prescription share still rising (see §3); (iii) multi-year capacity already committed/sold forward into plants in Indiana, Germany, Ireland, Netherlands, Texas ($6.5B Houston site, operational ~2030, for Foundayo/small molecules), plus a $3B decade-long China commitment announced Mar 2026 for orforglipron.

**3. Category position — clear #1**
- #1 in incretins and pulling away from Novo Nordisk (#2). US incretin-analog share by prescriptions 60.9% as of June 2026; Zepbound ~60% of US branded obesity prescriptions at end-Q2; international share by sales 54.9% in Apr–May 2026 (Lilly Q2 reporting via Zacks/Old Mutual, Aug 2026). Foundayo (oral orforglipron, launched Apr 2026) already ~1/3 of new patients starting an oral GLP-1 per CEO David Ricks (CNBC, cited Sep/Oct 2026).

**4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation — 8/10**
- Moat: (a) Patent — tirzepatide core US protection to ~2036 (vs semaglutide loosening ~2031–33, and already expiring in China/India in 2026); (b) Manufacturing — peptide + fill-finish capacity is the scarce asset on earth, and Lilly is furthest ahead after >$50B capex; customers/payers cannot in-source a patented drug; (c) Pipeline succession — Foundayo oral, retatrutide (triple agonist, Phase 3 TRIUMPH-2: up to 20.8% weight loss at 80 weeks, filing planned Q1 2027), eloraTZP combo (17% in 16 weeks Phase 1).
- Concrete bypass threat: molecule layer is commoditising — Novo, Amgen, Viking, Boehringer and ~39 GLP-1 drugs from 34 companies (many Chinese) in development; compounded semaglutide and, from 2026, generic semaglutide in India/Canada anchor prices lower even though they do not touch tirzepatide's US patent.

**5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)**

- Medicare GLP-1 Bridge live 1 July 2026. Next catalyst is imminent: Q3 print 2026-10-29.

**6. Risks**

Pricing: MFN/government deal, PBM rebates, China NRDL cuts — realised prices already falling low-to-mid teens; volume must keep compounding. Valuation: fwd P/E 27.7 with only +1.9% analyst upside on screen — perfection priced; any Q3/guide slip reprices hard. Competition crowding 2027–29, safety/GI headlines, and execution on an unprecedented plant build-out.

## NEM — Newmont

**1. Shortage thesis — gold: structural supply flatness vs sovereign demand surge**
- Driver: central-bank de-dollarisation buying + Asian investment demand hitting inelastic mine supply. Evidence: WGC-projected central-bank buying ~850t in 2026 (vs 863t in 2025); CBs bought 289t in Q2 2026 alone and ~345t in H1 on one tally, with Sep ~63t the highest month since Nov 2024 (NDB Wealth via ANI, 2026-10-21; Discovery Alert, Oct 2026). Goldman Sachs estimate cited Oct 2026: official buying has averaged 900–1,000t/yr since 2022, ~25% of annual mine production.
- Supply cannot respond: global mine production forecast +2.4% to 3,907t in 2026 (NDB/WGC); 2025 output 3,672t, +1% — "essentially flat for a decade / 15 years" (WGC/Crux, Oct 2026; WGC CB survey Jun 2026: record 45% of 74 central banks plan to *increase* reserves, 1% to reduce). S&P Global: discovery-to-production 15.2 years — a price signal today yields ounces in the 2040s.
- Price confirms rationing: NEM realised $4,317/oz in Q3 2026 vs $3,539/oz in Q3 2025; Metals Focus forecasts 2026 average price +43% to ~$4,920/oz.

**2. Backlog / order book**
- Not found / not applicable — a miner sells spot into a deep market; there is no order book. Functional equivalents: FY26 attributable gold production guidance held at ~5.26M oz (reaffirmed Q3, 2026-10-22); 2027 production guided to grow low-to-mid single digits (higher grades at Lihir/Boddington, Tanami Expansion 2 — shaft completed ahead of schedule, commercial production H1 2027 — Cerro Negro Vine Zone, Yanacocha); Red Chris block cave (275–325k oz gold + 65–85kt copper/yr post-ramp, $4.2–4.8B capex) heading to early-2027 investment decision. Reserve base cited at >125M oz (company-profile reporting, Feb 2026 — treat as approximate, not re-verified in the Q3 release).

**3. Category position — clear #1**
- World's largest gold miner by attributable production: 5.889M oz in 2025 vs Agnico Eagle 3.447M oz and Barrick 3.26M oz on each company's own reporting (Mining.com ranking, Feb 2026). Note ordering differs by source/year — IG's 2026 table puts Barrick #2 at ~4–4.5M oz — but no source disputes NEM #1, roughly 1.3–1.8x the #2 producer. Also a growing copper/silver by-product producer (Q3 2026: 23kt copper, 11M oz silver).

**4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation — 7/10**
- Moat: geology + time + capital. Nobody — customer, sovereign, or competitor — can manufacture gold or replicate a Tier-1 reserve base in less than ~15 years; NEM's portfolio (Nevada Gold Mines JV, Cadia, Lihir, Tanami, Ahafo, Peñasquito) took decades and M&A (Newcrest) to assemble. There is no substitute technology for monetary gold held by central banks — that is precisely why they are buying it.
- Discount from 10: the *product* is perfectly fungible — buyers do not need NEM specifically, any ounce clears at spot; NEM has zero pricing power (price-taker), and its moat protects volume/cost position, not price. In-sourcing is irrelevant (customers are investors/CBs, not industrial users).

**5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)**
- 2026-10-22 Q3 2026 beat — freshest in this batch: adj. EPS $2.20 vs $1.94 consensus, revenue $6.363B (+15% YoY), record adj. net income $2.309B, FCF $1.912B in Q3 / $7.261B 9M, cash $8.83B, net cash ~$3.2B, $12.8B liquidity; FY26 production guidance maintained and gold by-product AISC guidance *improved* to ~$1,520/oz (~10% below initial guide; Kitco/Zacks, 2026-10-22/23).
- Q2 (late Jul/Aug): board approved an additional $6.0B buyback authorisation; ~$1.9B returned in Q3 alone (dividends + buybacks), $0.26/qtr dividend declared Oct 2026.
- Portfolio growth inflection guided for 2027 after a flat/declining 2025–26 production profile.

**6. Risks**
- Pure gold-price beta: earnings are the gold price minus AISC — a $500/oz reversal overwhelms all operational skill; stock was -4.6% on 2026-10-23 close data despite the beat, showing sentiment fragility at ~$4,100–4,300 gold. ## CF — CF Industries

**1. Shortage thesis — nitrogen: geopolitically removed supply on top of a structurally tight, high-incentive-price market**
- Driver 1 (acute, 2026): Iran conflict / Strait of Hormuz. CF's own Q1/Q2 2026 releases: prices rose on "a tight global nitrogen supply-demand balance, which was further tightened by supply disruptions related to the conflict with Iran." CF estimates the conflict removed ~4.0–4.5M metric tons of traded urea and ~1M tons of ammonia; the Middle East is normally 25–30% of traded ammonia and 35–40% of traded urea. CF (Q2 release, 2026-08-05): supply "to remain constrained… through the end of 2026 and into 2027," with Middle East supply staying below pre-conflict levels.
- Driver 2 (structural): CF Q2 call (Aug 2026) argues higher global capital costs have "structurally raised the incentive price required for new global nitrogen capacity, lifting CF's baseline mid-cycle earnings power" — new plants are not being sanctioned at old prices, and European producers face uneconomic gas.
- Price evidence: granular urea average selling price $593/product ton in Q2 2026 vs $460 a year earlier (H1: $525 vs $426); US distributor urea asking prices +9.1% w/w to $796.25/short ton week ending 2026-10-02; DTN retail (19 Oct 2026): anhydrous ammonia ~$945/t, +22% YoY, 7 of 8 nutrients above year-ago. Counter-evidence to note honestly: CF itself said prices had fallen back toward pre-conflict levels by end-Q2 on seasonal demand, and a Sep 2026 market note attributes further easing to Hormuz-reopening hopes — the squeeze is real but partially mean-reverting.

**2. Backlog / order book**
- No dollar backlog disclosed — not found. Best concrete evidence is unusually specific for a commodity producer: Q2 2026 call — after June channel destocking, "strong participation in our UAN and ammonia fill programs in July… we built a substantial UAN order book that extends into November [2026] and expect a strong fall ammonia season." Forward production guide: ~9.5M tons ammonia in 2026 (incl. Yazoo City outage, restart H1 2027). Growth book: Blue Point low-carbon ammonia JV (CF 40% / JERA 35% / Mitsui 25%, ~$4B, >1.5M tons gross capacity, construction permitted to start Aug 2026, start-up late 2029) — the JV partners are themselves the offtake channel into Japanese power co-firing.

**3. Category position — #1 in its niche**
- World's largest producer of anhydrous ammonia and North America's largest pure-play nitrogen manufacturer (company-profile research, 2026). ~40% of North American ammonia capacity, ~41% granular urea, ~44% UAN, ~19% ammonium nitrate; ~39% of US domestic nitrogen capacity. Top-4 North American producers (CF, Nutrien, Koch, Yara) hold ~82% — Nutrien is #2 in nitrogen (larger overall only via potash/retail), Yara is the main global nitrogen rival but on high-cost European gas.

**4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation — 6/10**
- Moat is cost + location + logistics, not technology: Haber-Bosch is century-old, universally licensed technology — anyone with cheap gas *can* make ammonia. What they cannot quickly replicate: CF's realised gas cost $3.31/MMBtu in 2025 vs $12–15/MMBtu LNG-linked marginal production in Europe/Asia (a ~$300/ton cost advantage vs European peers cited early 2026), 8–9 world-scale North American complexes, and the river/rail/pipeline distribution network into the US Corn Belt. Customers (farmers/distributors) cannot in-source at all.
- Why not higher: the product is a fungible commodity priced off the *global marginal ton* — if Hormuz reopens and European/Russian/Middle East tons return, CF's price realisation falls regardless of its cost edge (it keeps margin, loses windfall). New capacity (incl. its own Blue Point) and imports are a permanent route-around on price, just not on cost.

**5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)**
- 2026-08-05 H1/Q2 results: Q2 net earnings $727M, +88% YoY; adj. EBITDA $1.19B (Q2) / $2.18B (H1); H1 net earnings $1.34B; Q2 buyback 2.0M shares for $230M; management guide that nitrogen stays constrained into 2027.
- Q1 (for context): EPS $3.98 reported ($2.89 cited ex-items in some coverage) vs $2.50 consensus, adj. EBITDA $983M.
- 2026-10-07: $0.60/qtr dividend declared (payable 2026-11-30) and Q3 date confirmed for 2026-11-04; stock +3.8% on 2026-10-09 on firmer urea pricing. RBC projection cited in coverage: ~$2.5B 2026 FCF, +40% vs 2025's $1.79B.
- Blue Point permitted construction start Aug 2026; Donaldsonville CCS with ExxonMobil already sequestering since mid-2025.

**6. Risks**
- This is the most explicitly cyclical/geopolitical thesis in the batch: Mizuho downgraded CF to Underperform in Mar 2026 arguing the Hormuz surge was captured and temporary (PT $100) — if the conflict settles, urea/ammonia normalise and 2027 earnings fall. US gas is the other leg: a Henry Hub spike compresses the spread from the cost side (Q1 realised gas already $4.57 vs $3.68 YoY). Farm economics (soft crop prices, deferred purchases seen in June and in H2 application regions), China export policy swings, Yazoo City outage into H1 2027, and $1.3B 2026 capex incl. Blue Point execution. Valuation (fwd P/E 10.4, +56% YTD per Finnhub 2026-10-23) already pays for a long squeeze.

**7. Shortage score: 7/10 — Verdict:** A genuine, company-confirmed nitrogen shortage (4–4.5Mt of traded urea physically removed, order book built into November, tightness guided into 2027) sitting on a structurally advantaged lowest-cost asset base — but the shortage is geopolitical and can unwind by headline, which caps the score.

## V — Visa Inc.

**1. Shortage thesis — none. This is a toll booth, not a shortage**
- No structural shortage or unmet-demand surge exists for Visa's core service: network capacity is software, not physical — it processed a record >$4 trillion payments volume and 72B transactions in fiscal Q3 2026 (+10% / +10% YoY) with no capacity constraint, lead time, or price rationing reported. Demand growth is steady consumer-spending/cash-to-card conversion (~9–11% volume growth), not a surge supply can't meet.
- The nearest "surge" adjacencies are ones Visa is *supplying into*, not short of: value-added services (~30% of revenue, +27–28% constant-dollar growth), Visa Direct (3.7B transactions in Q2, +23%), stablecoin settlement ($4.6B run-rate Q1 → ~$7B Q2 → >$20B annualised run-rate cited by Zacks Oct 2026, 160+ stablecoin-linked card programmes), and agentic commerce tooling. These are growth options, not shortage rents.

**2. Backlog / order book**
- Not found / not applicable — Visa discloses no backlog; revenue is per-transaction as volume occurs. Proxies for forward visibility: FY guidance raised at Q2 (28 Apr 2026) to net revenue growth "low double-digit to low-teens" and adj. EPS growth low-teens; Q4 guided at Q3 to high end of low-double-digits; multi-year client incentive/renewal contracts exist but are not quantified as backlog in filings. Capital return is the disclosed forward commitment: $7.9B buybacks in Q2 alone, new $20B authorisation Apr 2026 (~$33B total capacity).

**3. Category position — clear #1, in a duopoly**
- #1 global card network. Estimates vary by methodology (treat ranges, not points): ~51–52% global credit-card network share vs Mastercard ~32% (Dataintelo 2025/26) or ~45% vs ~27% of all global card transaction value; $14.2–14.5T payments volume in FY2025 vs Mastercard ~$9.2T gross dollar volume; 70.4% of combined US Visa+Mastercard purchase volume (Axis Intelligence, Jul 2026). 4.9B credentials, 257.5B transactions in FY2025, 130M+ merchant locations (stablecoin-comparison source, Oct 2026). #2 Mastercard is the only comparable global rival; Amex/UnionPay are segment/regional.

**4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation — 8/10**
- Moat: two-sided network effects + acceptance/certification lock-in. A bank, merchant, or hyperscaler cannot in-source Visa: Apple Pay, Google Pay, PayPal and X Money all ultimately ride Visa's rails because merchants and issuers are already there; building a rival global acceptance network from zero has failed repeatedly for 50 years. Fraud/risk, tokenisation (17.5B+ tokens, >50% tokenisation rate) and chargeback trust are bundled into the toll.
- Concrete bypass threats — real, and the reason this is not 9–10: (i) Account-to-account real-time rails (FedNow, RTP, Pix-style systems, open-banking/Plaid-type pay-by-bank — Visa tried to buy Plaid for $5.3B in 2020, blocked, with DOJ alleging Visa saw it as an "insurance policy" against a debit threat); (ii) stablecoins settle in seconds at 0.01–0.10% vs 1.5–3.5% card cost — Visa's defence is to become the stablecoin settlement layer itself, which converts the threat into lower-take volume; (iii) regulation as forced disintermediation — DOJ debit-monopoly suit (filed Sep 2024, survived motion to dismiss Jun 2025; >60% of US debit, >$7B/yr fees alleged) and the proposed Credit Card Competition Act routing mandate.

**5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)**
- 2026-07-28 fiscal Q3 beat: net revenue $11.6B, +14% YoY; EPS $3.32 vs $3.23 consensus; first-ever $4T+ payments-volume quarter; VAS revenue $3.8B, +~36% YoY per Zacks tally.
- Three consecutive quarterly beats in 2026 (Q1 EPS $3.17 vs $3.14; Q2 $3.31 vs $3.10, revenue +17%, highest since 2022, guidance raised; Q3 above).
- Stablecoin/agentic-commerce build-out through H2: five additional blockchains added for settlement, stablecoin-linked programmes expanding, Visa-as-a-Service/agentic tooling launched with partners. Next print: fiscal Q4, 2026-10-27, EPS consensus ~$3.44.

**6. Risks**
- Thesis risk for *this screen* is primary: there is no shortage, no backlog, no supply catalyst — explosive return would require multiple expansion on a fwd P/E 27.6 stock with only +2.7% analyst upside. Substantive risks: DOJ debit case remedies (fee/routing), Durbin/CCCA interchange caps, consumer-spending slowdown, cross-border/travel softness (CEMEA/Middle East headwind already flagged in Q2), rising client incentives compressing net yield, and slow A2A/stablecoin share loss at the high-value B2B margin first. Quality is not the question; acceleration is.

**7. Shortage score: 2/10 — Verdict:** Visa is arguably the best business in this batch and the worst fit for a shortage/momentum screen — an irreplaceable toll booth compounding at ~11% with no supply constraint, no backlog, and no near-term explosive catalyst beyond another steady beat on 2026-10-27.
