# Panelist A — Supply-chain analyst — Momentum ballot — 2026-10-24

## (1) SINGLE-PICK BALLOT

- **Top pick:** MU
- **Runner-up:** SNDK
- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** MU owns the broadest, hardest shortage in the dossier — HBM, conventional DRAM and NAND are all short simultaneously, with management stating demand will exceed supply in 2027 and 2028 and no line of sight to balance. Unlike visibility-only stories, customers have wired cash ahead and signed take-or-pay to lock supply, giving multi-year revenue visibility through 2030. Every hyperscaler custom ASIC still needs merchant HBM stacked on it, so the in-sourcing wave that threatens NVDA and ANET actually routes demand to MU. At a screen forward P/E of 4.1 with +82.4% analyst upside, the market is still pricing a normal memory cycle, not a contracted, prepaid shortage.
- **Key shortage/backlog evidence:** Customers paid $12.747B in contract-liability deposits in FY2026 ($12.9B non-current contract liabilities), alongside >$100B of take-or-pay contracts, 26 long-term agreements covering >35% of revenue through 2030, 75% of capacity booked through end-2027, and entire 2026 HBM output committed — while Micron can satisfy only 50–66% of key customers' demand.
- **Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk:** low — Hyperscalers design accelerators, not memory fabs; DRAM/HBM fabrication is a 3-player oligopoly with decade-long process learning and no realistic in-sourcing path. The concrete threat is share loss to SK hynix (reported >2/3 of NVIDIA HBM4 orders, 50% HBM share vs MU 18%), not customers making memory themselves; co-designed custom HBM4E with NVIDIA deepens the lock.
- **12-18 month return scenario:** Base +40% to +60% as 2027 HBM pricing increases and contracted volumes convert at guided scale (FYQ1 2027 guide $61.5B ±$1.5B); bull +80%+ toward the screen target mean if tightness intensifies in 2027–2028 as management guided and margins hold near FYQ4 levels.
- **Top risk:** New capacity arriving in 2028 plus floor/ceiling LTA pricing caps ends the shortage just as upside is capped, compounded by HBM share/yield execution versus SK hynix — a hyperscaler capex pause would hit this most cyclical name first.
- **Conviction (1-10):** 9

**Top 3 — shortage/backlog deep dives:**

**1. MU:** The single most compelling point is the cash, not the claim — $12.747B of customer deposits received in FY2026 to secure supply years ahead, backing >$100B take-or-pay and agreements through 2030/2031, with 2026 HBM entirely committed and demand guided above supply through 2028. That is the longest hard visibility in the set, with irreplaceability 8/10. 12–18mo scenario: base +40% to +60%, bull +80%+ toward the screen's +82.4% analyst upside.

**2. SNDK:** The New Business Model converts a spot NAND cycle into contracted floors — $93.9B minimum revenue from signed contracts, $41.6B in remaining performance obligations, >50% of FY2027 capacity and ~2/3 of FY2028 capacity already committed, plus >$11B in financial guarantees. The discount versus MU is structural: #5 share at 11.4%, irreplaceability 6/10, NAND is the most commoditised tier, and the variable leg of NBM pricing resets down if spot falls. 12–18mo scenario: base +35% to +55%, bull +75% toward the screen's +75.5% analyst upside if 2027 pre-booked tightness holds.

**3. NVDA:** Visibility is unmatched in scale — $0.5 trillion in Blackwell and Rubin revenue visibility through end-2026, doubled at GTC 2026 to $1 trillion in Blackwell + Vera Rubin purchase orders through 2027, with cloud GPUs sold out. It ranks below MU/SNDK on this lens because that figure is management visibility, not a contractual RPO, and disintermediation is live: Google TPU, Amazon Trainium, Microsoft Maia and Meta MTIA are in-sourcing inference, where share is projected to fall materially. 12–18mo scenario: base +25% to +40%, bull +55% toward the screen's +55.0% analyst upside if training demand absorbs the ASIC shift.

**Deliberately left out of top 10:** META, V, and NUE — META (shortage 3/10) and V (shortage 2/10) disclose no product backlog/RPO and sell no scarce product as suppliers, while NUE has only qualitative "record backlogs," the lowest irreplaceability at 4/10 as a fully substitutable commodity, and tariff-rented scarcity whose duration depends on policy, not contracted demand.
