## WST — West Pharmaceutical Services

### 1. Shortage thesis
**Demand surge, not a classic sold-out shortage.** Driver is twofold: (a) GLP-1 / biologics injectable volume, and (b) EU Annex 1 sterility/contamination-control upgrades forcing customers to convert standard closures to high-value product (HVP) components.

- Reuters, 2026-07-23 (Q2 report): West "raised its annual profit and revenue forecasts… as strong demand for components used in injectable drugs, including diabetes and obesity treatments" — stoppers, plungers and delivery systems for Ozempic/Wegovy (Novo) and Mounjaro (Lilly); CEO Eric Green cited generic GLP-1s in China/India/South Korea as an added driver, and CFO Bob McMahon said both GLP-1 and non-GLP-1 HVP components are now expected to grow at a high-teens rate in 2026.
- GLP-1 weight: ~10% of Q2 2026 revenue for GLP-1 elastomer components per analyst narratives summarising management disclosure, with broader GLP-1-related sales cited at ~17–18% of Q2 sales (Vista Research, Oct 2026, from filings) — definitions differ, treat as a range, not a single audited figure.
- Annex 1 pipeline is the quantifiable "book of work": management on the Q2 call (2026-07-23) — "just shy of 800 total projects in hand, that's up 50% from the same time period last year," against a pool of roughly 6 billion eligible components; Vista (Oct 2026) notes only ~15% of that pool had been converted by end-2025, and West expects Annex 1 to add ~200 bps to 2026 revenue growth.
- Capacity evidence: West opened a 165,000 sq ft expansion at Damastown, Dublin in March 2026 "to increase global capacity for high-volume injectable therapies, including GLP-1 treatments" (Packaging Digest, Oct 2026). On the Q1 call management described unlocking throughput at existing plants and qualifying second sites — i.e. it is chasing demand, but **no "sold out" claim, lead-time extension, or allocation disclosure was found**.

### 2. Backlog / order book
**Not found / not disclosed.** West does not report a dollar backlog or bookings figure in its Q2 2026 10-Q (filed 2026-07-23) or Q2 release. Best proxies:
- ~800 Annex 1 / HVP upgrade projects in hand, +~50% YoY (Q2 call, 2026-07-23), multi-year monetisation.
- >90% participation/win rate on new biologic molecules and ~95% participation in U.S. drug launches in 2025 (management via Zacks, Oct 2026) — i.e. the forward pipeline is embedded in customers' approved drugs rather than in a stated backlog.

### 3. Category position
**Clear #1.** Described as the global market leader in primary packaging/delivery components for injectable therapeutics, with an estimated ~70% share of elastomer components for injectable drugs; the remaining ~30% is split mainly between Switzerland's Datwyler and AptarGroup (secondary-source estimate, Apr 2026 — no primary-source share figure found, treat as approximate). HVP components were ~49% of Q2 2026 sales (Vista, from filings).

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk — **8/10**
- Moat is regulatory qualification lock-in, not just process tech: "West's stoppers and seals are written into the approved filings of the drugs they close, and switching suppliers means the drug maker must generate data showing the substitute is equivalent and poses no new risk to patients" (Vista Research, Oct 2026). Each approved drug then pays West for as long as it sells in that form.
- Even an internal site transfer takes time: management, Q1 2026 call — multi-site qualification "could be anywhere between 6–12 months for a transfer to occur"; third-party analyses put new-supplier elastomer qualification at 24–36 months minimum (stability testing + FDA/EMA filing).
- In-sourcing by Lilly/Novo is unrealistic near-term: the component is pennies per dose, the cost of a failed batch / sterility failure is catastrophic, and West's FluroTec® barrier film, NovaPure® and Daikyo Crystal Zenith® polymer know-how (Daikyo agreements renewed for 10 years, effective 2026-07-14) is not a pharma maker's core competence.
- Concrete bypass threats (why not 9–10): (i) Datwyler announced in its 2026 half-year results that it has begun supplying components for a leading GLP-1 weight-loss medicine — a qualified second source now exists inside GLP-1; (ii) route-around via oral GLP-1s — West/Stevanato have guided to oral eventually reaching ~30% of the GLP-1 market, though KeyBanc (Jan 2026) expects oral Wegovy to add only ~200 bps of share by 2030 and injectables to remain primary.

### 5. Recent catalysts (~last 3 months)
- **Q2 beat + raise, 2026-07-23:** revenue $872.3m (+13.8% YoY) vs ~$839–843m expected; adj. EPS $2.37 vs $2.08 consensus; FY2026 guidance raised to revenue $3.35–3.38bn (from $3.295–3.35bn) and adj. EPS $8.85–9.05 (from $8.40–8.75), organic growth guide raised to 10–11%.
- **Daikyo renewal, 2026-07-14:** amended/restated Technology Exchange, Crosslicense and Distributorship agreements, 10-year term, West exclusive distributor of Daikyo products outside Japan.
- **Portfolio:** SmartDose 3.5 mL on-body delivery system sold to AbbVie (completed in 2026, referenced in Q2 coverage); Dublin drug-handling contract-manufacturing programme guided to >$60m revenue at full ramp by ~2028.
- **CEO transition, 2026-08-31:** Michel Lagarde (former Thermo Fisher COO) succeeded Eric Green — announced Mar 2026, effective Aug 31.
- **Analyst upgrades:** UBS raised target $340 → $415 (2026-09-09); Morgan Stanley upgraded Equal-Weight → Overweight, target $400 (Sep 2026); TD Cowen raised $410 → $420 (2026-10-13). Stock +33–36% YTD into mid-October (Zacks).

### 6. Risks
- **Valuation / upside spent:** fwd P/E 39.5 (screen); MarketBeat consensus target $391.17 (2026-10-23) is essentially at/below the $394.03 screen price — the re-rating after the +43% 120-day run (AInvest, Aug 2026) leaves little margin for a miss. Vista: at $364.98 on Oct 2 the stock already needed ~16.75% annual EPS growth for five years for a 10% return.
- **Customer concentration:** largest customer was $485.9m, 15.8% of 2025 sales, and accounted for at least $129.5m of West's $180.9m 2025 growth (Vista, from filings); top-10 customers ~50% of sales (secondary source).
- **Oral GLP-1 substitution** and GLP-1 price compression / generic mix; **second-sourcing** by Datwyler/Stevanato/SCHOTT as customers de-risk supply.
- **Execution:** May 2026 cyber incident cost West Vantage ~$7m of Q2 sales and cut its operating margin to 8.6% from 12.1% (10-Q via Vista); commodity inflation and tariffs cited by management/Zacks; implied Q3 organic growth guide decelerates to ~7–9%.
- **Leadership transition** under a new CEO inheriting a guidance bar set by his predecessor.

### 7. Scores
- **Shortage score: 6/10**
- **Irreplaceability: 8/10** (above)
- **Verdict:** A genuine regulatory-lock-in toll collector on the GLP-1/biologics surge with a visible ~800-project Annex 1 conversion runway, but it is a demand surge West itself is expanding capacity to meet — not a hard shortage — and at 39.5× forward earnings with consensus target at the price, the explosive-return case needs Q3 (2026-10-29) to beat an already-raised bar.

## AME — AMETEK, Inc.

### 1. Shortage thesis
**No single-product shortage; a broad, multi-front demand surge into a "mission-critical infrastructure build-out."** Drivers: AI datacenter / semiconductor capital equipment, power-grid infrastructure, defence modernisation, commercial aerospace and MedTech.

- CEO David Zapico, Q2 call (2026-08-04): "I believe we're in the beginning stages of a multiyear infrastructure build-out, and we're incredibly well positioned" (Fool/MarketBeat transcript). TIKR summary of the call: Zapico called AI, defence and power-grid demand "durable, not cyclical."
- On the Q2 call Q&A, Zapico put the combined bucket of data centre/AI + military modernisation + commercial aerospace + power infrastructure at "about half of our business," while stressing direct data-centre exposure alone is "not big enough to report on a specific segment" — an important honesty check against an "AI shortage pure-play" framing.
- Hard demand evidence is orders, not sold-out capacity: Q2 orders were a record $2.3bn, +28% YoY (organic +25%), book-to-bill 1.12 — demand is outrunning shipments. Standout markets per Zapico: semiconductor in EIG ("especially… semiconductor orders stood out," Zygo metrology/optics for advanced chip production) and MedTech in EMG (Paragon Medical new-product wins; EMG organic orders +35%).

### 2. Backlog / order book
- **Record backlog $4.11 billion at end-Q2 2026** (company PR / call, 2026-08-04), up ~21% since year-end 2025 (TIKR); **~80% expected to ship within 12 months**.
- Orders: record **$2.3 billion in Q2, +28% YoY, organic +25%**, following Q1 organic orders +22% — two consecutive quarters of >20% organic order growth (Q2 call).

### 3. Category position
**Not a single-category #1 — a portfolio of niche #1s.** AMETEK's stated model is leadership in dozens of niche markets via 40+ autonomous businesses across Electronic Instruments Group (EIG, Q2 sales $1.32bn, +14%) and Electromechanical Group (EMG, Q2 sales $723.2m, +17%). Management, Q1 2026 call: "We are leaders in niche markets where our differentiated technology solutions play a mission-critical role in our customers' most demanding applications." **No single overall market-share-vs-#2 figure exists or was found** — rivals are niche-specific (Fortive, Emerson, Teledyne, HEICO/Curtiss-Wright in aerospace content) rather than one #2.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk — **7/10**
- Moat: designed-in, specification-locked, regulated content. Management, Q1 2026: products are "designed into applications governed by strict regulatory and compliance requirements, creating high switching costs… long-cycle industries with long asset lifespans, resulting in low obsolescence risk."
- Pricing-power proof, Zapico, Q2 call: "we're niche leaders, mission-critical products… there are high switching costs and regulatory-driven markets. So the price of failure is high" — AMETEK offset inflation and tariffs "a little more than [with] price" in Q2; product vitality was 25% (share of sales from new products).
- Hyperscaler in-sourcing is not a realistic threat: Microsoft/Google/Amazon do not manufacture semiconductor metrology (Zygo), power-quality analysers, aerospace sensors or specialty motors, and AMETEK content is a small, failure-critical slice of their capex. The realistic erosion path is slower: dual-sourcing / redesign at the next equipment generation, and larger diversified rivals contesting individual niches — hence 7, not higher. There is no single choke-point product nobody else on earth can make.

### 5. Recent catalysts (~last 3 months)
- **Q2 beat + raise, 2026-08-04:** record sales $2.04bn (+15%, organic +10%), adj. EPS $2.09 (+17%) vs $1.99 consensus and $1.96–2.00 guidance; operating income record $544m, margin 26.6% (core 27.1%, +110 bps); FCF $452m (+37%, 111% conversion). FY2026 guidance raised to sales up ~10% and adj. EPS $8.20–8.30 (from $7.94–8.14); Q3 EPS guide $2.08–2.10.
- **Indicor Instrumentation closed, 2026-08-26:** all-cash $5.0bn acquisition (largest in company history) of mission-critical instrumentation businesses from Indicor, LLC; expected to contribute ~$350m to 2026 sales, modestly accretive to 2026 adj. EPS, with management citing 10–12% cost synergies; businesses folded into EIG and EMG (company PR, 2026-08-26).
- **Analyst action:** BNP Paribas Exane target $280 → $290, Outperform (2026-09-11); KeyCorp $295 target (2026-09-30); Zacks upgraded Hold → Strong Buy (2026-10-08).

### 6. Risks
- **M&A execution is now the thesis risk:** Indicor is a $5.0bn bet closed eight weeks ago; Q2 GAAP EPS was $1.77 vs $2.09 adjusted (a $0.32 gap from acquisition amortisation, bridge-financing fees and integration costs), and Simply Wall St flags dependence on successful integration as the biggest risk. A stumble is punished at this multiple.
- **Cyclicality, not shortage:** semis, aerospace, oil & gas and general industrial capex are cyclical (Trefis/TrendEdge risk summaries); the current +28% orders print is a peak-cycle datapoint until proven otherwise, and management itself guided Q3 organic growth down to high-single digits.
- **Valuation:** fwd P/E 25.0 (screen) for a diversified industrial with mid-to-high-single-digit guided organic growth; consensus target ~$267–290 vs the $261.16 52-week high — upside exists per screen (+18.1%) but assumes the infrastructure cycle persists.
- **Other:** academic/government research funding (~10% of revenue) cited as a headwind in analyst narratives; tariff/China order-timing volatility; diversification itself caps explosiveness — no single end market is large enough to re-rate a ~$55bn company on its own.

### 7. Scores
- **Shortage score: 5/10**
- **Irreplaceability: 7/10** (above)
- **Verdict:** AMETEK is a high-quality, designed-in compounder riding a real, broad infrastructure/semiconductor/defence order surge (record $4.11bn backlog, book-to-bill 1.12), but there is no structural shortage of an AMETEK choke-point product — the explosive case rests on the cycle staying hot and on flawless integration of the $5.0bn Indicor deal, not on scarcity.
