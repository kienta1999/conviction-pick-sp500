## 1. VRSN — VeriSign, Inc. ($308.67; rev +5.5%, op margin 68.6%, fwd PE 28.2x, upside +10.0%, 12m +28.1%)

### 1. Shortage thesis
No structural supply shortage — VeriSign sells a registry service, not a scarce physical good. The demand side is surging, however: AI tools are driving a record wave of new domain creation. CEO Jim Bidzos on the Q3 2026 call (Oct 22, 2026): "Registrars have successfully engaged with our marketing programs and are focused on customer acquisition, plus AI tools are making content and website creation faster and easier." Management also flagged "agentic AI" as an emerging tailwind on the call (per Earnings Whispers summary of the call). New .com/.net registrations hit a record 13.1 million in Q3 2026, up from 10.6 million a year earlier and 12.7 million in Q2. This is a demand-acceleration story with a price lever, not a capacity-constrained shortage: a $0.71 .com wholesale price increase (to $10.97, from $10.26) took effect Nov 1, 2026, with ~50% of the revenue benefit flowing through in 2027.

### 2. Backlog / order book
No order backlog is reported (registry model — revenue is recognised as domains are registered/renewed). Closest proxy: deferred revenues of $1.46 billion at Sep 30, 2026, up $79 million from year-end 2025 (8-K, Oct 22, 2026) — prepaid registrations not yet recognised. Domain name base: 182.5 million .com/.net names, +6.1% YoY, net +3.36 million in Q3. Full-year base-growth guidance raised for the third straight call, to 6.5–7.0% (from an initial 1.5–3.5%).

### 3. Category position
#1 by definition: sole operator of the .com registry — 100% share of the .com registry market, ~160+ million active .com registrations worldwide (Hagens Berman class-action filing, Sep 4, 2026; the 100% figure is the plaintiffs' characterisation, but the sole-operator fact is undisputed). #2 does not exist at the registry layer; competition exists only at the retail registrar layer (GoDaddy, Namecheap, Cloudflare), all of whom must pay VeriSign the same non-negotiable wholesale fee.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk — score 9/10
Moat: contractual/legal monopoly — the .com registry under the ICANN Registry Agreement plus the Cooperative Agreement with the U.S. Dept. of Commerce (NTIA), with a "presumptive right of renewal." No customer can in-source it: a .com domain only resolves through VeriSign's authoritative database; there is no alternative supplier. Substitute risk is limited to users choosing a different TLD (.ai, .io, .xyz) instead of .com — real at the margin for AI startups, but .com remains the default for commercial credibility, and VeriSign is itself launching .web (sunrise mid-Dec 2026, general availability ~Apr 2027) with unregulated pricing. Deduct one point because the moat is regulatory/contractual, not technological — a government or court decision could in principle change it (see Risks).

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- Q3 2026 (Oct 22, 2026): revenue $443m +5.7% YoY (beat $441.75m consensus); adj. EPS $2.41 vs $2.42 consensus (a 1-cent miss, GAAP $2.35 vs $2.27 a year ago); record 13.1m new registrations; guidance raised — FY26 revenue $1.755–1.760bn, base growth 6.5–7.0%.
- .com price increase Nov 1, 2026 ($10.26 → $10.97), first since early 2024.
- .web TLD delegated into the root zone; staged launch Dec 2026–Apr 2027; new "Verisign Informed" certificate-revocation security service operational, priced by year-end, production H1 2027.
- Dividend $0.81/share declared, record Nov 17, 2026, payable Nov 24, 2026. $1.36bn remaining under buyback authorisation at quarter-end.
- No analyst upgrade found in this pass.

### 6. Risks
- Antitrust: Hagens Berman filed a class action Sep 4, 2026 (Kessler v. VeriSign, Case No. 2:26-cv-09960, C.D. Cal.) alleging an illegal .com monopoly; claims price rose ~30% since 2021 ($7.85 → $10.26) while registry costs fell. Allegations only — nothing proven — but a adverse outcome could cap pricing power, which is the core thesis.
- Renewal quality slipping: expected Q3 renewal rate 74.3% vs 75.4% a year ago (first-time renewals in the mid-40% range vs mid-80% for previously renewed names). The record 2026 cohort faces its first renewal test in 2027.
- Growth is still only ~6%: at 28.2x forward PE and +28% over 12 months, a lot of the AI-registration story may already be priced in; Q4 registrations are seasonally slower.
- AI-driven server/memory cost inflation pressuring capex (per Earnings Whispers call summary).

### 7. Shortage score: 4/10
**Verdict:** Not a shortage play — a legal-monopoly toll booth riding an AI-driven registration surge, with a contractual price escalator; explosive upside depends on .web and renewal quality holding, not on scarcity.

### Earnings-quality flag — RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN (recv_rev_divergence +1.8521)
**One-line verdict: benign artefact, not a collection problem.** VeriSign runs a prepaid registry model — registrars pay upfront, so revenue sits in deferred revenue ($1.46bn, +$79m YTD), not receivables; CFO/NI of 1.25 and an accrual ratio of −0.1581 both show earnings are cash-backed, so the receivables-vs-revenue divergence is a small-base/timing artefact in a prepaid business, not customers failing to pay.

## 2. CME — CME Group Inc. ($305.89; rev +7.6%, op margin 65.9%, ROE 16.1%, fwd PE 23.1x, upside +3.6%, 12m +18.6%)

### 1. Shortage thesis
No supply shortage — CME sells market infrastructure, and its "product" (liquidity) is abundant when volatility is high. The demand driver is the opposite of scarcity: persistent geopolitical uncertainty, tariff volatility, rate-cut expectations and the retailisation of futures are driving record hedging/trading volumes. Q3 2026 ADV hit a record 29.4 million contracts, +16% YoY, with double-digit growth in all six asset classes (interest rates 15.1m, +13%; equity indexes 7.7m, +23%). October 2026 was running +17% ahead of last year's record Q4. This is a volatility-cycle volume surge, not a structural shortage — volumes can fall as fast as they rise if markets calm.

### 2. Backlog / order book
Not applicable — no backlog; an exchange has no order book of future deliveries. Closest forward indicators: Q3 revenue $1,737.3m (+13% YoY, best Q3 ever, second-best quarter ever), market-data revenue a record $243.1m (+20%, 34th consecutive quarter of growth), and open interest / non-U.S. ADV at a Q3 record 9.0m contracts (+22%). No guidance is given on volume or revenue.

### 3. Category position
#1: the world's largest derivatives marketplace by volume and notional value (CME, CBOT, NYMEX, COMEX under one roof; 6–7 billion contracts/year; participants in 150+ countries). #2 is Intercontinental Exchange (ICE) — strong in energy (Brent), softs and NYSE equities, and ~1.2bn contracts in H1 2025 — plus Eurex in Europe. CME dominates U.S. rates (SOFR/Treasuries), equity-index futures (E-mini S&P/Nasdaq) and U.S. ag/energy; ICE dominates Brent. No precise market-share figure found in this pass.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk — score 8/10
Moat: liquidity network effect + clearinghouse capital efficiency — CME reported a record $100bn/day average daily margin savings for customers in Q3 2026; traders go where the deepest pool and the cheapest margin offsets are. Regulatory status as a CFTC-regulated designated contract market and clearinghouse, plus exclusive licences (e.g. Nasdaq futures licence extended through 2029). Customers cannot realistically in-source a clearinghouse — capital, regulatory approval and, above all, the liquidity pool are the barriers. Deduct two points because the threat is real and current: the CFTC approved Kalshi's bitcoin perpetual (May 29, 2026) and Coinbase launched perpetual-style equity-index futures; CME sued the CFTC on Jun 18, 2026 (Chicago Mercantile Exchange Inc. v. Selig, D.D.C.) arguing perps are swaps, not futures. If perps go mainstream on rival venues, some flow — especially retail/crypto — could bypass CME's dated-futures franchise.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- Q3 2026 (Oct 21, 2026): record Q3 revenue $1.74bn (+13%), adj. EPS $3.02 (+13%, beat ~$2.98–2.99 consensus), adj. operating margin 69.4% (+100bps). Best Q3 in history; on track for a fifth consecutive record year.
- 24/7 trading for crypto futures and options launched May 29, 2026, with plans to extend to non-crypto products if banking supports it.
- CEO transition announced Jun 17, 2026: Terry Duffy steps down as CEO Mar 1, 2027 (becomes executive chairman); President/CFO Lynne Fitzpatrick becomes CEO. Shares fell ~5% on the announcement day.
- No analyst upgrade found in this pass.

### 6. Risks
- Cyclicality: revenue is volume × rate-per-contract; average rate per contract fell to $0.679 from $0.702 (−3.3% YoY) in Q3 — mix shift toward lower-fee products. A calm-volatility regime would cut ADV sharply.
- Perpetual futures / prediction markets: the CFTC moved to dismiss CME's suit (Sep 2, 2026; formal motion Oct 16, 2026), arguing competition alone gives no standing — CME could lose the legal fight and face a structurally cheaper competitor class.
- Leadership transition execution risk (Duffy has led since 2016; Fitzpatrick is an insider, which mitigates this).
- Valuation: fwd PE 23.1x is above ICE (~20.5x) and the exchange-industry average (~22.4x) per Zacks (Mar 2026), with only +3.6% analyst upside — limited headroom if volumes normalise.

### 7. Shortage score: 3/10
**Verdict:** A best-in-class volatility toll booth printing record volumes, not a shortage — explosive upside needs sustained market stress, and the perps fight is a genuine disintermediation threat to the franchise.

## 3. EMR — Emerson Electric Co. ($162.35; rev +5.4%, op margin 25.9%, ROE 13.1%, fwd PE 20.3x, upside +7.7%, 12m +24.5%)

### 1. Shortage thesis
Yes — the strongest genuine shortage-adjacent thesis in this batch. The driver is the AI datacenter power buildout plus LNG and grid modernisation, where demand for turbines, control systems, valves and instruments is outrunning delivery capacity. POWER magazine (Dec 1, 2025): "Manufacturers of turbines, heat recovery steam generators, control systems, valves, and instruments are all facing unprecedented orders" as "the race to deliver power for AI and data center applications has made quick power delivery a top priority." Emerson's own evidence: Ovation (power-plant controls) orders surged 74% in fiscal Q1 2026, driven by behind-the-meter datacenter projects; in Q4 FY26 Ovation orders were +26%, control systems & software orders +20%, test & measurement orders +14% with semiconductor +59%. CEO Lal Karsanbhai (Q1 FY26 call): "Emerson was chosen to automate on-site power generation for a new 1.7-gigawatt AI data center in the United States." Emerson also launched the DeltaV Automation Platform for Data Centers (Aug 3, 2026) aimed at compressing gigawatt-scale build timelines.

### 2. Backlog / order book
Backlog $8.2 billion at Sep 30, 2026, +11% YoY (excluding AspenTech) — Q4 FY26 earnings release, Nov 4, 2026. Underlying orders +14% in Q4 (Software & Systems +18%, Intelligent Devices +13%); book-to-bill 1.01. Project funnel $13 billion, +$600m sequentially. Caveat: the share of backlog shipping within 12 months fell from 75% to 70%, so the FY27-shippable book grew only ~3–4% — backlog is lengthening, not just growing. Growth verticals (power, LNG, semis, life sciences, aero/defence) +21% YoY.

### 3. Category position
Not #1 overall in industrial automation — that is a fragmented market led by Siemens, Schneider Electric, ABB and Rockwell (each ~2–3% of the broad control-panel market; Siemens is generally cited as global leader). Emerson is #1 in the niches that matter here: DeltaV runs "more than 50% of every LNG installation on the planet" (Tikr summary of Q1 FY26 call; Emerson won the Sempra Port Arthur LNG Phase 2 automation contract on that basis), and Ovation is a leading power-generation control platform. In power-plant control systems Emerson ranks in the top 4–5 globally (Spherical Insights, Oct 2026). So: challenger overall, dominant in LNG and power controls.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk — score 7/10
Moat: qualification lock-in + installed-base switching costs. A DCS (distributed control system) running an LNG terminal or a power plant is safety-certified, embedded in the plant's operating procedures, and effectively impossible to rip out mid-life; Emerson's DeltaV/Ovation installed base and its AspenTech software stack (majority-owned) create a software-defined lock-in. Hyperscalers/datacenter owners cannot realistically in-source industrial process control — they buy the automation layer from Emerson/Siemens/Honeywell and focus on their own power/cooling hardware. Deduct three points because the automation market is genuinely competitive (Siemens, ABB, Honeywell, Schneider all offer credible DCS/SCADA), switching happens at greenfield builds, and Schneider's $22.6bn PTC acquisition (Oct 5, 2026) strengthens a direct rival's software stack.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- Q4 FY26 (Nov 4, 2026): sales $5.20bn (+7%, beat ~$5.12bn consensus), adj. EPS $1.86 (+14.8%, beat $1.85), adj. segment EBITA margin 29.1% (+160bps); underlying orders +14%; FY26 adj. EPS $6.59 — sixth consecutive year of double-digit adj. EPS growth. FY27 guidance: ~5% underlying sales growth, adj. EPS ~$7.25 (~+10%), FCF $3.8–3.9bn.
- Aug 3, 2026: DeltaV Automation Platform for Data Centers launched.
- Q1 FY26 (reported ~Feb 2026, within the demand narrative): 1.7 GW AI-datacenter on-site power win; Sempra Port Arthur LNG Phase 2 win.
- Stock reaction: shares fell 4.6% on Nov 5, 2026 despite the beat (to $162.61) — the market focused on the FY27 guide (~5% sales) lagging the +14% order print, i.e. backlog conversion timing.
- No analyst upgrade found in this pass.

### 6. Risks
- Backlog conversion: the 75%→70% twelve-month convertibility drop means the order surge converts to revenue more slowly than the headline suggests; FY27 sales guide is only ~5%.
- Cyclicality: power/LNG capex is a long-cycle bet; a datacenter spending pause or LNG final-investment-decision delays would hit orders with a lag. China orders remain negative (chemicals/refining weakness).
- Cash: FY26 FCF of $3,535m missed the ~$3.6bn August guide by ~$65m.
- Competition/consolidation: Schneider-PTC, Siemens-Altair and Synopsys-Ansys deals are arming rivals' software stacks; Emerson has no product-design software of its own.
- Valuation already reflects much of it: +24.5% over 12 months, though at 20.3x forward PE it is the cheapest in this batch.

### 7. Shortage score: 7/10
**Verdict:** The only true shortage thesis in the batch — AI power and LNG demand is outrunning control-system delivery capacity, with a $13bn funnel behind an $8.2bn backlog — but the payoff is gated by slow backlog conversion, so "explosive" is a 2027–28 story, not a quarter.

## 4. IDXX — IDEXX Laboratories, Inc. ($676.04; rev +9.8%, op margin 33.1%, ROE 68.9%, fwd PE 40.0x, upside +6.1%, 12m −6.1%)

### 1. Shortage thesis
No shortage. The thesis is underpenetration + recurring-revenue compounding, not scarcity: blood work appears in only ~13% of U.S. wellness visits, with even lower inclusion internationally, and sector-wide inclusion is rising ~50bps a year, which IDEXX translates into ~150bps of incremental recurring revenue annually (per Tickeron summary of management commentary). Demand driver is pet-humanisation and the expanding test menu (inVue Dx cytology, Cancer Dx, Catalyst proBNP cardiac test — the first dual-species in-clinic cardiac marker), plus AI workflow tools pulling more tests per visit. Nothing is sold out; no lead times or price spikes cited — instead ~4% global net price realisation in CAG recurring revenue.

### 2. Backlog / order book
Not applicable — no backlog is reported; ~80% of revenue is recurring diagnostics (consumables + reference-lab services), i.e. a flow business, not an order book. Closest forward indicators: CAG Diagnostics recurring revenue $967m in Q3 2026, +11% organic; premium instrument installed base growing (record quarter of U.S. DR50 PLUS installations in Q3); software ACV-style recurring revenue growing with 10,000+ clinics on IDEXX cloud PIMS.

### 3. Category position
#1, clearly: the global leader in companion-animal veterinary diagnostics. Verified Market Research (Feb 2026, Q1 2026 report) puts IDEXX at 31.4% global market share in veterinary diagnostics vs Antech at 22.8% (incl. Heska hardware, North American lab volume) and Zoetis at 18.5%. CSIMarket (Q3 2026 peer data, updated Nov 7, 2026): IDEXX holds 84.5% of combined revenue among its tracked in-vitro/in-vivo diagnostics peer group and 76.2% of industry market cap; #1 of 19 by market cap. (Note: share figures vary by market definition — biomarkers-only studies show a far more fragmented market — but on the core companion-animal diagnostics definition IDEXX is the undisputed leader.)

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk — score 8/10
Moat: razor-and-blade installed base + workflow lock-in. ~80,000 Catalyst chemistry analysers and 9,000+ inVue Dx instruments in clinics generate high-margin recurring consumables; the reference-lab network, VetConnect PLUS data platform, and PIMS (Cornerstone, ezyVet, Neo, Animana — 10,000+ clinics) make switching a multi-system migration. The Sep 16, 2026 CoVetAI acquisition extends the lock-in into the exam room itself (ambient AI scribe → test ordering). Customers (vets) cannot in-source reference-lab diagnostics or analyser chemistry; the realistic threat is a rival platform (Antech/Mars, Zoetis VETSCAN Imagyst) winning the next instrument placement cycle, or a low-cost point-of-care entrant. Deduct two points because Antech (Mars-owned) is well-funded and has historically matched IDEXX menu moves within 6–18 months, and Zoetis bundles diagnostics with its pharma franchise.

### 6. Risks
- Valuation: 40.0x forward PE — the richest in this batch — with only +6.1% analyst upside; the stock is −6.1% over 12 months, so the market has already cooled on it, but any growth disappointment is punished at this multiple.
