## Single-pick ballot

- **Top pick:** MSI
- **Runner-up:** WST
- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** MSI is the only name in the field that clears the doctrine on all three legs at once: the biggest in its niche (#1 in mission-critical communications, ~24.5% global critical-communications share, 28.4% in North America), riding a genuine demand-surge shortage, and owning technology customers cannot in-source. Orders grew >20% YoY in Q3 2026 against revenue +12% — demand demonstrably outrunning supply — while the defence kicker (Silvus MANET, described by management as a "one-of-one asset", plus counter-drone D-Fend) adds a second, structural growth engine. The $12.4bn Software & Services backlog is mostly recurring multi-year contracts, and command-center software sits in >60% of North American public-safety agencies, so the revenue is locked in, not merely hoped for. At 26.4x forward PE with ROE 77.0%, this is quality compounding with a visible order book, not a multiple bet.
- **Key shortage/backlog evidence:** Record ending backlog $16.6bn at Q3 2026 (4 Nov 2026), +14% YoY (+$2.1bn) and +$1.0bn sequentially — ~1.3 years of revenue at ~$13bn annual revenue, the longest visibility in the field, with record Q3 orders in all three technologies.
- **Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk:** low — P25/TETRA networks are multi-decade infrastructure that agencies cannot in-source or build themselves; switching a mission-critical radio network is a procurement and safety exercise, not a purchasing decision. The only real erosion path is the slow-burn, long-run migration from LMR to broadband push-to-talk-over-cellular / 5G, which does not threaten the $16.6bn backlog or the 12–18 month window.
- **12-18 month return scenario:** Base +10–15% — backlog converts at the guided pace (preliminary 2027 revenue ~$14.25bn vs FY2026 guided ~$13.05bn) with the $2bn buyback increase supporting the share count; analyst target $547.18 (+6.4%) is the low end of that range. Bull +25–30% — Silvus (~$850m revenue expected in 2026) and defence/counter-drone orders keep orders growing >20% and the market re-rates a defence-grade compounder off its 26.4x forward PE.
- **Top risk:** The growth is partly bought and the balance sheet carries the bill — $211m of Q3 revenue came from acquisitions (Silvus cost $4.4bn, D-Fend $1.5bn), long-term debt is $9.36bn vs cash of $774m (net debt/EBITDA 2.70x), and the INVENTORY_BUILD earnings-quality flag (+41.5pts divergence) needs the 10-Q to confirm the build is finished goods for named orders rather than unsold stock. Government-budget dependence is the second risk.
