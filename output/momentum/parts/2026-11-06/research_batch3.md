## MA — Mastercard

### 1. Shortage thesis
Weak / not a shortage story. Mastercard sells network capacity that is effectively infinite — there is no physical product sold out, no lead time, no price-increase-from-scarcity dynamic. The demand-surge analogue is cross-border and value-added services: Q3 2026 cross-border volume +11% YoY (local currency), switched transactions +9%, contactless 81% of in-person switched purchases (+5pp YoY), 3.8bn cards issued (+5% YoY) — Mastercard Q3 2026 earnings release, 2026-10-29. Value-added services & solutions revenue +19% to $4.1bn, now >41% of revenue. That is durable volume growth, not a supply-constrained shortage. Shortage framing does not fit; the "surge" is secular cash-to-card conversion plus cross-border travel/non-travel spending.

### 2. Backlog / order book
No backlog figure — card networks do not report an order book or RPO. Closest forward indicators: full-year 2026 net revenue guidance at "the high end of the low double-digit range" (currency-neutral, ex-inorganic) — Q3 2026 call, 2026-10-29; and deal timing: management said Q3 revenue beat expectations partly on "the timing of both services projects and payment network deals." No quantified pipeline disclosed. **Not found** as a backlog number.

### 3. Category position
#2 globally, behind Visa. Calendar-aligned 12 months to 2026-06-30 (Axis Intelligence analysis of both companies' filings, updated 2026-10-27): Visa 62.27% vs Mastercard 37.73% of combined purchase volume ($15.34tn vs $9.29tn); in the US Visa ~71% vs Mastercard ~29% (Q2 2026). Nilson Report (Apr 2026): Visa ~30–31% of US credit/debit transactions vs Mastercard ~12–14% — Visa #1, Mastercard a clear #2. Offset: Mastercard earns more per dollar — 37.75bp net revenue per dollar of volume vs Visa's 29.01bp (+30% yield premium), driven by cross-border mix and services.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk — score 6/10
Moat: two-sided network — 3.8bn cards, acceptance in 200+ countries, plus switching costs for issuers/acquirers and the services stack (fraud, tokenisation, Mastercard Move reaching 17bn+ endpoints). A single bank cannot replicate global merchant acceptance.
Real threat, honestly weighed: (a) Stablecoins / A2A: stablecoin transfer volume hit $33tn in 2025 (+72% YoY, per Cryptonomist, Mar 2026); stablecoin market cap ~$303bn (DeFiLlama, Oct 2026). Citrini Research (Feb 2026) warned AI agents optimised to minimise cost could route around the 2–3% interchange. (b) Mastercard's own response is to absorb the rail rather than fight it: BVNK acquisition agreed Mar 2026 for up to $1.8bn ($1.5bn + $300m earnout), closed 2026-08-03; Global Money Platform launched 2026-10-15 (fiat+stablecoin accounts, stablecoin-linked cards in 130+ countries, payouts via Mastercard Move); issuers can settle in USDC/PYUSD/RLUSD since June 2026; SoFi Bank's whole card portfolio (>$25bn annualised volume) went live on on-chain settlement via SoFiUSD in late Sep 2026. (c) Domestic A2A (FedNow/RTP, Pix-style rails, Apple/Google Pay on bank rails) and merchant bypass remain slow-burn, not a cliff — cards still own dispute/chargeback, credit, and rewards, which stablecoins do not replicate. (d) Customer concentration: Capital One debit migration dented US debit growth (+1% in Q3; US GDV +6% overall) — a reminder a single large issuer move matters. Score 6/10: very hard to replace as a network, but the toll itself (interchange) is the thing being routed around, and the defence is to become the settlement layer for the new rails.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- Q3 2026 earnings beat, 2026-10-29: net revenue $9.8bn +14%, adj EPS $5.31 +21% (beat by 3.3%); payment network +11%, VAS +19%; adj op margin 60.5%. Buybacks: $3.0bn in Q3 + $648m through Oct 26.
- BVNK closed 2026-08-03; Global Money Platform launched 2026-10-15 with Nuvei, Marqeta, Ontop, Beem, Almond FinTech as first integrators.
- Cross-border card-not-present ex-travel +15% (though down from +20% in Q2) — still strong.
- No analyst upgrade found in this window; guidance nudged to high end of low-double-digit revenue growth.

### 6. Risks
- Disintermediation (see §4): stablecoin/A2A/AI-agent routing attacks the 2–3% interchange; if it scales in cross-border (MA's highest-yield segment), pricing power erodes.
- Cyclicality: consumer spending / travel slowdown hits GDV (+7%) and cross-border (+11%) directly; intra-Europe cross-border already slowed sequentially on a new EU customs fee, promo timing, and lower crypto purchases.
- Issuer concentration: Capital One debit migration showed single-client impact on US debit.
- Valuation: fwd PE 28.3x — the highest of the four names here — already prices durable double-digit growth; no shortage-driven repricing lever.
- Regulation: interchange caps floated in the US (Trump 10% credit-card rate-cap proposal cited as a pressure on the whole card group in Mar 2026 press) and EU fee scrutiny.

### 7. Shortage score: 2/10
**Verdict:** A superb compounding toll-road, not a shortage bet — volume grows steadily but nothing is scarce, so there is no explosive supply/demand repricing; the upside case is services mix and cross-border, the downside case is the toll itself being bypassed.

## V — Visa

### 1. Shortage thesis
Weak / not a shortage story — same structural point as MA. VisaNet capacity is not scarce. The demand-surge analogue is stronger than MA's in scale: FY2026 payments volume +9% (constant $), cross-border ex-intra-Europe +12% (travel +11%, e-commerce +15%), processed transactions 281.3bn (+9%), Visa Direct >15bn transactions / ~$1.5tn volume, 5.2bn credentials (+6%), 200m+ seller locations — Visa fiscal Q4/FY2026 call, 2026-10-27. Data-processing revenue (risk/security services) grew 16% in Q4 vs 10% transaction growth — the services layer is outgrowing the toll. Still: no sold-out capacity, no lead times, no scarcity pricing.

### 2. Backlog / order book
No backlog/RPO figure — card networks do not report one. **Not found.** Forward indicators: FY2027 guidance of low-double-digit net revenue growth (Q1 and full year); ~25% of total payments volume is scheduled for contract renewal in FY2027 (up from 20% in FY2026) — a renewal wave, not a backlog, and it cuts both ways (see Risks).

### 3. Category position
#1 globally, by a wide margin. Calendar-aligned 12m to 2026-06-30: Visa 62.27% vs Mastercard 37.73% of combined purchase volume; US share ~71% (Axis Intelligence, 2026-10-27). Nilson Report (Apr 2026): Visa handled ~31% of US debit and ~30% of US credit transactions vs Mastercard ~12–14% — Visa #1 in both. FY2026 net revenue $45.9bn (+15%, "strongest growth in over 15 years ex post-pandemic recovery and acquisitions" — CEO Ryan McInerney, 2026-10-27). Visa is ~1.56x Mastercard's size across volume/transactions/revenue (Axis VMLM index).

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk — score 7/10
Moat: largest network on earth — 5.2bn credentials, 281bn transactions/yr, ~80% processing penetration globally, acceptance in 200+ countries, plus the trust/security/data layer (data processing +17% in FY2026 vs +9% transactions). No single customer can build this; even Capital One, after acquiring Discover (May 2025), still issues mainly on Visa/Mastercard.
Real threat, honestly weighed: same as MA, but Visa's defence is arguably further along in volume terms — stablecoin settlement surpassed a $25bn annualised run rate, "tenfold increase year over year" (Q4 FY2026 call, 2026-10-27; ~$20bn run rate reported Sep 2026, +~200% YoY); pilots with MVB Financial/Velocity for stablecoin settlement inside Visa Direct (Sep 2026), VisaNet data + blockchain lending pilot with Credit Coop, first stablecoin-linked credential with Norwegian Block Exchange, partnership with Stripe-owned Bridge. The honest caveat: stablecoins "solve movement of value, not distribution" — they lack chargebacks, credit, rewards and ubiquitous acceptance — so the threat is to the settlement slice and to interchange over years, not to the network tomorrow. DOJ antitrust suit (filed Sep 2024, alleging monopolisation of US debit) is a live overhang — a court-ordered opening of debit routing would be a genuine disintermediation event. Score 7/10: the hardest network in payments to replicate, one notch above MA on scale, docked for the DOJ case and the interchange itself being the target.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- Fiscal Q4 2026 beat, 2026-10-27: net revenue $12.1bn +13%, adj EPS $3.48 +17% (beat by 1.5%); payments volume +9%, cross-border ex-intra-Europe +14% (travel +13% in Q4, helped by FIFA World Cup period in July; US payments volume +10%).
- FY2026 record revenue $45.9bn +15%; commercial payments volume +12% for the year, "fastest annual growth in eight years ex post-pandemic recovery."
- Value-added services $14bn for FY2026, +30% in constant dollars, more than double FY2023 level.
- Stablecoin settlement >$25bn annualised run rate, 10x YoY; Visa Direct >15bn transactions.
- Cash/investments $14.2bn at 2026-09-30. No major analyst upgrade found in this window.

### 6. Risks
- Disintermediation: stablecoins/A2A/AI-agent routing (Citrini Feb 2026 warning) targeting 2–3% interchange; Visa processed ~$17tn annual volume, so even small share loss is material in dollars.
- Renewal wave: ~25% of payments volume up for renewal in FY2027 — client incentives already +21% in Q4 to ~$5.1bn (29.7% of gross revenue lines); renewals could push incentives higher and compress net revenue yield.
- Regulation/antitrust: DOJ debit monopolisation suit (active); proposed 10% credit-card interest-rate cap; EU fee pressure.
- Cyclicality: consumer spending slowdown hits payments volume (+9%) and cross-border travel directly.
- Valuation already prices quality: fwd PE 24.7x, +25% over 12m — no shortage catalyst to re-rate it explosively.

### 7. Shortage score: 2/10
**Verdict:** The #1 payments network compounding at low-double digits with a fast-growing services layer — a quality hold, not an explosive shortage bet; the real debate is whether the toll survives stablecoins and the DOJ, not whether supply is scarce.

## CBOE — Cboe Global Markets

### 1. Shortage thesis
Moderate — the closest thing to a genuine demand-surge story in this batch, but the "shortage" is of volatility-hedging capacity, not a physical product. Demand for SPX index options is surging faster than any competing venue can absorb because Cboe is the *only* venue that can legally list them: index-options ADV +24% YoY to 6.1m contracts in Q3 2026, with "ADV records across several products," 0DTE SPX at a record 65% of overall SPX activity, SPX ADV +26% to 4.9m contracts, SPX 0DTE ADV a record 3.2m contracts (+34%), XSP (Mini-SPX) volume more than doubled YoY (251k/day), Global Trading Hours volume +130% (Asia now >30% of GTH) — Q3 2026 earnings call, 2026-10-30. Retail SPX 0DTE volume +46% YoY (vs +20% institutional), retail now ~58% of 0DTE volume. The constraint is not Cboe's capacity but the market's appetite for same-day hedging — and Cboe owns the only pipe. Price evidence: total options revenue per contract +9% to 30.7¢; index-options RPC +3% — pricing power via mix, not a posted price hike.

### 2. Backlog / order book
No backlog — exchanges do not report an order book; revenue is flow-driven (ADV × RPC). **Not found** as a backlog figure. Closest forward indicators: raised 2026 organic net revenue growth outlook to high-teens (from mid-to-high-teens) — Q3 2026 call; the 25-year S&P DJI licence through 2051 locks in the revenue stream rather than a booked pipeline. Note the flow is not contracted: a volatility drought cuts it (see Risks).

### 3. Category position
#1 in US options by a wide margin on its proprietary franchise, but not #1 in all equities trading. Total options market share 30.5% in Q3 2026 (down slightly from 30.9% a year earlier — multi-listed options are competitive); US equities exchange share 8.9% (off-exchange 21.3%). The proprietary position is what matters: Cboe is the *sole* venue for SPX options, VIX options/futures and Mini-SPX — a legal monopoly on the world's most-traded index derivative (SPX record 970.6m contracts in 2025, ADV 3.9m, +25% YoY). No #2 exists for SPX options; the #2 in US options overall is a multi-listed competitor (Nasdaq/ NYSE Arca complex) that cannot list SPX.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk — score 9/10
Moat: exclusive licence from S&P Dow Jones Indices — extended 2026-09-29 for 25 years through 2051 (announced Sep 29–30, 2026; 8-K filed), the longest agreement ever, covering SPX, Mini-SPX, VIX and S&P binary options, with exclusivity carrying over to new products including tokenised options. CEO Craig Donohue, Q3 call: "we will continue to have the exclusive rights to offer trading in SPX options." This is a contractual monopoly, not just a network effect. Could a customer in-source it? No — a bank or broker cannot create an SPX option; only Cboe can list it, and clearing/liquidity are centralised on Cboe. Could a substitute route around it? Partially: SPY/ETF options (multi-listed), OTC variance, futures, or a rival index (Nasdaq-100 options at Nasdaq) — but none replicates SPX's liquidity, tax treatment (Section 1256, 60/40) and 0DTE ecosystem, and the 0DTE complex is self-reinforcing (market-makers, retail flow, GTH overnight session +117%). The 2027 royalty reset is guided as "de minimis" to net revenue growth. Score 9/10 — docked one point because the *demand* is discretionary (volatility-driven flow, not a must-have input) and because multi-listed share slipped.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- 25-year S&P DJI exclusive licence extension through 2051 (2026-09-29/30) — removed the existential overhang; stock +5.17% on 2026-09-30 to ~$275, then to ~$297 by Oct 8.
- Analyst upgrades on the deal: Morgan Stanley double-upgraded to Overweight from Underweight (Oct 9, 2026, per CNBC); TD Cowen upgraded to Buy, target $334; Goldman Sachs dropped Sell to Neutral ($300 target); Barclays kept Overweight ("meaningful positive").
- Q3 2026 earnings beat, 2026-10-30: net revenue $712.2m +18%, adj EPS $3.63 +36% (beat $3.43 consensus by 5.8%); GAAP EPS record $4.08 (aided by $71.5m gain on sale of Cboe Australia to TMX, closed Jul 31); derivatives net revenue +22% to $402m; options net revenue +23% to $469.5m; Data Vantage record $179.1m +13%; Global FX +23% to $27.7m. Guidance raised to high-teens organic growth.
- New products: SEC approvals in Oct 2026 for securities-based KPI binary contracts on 23 companies (Robinhood exclusive initial launch broker, fees waived through end-2026); KPI contracts announced at Robinhood HOOD Summit, 2026-09-30. Prediction-market expansion.
- Buyback: $1bn total authorisation incl. $550m increase approved Oct 2026.
- Early-October vol spike: VIX call volume hit its highest daily level in >2 years (management, Q3 call).

### 6. Risks
- Volatility cyclicality: VIX options ADV only +3% in Q3 ("stable volatility environment," VIX in a tight 14–20 range); futures net revenue −5% on lower VIX futures activity. A prolonged low-vol regime cuts the growth engine even as SPX 0DTE holds up.
- 0DTE concentration: 65% of SPX activity is 0DTE; a regulatory crackdown on 0DTE/retail speculation or a blow-up would hit the flagship. Retail is now 58% of 0DTE — the hottest, most sentiment-driven slice.
- Multi-listed competition: total options share slipped to 30.5%; US equities share 8.9% and falling; off-exchange trading at 21.3%.
- KPI/prediction-market products are unproven and regulatory-timing-dependent.
- Valuation: +42% over 12m — the licence extension is now priced in; the next leg needs vol or continued 0DTE growth, not just the moat.

### 7. Shortage score: 6/10
**Verdict:** The only name here with a genuine supply-constrained franchise — sole legal venue for SPX/VIX options into a record 0DTE demand surge, now de-risked for 25 years — but the "shortage" is of investor demand for hedging, which can vanish in a calm market, so it is a flow-momentum bet, not a booked backlog.

## MSFT — Microsoft

### 1. Shortage thesis
Strong — the clearest genuine shortage in this batch. Azure demand is outrunning Microsoft's ability to build datacenters. CFO Amy Hood, Q1 FY2027 earnings call, 2026-10-28: "Customer demand continues to exceed available supply." The same sentence has now run for ~2 years with the catch-up date repeatedly pushed back — and on the Oct 2026 call management gave *no* date for supply catching up (per Convexity/Trefis summaries of the call). Concrete evidence: (a) capex incl. finance leases $45.2bn in the quarter came in below the "over $50bn" guide because "some third-party capacity deliveries we expected in Q1 were delayed" (Hood) — capacity Microsoft had paid/planned for did not arrive; some December-quarter third-party capacity also slipped into H2 FY2027; (b) Azure still grew 48% to $33.2bn (first-ever dollar disclosure) *despite* the shortfall, because efficiency gains (GPU/CPU fleet optimisation, Foundry monetisation, faster dock-to-live — GB300 deploy times −20% QoQ in largest regions) freed capacity that "customers immediately consumed"; (c) calendar-2026 capex ~$170bn, with Q2 FY2027 capex guided >$50bn — the buildout is accelerating into the shortage, not slowing; (d) 24 new datacenters added across five continents in the quarter (Q4 FY2026: 31 added, 88 for the year, +1 GW brought online). Chips, power and construction are the binding constraints — a textbook structural shortage with price evidence in the form of rationing-by-allocation rather than a posted price rise.

### 2. Backlog / order book
Commercial remaining performance obligation (RPO): **$677bn at 2026-09-30, +73% YoY** (Q1 FY2027 earnings release, 2026-10-28); $678bn at 2026-06-30 (+84% YoY). Weighted-average duration 2.3 years (Q4 FY2026 call); ~30% recognised in the next 12 months, that near-term slice +37% YoY; the beyond-12-months portion +112% (Q4 FY2026). How far out it is booked: effectively ~2.3 years weighted average, with multi-year AI commitments.
Essential caveat (do not ignore): RPO ex-OpenAI grew only +25% (vs +73% headline) and nearly all sequential RPO growth came from outside frontier-model companies in Q4 — i.e. a large share of the headline backlog is long-dated OpenAI contracts signed in the prior year, and the backlog grew only ~8% since December 2025 (the quarter the OpenAI contract landed). ~90% of FY2026 Microsoft Cloud revenue came from customers outside frontier-model companies, so the *revenue* is diversified even if the *backlog* is concentrated. Also new: Microsoft is an Anthropic shareholder and Anthropic has committed to buy Azure capacity.

### 3. Category position
#2 in cloud infrastructure, behind AWS, and closing the growth gap. Synergy Research via CRN, Q3 2026: AWS 26% share (down from 29% a year earlier), Azure 19% (down 1pp from 20%), Google Cloud gaining (+3pp). Q2 2026 Synergy: AWS ~28%, Azure ~20%, GCP ~15%. Azure revenue growth (+48% in Q1 FY2027; AWS +39% to $46bn in the same quarter) is faster than AWS, but AWS remains #1 by revenue ($46bn vs $33.2bn in the quarter). In the broader Microsoft stack (M365/Entra/Windows) Azure is #1 with enterprise incumbents — the moat is distribution, not raw cloud share.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk — score 7/10
Moat: (a) the enterprise stack — M365, Entra ID, Windows, and now Foundry/Copilot — makes Azure the default for existing Microsoft shops; switching clouds means re-platforming identity, productivity and AI tooling at once; (b) scale of the buildout itself (~$170bn capex in calendar 2026) is unreplicable by any challenger except AWS/Google; (c) Foundry revenue more than tripled in Q1 FY2027 — the AI-agent platform is becoming the commercialisation hub, with token/CPU monetisation improving per workload.
In-sourcing/substitution threats, honestly weighed: (a) the biggest AI customers *are* building their own capacity — OpenAI's multi-cloud move (models now available through Google Cloud) and its own compute ambitions, Anthropic's multi-cloud posture, and hyperscaler customers training on their own silicon all cap Azure's pricing power with frontier labs; (b) enterprises can multi-cloud or repatriate steady workloads; (c) the "shortage" itself invites substitution — customers wait-listed on Azure buy from AWS/GCP/neoclouds instead. But for the broad enterprise base (90% of cloud revenue ex-frontier), there is no realistic in-source path to a global datacenter fleet. Score 7/10: irreplaceable for the enterprise core, genuinely contestable at the frontier-model margin where the backlog is concentrated.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- Q1 FY2027 earnings beat, 2026-10-28: total revenue $92.3bn +19% (beat ~$90.6bn consensus), adj EPS $4.89 vs $4.73 expected, GAAP EPS $4.92 +32%; net income $36.6bn +32%. First-ever Azure dollar disclosure ($33.2bn, +48%, ahead of 44–45% guide); Microsoft Cloud $63.9bn +30%; Agents & Infra segment $76.8bn +25% (new reporting structure).
- Guidance raised: Q2 FY2027 Azure growth 49–50% constant currency; total revenue $96.0–97.1bn.
- Foundry revenue more than tripled; Copilot throughput +50%+ QoQ on workload optimisation.
- Capex $45.2bn (below >$50bn guide only on third-party delays); Q2 capex guided >$50bn, calendar-2026 capex ~$170bn.
- Analyst price-target increases ahead of the print (per WindowsForum preview, Oct 2026); stock rose ~4% on the print (FinanceFeeds, 2026-10-29). Screen shows analyst upside +18.1% — the highest of the four names.

### 6. Risks
- Capex/return risk — the central debate: ~$170bn calendar-2026 capex, free cash flow −27% in Q1 FY2027 to $18.6bn; if AI demand cools or pricing falls, the datacenter fleet becomes stranded capacity with heavy depreciation. Management's answer (Nadella) is fungibility — one pool of capacity allocated to highest-value use — but that is unproven at this scale.
- Backlog concentration: headline RPO +73% flatters; ex-OpenAI +25%, and OpenAI is simultaneously diversifying to Google Cloud. A renegotiation or slowdown by one or two frontier customers moves the backlog materially.
- Supply execution: third-party capacity delays have already slipped twice (Q1 and December-quarter deliveries into H2); power/grid constraints and GPU availability are outside Microsoft's control.
- Competition: AWS still #1 and Google Cloud is gaining share fastest (+3pp in Q3 2026); Anthropic/Google and OpenAI/Google deals show the frontier labs multi-sourcing.
- Valuation/macro: fwd PE 22.2x is the lowest of the four (screen), but that is on earnings that now carry a much heavier capex/depreciation load; a cyclical enterprise IT slowdown would hit Azure's 49–50% guide.
- Devices & Consumer segment −3% — a small drag, not thesis-relevant.

### 7. Shortage score: 8/10
**Verdict:** The only true capacity shortage in the batch — demand exceeds supply by management's own words, a $677bn backlog (+73%) is booked ~2.3 years out, and the constraint is physical (chips, power, construction) — but the backlog is flattered by OpenAI concentration and the shortage is being answered with $170bn of capex, so the explosive leg depends on demand staying ahead of that buildout.
