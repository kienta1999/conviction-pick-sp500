## LLY — Eli Lilly

### 1. Shortage thesis
Demand surge, yes; structural shortage, **no longer**. The 2024 shortage is over: FDA declared the tirzepatide shortage resolved 2 Oct 2024 (reaffirmed 19 Dec 2024) and semaglutide resolved 21 Feb 2025 (NMSC GLP-1 landscape, Oct 2026: "Supply is no longer the constraint"). What remains is a demand surge Lilly can now mostly serve: Q3 2026 volume rose 53% YoY while realised prices fell 15% (Lilly Q3 release, 29 Oct 2026). The constraint has flipped from supply to price/coverage: ~55% of new Zepbound prescriptions in Q2 2026 were self-pay, and Medicare pricing was set at $245/month under the Nov 2025 White House agreement, with Foundayo/Zepbound covered under the Medicare GLP-1 Bridge programme from 1 Jul 2026. Capacity is still being built ahead of demand — $4.5bn additional Indiana (Lebanon) investment announced 6 May 2026, total Indiana commitments >$21bn since 2020, >$50bn US since 2020; Lebanon API site to open 2027, described by Lilly as the largest API site in US history, to make Zepbound/Mounjaro/Foundayo/retatrutide. No current sold-out / lead-time evidence was found; instead Lilly cut Zepbound vial cash prices (to $299/$399/$449).

### 2. Backlog / order book
**Not found — pharma does not report an order backlog.** Closest proxies: Q3 revenue $24.40bn (+39% YoY); Mounjaro $10.72bn (+65%), Zepbound $5.35bn (+49%), combined >$16bn, two-thirds of revenue (Lilly, 29 Oct 2026). No "booked out to [date]" disclosure exists for a drug sold through pharmacies/wholesalers.

### 3. Category position
#1 in incretins, in a two-player market. ~6 of 10 US anti-obesity prescriptions and ~7 of 10 injectable prescriptions in Q2 2026; ~55% incretin share internationally in Q2 2026 (company/SEC-filing summaries, Oct 2026). #2 is Novo Nordisk (Wegovy/Ozempic). Mounjaro+Zepbound were ~65% of Lilly revenue in Q2 2026 ($14.9bn of $23.0bn). Foundayo (oral orforglipron, FDA-approved 1 Apr 2026) was ~1 in 4 new US incretin starts by late Jul 2026, but Novo's Wegovy pill (launched 5 Jan 2026, >5m US prescriptions by 4 Aug) leads the oral race so far.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk — score 7/10
Moat: patented tirzepatide (dual GIP/GLP-1), peptide API + sterile fill-finish scale that "takes years and billions to build" (Lilly invested early alongside Novo), plus the pipeline — retatrutide (see §5). Customers (patients/payers) cannot in-source a patented drug; the realistic bypass is the **competitor's** drug, not self-manufacture: Novo's semaglutide is a direct substitute, and oral pills lower the manufacturing barrier vs injectables. Compounding was a bypass while shortages lasted; with shortages resolved and FDA proposing (30 Apr 2026) not to add these APIs to the 503B bulks list, that route is narrowing. Score 7, not higher, because Novo is a genuine #2 with its own scale and the oral transition erodes the injectable-manufacturing moat.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- **Q3 2026 beat + raise (29 Oct 2026):** revenue $24.40bn (+39%, vs ~$22.1–22.6bn expected), non-GAAP EPS $7.48 (vs $5.38 Zacks consensus; +7% YoY despite $4.57/share acquired-IPR&D charges). 2026 revenue guidance raised to **$91–92bn from $85–87bn** (+$5.5bn midpoint); performance-margin guide raised to 52.0–52.5% (from 49.0–50.5%); non-GAAP EPS $35–36 (printed range down $0.50 only because of the $4.57 Q3 deal charges; underlying guide +$4.07). ~$900m of Q3 revenue was non-recurring (incl. $410m final Jardiance milestones + rebate-estimate adjustments).
- **Retatrutide beats Ozempic head-to-head (28 Oct 2026, TRANSCEND-T2D-2, n=1,298, 80 wks):** 12 mg cut A1C 2.2 pts vs Ozempic 1.8 pts, weight −20.9% vs −9.4% (9 mg: −19.5%); FDA filing planned Q1 2027. Caveat: discontinuations for side effects 7.9% (12 mg) vs 2.7% Ozempic. Sixth positive Phase 3 readout; TRIUMPH-1 obesity weight loss up to −28.3% (May 2026).
- Medicare GLP-1 Bridge coverage (Foundayo + Zepbound) driving US demand; management cited it in the Q3 raise. Shares +~4% pre-market on the print.

### 6. Risks
Price, not volume, is now the headwind: realised prices −15% in Q3 (−13% in Q2) and management expects net realised price to remain a headwind into 2027. Concentration: one molecule (tirzepatide) is two-thirds of revenue. Novo competition in orals (Wegovy pill leading) and in next-gen (CagriSema); retatrutide tolerability. Policy: US drug-pricing pressure ($245 Medicare price), patent expiry in the 2030s. Valuation: ~$1.1tn market cap (first pharma to pass $1tn, Nov 2025) — much of the GLP-1 runway is priced in; the "explosive" leg now depends on retatrutide, not on resolving a shortage that no longer exists.

### 7. Shortage score: 4/10
**Verdict:** The best momentum name in the batch on growth and catalysts, but the shortage thesis is stale — demand is surging and supply has caught up, so the bet is on volume + retatrutide, at a price that already assumes it.

## WST — West Pharmaceutical Services

### 1. Shortage thesis
Indirect GLP-1 picks-and-shovels shortage, not a West product shortage. Driver: biologics/GLP-1 makers need high-value elastomer stoppers/plungers and ready-to-use components, and EU GMP Annex 1 (fully effective Aug 2023) is forcing upgrades to sterile RTU products — a barrier favouring compliant incumbents. Evidence: Proprietary Products +16.6% organic in Q3 2026, led by High-Value Product (HVP) Components +20.3% organic to $466.7m (53% of total sales); HVP Delivery Devices +26.4% organic. GLP-1 elastomers are ~9% of total sales (company disclosure cited Oct 2026). Management is investing in HVP finishing capacity in Europe and the US. No "sold out / lead time" quote was found; the signal is sustained 20% organic HVP growth and price increases contributing to sales, not a published backlog. KeyBanc (Jan 2026) sees limited risk from oral GLP-1s through 2030 — injectables remain the primary driver, orals assumed ~30% of GLP-1 market eventually.

### 2. Backlog / order book
**Not found — West does not disclose a backlog/order-book figure.** Proxies: Q3 net sales $874.5m (+8.7%, +11.9% organic); FY2026 revenue guidance raised to $3.41–3.42bn (from $3.345–3.38bn), organic growth 11.3–11.6%; Q4 guide $820–830m (+6–7% organic). No "booked out to" horizon disclosed.

### 3. Category position
#1 in its niche (elastomer containment for injectables) within a fragmented market, but not a monopolist. Parenteral packaging: Tier-1 trio Schott/Gerresheimer/West ~39% of market combined (Future Market Insights, 2025); top-3 players collectively ~20% by a narrower measure — figures vary by definition, so treat as "co-leader, not dominant". Named rivals: Stevanato Group, Schott Pharma, Gerresheimer, Datwyler, BD (prefillable syringes). West's edge is proprietary elastomer formulations + Crystal Zenith cyclic-olefin polymer + regulatory/analytical services bundled with the component.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk — score 7/10
Moat: a stopper/plunger is a drug-contact component — switching supplier requires re-validation, stability studies and regulatory filings for that specific drug, so pharma customers dual-source cautiously and rarely switch mid-product-life; West's formulations are proprietary and its plants are Annex 1-compliant. Customers cannot realistically in-source: building sterile elastomer capacity + regulatory expertise for a non-core input makes no economic sense, and no big-pharma customer has announced doing so. The concrete threat is **dual-sourcing / share shift**, not in-sourcing: Stevanato and Schott are adding US/EU capacity, and an oral-GLP-1 shift (if orals took >30% share) would shrink the injectable-component pool. Also, large customers (Lilly/Novo) have pricing power over a supplier. Score 7 — sticky and hard to replicate per-drug, but substitutable at the margin by named peers.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- **Q3 2026 beat + raise (29 Oct 2026):** sales $874.5m (+8.7%) vs ~$826–828m consensus; adjusted diluted EPS $2.66 (+35.7%, vs ~$2.18–2.21 expected, ~+20% beat); GAAP EPS $3.02 (+57.3%, incl. $55.7m net gain on SmartDose 3.5mL sale to AbbVie, 1 Jul 2026). Gross margin 39.7% (+310bp); adjusted operating margin 25.3% (+420bp). FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance raised to **$9.38–9.48 (from $8.85–9.05)**.
- Dividend raised 18% to $0.26/share (declared 19 Oct 2026). Buybacks: $231m in Q3, $685m YTD (2.4m shares).
- Drags inside the beat: West Vantage (contract manufacturing) −6.6% organic on expiry of a glucose-monitoring contract + a May cyber incident causing production downtime (recovered deferred revenue in Q3); SmartDose 3.5mL line sold, $11.8m Q3 sales, none expected for the rest of 2026 — a 2027 growth headwind.

### 6. Risks
Earnings-quality flag (see box below) — 9M net income +40% while operating cash flow fell ~20%. Customer concentration in GLP-1 (Lilly/Novo order patterns + destocking cycles have hurt West before). Oral-GLP-1 shift is the structural threat to the injectable-component pool. West Vantage is shrinking/transitioning. Valuation: fwd PE 37.8x for ~11% organic growth is demanding; the stock is +37% in 12m and −5.8% off its high, and it fell ~4% on 5 Nov 2026 (to $392.48) despite the beat — the market may already be pricing the HVP recovery.

### 7. Shortage score: 6/10
**Verdict:** The purest shortage-adjacent play in the batch — a picks-and-shovels supplier riding a real capacity-constrained input — but it reports no backlog, and the earnings-quality flag (below) needs clearing before treating the 40% profit jump as cash-backed.

#### Earnings-quality flag — RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN: verdict
**Flag HOLDS on the numbers, but is partly explained and not (yet) a value-trap verdict — monitor, don't dismiss.**
Primary source (10-Q for quarter ended 30 Sep 2026, filed 29 Oct 2026): 9M net income **$506.8m vs $361.6m (+40.1%)**; 9M operating cash flow **$403.4m vs $503.7m (−19.9%)**; accounts receivable **$748.2m at 30 Sep 2026 vs $574.4m at 31 Dec 2025 (+$173.8m, +30.3%)** on 9M sales +14.2% ($2,591.7m vs $2,269.1m) — receivables clearly outran sales. Inventories rose only $443.9m → $464.7m (+4.7%), so the drag is receivables, not stockpiling. Free cash flow $278.4m vs $293.9m (−5%). Mitigants: (i) 9M net income includes a **$55.7m non-cash gain** on the SmartDose sale — strip it and the NI/OCF gap narrows; (ii) Q3 alone looks healthier — Q3 OCF $189.5m (YCharts) and Q3 FCF $150.4m vs $133.9m a year earlier, i.e. the cash shortfall is a H1/9M phenomenon, not a Q3 one; (iii) cash also fell ($791.3m → $464.1m) largely because of **$685m of buybacks**, a choice, not an operating leak. No company disclosure was found attributing the receivables build to a specific customer or terms change. **One-line verdict: RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN — confirmed arithmetically (AR +30% vs sales +14%, OCF −20% while NI +40%), driven by receivables plus a $55.7m non-cash gain, with Q3 cash flow recovering; a yellow flag to watch into Q4, not proof of fake earnings.**

## ISRG — Intuitive Surgical

### 1. Shortage thesis
Demand surge, not a shortage. There is no product shortage: Intuitive places robots as fast as hospitals buy them — 546 da Vinci placements in Q3 2026 (+28% YoY), incl. 315 da Vinci 5. The closest thing to a supply constraint is historical: da Vinci 5 was on phased launch with supply-chain optimisation in 2024, and force-feedback instrument supply constraints were flagged (Oct 2025) as extending into early 2026 — no current sold-out/lead-time evidence was found. The demand driver is structural: robotic procedures are only ~5% of world surgeries, and hospitals are in a da Vinci 5 upgrade cycle (7,000+ Xi/X systems not yet upgraded). Procedures: da Vinci +14%, Ion +31% in Q3; >10,000 procedures/day on average.

### 2. Backlog / order book
**Not found — Intuitive does not disclose a system backlog.** Proxies: installed base 12,094 da Vinci systems (+12% YoY) + 1,152 Ion = ~13,246 total; Q3 systems revenue implied by 546 placements at ~$1.5m ASP (Q2 ASP $1.5m vs $1.44m a year earlier, Zacks Oct 2025). No "booked out to" horizon; placements, not backlog, are the demand signal.

### 3. Category position
Clear #1. ~70–80% global share of minimally-invasive robotic platforms (industry estimates cited Oct 2026); ~86% of the US robotic-surgery systems-and-accessories market (and ~58% on a narrower systems-only measure). #2 is Medtronic's Hugo (FDA urology clearance Dec 2025 only; ~12% US share in one table) — 26 years behind da Vinci's first approval, and still limited to urologic procedures (~230k US cases/yr) vs da Vinci's broad indications. J&J's Ottava is nearing submission. No rival matches the installed base.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk — score 9/10
Moat: installed base + surgeon training + razor-and-blade. >60,000 surgeons trained on da Vinci; 84% of revenue is recurring (instruments/accessories $1.80bn in Q3, +18%, plus services $482m); each system consumes ~$250–400k/yr in single-use instruments plus ~$200–400k/yr in service. Hospitals cannot in-source a surgical robot — no hospital builds its own; the only bypass is a rival robot, and switching means retraining surgeons, rebuilding workflows and accepting a thinner evidence base. The antitrust case itself is evidence of the moat: the $450m settlement (see §5) concerned third-party remanufacturing of da Vinci instruments and third-party servicing — i.e. the fight is over who gets to service the installed base, not over replacing it. Score 9 — the highest in the batch — docked one point only because Hugo/Ottava are now real, funded, approved-or-near rivals.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- **Q3 2026 beat (20 Oct 2026):** revenue $3.05bn (+22%, vs ~$2.91bn consensus), non-GAAP EPS $3.00 (+25%, vs ~$2.64–2.65 consensus); 84% recurring revenue; non-GAAP operating margin 41.7%. Placements +28%; da Vinci 5 now at >2,000 sites; SP procedures +55%; Ion +31%; international procedures +20% (India +42%, benign ex-US +41%).
- **Negatives in the same print (why the stock fell ~7% after-hours / ~13% at one point):** $450m pre-tax charge to settle a US antitrust class action over third-party instrument remanufacturing/servicing (GAAP EPS $1.49 vs $1.95 a year earlier; settlement subject to court approval, no admission of liability); management will **cut da Vinci instrument/accessory revenue per procedure by ~3–5%** (extended-use programme + volume pricing from H1 2027, plus a China charge-code cut from Nov 2026) — a deliberate price give-back on the highest-value recurring line; 2026 da Vinci procedure guidance narrowed to 14–15% (from 13.5–15.5%), midpoint held, with US growth only +10% (lowest since COVID, per Wells Fargo, on a tough +16% comp).
- Buybacks: $2.94bn in Q3 at ~$377/share average. da Vinci 5 cardiac clearances; Force Feedback used in ~8% of da Vinci 5 cases; Penang (Malaysia) plant planned.

### 6. Risks
The pricing give-back is the thesis risk: management is voluntarily cutting per-procedure economics to defend volume in benign procedures — if volume doesn't respond, the annuity shrinks. US procedure growth decelerating (+10% in Q3); ACA subsidy changes, payer shifts, China tender pressure. Tariffs: ~0.9pp gross-margin drag in FY26 guide; "material adverse impact" if tariffs persist. Competition is finally real (Hugo approved Dec 2025; Ottava coming). Valuation: fwd PE 37.6x, stock −13% in 12m and −21% off its high — the market is already discounting the deceleration; an "explosive" leg needs procedure growth to re-accelerate, not just placements.

### 7. Shortage score: 5/10
**Verdict:** The highest-quality moat in the batch (9/10 irreplaceability) and a genuine upgrade-cycle demand surge, but it is not a shortage play — and management just told the market it will charge less per procedure, which caps the explosive-return case until volume proves it out.

## PM — Philip Morris International

### 1. Shortage thesis
**Expired shortage.** ZYN was in a genuine 15-month shortage (summer 2024 peak) that PMI confirmed over in Jul 2025, with distributor restocking complete and retail availability "approaching normal" (per-store sales ~82 cans/week vs 65–70 during 2024). The response — Owensboro expansion to ~900m cans/yr capacity plus a new $600m Aurora, Colorado plant (ground broken Dec 2024; first commercial production Sep 2025; officially opened Jul 2026 as a $1.2bn campus through 2028, 780k sq ft, ~500 jobs) — has now overshot: in **Apr 2026 PMI scaled back Owensboro ZYN production from 24/7 to 24/5** "amid demand normalization". No current shortage, sold-out or lead-time evidence; the Q3 story is the opposite — PMI is spending to defend share, not rationing supply.

### 2. Backlog / order book
**Not found — consumer staples does not report a backlog.** Proxies: Q3 HTU shipments 41.5bn units (+1.5%); US ZYN shipments 3.2bn pouches (+4.2%, +13% ex last September's promotion); VEEV +62%. No booked-out horizon exists for this category.

### 3. Category position
#1 in both smoke-free categories it leads. IQOS: ~72–77% of the global heated-tobacco category (Euromonitor/PMI-cited; ~76% per PMI FY2025 release), ~68% in Japan; IQOS adjusted IMS share of cigarette+HTU volume 11.6% (+0.9pp) in Q3, and IQOS passed Marlboro as the #1 nicotine brand by volume in markets where both are present (Q1 2026). Marlboro: record 11.4% global category share. ZYN: #1 US nicotine-pouch brand; US category offtake share +1.4pp Jun→Sep 2026. Smoke-free is ~42% of revenue (9M 2026), in 110 markets, ~43m adult users; management targets two-thirds smoke-free revenue by 2030 (independent forecasts ~49%). #2s: Japan Tobacco's Ploom and BAT's glo in heated tobacco; BAT's Velo and Altria's on! in US pouches.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk — score 7/10
The "customer in-sourcing" frame barely applies — PMI's customers are consumers and distributors, not OEMs. The moat is brand + regulation + distribution: IQOS's heated-tobacco IP and device ecosystem, ZYN's first-ever FDA Modified Risk Tobacco Product authorisation for a nicotine pouch (20 variants, 30 Jun 2026 — a regulatory precedent rivals must now match), and a $16bn+ smoke-free R&D investment since 2008. A consumer can switch pouch/heat brand easily (low switching cost — the product is a commodity pouch), and illicit/trade-down alternatives exist; but no customer can "build" an IQOS/ZYN equivalent without the IP, the MRTP claim and the distribution. The concrete bypass is regulatory: IQOS ILUMA still awaits FDA PMTA authorisation for the US — PMI's largest unaddressed market — and flavour bans (Poland) and excise hikes (Japan) have already dented volumes. Score 7 — dominant share, but in categories with low consumer switching costs and heavy regulatory gatekeeping.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- **Q3 2026 beat (21 Oct 2026):** net revenue $11.44bn (+5.5%, +7.1% organic, vs ~$11.32–11.42bn expected); adjusted EPS $2.36 (+5.4%, vs ~$2.30 consensus, top of company's Sep guide); adjusted operating income >$5bn for the first time, 44.7% margin (+1.6pp). IQOS adjusted IMS +7.1% (from +5.1% in Q2; ~+10% ex Japan/Poland); combustibles pricing +8.9% on flat volume vs a −1.1% industry; Marlboro record 11.4% share.
- **But management spent the beat (Q3 call):** flagship ZYN moves from 15- to 20-pouch cans at the 15-pouch price (~25% less revenue per pouch); US SG&A ~$370m in Q3, +~10% sequentially in Q4; **Q4 US adjusted operating income expected around zero or slightly negative**. Full-year EPS guidance maintained at $8.29–8.44 but delivery now expected at the midpoint/lower half of the 7–9% organic OI and 7.5–9.5% EPS growth ranges, with ~1pp of geopolitical cost (Middle East energy inflation, Ukraine disruption).
- Aurora $1.2bn campus officially opened Jul 2026; FDA MRTP for 20 ZYN variants 30 Jun 2026; dividend +8.8% to $1.60/quarter. 2026–28 targets: 6–8% organic revenue, 9–11% adjusted EPS growth.

### 6. Risks
The ZYN trade is now share-for-price: volume gains bought with a 25% per-pouch price cut that weighs on revenue for three quarters of 2027, plus a Q4 US profit wipe-out — the opposite of pricing power. IQOS growth is hostage to Japan excise timing (pantry-loading flattered Q3; underlying Japan IMS only +1.3% ex loading) and Poland flavour restrictions. Cigarettes still fund the transition; excise/tax and regulation can move volumes faster than PMI can. Valuation is the least demanding in the batch (fwd PE 22.3x), but that reflects a 5–7% organic grower, not an explosive one.

### 7. Shortage score: 2/10
**Verdict:** A dominant-share, high-margin transition story whose actual shortage ended over a year ago — PMI is now cutting ZYN prices to buy share, so there is no scarcity premium left to capture.
