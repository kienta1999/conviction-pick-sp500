## NEM — Newmont Corporation (gold mining)

### 1. Shortage thesis
Price surge, not a physical volume shortage. World Gold Council, *Gold Demand Trends Q3 2026* (3 Nov 2026): total Q3 demand 1,369t (−1% y/y); YTD demand 3,881t, "virtually matches the 2025 record (3,884t)", while value set a record US$566bn. Central-bank buying "jumped to 321t in Q3, eclipsing recent typical quarterly buying levels" (+42% y/y; YTD 675t, +6% y/y), gold ETFs added 211t in Q3 to record holdings of 4,256t. On the supply side, mine production was up 3% y/y to a Q3-record 1,071t — but total supply still edged down 1% because recycling fell 8% y/y to a three-year low of 314t. WGC: "central bank buying likely to at least match 2025" and it expects "a modest supply decline for the full year". Meanwhile jewellery demand fell 17% y/y to 313t (weakest Q3 in the series) and bar/coin fell 19% — high prices are destroying volume demand even as investment demand surges. So the driver is monetary/fiscal-hedge demand meeting flat total supply, not an industrial supply crunch. Newmont's own realised price proves it: Q3 realised gold $4,317/oz, +22% y/y (Q3 2026 earnings, 22 Oct 2026).

### 2. Backlog / order book
Not applicable — gold is sold spot/forward at market price; Newmont reports no order backlog. Closest analogue is reserves: 118.2 Moz proven & probable gold reserves at 31 Dec 2025, ~20 years of output at current rates (company filings, via Metal Pilot analysis 15 Oct 2026). Production is the constraint, not orders: Q3 attributable production 1.302 Moz, −8% y/y; FY2026 guidance maintained at ~5.26 Moz, with Q4 guided strongest at ~1.4 Moz.

### 3. Category position
#1 gold miner in the world. 2025 attributable production 5.89 Moz, vs Agnico Eagle 3.45 Moz and Barrick 3.26 Moz (Farmonaut ranking of 2025 filings); H1 2026: Newmont 2.59 Moz retained #1 despite declines. That is ~1.7x the #2 producer by output, but only ~5–6% of world mine production (~3,300t ≈ 106 Moz in 2025, USGS) — a fragmented commodity market, not a dominant share.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk
Low. Gold is perfectly fungible; any buyer can buy another miner's ounces, an ETF, or physical metal. No customer can be locked in, and no customer needs to in-source mining — they simply buy the metal. The moat is scale + reserve life + low-cost assets (Nevada Gold Mines JV 38.5%, Tanami, Cadia), not irreplaceability: a 20-year reserve base at ~$1,408/oz 2025 AISC vs a $4,317/oz realised price is a margin moat, not a switching-cost moat. Substitute threat: none for gold as a monetary asset, but jewellery/industrial demand is price-elastic (see −17% jewellery volumes above), and a stronger dollar / higher real rates would cut investment demand. **Irreplaceability: 3/10.**

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- Q3 2026 earnings, 22 Oct 2026: revenue $6.36bn (+15.2% y/y, beat $6.01bn consensus), adjusted EPS $2.20 (+28.7% y/y, beat $1.94 consensus by 13.4%, a Q3 record); net income $2.48bn; FCF $1.9bn in Q3, $7.3bn in 9M 2026 (Motley Fool transcript; Zacks 23 Oct 2026).
- Cost guidance improved: FY2026 gold AISC cut to ~$1,520/oz (by-product), ~10% better than initial guidance; Q3 AISC $1,454/oz.
- Tanami Expansion 2 commercial production now expected by end-Q2 2027, earlier than prior estimates; Red Chris block-cave investment decision expected early 2027 (capex $4.2–4.8bn, production not until ~2033).
- Management guided low-to-mid-single-digit production growth starting 2027, exiting a 2026 production trough.
- Offsetting: gold fell 2.3% on 23 Oct to a three-month low near $4,000/oz and the mining complex slid with it (AInvest 24 Oct 2026) — the stock is a levered gold-price proxy, and the price was already retreating from the Q3 average of $4,262/oz.

### 6. Risks
Pure gold-price beta: production fell 8% y/y even in a record-price quarter — earnings are the spread between price and cost, and price has already corrected off highs. AISC is rising y/y on a co-product basis ($1,963/oz, +25.4% y/y in Q3 per Zacks) and Q4 AISC is guided to ~$1,900/oz on heavy sustaining capex and lower silver credits. Jurisdiction/operational risk: El Niño water constraints at Lihir; a live dispute with Barrick over Nevada Gold Mines (Newmont alleged mismanagement / diversion of JV resources to Barrick's Fourmile project, formal default notice 3 Feb 2026; NGM is ~18% of production). Silver guidance for 2027 ~30% lower. Valuation: +53% in 12m already prices in much of the gold rally; if central-bank buying normalises, the multiple compresses with the price.

### 7. Shortage score & verdict
**Shortage score: 5/10. Verdict: A levered gold-price bet with record margins and the deepest reserve base in the sector, not a structural shortage — supply is flat, investment demand is strong, but volume demand is already cracking under high prices.**

## VLO — Valero Energy (independent refining)

### 1. Shortage thesis
Strongest shortage in this batch — a diesel/refined-products shortage, not a crude shortage. Kansas City Fed bulletin (Nov 2026): since the Iran conflict began 28 Feb 2026, oil prices are ~63% higher while gasoline is ~75% and diesel ~93% higher; "diesel crack spread is more than three times year-ago levels" and gasoline crack "a little over double". OilPrice (Oct 2026): US refineries ran ~96% of capacity in Q3; US operable crude-distillation capacity fell to ~18.2m bpd at start-2026, down ~250k bpd y/y, with 130 operable refineries left; EIA expects distillate inventories to stay below the five-year range through much of 2027 and fall below 100m barrels "for the first time in more than two decades". Seeking Alpha (22 Oct 2026): US refinery utilisation hit 98% in late Aug 2026, highest since 2018; distillate exports hit a record 1.9m bpd in early Aug; US retail diesel $6.53/gal in week ending 21 Sep 2026 vs $3.75 a year earlier; NY Harbor diesel crack "surged past $100 per barrel", a record. Drivers: Iran-conflict disruption to Middle East refining/shipping, Ukrainian strikes cutting Russian refining throughput ~30% y/y in June (IEA), China suspending most fuel exports in October, plus US refinery closures (Valero Benicia ~145–170k bpd shutting from Jan–Apr 2026; Phillips 66 LA closed end-2025 — together ~17–18% of California capacity). Quote: "America does not have a shortage of crude oil. It has a shortage of available diesel." (OilPrice). Valero's own print confirms it: Q3 refining margin $39.24/bbl vs $13.14 a year earlier.

### 2. Backlog / order book
Not applicable — refined products are sold spot into a commodity market; no backlog is reported. Closest analogue is utilisation/throughput: Q3 throughput averaged 2.97m bpd (down from 3.09m bpd y/y on turnarounds), at "near-record utilization rates" per CEO Lane Riggs; Q4 throughput guided slightly lower at 2.875–2.975m bpd (company release 22 Oct 2026). The "booked out" signal is 96–98% industry utilisation — there is essentially no spare capacity to sell.

### 3. Category position
Largest independent refiner in the US / North America. ~3.2m bpd throughput capacity across 14 refineries (US, Canada, UK) plus 1.2bn gal/yr renewable diesel and 1.6bn gal/yr ethanol (company profile). That is ~17–18% of US operable capacity (3.2 / 18.2m bpd). Exact #2 share not found in sources checked — Marathon is the largest US refiner overall by capacity and the AgManager note (1 Oct 2026) puts the largest US refiner at ~16% of national capacity with the top four at ~50%, so Valero and Marathon are close at the top and the industry is far less concentrated than nitrogen (top-4 refining ~50% vs top-4 ammonia ~70%). Valero is #1 among independents (non-integrated refiners).

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk
Moderate-high as infrastructure, low as product. Nobody can realistically build a new US refinery quickly — permitting, multi-billion-dollar capex, multi-year build, and California is actively losing capacity (Benicia closure was driven by what the CEO called the "most stringent and difficult" regulatory environment in North America). Valero is even buying/restarting an idled LyondellBasell Houston refinery (165k bpd, by 2027, reported Oct 2026) because restarting beats building. But the product is fungible: customers (drivers, airlines, fleets) buy gasoline/diesel from whoever has it, imports can substitute (at a price), and EVs / demand destruction are the long-run substitute. The moat is scale, complexity (ability to run heavy/sour crude) and Gulf Coast logistics — not customer lock-in. **Irreplaceability: 6/10.**

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- Q3 2026 earnings, 22 Oct 2026 — the best quarter in company history: net income $6.83bn ($23.89/share) vs $1.10bn ($3.53) a year earlier; adjusted EPS $23.90 (+553% y/y, beat ~$21.17 consensus by ~13%); revenue $46.10bn (+43.3% y/y, beat by 23%); refining margin $39.24/bbl (+199% y/y); refining operating income $8.78bn vs $1.61bn; renewable diesel swung to $353m operating income (from −$28m); ethanol at record production (company release; Zacks 22 Oct 2026).
- St. Charles FCC optimisation project completed on schedule and within budget (Q3 call).
- Plan to acquire/restart the idled LyondellBasell Houston refinery (165k bpd) by 2027 (Oct 2026 reports).
- Offsetting: shares fell ~9% on 27 Oct as peers sold off on fears of shrinking fuel margins; management warned a rise in crude prices could compress margins and flagged Russian diesel imports and seasonal gasoline-crack moderation (MarketBeat/Tickeron, Oct 2026). Aardvark Labs downgraded to Underperform from Hold after the print, arguing the $426 reaction close was "21% above the top of our normalized range and match[es] a bull case of eight more quarters at the September-quarter margin".

### 6. Risks
The core risk is mean reversion of a war-driven crack spread: Q3 margin ($39.24/bbl) is ~3x a year ago and the stock is −23.6% off its 52-week high despite the record print — the market is already discounting normalisation. Any Iran ceasefire / Strait of Hormuz reopening, Russian refinery recovery, Chinese export resumption, or a US diesel-export ban (reportedly under consideration, 90 days) would compress cracks fast. Crude-price spikes compress margins from the input side. Regulatory/political risk cuts both ways: windfall scrutiny and margin caps (California SB X1-2) vs DPA-backed restarts. Refinery incidents are real: Port Arthur fire/explosion 23 Mar 2026 ($4m repair costs in Q3). Long-run demand erosion from EVs. Valuation on trailing earnings looks cheap (fwd PE 6.6x) precisely because the market expects earnings to fall — classic value-trap shape if cracks normalise.

### 7. Shortage score & verdict
**Shortage score: 8/10. Verdict: The purest shortage play in the batch — diesel is physically scarce, refineries are running at 96–98% with no spare capacity, and Valero just printed the best quarter in its history — but the shortage is war-driven and the market is already pricing mean reversion, so the explosive upside depends on the disruption lasting.**

## OXY — Occidental Petroleum (E&P, Permian-focused)

### 1. Shortage thesis
Weak as a standalone shortage — OXY sells crude, and crude is not short. EIA: US crude production is on track to average a record 13.8m bpd in 2026 (OilPrice, Oct 2026). What is tight is refined products (see VLO) and, transiently, crude pricing: KC Fed (Nov 2026) — since the Iran conflict began, oil prices are ~63% higher on supply shocks, and global petroleum inventories have fallen ~400m barrels in 2026. OXY's Q3 pre-earnings 8-K (13 Oct 2026) shows worldwide realised oil prices up 32.6% y/y. So OXY benefits from a war-driven oil-price spike, not from a structural inability of supply to meet demand — US shale can and does respond to price, and OPEC spare capacity exists. No capacity-sold-out / lead-time evidence: OXY's own production is flat by design (see below).

### 2. Backlog / order book
Not applicable — crude/NGLs are sold spot; no backlog is reported. Closest analogues: production guidance and inventory. Q3 2026 production guided 1.40–1.44m boe/d and the 13 Oct 8-K confirmed production "within guidance" despite temporary Gulf of America curtailment (third-party gas pipeline constraint; Gulf net sales 110 Mboe/d). Full-year 2026 production outlook ~1.42–1.45m boe/d; Q3 Permian guided 795–815k boe/d. Drilling inventory: CrownRock added ~1,700 undeveloped locations (750 economic below $40 oil), +25% to OXY's sub-$40 inventory (announced Dec 2023). That is years of runway, not a booked order book.

### 3. Category position
Not #1. OXY is one of the largest Permian acreage holders and a top Permian producer, but ExxonMobil (post-Pioneer) and Chevron (post-Hess) are larger overall, and OXY is described as the "ninth-largest energy company in the US" by market cap (~$49.7bn at CrownRock announcement). In the Permian specifically it is a top-tier operator — CrownRock ($12bn, closed Q1 2024) "solidified OXY's dominance in the Midland Basin" — but no source found gives OXY a #1 Permian production share, and its total production (~1.43m boe/d in Q2 2026) is well below ExxonMobil/Chevron. Market share vs #2: not found in a citable form.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk
Very low. Crude oil is the textbook fungible commodity; buyers (refiners) buy the cheapest suitable barrel, and OXY is a price taker. No customer needs OXY specifically, and the "in-sourcing" analogue — majors expanding their own Permian output, or refiners switching crude slates — is routine. The genuine moat is different: (a) low-cost Permian acreage with sub-$40 drilling inventory; (b) a decade of CO2-EOR expertise that lets it squeeze more from mature fields — CEO Richard Jackson's plan is to deploy CO2-EOR at scale across the enlarged Permian footprint to fund debt reduction (Energy Intelligence, Oct 2026); (c) Stratos DAC / 1PointFive carbon-removal optionality. None of these stops a customer bypassing OXY. **Irreplaceability: 3/10.**

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- Q3 2026 earnings due after close Mon 9 Nov 2026, call 10 Nov — the immediate catalyst for this panel. Consensus has risen sharply: Zacks (5 Nov 2026) reports EPS estimate up 22.1% in 30 days to $1.43 (vs $0.64 a year earlier, +123%) on revenue ~$6.73bn; earlier consensus was ~$1.29 on $6.52bn. Production guided 1.40–1.44m boe/d; 8-K (13 Oct) confirmed in-guidance production, domestic LOE $8.54/boe (better than ~$8.75 guided), oil mix 48.8%, but a −$94m hit to operating cash flow from crude collar settlements.
- Analyst momentum: Goldman Sachs upgraded to Buy, $63→$69 (1 Oct 2026); Roth Capital upgraded to Buy, $70 target (16 Oct); Wells Fargo raised target $79→$82, Overweight (14 Sep); UBS $67→$73 (14 Oct); Susquehanna $70→$77; Seaport initiated Buy, $73 (3 Sep); Piper Sandler initiated Neutral, $69 (28 Oct). 13 Buys vs 14 Holds (MarketBeat data, Oct 2026).
- Balance-sheet inflection: OxyChem sold to Berkshire Hathaway for $9.7bn cash, closed 2 Jan 2026; principal debt ~$11.8bn at end-June 2026, down from ~$40bn at end-2019; target ~$15bn met. CEO transition: Vicki Hollub retired June 2026, succeeded by Richard Jackson.
- Berkshire Hathaway owned ~23.6% of OXY by July 2026 (~$12.6bn) plus $10bn of 8% preferred and warrants; Buffett has said he will not seek control.
- Management targets +$1.2bn annual FCF in 2026 and a path to +$4bn by 2030 before higher oil prices, with sustaining capex falling toward ~$4.5bn by 2030 (Q2 2026 commentary).

### 6. Risks
Oil-price dependence dominates: a ceasefire or demand shock would cut the price that drives the entire thesis, and Q3 already showed hedging drag (−$94m collars). Production is flat-to-down y/y (worldwide production forecast 1,416.6 Mboe/d vs 1,465.0 a year earlier, per Zacks) — this is a price-and-efficiency story, not a growth story. Legacy leverage: $11.8bn principal debt plus Berkshire's $10bn 8% preferred is a heavy prior claim on cash flow. Gulf of America operational risk (pipeline constraints, Hurricane Isaias shut 63% of Gulf oil in Oct 2026). DAC/Stratos is capital-hungry and "remains a developing business rather than the core earnings engine". Valuation: +43% in 12m, and the stock fell ~10% in the five days to 5 Nov 2026 (Finnhub) into earnings — the upgrade cycle may already be priced in.

### 7. Shortage score & verdict
**Shortage score: 5/10. Verdict: A well-executed, Berkshire-backed Permian operator riding a war-driven oil-price spike into a heavily upgraded Q3 print on 9 Nov — but crude itself is not structurally short, production is flat, and the explosive case needs oil to stay high.**

## CF — CF Industries (nitrogen fertilizer)

### 1. Shortage thesis
Strong and structural, with multiple independent drivers converging in 2026. CF Q3 2026 release/call (4–5 Nov 2026): "higher average selling prices, supported by a tight global nitrogen supply-demand balance that was further tightened by supply disruptions tied to the conflict with Iran"; "constrained global supply, low channel inventories, robust demand". Specifics: ~9m metric tons of urea supply lost to the global market since February 2026 due to Middle East hostilities (Q3 call); ~37% of Europe's nameplate ammonia capacity reported offline or operating at reduced rates in Oct 2026 (high natural-gas costs); India urea imports expected to exceed 10m metric tons in 2026; North American nitrogen purchasing rebounded in Q3 across North America, India, South America and Europe after customers deferred earlier in the year. CF ran at 97% ammonia capacity utilisation in the first nine months of 2026 — effectively sold out — and UAN sales volume of nearly 2m tons in Q3 was "the highest quarterly volume for this decade". Low-carbon ammonia earns a price premium of over $20/ton. Management: "the global nitrogen supply-demand balance [will] remain constructive for low-cost, low-risk North American producers in the near and longer term". Structural kicker: management raised the incentive urea price needed to justify new capacity to $385/ton NOLA (from $355) — rising capital costs and gas-price shifts mean new supply is expensive and slow to arrive.

### 2. Backlog / order book
No formal backlog is reported (fertilizer is sold via fill programs / spot). Closest analogues: (a) utilisation — 97% of available ammonia capacity in 9M 2026, 98% in H1 2026; (b) "strong July UAN and ammonia fill programs, reflecting low channel inventories" (Q2 commentary, Aug 2026); (c) full-year 2026 gross ammonia production guided ~9.5m tons despite the Yazoo City outage. Low channel inventories + fill-program strength is the industry's version of being booked out — but no figure for months of forward cover was found.

### 3. Category position
Clear #1. CF holds 39% of US domestic ammonia production capacity, vs Nutrien 16%, Koch 10%, LSB 5%; top four hold 70% (farmdoc daily, Univ. of Illinois, 26 May 2026; corroborated AgManager, 1 Oct 2026). HHI 2,010 — "highly concentrated" under 2023 DOJ/FTC guidelines. CF is also described as the world's largest producer of ammonia. In the broader US agricultural macronutrients market CF leads with 16–19% share vs Nutrien 14–17% (BIS Research, 2025). Donaldsonville, Louisiana is the world's largest ammonia production complex. The gap to #2 is ~2.4x in domestic ammonia capacity.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk
High for the product, low for the supplier. Farmers cannot grow crops without nitrogen — there is no substitute for fixed nitrogen at scale, and customers cannot in-source it (building an ammonia plant takes years, billions, gas supply, permits; the incentive price just rose to $385/t). CF's moat is the lowest-cost North American gas feedstock + scale + Donaldsonville + a 39% capacity share. But CF itself is replaceable at the margin: nitrogen is a fungible commodity, and imports — Middle East urea, Chinese urea exports (whose re-entry is a named risk), Russian supply — can and do substitute when prices spike. Low-carbon ammonia (Blue Point, 45Q credits) is CF's attempt to differentiate a commodity. No single customer concentration was found. **Irreplaceability: 7/10** (product irreplaceable, supplier advantaged but not locked-in).

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- Q3 2026 earnings, 4 Nov 2026 (call 5 Nov): net earnings $436m ($2.87/share, +31% y/y) vs $353m ($2.19) a year earlier; adjusted EBITDA $833m (+25% y/y); 9M adjusted EBITDA $3.01bn (vs $2.07bn); net sales $1.86bn (+12% y/y, beat consensus by 5.6%); sales volumes +7% y/y. EPS missed the $2.93 consensus by 2.05% (Zacks, 4 Nov 2026) — a small miss on a strong print; shares rose 6.2% on the day (Finnhub: $118.57 close 5 Nov, +6.17%).
- Q2 2026 (6 Aug 2026): net income $727m ($4.73/share, +99.6% y/y); mid-cycle EBITDA target raised $2.9bn → $3.3bn by 2030; dividend raised 20% to $0.60/quarter (doubling since 2021); $958m repurchased over trailing 12 months (29% of shares retired since 2021). Shares +6.24% on 10 Aug 2026.
- Blue Point One: all environmental permits received July 2026; construction began Aug 2026; $3.7bn total cost ($500m contingency), 1.4–1.5m tons nitrogen-equivalent capacity, completion 2029, with JERA and Mitsui as partners. Low-carbon UAN certifications secured; CBAM certification in progress.
- Analyst: named "#1 Pick" in the fertilizer sector in a 10 Aug 2026 sector report (27.1% net margin, 11% FCF yield, 7.6x fwd PE); UBS raised target to $123, Goldman Sachs maintained $123, top targets $145–$150 (Aug 2026).
- Dividend $0.60/share declared, payable 30 Nov 2026 to holders of record 16 Nov 2026.

### 6. Risks
Cyclicality is the whole story: nitrogen prices are set globally, and the current tightness is partly war-driven (Iran conflict removed ~8–9m tonnes of urea). A ceasefire, Chinese urea export resumption, or European capacity restart would cut prices fast. Urea and UAN average selling prices already fell y/y in Q3 even as ammonia rose — pricing is mixed, not uniformly strong. Operational: Yazoo City complex outage (volumes −13% y/y at the site in Q3; restart slipped to H1 2027 on electrical-gear procurement delays) cost 9M volumes and AN segment swung to a $12m gross-margin loss. Execution: $3.7bn Blue Point is a multi-year bet with cost-overrun risk. Legal/regulatory overhang: a class-action complaint filed 13 Mar 2026 (USDC Colorado, 1:26-cv-01043) alleges nitrogen price-fixing by CF, Nutrien, Koch and Yara since 2021 — unproven, but a real overhang. Valuation: +43% in 12m; the stock is ~15% off its 52-week high ($141.96) after a strong run.

### 7. Shortage score & verdict
**Shortage score: 8/10. Verdict: The most structural shortage in the batch — 97% utilisation, a third of European capacity down, ~9m tonnes of Middle East urea gone, low channel inventories, and a #1 player with 39% of US ammonia capacity — but the tightness is fertiliser-cyclical and war-amplified, so the explosive case needs the supply losses to persist.**
