## MSFT — Microsoft (tentative single pick)

### 1. Commercial RPO $677bn at 30 Sep 2026, +73% YoY, weighted-average duration ~2.3 years — **CONFIRMED**
- Microsoft FY27 Q1 earnings release, 28 Oct 2026 (microsoft.com / news.microsoft.com): "Commercial remaining performance obligation increased 73% to $677 billion."
- Q1 FY2027 earnings call transcript, 28 Oct 2026 (Motley Fool / Microsoft): Amy Hood — "Commercial remaining performance obligation grew 73% to $677 billion… Our RPO has a weighted average duration of 2.3 years and roughly 30% will be recognized in revenue in the next 12 months, up 29% year-over-year. The remaining portion recognized beyond the next 12 months increased 98%."
- Note: RPO was roughly flat vs. the prior quarter ($678bn at 30 Jun 2026); the +73% is year-on-year, not sequential.

### 2. Amy Hood: "customer demand continues to exceed available supply" (Q1 FY2027 call, 28 Oct 2026) — **CONFIRMED**
- Verbatim in the official call transcript (microsoft.com) and the Motley Fool transcript, in the Q2 Azure outlook: "In Azure, we expect revenue growth of 49% to 50% in constant currency with continued efficiency gains across our CPU and GPU fleet, helping to offset some 3P GPU-as-a-Service contracts that have been delayed to H2. Customer demand continues to exceed available supply."
- Also quoted verbatim by the Motley Fool's 31 Oct 2026 earnings review, attributed to CFO Amy Hood.

### 3. Azure revenue $33.2bn in Q1 FY2027, +48% YoY — **CONFIRMED**
- Microsoft FY27 Q1 release, 28 Oct 2026: Azure revenue $33,165m, +48% (reported and constant currency), up from $22,384m a year earlier — the first quarter Microsoft disclosed Azure revenue in dollars. Corroborated by Zacks, Neowin and multiple outlets, all 28–29 Oct 2026.

### 4. Calendar-2026 capex ~$170bn; Q1 FY2027 capex $45.2bn below the "over $50bn" guide because third-party capacity deliveries were delayed — **CONFIRMED**
- Call transcript, 28 Oct 2026 (Hood): "Capital expenditures were $45.2 billion as some third-party capacity deliveries we expected in Q1 were delayed." And on guidance: "In Q2, we expect CapEx spend will be over $50 billion. Therefore, for the calendar year, spend should be approximately $170 billion due to the 3P capacity delays noted earlier."
- The ~$170bn is calendar 2026 (Jan–Dec 2026), not fiscal 2027 and not calendar 2027 — some early coverage (CNBC) mislabelled it as calendar 2027; the transcript wording and Reuters' nine-month capex tally (~$118.1bn for Jan–Sep 2026) fit calendar 2026. Capex as defined by Microsoft includes finance leases ($7.4bn of the $45.2bn); cash paid for PP&E was $39.4bn.

### 5. Moat's claim: RPO ex-OpenAI grew only +25% (vs +73% headline) — **UNVERIFIED** for Q1 FY2027
- I could **not** confirm this figure for the September quarter from any primary source. The Q1 FY2027 earnings release and the full call transcript (Motley Fool, 28 Oct 2026) contain **no** ex-OpenAI RPO disclosure: Hood gives only the headline +73%, the 2.3-year duration, ~30% within 12 months, and +98% beyond 12 months. A text search of the transcript for "excluding OpenAI" returns no match, and "OpenAI" appears only in the non-GAAP EPS, Foundry/Azure OpenAI, and Q&A contexts.
- The +25% ex-OpenAI figure **is** verified — but for the **prior** quarter: on the Q4 FY2026 call (29 Jul 2026) Hood disclosed RPO grew 25% excluding OpenAI vs +84% headline ($678bn). Multiple secondary sources (Motley Fool, Windows Forum, Farstar) document that Q4 figure.
- The only Q1-specific source I found stating 25% ex-OpenAI is a TradingView "Key facts" item dated 29 Oct 2026 attributing it to AFP — but that item is explicitly labelled AI-generated ("may contain inaccuracies"), and the figure is identical to the prior quarter's, so it may be a carry-over. **Do not rely on +25% ex-OpenAI for Q1 FY2027 until Microsoft or AFP/Reuters confirms it directly.**
- The underlying direction of the moat concern is still supported: Microsoft disclosed in Jan 2026 that ~45% of the then-$625bn RPO was attributable to OpenAI, and the FY2026 10-K shows $24.1bn of revenue from commercial arrangements with OpenAI; the Q1 call also notes bookings volatility from "significant Azure commitments" and that the beyond-12-months RPO slice grew 98%.

## MSI — Motorola Solutions (top 3)

### 6. Record backlog $16.6bn at end-Q3 2026 (quarter ended 3 Oct 2026), +14% YoY, ~1.27x FY2026 guided revenue; orders +25% YoY in Q3 2026 — **CONFIRMED**
- Motorola Solutions Q3 2026 results, Business Wire, 4 Nov 2026: sales $3,376m; "The company ended the quarter with record backlog of $16.6 billion, up 14% or $2.1 billion from the year-ago quarter driven by record Q3 orders in all three technologies." Quarter ended 3 Oct 2026 per the release tables.
- Orders: CEO Greg Brown on the call said orders grew "over 20%"; CFO Jason Winkler specified 25% overall (per call summaries, e.g. Biggo/TradingView-Seeking Alpha, 4–5 Nov 2026).
- Ratio check (my arithmetic): FY2026 guided revenue was raised to ~$13.050bn in the same release; $16.6bn / $13.05bn = 1.27x. Confirmed.

### 7. Silvus lead times fell from ~20–22 weeks to 12–14 weeks after LA capacity doubled, with a new Salt Lake City facility from Q1 2027 — **CONFIRMED** (with a sourcing caveat on "doubled")
- Lead times, verbatim from the Q3 2026 call Q&A (transcript via Bullfincher/MarketBeat, 4 Nov 2026), COO Jack Molloy: "We've been able to remediate from what was at one point in time around 20, 21, 22 weeks. We're down around 12 weeks right now, 12 to 14 weeks, depending on the line. And obviously, as I alluded to earlier, Salt Lake City puts us in a better position as we roll into '27."
- "LA capacity doubled": stated in call summaries (Biggo, InvestReport, 4–5 Nov 2026) as "capacity has doubled in Los Angeles"; I did not retrieve a verbatim primary-source quote using the word "doubled" for LA. The underlying expansion is primary-sourced: Motorola's 14 May 2026 press release announced a $100m Silvus manufacturing expansion "in addition to our expansion in Los Angeles", and COO Molloy at a Truist conference (Sep 2026) said a production line had been added in Culver City, California.
- Salt Lake City from Q1 2027: Molloy at the same Truist conference said the Salt Lake City facility is "expected to begin contributing in the first quarter of 2027" (The Stock Observer, 19 Sep 2026); the 14 May 2026 release announced the new 165,000 sq ft Salt Lake City facility.

### 8. Q3 2026 revenue $3.376bn (+12.2% YoY) and non-GAAP EPS $4.69 (+15.5%), beating ~$4.41 consensus; preliminary 2027 revenue ~$14.25bn — **CONFIRMED**
- Business Wire release, 4 Nov 2026: sales $3,376m vs $3,009m a year earlier (+12.2% by arithmetic; release rounds to +12%); non-GAAP EPS $4.69 vs $4.06 (+15.5% by arithmetic; the release text rounds this to +16%). GAAP EPS was $3.06 (down 8%, hit by a $0.52/share non-cash Silvus earnout charge).
- Consensus: Investor's Business Daily, 5 Nov 2026, reports EPS of $4.69 was "27 cents ahead of the FactSet consensus estimate", implying a FactSet consensus of $4.42 — the dossier's "~$4.41" is within $0.01 of that (different vendor/rounding). Revenue was $112m ahead of estimates per the same report.
- Preliminary 2027 revenue ~$14.25bn: confirmed — Seeking Alpha (5 Nov 2026): "Motorola Solutions signals 2027 revenue in the 'ZIP Code of $14.25B'"; same figure in the Biggo call summary ("anticipating revenue around $14.25 billion").

## CF — CF Industries (top 3)

### 9. Ammonia capacity utilisation 97% in the first nine months of 2026 — **CONFIRMED**
- CF Industries Q3/first-nine-months 2026 results, Business Wire, 4 Nov 2026: "Year-to-date, the Company has operated at a 97% utilization rate of available ammonia capacity." Repeated verbatim on the earnings call, 5 Nov 2026 (Motley Fool transcript): "Since the beginning of the year, we have operated our available ammonia capacity at 97% utilization."
- Caveat: "available" capacity excludes the Yazoo City, Mississippi complex, idled since a November 2025 incident and expected to resume in H1 2027. Gross ammonia production was ~7.2m tons for the nine months; full-year 2026 expected at ~9.5m tons.

### 10. ~37% of Europe's nameplate ammonia capacity offline or at reduced rates in Oct 2026; ~9m metric tons of urea supply lost since Feb 2026 due to Middle East hostilities — **CONFIRMED**
- CF's Q3 2026 press release (Business Wire, 4 Nov 2026), verbatim: "An estimated 37% of Europe's nameplate ammonia capacity was reportedly offline or operating at reduced rates in October 2026."
- Urea loss: earnings call, 5 Nov 2026 (Motley Fool transcript), CCO Bert Frost: "Since hostilities began in the Middle East in late February, we estimate that the global market has lost approximately nine million metric tons of urea supply."
- Note the call used a different phrasing for Europe (">35% of ammonia capacity in the region is curtailed" due to high natural gas prices); the 37% figure is the press-release figure for October 2026 and is the one to cite. (For context, the same release notes ~35% of Trinidad's ammonia capacity is being permanently shut down.)

### 11. CF holds 39% of US domestic ammonia production capacity (vs Nutrien 16%) and is the world's largest ammonia producer — **CONFIRMED**
- Shares: farmdoc daily (Monaco, Paulson, Schnitkey & Zulauf, May 2026), summarised by AgManager.info, 1 Oct 2026: "CF Industries held 39 percent, Nutrien 16 percent, Koch Fertilizer 10 percent, and LSB Industries 5 percent" of US domestic ammonia capacity (33 plants operating in the US in 2023; top four hold 70%). Corroborated by USGS-based IndexMundi data: CF ~7,002k t/yr of 18,200k t/yr total domestic capacity in 2023 = ~38.5% ("nearly 39%"); Nutrien ~2,965k t.
- "World's largest ammonia producer": CF's own company page (cfindustries.com, "Advantaged Production and Distribution Network"): "CF Industries is the world's largest producer of ammonia", with ~10.5m tons/yr gross ammonia capacity across 9 complexes.
- Caveat: the 39%/16% shares are US domestic capacity shares based on 2023 plant data as analysed in the 2026 farmdoc paper, not a 2026 production-share figure.

### 12. Q3 2026 net earnings $436m ($2.87/share, +31% YoY) and adjusted EBITDA $833m (+25% YoY) — **CONTRADICTED in part**
- The dollar figures are correct; the **+31% is attached to the wrong line**. CF's release (Business Wire, 4 Nov 2026): Q3 2026 net earnings attributable to common stockholders $436m, or $2.87 per diluted share; EBITDA $822m; adjusted EBITDA $833m. Prior-year Q3 2025: net earnings $353m, $2.19/share; adjusted EBITDA $667m.
- Corrected growth rates (my arithmetic, confirmed by TradingKey's Q3 table): **net earnings +24%** ($436m vs $353m = +23.5%); **diluted EPS +31%** ($2.87 vs $2.19 = +31.1% — the larger EPS gain reflects a lower share count after buybacks); **adjusted EBITDA +25%** ($833m vs $667m = +24.9%).
- Corrected claim: "Q3 2026 net earnings $436m ($2.87/share, **+24% YoY in dollars; EPS +31%**) and adjusted EBITDA $833m (+25% YoY)."
