# Panelist A — Supply-chain analyst — Stock-pick-momentum ballot, 2026-11-06

Lens: shortage/backlog evidence and multi-year revenue visibility above all. Live, physical shortage or a booked order book outranks moat, growth, or valuation. All figures below are from the consolidated dossier only.

## Single-pick ballot

- Top pick: MSFT
- Runner-up: MSI
- Thesis (3-5 sentences): Microsoft is the only name in the field with both a management-confirmed live capacity shortage and a booked backlog measured in hundreds of billions. Azure grew 48% to $33.2bn in Q1 FY2027 despite the shortfall, so the constraint is supply, not demand — every incremental megawatt of capacity delivered converts directly into recognised revenue. The backlog gives multi-year visibility no other name here can match, and the stock has lagged the field (+5.1% over 12 months, -3.6% off its high) while the backlog compounded, leaving room for the capacity unlock to re-rate it. This is the cleanest supply-chain setup in the dossier: demand exceeding supply for ~2 years, with a $170bn capex programme aimed squarely at closing the gap.
- Key shortage/backlog evidence: Commercial RPO of $677bn at 30 Sep 2026, +73% YoY, with a 2.3-year weighted-average duration and ~30% recognised in the next 12 months (+37% YoY) — alongside CFO Amy Hood's Q1 FY2027 statement that "Customer demand continues to exceed available supply".
- Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk: medium — enterprise lock-in via the M365/Entra/Windows stack and a ~$170bn buildout no challenger except AWS/Google can replicate are strong, but the frontier-model margin is contestable: OpenAI is now multi-cloud (on Google Cloud) and Anthropic is multi-cloud, and hyperscaler customers are training on their own silicon, so the most concentrated part of the backlog can be routed elsewhere.
- 12-18 month return scenario: Base ~+15–20% (in line with the +18.1% mean-target upside in the dossier) as Azure capacity comes on and the ~30% of RPO due within 12 months converts; bull ~+30–40% if third-party capacity deliveries stop slipping and Azure sustains ~48% growth into a capacity-unlock year.
- Top risk: Capex/return risk — FCF fell 27% in Q1 FY2027 to $18.6bn on the buildout, and if AI demand cools the datacentre fleet becomes stranded capacity; backlog concentration in long-dated OpenAI contracts (RPO ex-OpenAI grew only +25%) amplifies that risk.
- Conviction (1-10): 7

### Top 3 — shortage/backlog detail and scenarios

**1. MSFT:** The single most compelling data point is the $677bn commercial RPO (+73% YoY, 2.3-year duration), the largest booked order book in the field by an order of magnitude, paired with two years of management saying demand exceeds supply. Base ~+15–20% over 12–18 months as capacity converts; bull ~+30–40% on a clean capacity unlock with Azure holding ~48% growth.

**2. MSI:** The single most compelling data point is the record $16.6bn backlog at end-Q3 2026 (+14% / +$2.1bn YoY, +~$1.0bn sequentially), ~1.27x FY2026 guided revenue with services stretching over multi-year (up to 10-year) contracts, on top of orders +25% YoY and a third guidance raise of 2026. Base ~+10–15% over 12–18 months as the backlog converts into the guided ~$14.25bn 2027 revenue; bull ~+25–30% if Silvus/drone demand and the D-Series refresh keep orders at Q3's pace.

**3. CF:** The single most compelling data point is 97% ammonia capacity utilisation in 9M 2026 (98% in H1) — effectively sold out — against ~9m metric tons of urea supply lost since Feb 2026 and ~37% of Europe's nameplate ammonia capacity offline or reduced in Oct 2026. Base ~+8–15% over 12–18 months (tight nitrogen pricing holding while utilisation stays near-full); bull ~+25–35% if the Iran-driven disruption and European outages persist into another planting season.
