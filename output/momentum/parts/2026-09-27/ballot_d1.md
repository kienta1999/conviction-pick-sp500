## BALLOT 1 — Single pick

- **Top pick:** KLAC
- **Runner-up:** CF

- **Thesis (3–5 sentences):** KLA owns the most monopolistic franchise in semiconductor equipment — ~74% of the metrology/inspection market, with a ~$13B backlog and management saying WFE demand is outpacing what the industry can physically ship ("WFE is literally just as fast as we can go as an industry"). The shortage thesis is real and durable: advanced-packaging process-control revenue is being raised to ~$1.1B, +70% Y/Y, as HBM and hybrid bonding drive inspection demand that cannot be deferred. Crucially, unlike the other equipment and memory names on this panel, the market is NOT pricing perfection here: shares spiked to $307 in early July and have corrected ~40% to ~$183, reopening the entry. That drawdown, combined with a 24%-of-revenue recurring services floor and an irreplaceability score of 10/10, gives this lens its best risk-adjusted, underappreciated shortage setup.

- **Key shortage/backlog evidence:** Backlog "just short of $13 billion" and expected to keep growing (CEO Wallace, Citi TMT Conference, 9/10/2026), with demand exceeding supply — CFO Higgins: 2H CY2026 revenue up ~20% vs 1H "supported by improving supply availability," i.e., demand was waiting on supply.

- **Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk:** LOW. The moat is algorithmic, not just optical: ~25 years of cross-customer defect-classification data that newcomers cannot replicate; fabs tune recipes layer-by-layer on KLA baselines, so switching vendors requires re-baselining entire processes. Hyperscalers have zero path to in-source this (KLA's customers are foundries), and competitors (Onto, Camtek, AMAT inspection) compete only in narrow niches. 52 consecutive quarters of Y/Y services growth. Score: 10/10 in the dossier — the highest of any name on the panel.

- **12–18 month return scenario:** Base — the ~$13B backlog converts through late 2026/2027, 2H CY2026 runs ~20% above 1H as guided, and advanced-packaging +70% growth sustains; shares recover from the ~40% correction toward the prior $307 peak → roughly +50–70% from ~$187. Bull — 2027 WFE growth lands in the "low-20%, maybe higher" range management flagged at Citi, and the market re-rates the monopoly multiple; a return to and beyond the July peak → +80–120%.

- **Top risk:** WFE is cyclical: a premium-valued, high-fixed-cost business (41.7% operating margin) gets hit hard if the cycle rolls — and China/export controls are a live drag ($300–350M CY2026 revenue impact plus ~100bps of gross margin on tariffs). The ~40% drawdown is partly the market voting on that risk.

- **Conviction (1–10):** 8

### Top 3 — shortage/moat evidence and scenarios

**1. KLAC.** The single most compelling data point: ~74% share of global metrology/inspection (up from ~50% in 2010), monopoly-class ~80%+ in patterned-wafer and reticle inspection, sitting on a backlog "just short of $13 billion" that management expects to keep growing — with WFE supply, not demand, as the binding constraint. Base case (+50–70%): backlog converts, 2H CY2026 runs ~20% above 1H, services floor holds, shares grind back toward the $307 July peak. Bull case (+80–120%): 2027 WFE grows low-20%+ and advanced packaging (+70% Y/Y) compounds the multiple.

**2. CF.** The textbook commodity shortage: Hormuz disruption removed ~1/3 of traded nitrogen supply (the Gulf = ~half of seaborne urea, 20% of ammonia exports), urea prices rising across all major regions (NOLA ~$520/t), CF running ammonia capacity at ~98% with volumes down 15.3% YoY for lack of supply — all at P/E 8.4 with the stock −14.3% in 5 days (RSI ~27). Base case: nitrogen stays constrained into the 2027 application season, earnings stay explosive (+99.6% YoY EPS in Q2), multiple drifts off 8.4× → +40–60%. Bull case: shortage persists through 2027, India demand (9–10 Mt imports) absorbs Gulf tightness, earnings double again and the market pays up for the lowest-cost producer → +80–100%+.

**3. GOOGL.** The shortage is AI compute itself: CEO Pichai says Google is "compute constrained in the near term," and the company is paying SpaceX ~$920M/month (~$30B total) for ~110,000 Nvidia GPUs as short-term "bridge capacity" — with a $514B Cloud backlog (+$50B sequentially) and existing customers expanding consumption >50% beyond commitments. Yet GOOGL sits ~16% below its $408.61 52-week high at ~17× trailing earnings — the cheapest mega-cap on the panel — because Q2 posted the first negative FCF print since IPO on the $195–205B capex guide. Base case (+30–40%): Cloud backlog converts, margins expand on the +82% Cloud growth, the capex fear fades. Bull case (+60–80%): TPU vertical integration compounds the cost advantage and the market rerates the cheapest hyperscaler.

### Deliberately left out of my top 10, and why

**ANET** — lost the #1 DC switch seat to Nvidia (21.5% vs 20.7% in Q1 2026) and trades at ~64× earnings; this is a share battle against the company that owns the accelerator, not a shortage, and the market already believes the bull case. **CSCO** — 59% 12-month run, margins down 210bps, and Nvidia now leads the datacenter Ethernet segment it needs; priced-in demand boom with a real disintermediation threat. **LLY** — the acute GLP-1 shortage is over (FDA shortage list resolved); momentum rests on dose growth and orals, all real but already priced into a ~$1.1T valuation. **NEM** — not a shortage play at all; it's a lever on the gold price, gold is ~21% off its January record, and as a fungible commodity supplier its irreplaceability is 3/10 — there is no order book or sold-out lead time to underappreciate.
