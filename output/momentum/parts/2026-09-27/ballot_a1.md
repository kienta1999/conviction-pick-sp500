## Ballot 1 — single pick

- **Top pick:** CF
- **Runner-up:** SNDK

- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** CF Industries is the dossier's only genuine,
  supply-side commodity shortage: the Hormuz closure removed roughly a third of
  traded nitrogen supply, and CF — the world's largest ammonia producer and the
  lowest-cost ton on earth at ~$3.31–3.37/MMBtu gas cost vs a global marginal ton
  set by LNG at $12–15/MMBtu — ran its available ammonia capacity at ~98% in H1
  2026 with sales volumes down 15.3% YoY because it could not source supply, not
  because demand faded. Q2 2026 EPS was $4.73 (+99.6% YoY), H1 net earnings were
  +92.3% YoY, and the stock still prices at ~8.4× earnings after a -14.3% five-day
  slide that left RSI ~27 (oversold) — the cheapest multiple in the entire
  14-name pool. Nitrogen is agronomic non-substitutable ("without fertilizers, it
  is impossible to secure sufficient crop yields"), and with the spring 2027
  application season the next clearing event, a Hormuz-persisting tape gives CF a
  low-multiple, record-margin compounding setup that no other candidate matches
  on shortage × valuation.

- **Key shortage/backlog evidence:** the single most compelling data point —
  Hormuz removed ~1/3 of traded nitrogen supply (Persian Gulf = ~half of seaborne
  urea, 20% of ammonia exports; ~600,000 MT of urea reportedly stuck aboard
  vessels) AND the market leader ran at ~98% ammonia utilization in H1 2026 with
  total sales volumes down 15.3% YoY for lack of supply. A supply shock plus a
  capacity wall plus volume decline on the shortage side of the ledger is the
  textbook definition of a physical commodity shortage.

- **Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk:** medium — scored 7/10 in the
  dossier. Farmers cannot in-source a Haber-Bosch plant and there is no
  substitute for nitrogen in crop yields, so customer disintermediation risk is
  effectively nil; docked because nitrogen itself is fungible and global swing
  capacity (China, Russia) can return, so the moat is cost position
  (~$3.35/MMBtu gas vs $12–15/MMBtu marginal ton) rather than uniqueness.

- **12-18 month return scenario:** Base — the Hormuz restriction persists through
  the spring 2027 application season, urea prices stay elevated, CF compounds
  record-margin earnings, and the stock re-rates from ~8.4× toward normal
  mid-cycle multiples: roughly +40–60% from the ~$114.67 recent price. Bull —
  the closure extends into 2027, European producers curtail further on €73/MWh
  gas, India absorbs 9–10 Mt of import demand, and nitrogen prices spike through
  the 2027 application season: +100% or more is on the table at a still-single-digit
  earnings multiple.

- **Top risk:** Hormuz de-escalation — the shortage premium is geopolitical, and
  a reopened strait would flood the market with Gulf urea and collapse prices,
  unwinding the entire thesis regardless of CF's cost position.

- **Conviction (1-10):** 7

### Top-3 detail

**CF (rank 1):** The dossier's most compelling physical shortage data point: the
Hormuz disruption since late Feb 2026 removed roughly a third of traded nitrogen
supply, Middle East/North Africa/NOLA urea prices are rising on every continent
(w/e 18.09.2026), European producers are curtailing on record gas prices, and
the world's largest ammonia producer ran at ~98% utilization with volumes down
15.3% YoY — supply-constrained, not demand-soft. No other candidate pairs a
genuine supply removal with an ~8.4× P/E. Base case: Hormuz holds through the
spring 2027 application season, record margins compound, multiple normalizes →
~+40–60%; bull case: disruption extends, European curtailments deepen → +100%+.

**SNDK (rank 2, runner-up):** The most compelling multi-year revenue-visibility
data point in the dossier: 8 new-business-model agreements covering >50% of
FY2027 bits and ~two-thirds of FY2028 bits, weighted-average duration >4 years,
representing ≥$93.9B of expected revenue with $16.5B of financial guarantees —
all of 2026 industry NAND production effectively sold out, with Goldman
estimating the 4.2% NAND deficit for 2026 as the largest since 2011. The
counterweight keeping it out of #1: the price curve is already decelerating
(TrendForce: Q3 contract prices +10–15% QoQ, "noticeably slower pace"), Kioxia
says prices "have risen enough," and the stock is up ~3,192% YTD. Base case:
contracted visibility plus decelerating-but-positive pricing carries it +30–50%
on earnings leverage; bull case: shortages stretch into late 2027 and data-center
SSD mix deepens → +100%+.

**CAT (rank 3):** The strongest hard backlog in the pool: a record $72B at end
of Q2 2026, +92% YoY (+$9B sequentially), with Power & Energy customers placing
orders as far out as 2030 — hyperscale developers are ordering gensets as prime
power because grid interconnection takes 3–7 years, and "data center developers
keep asking for more units than Caterpillar can ship." One of fewer than five
qualified hyperscale-grade prime-power vendors in North America makes this a
multi-year structural capacity shortage, not a cyclical boom. Base case: the
booked 2030 visibility justifies ~+30–50% as margins hold through the capacity
ramp; bull case: the power gap widens and CAT triples large-engine output →
+70%+, with the binding constraint being what it can ship, not demand.

### Deliberately left out of the top 10

GLP-1 shortage is over (tirzepatide removed from the FDA shortage list); the
momentum now is dose growth and orals, all priced into a $1.1T valuation.
