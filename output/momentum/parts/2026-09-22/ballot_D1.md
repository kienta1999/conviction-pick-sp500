## Ballot 1 — Single pick

- **Top pick:** GOOGL
- **Runner-up:** MSFT

- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** Alphabet has the cleanest demand>supply print in the entire 14-name dossier — Cloud backlog $514B (up >$50B in a single quarter), Cloud revenue +82% YoY, and a record share-gainer (Synergy 15%, up from 13%) — yet the market is pricing it like a capex scare story after the first negative-FCF quarter since 2004 (−$5.9B) and paused buybacks. That FCF dip is not a demand problem; it is ~2x-YoY capex ($44.9B) being poured into converting a $514B contracted backlog while Google is literally *renting third-party cloud capacity* in Q3 because it cannot build fast enough. As a contrarian, this is the exact shape of an underappreciated setup: the bear case (overspending into thin air) is refuted by the dossier's own backlog math. Nobody needs AI to keep winning for this to work — they just need Alphabet to keep converting what customers have already committed to.

- **Key shortage/backlog evidence:** Google Cloud backlog of $514B at Q2 FY2026, up more than $50B quarter-over-quarter, plus renting third-party cloud capacity in Q3 — demand demonstrably exceeds Alphabet's own installed capacity.

- **Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk:** MEDIUM-LOW — dossier rates trap exposure MEDIUM (workload competition), but multi-cloud portability is a choice customers can only exercise when capacity exists; in a market where everyone is capacity-constrained, workloads stay where they are provisioned, and Google's share gains (13%→15%) show stickiness.

- **12-18 month return scenario:** Base case roughly +30–40% as the $514B backlog converts to revenue and capex intensity peaks then normalizes. Bull case roughly +60–70% if Cloud sustains 60%+ growth, FCF inflects back positive in 2027, and buybacks resume against a repriced earnings base.

- **Top risk:** The capex cycle runs longer than expected — ~$190B-scale annual spend keeps FCF negative into 2027 and Cloud growth decelerates off the +82% print, leaving the market's capex-skepticism proven right rather than a mispricing.

- **Conviction (1-10):** 7

### Top 3 deep dives

**1. GOOGL.** The single most compelling data point is the one above: $514B Cloud backlog growing >$50B QoQ while Alphabet rents third-party capacity — that is demand outrunning a ~$190B-annual-capex machine, and the dossier calls it the "cleanest demand>supply print" in the batch. The bear case is purely narrative (negative FCF quarter, paused buybacks); the bull case is arithmetic (contracted backlog → conversion). Base case +30–40% over 12–18 months as backlog converts and capex intensity normalizes; bull +60–70% if Cloud holds 60%+ growth and FCF inflects in 2027.

**2. MSFT.** The textbook shortage: CFO-admitted demand outpacing compute capacity for multiple quarters, RPO $678B (+84% YoY), $80B of unfulfilled Azure orders, and delivered GPUs sitting un-installable for lack of power — the constraint is literally electricity, not demand. It is less underappreciated than GOOGL (Stifel downgrade to Hold, $3T+ scale, capex compressing cloud margins), hence runner-up. Base case +20–30% as the unfulfilled order book converts; bull +40–50% if the market starts pricing the *duration* of this capacity-constrained reinvestment runway instead of fixating on margin compression.

**3. FCX.** The purest liquid copper-shortage play: 150–600kt structural deficits, a 17-year greenfield cycle, exchange inventories around 4 days of consumption, and LME copper at a record $14,875/t in September 2026 — and as a commodity there is no disintermediation concept (nobody in-sources copper). Contrarian caution: trading at its 52-week high with consensus price targets at the current price, plus a Goldman 300kt-surplus counter-call and Section 232 tariff uncertainty — the easy half of the move may be over. Base case +15–25% if deficits persist; bull +40–50% if the surplus call is wrong and Grasberg reaches full capacity by end-2027 into still-tight markets.
