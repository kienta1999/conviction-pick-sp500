## BALLOT 1 — SINGLE PICK

- **Top pick:** MU
- **Runner-up:** KLAC
- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** MU is the one shortage in this batch whose product the
  hyperscalers bidding for it physically cannot build themselves — trap exposure LOW,
  "hyperscalers cannot build HBM." Micron is fulfilling only half to two-thirds of HBM
  demand with no new fab output until FY2028, while contract memory prices ripped
  +70–75% QoQ in Q2. Customers are voting with balance sheets: ~$100B minimum contracted
  revenue floor and $22B in customer prepayments — prepaid cash is the most honest shortage
  signal there is. Q3 FY2026 printed $41.46B revenue (+346% YoY) at 84.9% gross margin —
  peak-cycle margins on a sold-out, prepaid order book. The HBM4 lead is temporal (Samsung
  will qualify; SK Hynix neck-and-neck), but a fab build-out is a multi-year barrier, not
  a design cycle — so the sold-out window is durable, not just this quarter's print.
- **Key shortage/backlog evidence:** $22B in customer prepayments against a ~$100B minimum
  contracted revenue floor, with HBM sold out through CY2027 and only half to two-thirds
  of demand being fulfilled (Jun 24 call).
- **Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk:** LOW — hyperscalers cannot fabricate HBM;
  in-sourcing the fab is not an option, and dual-sourcing requires years of qualification.
  The real threat is competition (Samsung/SK Hynix qualification), not disintermediation.
- **12-18 month return scenario:** Rough base: sold-out through CY2027 with elevated memory
  pricing sustains record-margin earnings expansion, ~+40–60%. Rough bull: the HBM4 lead
  extends into FY2028 capacity additions, pricing stays elevated, and earnings power
  resets permanently higher — ~+100%+ is not fanciful off a capital-equipment-grade
  earnings multiple.
- **Top risk:** Priced for perfection — the stock is +250% YTD and any wobble in memory
  pricing or Samsung HBM4 qualification ahead of schedule would compress the multiple hard.
  Memory is still ultimately a cycle, and 84.9% gross margins are peak-cycle margins.
- **Conviction (1-10):** 8

### Top-3 detail

**1. MU.** The single most compelling data point is the $22B in customer prepayments on a
~$100B minimum contracted revenue floor: customers are financing Micron's working capital
because they cannot get HBM anywhere else, with Micron fulfilling only half to two-thirds
of demand and no new fab output until FY2028. From this lens, a prepaid, sold-out order
book is balance-sheet durability and pricing power fused into one number. Rough 12–18mo
base: ~+40–60% as elevated pricing converts through CY2027; bull: ~+100%+ if the HBM4
lead holds into FY2028 and pricing stays at record levels.

**2. KLAC.** 55–56% share in process control, $12.57B backlog (+60% YoY, ≈11 months of
revenue), $3.77B FY2026 FCF, and the dossier's cleanest disintermediation read: in-sourcing
threat ~zero, and custom hyperscaler silicon *increases* inspection intensity — the trap
arrow points the wrong way, making this the most anti-fragile moat in the batch (advanced-
packaging process-control guide raised to ~$1.1B CY2026). But the July beat-and-raise was
"skinny" and the stock fell 9% on the print — expectations are priced. Rough base:
~+20–30% on backlog conversion compounding; bull: ~+60–80% if the advanced-packaging
inspection boom outruns consensus.

**3. NVDA.** Meeting only 70% of order demand with a ~$1T Blackwell+Rubin backlog and an
80–85% share anchored by CUDA is the strongest raw shortage+moat combination in the batch.
But this lens exists for the dossier's HIGH trap exposure: hyperscaler custom-ASIC
in-sourcing of inference is *in progress* and share has already eroded 92%→80–85% — a
moat being actively dug at by your own largest customers is the cardinal sin for a
quality investor. Explosive, but with the disintermediation trajectory running the wrong
way. Rough base: ~+25–40% on backlog conversion; bull: ~+80%+ only if inference
in-sourcing stalls or Blackwell/ Rubin share re-expands.
