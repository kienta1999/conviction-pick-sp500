## MU (Micron Technology) — panel winner

### Claim 1 — ~$150B RPO under 26 take-or-pay Strategic Customer Agreements, >35% of revenue through 2030
**Verdict: CONFIRMED**

- Micron disclosed on its fiscal Q4 2026 report (30 Sep 2026) that it has signed 26 Strategic
  Customer Agreements (SCAs), up from 16 in June, all with take-or-pay volume provisions, currently
  estimated to represent more than 35% of its revenue through 2030 (some extending into 2031), with
  remaining performance obligations of approximately $150B.
- Nuance in the primary remarks: CFO Mark Murphy described the ~$150B as based on committed volumes
  and minimum pricing ("inherently conservative") and associated with the agreements that have a
  determined pricing framework (fixed price or floor/ceiling) — i.e. the $150B is the priced-framework
  subset of the SCA book, not a simple sum over all 26 at spot prices. The claim as stated matches how
  the company and press summarised it.

### Claim 3 — HBM sold out through calendar 2027, with >75% of FY27 output already committed
**Verdict: CONFIRMED — with a wording caveat**

- >75% committed: CONFIRMED. Mehrotra in Q&A: "2027, more than 75% of our output is already
  committed for 2027, and the majority of discussions with our customers today are already around
  2028." Several summaries render this specifically as fiscal-2027 output/shipments committed across
  SCA and non-SCA customers; the primary quote itself says "2027 output" without a fiscal/calendar
  qualifier, so the panel's "FY27" label is a reasonable but slightly more precise rendering than the
  quote.
- HBM sold out through calendar 2027: this was Micron's literal statement in fiscal Q3 (June 2026) —
  HBM3E/HBM4 "completely sold out through the end of calendar 2027". In the Q4 (30 Sep 2026) remarks

  supply" at significantly higher prices, and Mehrotra: "for 2027, a large part of the volume is
  already sold out for 2027 for HBM." So "sold out" is substantively supported and widely reported
  as such post-Q4, but the Q4 primary wording is "vast majority / large part", not a literal 100%.

**Verdict: UNVERIFIED (orders confirmed; the ~$2.8B backlog amount is not supported for Q3)**

- Orders: CONFIRMED from primary sources. Keysight's Q3 FY26 press release (18 Aug 2026, quarter
  ended 31 Jul 2026) and CFO Neil Dougherty on the call: "Orders of $2.091 billion were up 56% on a
  reported basis" (core +52%; acquisitions +5 pts, FX −1 pt) — a record, and the second consecutive

  found states $2.8B. Given Q3 alone built backlog by ~$245M (orders $2.091B − revenue $1.846B), a
  Q1-era $2.8B figure cannot simply be carried forward as the Q3 level. (Note: Keysight's 10-Q
  remaining performance obligations of ~$616M at 31 Jul 2026 are a different, narrower metric and
  are not "backlog".)

### Claim 6 — Wireline orders overtook wireless for the first time, driven by 800G/1.6T Ethernet test demand
**Verdict: CONTRADICTED (as worded — the first-time milestone was revenue, not orders)**

- Primary transcript, Q3 FY26 call (18 Aug 2026), CFO Neil Dougherty: "**Wireline revenue exceeded
  wireless revenue for the first time this quarter**." CEO Satish Dhanasekaran in Q&A repeated the
  point in revenue terms: "Wireline was greater than wireless, not just this quarter, but even year
  to date."
- On orders, management said something different and narrower: wireline delivered **record orders,
  more than doubling year-over-year** (also reported by Zacks). No primary or reputable secondary
  source found states that wireline **orders** overtook wireless orders. The claim conflates the
  revenue milestone with the orders growth.
- The demand driver is broadly supported: transceiver makers "rapidly scaling 800G and 1.6T optical
  transceivers using our industry-leading 224G digital communication analyzers" (CEO prepared
  remarks), within AI infrastructure scaling / speed transitions. That part is fine; the
  orders-vs-revenue error is the defect.
- Corrected claim: "Wireline **revenue** exceeded wireless revenue for the first time (in Q3 FY26
  and year-to-date), and wireline **orders** were a record, more than doubling YoY, driven by AI
  infrastructure / 800G–1.6T test demand."
