## LLY — Eli Lilly and Company

### 1. Shortage thesis — STRONG, but easing from acute shortage to capacity-gated growth
- Core driver is exactly as briefed: dual GIP/GLP-1 (tirzepatide — Mounjaro for diabetes, Zepbound for obesity) plus oral GLP-1 Foundayo (orforglipron) demand vs. peptide / injectable manufacturing capacity that takes 18–24 months per tranche to build.
- Evidence of rationing, in management's own framing: in early Feb 2025 CEO David Ricks said capacity already built was not enough to meet global demand and Lilly was still gating promotion and gating launches worldwide; the CFO later named Brazil, China, India and Mexico as markets where commercial activity was deliberately kept measured so demand would not outrun supply (Trefis summary of Lilly commentary, 2026-09-09).
- Capacity, not demand, as the revenue governor: Lilly produced >1.6× as many salable incretin doses in H1 2025 as in H1 2024, and one deep-dive synthesis puts supportable incretin revenue at ~$30B on 2025 capacity, targeting $45–50B in 2026 and >$60B in 2027 — i.e. reported revenue tracks the capacity ramp, not the demand curve (TickerXRay deep dive, 2026).
- The release valve is now visible in the numbers: Q2 2026 China revenue +93% constant currency and rest-of-world +136%, driven by Mounjaro in Latin America / Asia — the same geographies previously throttled (Trefis, 2026-09-09). Medicare GLP-1 Bridge program started 1 July 2026, lifting US covered lives for Lilly obesity medicines by ~35%; CEO Ricks said in Sept 2026 that ~700,000 new senior patients had entered via Bridge and ~70% were on Lilly medicines (TheStreet, Sept 2026).
- Pre-launch stockpiling as shortage insurance: Lilly built a reported ~$1.5B pre-launch inventory of orforglipron ahead of approval so Foundayo could launch without the injectable-style shortage (AInvest, Feb 2026).

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk
- Moat: (i) patent estate — tirzepatide protection cited as running into the **late 2030s** on formulation/indication layers (composition-of-matter debate is earlier; see Risks); (ii) best-in-class efficacy — tirzepatide beats semaglutide on weight loss, and Lilly owns the *next* generation too: retatrutide (triple GIP/GLP-1/glucagon) posted **28.3% average weight loss at the highest dose in Phase 3**, ahead of anything Novo has shown; (iii) manufacturing know-how / scale — peptide synthesis + device (KwikPen) + the $55B plant network is itself a barrier: a rival must replicate both molecule and factories; (iv) PBM/formulary and DTC channel lock-in (all 3 major PBMs; ~70% of Medicare Bridge new starts).

None is a *bypass* of Lilly today — the serious threat is Lilly being leapfrogged on efficacy, and its own retatrutide is currently the leapfrog candidate.
- Customers cannot in-source: patients/payers cannot synthesize a patented biologic/peptide at scale; disintermediation risk is low.

### 7. Scores

- **Verdict:** The only true supply-constrained mega-cap in pharma — Lilly sells every incretin dose it can manufacture, owns the #1 share, the best pipeline successor (retatrutide), and the formulary lock; the bet is execution on the plant ramp at a $1T price, not demand.

## EW — Edwards Lifesciences

### 1. Shortage thesis — WEAK as a *supply* shortage; REAL as an *under-treatment / access* shortage
- Honest framing first: **there is no evidence Edwards cannot manufacture enough SAPIEN valves.** No sold-out capacity, waitlist, lead-time or allocation claims were found in Q1/Q2 2026 reporting — **product shortage evidence: not found**. The constraint sits downstream: diagnosis, referral, Heart-Team evaluation and cath-lab / hospital procedure capacity.
- The demand-side shortage is large and documented: severe aortic stenosis (AS) is persistently **under-referred and under-treated** — the DETECT AS study (939 patients, 285 physicians) showed a simple echo alert raised aortic-valve-replacement treatment rates by **11% overall and 14% in symptomatic severe AS**, with larger gains in older patients and women (Edwards / ACC data via Nasdaq/BioSpace PR). EARLY TAVR (NEJM) showed asymptomatic severe AS patients treated early with Edwards TAVR beat watchful waiting, with no penalty for intervening — i.e. a large pool of diagnosed-but-untreated patients exists *today*.
- Demand surge driver #1 — indication expansion: FDA expanded SAPIEN 3 / 3 Ultra / 3 Ultra Resilia to **asymptomatic severe AS in April–May 2025** on EARLY TAVR.
- Demand surge driver #2 — coverage: on **10 Sept 2026 CMS finalized the TAVR National Coverage Determination** extending Medicare coverage to asymptomatic severe AS (under coverage-with-evidence-development), removing CED for symptomatic patients, and removing hospital-volume / operator barriers for new programs (MedTech Dive, 11 Sept 2026; CMS via company statement). Edwards itself cautioned implementation "will take time" and merely reaffirmed 2026 guidance — a volume tailwind, not an overnight surge.
- Demand surge driver #3 — adjacent untreated pools: TMTT (mitral/tricuspid) — historically almost no transcatheter options — grew **+47.3% in Q2 2026**, the clearest "supply of therapy finally meeting latent demand" signal in the portfolio.

### 2. Backlog / order book — visibility proxy
- Medical-device valves are sold procedure-by-procedure; **no backlog, RPO or order book is disclosed — not found / not applicable**.
- Visibility proxy — guidance (raised twice in 2026): Q1 2026 (23 Apr 2026) TAVR FY growth raised to 7–9% (from 6–8%). Q2 2026 (23 July 2026): total sales **$1.741B, +13.6%** (+12.5% cc); TAVR **$1.258B, +11.3%** (+10.5% cc); TMTT **$195.9M, +47.3%**; FY2026 total cc growth raised to **10–11%** (from 9–11%), TAVR cc to **8–9%**, TMTT to **$760–780M** (from $740–780M), total sales **$6.6–6.9B**, adj. EPS reaffirmed **$2.95–$3.05** (+17% at midpoint). Reaffirmed again on the 10 Sept 2026 CMS decision. Longer-term guide (~10% average total sales growth) to be updated at the December 2026 investor conference. Balance-sheet visibility: $2.9B cash vs. $600M debt at 30 June 2026.

### 3. Category position — #1 in TAVR, by a wide but contested margin
- **#1 in TAVR:** ~**66% global share** vs. Medtronic Evolut ~**28%**, Abbott ~2.9% (GlobalData, 2026); US share cited as **>60%**. TAVR is ~72–75% of Edwards revenue ($1.26B of $1.74B in Q2 2026).
- Q2 2026 TAVR growth also flattered by a competitor's exit in Europe in Q2 2025 (company noted global competitive position "increased slightly" in Q1 on that exit).
- In TMTT, Edwards is building a first-mover portfolio (PASCAL repair, EVOQUE tricuspid replacement, SAPIEN M3 mitral) with a **$2B-by-2030 TMTT target** — category creation rather than share defence.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk
- Moat: (i) clinical-evidence lock-in — SAPIEN has the deepest long-term durability dataset and >**1 million patients treated**; Heart Teams default to the valve with the longest follow-up; (ii) regulatory lock-in — first (and trial-proven) asymptomatic indication via EARLY TAVR, a trial only the incumbent could run; (iii) manufacturing know-how — bovine pericardial tissue processing / anti-calcification (Resilia) is tacit, decades-deep craft, not a blueprint a rival can copy; (iv) installed training / proctoring ecosystem in cath labs.
- Concrete threats — real, named, and already scoring points: **Medtronic Evolut** won the head-to-head **SMART trial** in small-annulus patients (mostly women, ~40% of TAVR population): significantly less bioprosthetic valve dysfunction, ~5× less valve thrombosis, ~9× less hemodynamic structural dysfunction at 2–3 years (GlobalData). That is a genuine route-around in a large sub-segment. Abbott Navitor is a third platform; Boston Scientific has signalled re-entry (MiRus investment). Hospitals (the "customer") multi-source valves routinely — there is no exclusivity; switching is a purchasing + retraining decision, not a rebuild.
- Hospitals cannot in-source valve manufacture, but they *can* and do shift share procedure-by-procedure — disintermediation risk is moderate, concentrated in small-annulus cases.
- **Irreplaceability score: 6/10** — dominant evidence/training moat, but a credible #2 with superior head-to-head data in a big niche caps it well below a true chokepoint.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months, to 2026-10-01)
- **Q2 2026 beat + raise (23 July 2026):** sales $1.741B vs. $1.700B consensus; adj. EPS $0.78 vs. $0.74; FY sales-growth and TAVR/TMTT guidance raised (details §2); stock +~7–11% on the print.

### 6. Risks
- **This is not a shortage story:** growth is mid-to-high single-digit TAVR + fast-small TMTT — durable, not explosive; procedure growth depends on referral behaviour and hospital capacity, which CMS coverage alone does not fix (Edwards' own "will take time" language).
- **Competition with data:** SMART-trial share loss in small annuli to Medtronic; Abbott/Boston Scientific entry; ASPs "stable" today — a share war could end that.
- **Valuation:** ~49–54× earnings cited in Sept 2026 coverage; stock had returned only ~+1% YTD vs. ~+12% for the S&P 500 by 11 Sept 2026 despite the beats — the market is already paying for the December guide and TCT data to land.
- **Concentration & lumpiness:** ~3/4 of revenue from one procedure family; trial readouts (PROGRESS moderate-AS) are binary-ish catalysts in both directions; FTC settlement shows deal-execution scrutiny.

### 7. Scores
- **Shortage score: 4/10** — a large *untreated-patient* backlog exists, but it is gated by diagnosis/referral/hospital throughput and payer rollout, not by Edwards' supply; nothing is sold out, and demand converts at a managed, guided pace.
- **Verdict:** A high-quality #1 franchise with a genuine coverage-driven volume tailwind (CMS asymptomatic NCD) and a second engine in TMTT (+47%), but for an *explosive-return* panel this is a steady compounder at ~50× earnings — the shortage is in patient access, not in anything Edwards rations.

## CDNS — Cadence Design Systems

### 1. Shortage thesis — a *complexity* shortage: design capacity, not Cadence's supply
- Driver as briefed, and well supported: AI accelerators at 2nm/1.4nm-class nodes with tens of billions of transistors, 3D-IC/chiplet packaging and exploding verification load have made EDA + emulation + IP the binding constraint on how many chips the world can design. Verification already consumes the majority of chip-development effort; every new node multiplies design rules and signoff work.
- Evidence of demand outrunning the old workflow: **system companies (hyperscalers designing own silicon) are now ~45% of Cadence's business** (CEO Anirudh Devgan, Q4/CadenceLIVE commentary) — an entirely new class of buyers on top of traditional semi firms. Q2 2026: revenue **+24.2% YoY**, with **double-digit growth in every product group**, IP **+>40%**, System Design & Analysis **+37%**, Core EDA **+18%**, and a **record hardware quarter** (Q2 call, 27 July 2026).
- The multiplier: agentic AI *increases* tool consumption rather than replacing seats — management and analysts (FBN Securities initiation, ~30 Sept 2026) stress that autonomous agents (ChipStack, ViraStack, InnoStack) invoke Cadence's engines *hundreds of times in parallel*, more often than human designers do. NVIDIA's Jensen Huang made the same point at CadenceLIVE (April 2026): agentic AI "will not bypass EDA software; it will call into it, repeatedly, and at scale."
- Scarcity proxy in hardware: Palladium Z3 / Protium X3 emulation systems ($1–10M per unit, custom silicon, capacity to 48B gates) added **12 new customers in Q2 2026 alone**; Q1 2026 was hardware's "best quarter ever," with Palladium Z3 winning competitive displacements. There is no claim Cadence is sold out — the shortage is of *engineer-hours and verification throughput* at customers, which Cadence monetizes. No lead-time/waitlist disclosure found — **not found**.

### 2. Backlog / order book — the cleanest visibility of the three
- **Record backlog $8.1B at 30 June 2026** (Q2 2026 release, 27 July 2026), vs. $8.0B at Q1 2026 and $7.8B at YE2025; **$4.2B expected to be recognized as revenue within 12 months**. Consensus had modelled $7.68B — bookings beat it.
- Q2 2026 detail: revenue **$1.584B (+24.2% YoY**, beat consensus ~0.5%), non-GAAP EPS **$2.11 (+27.9%)**, non-GAAP operating margin **45.5%**, operating cash flow **$635M** in the quarter, DSO **65 days**, cash $1.44B, debt principal $2.5B.
- Guidance raised (second raise of 2026): FY2026 revenue **$6.26–6.34B** (~+19%), non-GAAP EPS **$8.05–$8.15**; Q3 guided $1.595–1.625B. ~80% recurring revenue base underpins the backlog quality.

### 3. Category position — co-#1 in a duopoly (de facto #2 by a hair, #1 in key niches)
- EDA / Electronic System Design (~$21B 2025 industry revenue, SEMI ESD Alliance): **Synopsys ~31%, Cadence ~30%, Siemens EDA ~13%** — top two ≈ 61% of the broad market, and ~85% on narrower EDA-only cuts. Cadence is the leader in **analog/custom design (Virtuoso) and hardware emulation (Palladium)**, Synopsys in synthesis/digital and (post-$35B Ansys close, July 2025) silicon-to-systems simulation breadth.
- Cadence's counter-expansion: BETA CAE ($1.24B, 2024) and Hexagon Design & Engineering (~$3.16B, closed 2026 — goodwill jumped $2.75B → $4.91B and acquired intangibles $0.72B → $1.87B on the Q2 balance sheet) to match Synopsys-Ansys in multiphysics / System Design & Analysis (+37% in Q2).

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk
- Moat: (i) **design-flow ecosystem lock-in** — flows are validated over years per node; a tapeout at N2/A14 must use foundry-certified tools (Cadence certified on TSMC **N2P and A14 + 3DFabric**, expanded 23–28 Sept 2026), and a respin at leading edge costs $50–100M+ and 6–12 months, so no rational team experiments mid-program; (ii) switching costs measured in re-validation of entire libraries and engineer retraining; (iii) IP portfolio (HBM/DDR/PCIe/UCIe, silicon-proven on N3P/N2P) embedded in customers' chips; (iv) the emulation installed base. Every advanced chip from NVIDIA, AMD, Broadcom, Apple et al. passes through Cadence and/or Synopsys tools — there is no third full-flow option at the leading edge.
- Concrete threats: (a) **hyperscaler in-housing of the *agent layer*** — Google/Amazon/Microsoft silicon teams are already bootstrapping internal agent loops on frontier models (Futurum, Sept 2026, reports one lab's seven-figure token bill before restructuring); the risk is Cadence keeps the engines but loses the orchestration margin/pricing power. Management's defence — agents call Cadence engines more, monetized via usage/token models — is plausible and so far supported by bookings, but monetization is explicitly still being built. (b) **AI-native entrants:** Ricursive Intelligence ($300M raised, NVIDIA-backed), ChipAgents ($74M, ~80 customers), academic agentic frameworks (Purdue DRC-Aid, Sept 2026) attacking point tasks like DRC repair — niche erosion risk, not full-flow replacement. (c) Open-source EDA exists only at trailing nodes. (d) **Geopolitics as forced substitution:** China is building domestic EDA (Empyrean) under export controls — share there (~13% of Cadence revenue in 2025, $680M) can be route-around by decree, not by merit.
- Customers cannot realistically in-source the core engines: four decades of solver/PDK know-how and foundry certification cannot be rebuilt inside a hyperscaler on any useful timeline; they can only in-source the layer *above* it.
- **Irreplaceability score: 9/10** — the strongest chokepoint of this batch; docked one point for agent-layer disintermediation risk and China forced substitution.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months, to 2026-10-01)

### 6. Risks
- **Semi-capex / design-start cyclicality:** EDA has historically been resilient (ratable, ~80% recurring), but a hyperscaler capex digestion year hits new design starts, IP licensing and especially lumpy Palladium hardware first — Q2's record hardware quarter sets a hard comp.
- **Valuation:** priced as a flawless AI royalty; any bookings/backlog stall re-rates a ~30×+ sales-multiple name violently. Guidance also explicitly assumes export-control rules "remain substantially similar" for the rest of 2026 — a stated, binary assumption.
- **China / export controls:** ~13% of revenue, probation to 2028, and a state-sponsored domestic substitute (Empyrean) — downside is regulatory, not competitive.
- **Agent-layer economics unproven:** if hyperscalers keep orchestration in-house and bargain engine usage down, growth reverts to seat-like rates; Synopsys-Ansys breadth (and its Autopilot launch, with GA agentic revenue only from FY2027) makes the duopoly race tighter in systems simulation.
- **Acquisition digestion:** Hexagon D&E + BETA CAE lifted goodwill/intangibles sharply in 2026; leverage affirmed A−/stable by Fitch (8 Apr 2026) against a ~$2.0B FY2026 OCF target — manageable, but integration is now a real line item.

### 7. Earnings-quality flags — explicit verdicts (screen addendum)
Screen flags: RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN (recv_rev_divergence 0.162), INVENTORY_BUILD (inv_rev_divergence 0.484), accrual_ratio −0.044, CFO/NI 1.35.
- **INVENTORY_BUILD — Verdict: BENIGN business-model reason (hardware mix), watch the H2 drawdown.** Cadence is not pure software: it ships Palladium Z3 / Protium X3 emulation *hardware* ($1–10M systems built from custom emulation silicon / FPGA arrays, HBM and liquid-cooled chassis), so inventory is physical WIP/finished systems ahead of customer acceptances. Reported inventories rose **$303.5M (31 Dec 2025) → $318.0M (Q1 2026) → $390.4M (30 June 2026)**, i.e. the build is concentrated in Q2 — the same quarter management called out record hardware demand, 12 new emulation customers, and (in Q1) hardware's best-ever quarter. Building ahead of a guided record hardware year, with backlog at a record $8.1B to ship against, is the expected signature — not channel stuffing of a software product that has no inventory at all. Confirming test for next quarter: inventory should fall as systems are accepted in H2; a second consecutive build without hardware-revenue follow-through would flip this verdict.
- **RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN — Verdict: BENIGN / timing, not earnings ahead of cash.** Reported receivables, net rose **$944.9M (YE2025) → $1,065.0M (30 June 2026)** (+12.7%) against revenue growing +24% YoY in Q2 — receivables are growing *slower* than revenue on the reported face. Decisively, the cash check agrees: **CFO/NI 1.35 and accrual ratio −0.044 (negative accruals)** mean earnings are running *behind* cash flow, the opposite of the flag's concern — Q2 operating cash flow was $635M on $367M GAAP net income. This is billing-schedule arithmetic from term licenses + hardware leases, not revenue pulled forward.
- **Overall earnings-quality verdict:** Both flags trace to the hardware/term-license business model, corroborated by negative accruals and CFO/NI of 1.35 — earnings are cash-backed; no evidence of earnings running ahead of cash.

### 8. Scores
- **Shortage score: 7/10** — no physical shortage of Cadence product, but a structural shortage of *chip-design and verification capacity* industry-wide that only the duopoly relieves, now amplified by agentic-AI usage multiplication and a record, bookings-backed backlog; docked because EDA demand ultimately derives from semi capex, which can pause.
- **Verdict:** The bottleneck before the bottleneck — a ~30%-share duopolist with a record $8.1B backlog, +24% growth and 45.5% margins that gets paid more the more complex AI chips become; the risk is valuation and semi-capex cyclicality, not relevance.
