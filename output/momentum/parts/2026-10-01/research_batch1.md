## SNDK — SanDisk Corp.

**1. Shortage thesis.** Structural AI-driven NAND shortage. Every major NAND maker sold out 2026 capacity (Phison confirmed March 2026; SanDisk's own 2026 capacity sold out, 2027 being pre-booked). Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron were reported sold out of projected **2027** memory supply as of August 2026. CEO David Goeckeler (Goldman Sachs Communacopia, Sept 2025): market expected to remain undersupplied through 2026; 2/3 of fiscal Q4 sequential revenue growth came from pricing, not volume. TrendForce contract prices: +70–75% QoQ in spring 2026, decelerating to +10–15% in Q3 2026 (Sept 2026). Gartner: NAND prices up ~293% in 2026, +~30% more in 2027 (via Motley Fool, 9/29/2026). SanDisk hiked prices 10% for channel/consumer effective Sept 5, 2026.

**2. Backlog / order book.** ~$42B minimum contractual revenue in New Business Model (NBM) long-term agreements — three five-year hyperscaler deals signed in fiscal Q3 2026 (April), some extending to 2031; expanded to 10 NBM agreements by August 2026 with ~$93.9B at floor pricing cited in some reports. Remaining performance obligations ~$41.6B ($41.2B unbilled), backed by $11B+ financial guarantees tied to minimum purchase commitments. Over one-third of fiscal 2027 production is pre-sold/locked in.

**3. Category position.** Not #1. Q2 2026 NAND share (revenue, TrendForce/Counterpoint, Sept 2026): Samsung ~28–29%, SK Hynix ~19–22% (incl. Solidigm), Micron 15%, Kioxia 14%, YMTC ~13–14%, **SanDisk ~11–13%** (5th–6th). Enterprise SSD now 48% of all NAND shipments (vs 26% in Q2 2025) — the high-margin AI segment where SanDisk is concentrated.

**4. Irreplaceability.** NAND is fungible across 5–6 suppliers; hyperscalers can't build fabs cheaply but can and do switch vendors (Samsung, SK Hynix, Kioxia, YMTC, CXMT entering NAND). Moat = pure-play NAND with BiCS technology (BiCS8 QLC at 15% of bits; BiCS10 332-layer sampling July 2026, 59% denser) + the NBM contract architecture that locks in hyperscalers rather than spot exposure. But the shortage is the moat right now, not technology. **Irreplaceability score: 5/10.**

**5. Recent catalysts.** Fiscal Q4 2026 (Aug 2026): revenue $8.97B (+372% YoY, +51% seq, beat $8.25B guide ceiling), non-GAAP gross margin record 84.6%; FY2026 revenue $20.25B (+175%), $11.5B TTM free cash flow. $6B buyback authorized; ~$4.5B repurchased in a single quarter. Investor day (Aug 2026): non-GAAP operating margin target 75% by FY2030. Kioxia+SanDisk announced ¥5T (>$31B) joint fab investment through 2032 (Aug 27, 2026). Barclays upgraded to Overweight, target $1,200→$2,300. Stock +613% YTD (best S&P 500 performer), -35% off late-June peak.

**6. Risks.** Classic peak-cycle memory: 23x trailing P/E is cheapest exactly at peak earnings (value-trap signature). Price momentum is decelerating (70–75% → 10–15% quarterly per TrendForce). YMTC aims to be world's largest NAND producer by end-2027, expanding capacity ~150%; Samsung planning ~50% capacity expansion for 2026, P5 megafab by late 2028. Kioxia already declared NAND prices "have risen enough" (public cap signal, Sept 2026). Cyclical history: no memory peak has lasted.

**7. Shortage score: 9/10.** One-sentence verdict: The purest AI-memory shortage vehicle with locked-in contract revenue — but it's a commodity-cycle peak at the 6th NAND vendor, priced off peak earnings.

## MU — Micron Technology

**1. Shortage thesis.** Structural DRAM+HBM shortage. Management on 9/30/2026 call: expects "memory and storage supply-demand conditions to be much tighter in calendar 2027 and 2028 than they were in 2026" and has "no line-of-sight to when supply and demand will return to balance." 75% of calendar 2027 output already committed; customer discussions now on 2028. 26 take-or-pay strategic customer agreements. Humanoid-robot upside: management says each would need 200+ GB DRAM and multiple TB of NAND.

**2. Backlog / order book.** ~$150B in remaining performance obligations under agreements with qualifying pricing frameworks (take-or-pay volumes, floor/ceiling pricing; CFO calls the figure "inherently conservative"). Covers >35% of revenue through 2030, some agreements into 2031. $32B in customer financial commitments (vast majority cash deposits; $12.3B in Q4 alone). Agreement count: 26 (up from 16 in June 2026). HBM4: >$1B revenue already booked; most FY2027 HBM supply locked at higher prices (closing the HBM-vs-DDR5 margin gap).

**3. Category position.** DRAM: #3 globally — Samsung 39.4%, SK Hynix 24.9%, Micron 23.3% (Q2 2026, TrendForce/Counterpoint, Sept 2026). HBM: #3 — SK Hynix ~50–58%, Samsung ~21–33%, Micron ~18–21%. NAND: #3 at ~15%. Bright spot: **sole U.S.-based HBM manufacturer** (federal incentives + domestic-supplier preference); began high-volume HBM4 production ahead of schedule.

**4. Irreplaceability.** Hyperscalers cannot realistically in-source DRAM/NAND fabrication — fabs cost tens of billions, and three vendors control 90–95% of DRAM. But product is fungible across the three: no customer moat, margin depends on allocation discipline. Rising threat: China's CXMT DRAM share 7–10% and rising, YMTC at 13–14% in NAND. Moat = DRAM oligopoly + take-or-pay contract walls, not technology differentiation. **Irreplaceability score: 6/10.**

**5. Recent catalysts.** Fiscal Q4 2026 (reported 9/30/2026 after close): revenue $54.23B vs $51.07B consensus (+379% YoY), non-GAAP EPS $33.42 vs $31.61, non-GAAP gross margin 87.0% vs 86.1% est.; operating income $44.64B (82.3% margin); OCF $43.97B, adjusted FCF $33.20B. FY2026: revenue $133.19B, non-GAAP EPS $75.52, $62.3B FCF, $73.5B cash, dividend $0.15 declared. Q1 FY27 guide: revenue $61.5B ±$1.5B (vs $57.0B consensus), EPS $38.15 ±$1.00 (vs $35.40), gross margin 86.25% — **a miss vs ~86.7% expected**, which is why the stock faded in thin after-hours (+~1.5–2%). CFO: Q1 is "the floor" for FY27 margins; revenue to grow sequentially every quarter of FY27; "more moderate rate of price increases" beyond Q1. Analysts: UBS reaffirmed $1,625 target; Citi raised to $1,300 (buy). Stock +277% YTD, ~12–14% below June record close. $250B U.S. fab plans (NY + Idaho) announced.

**6. Risks.** Valuation now prices perfection: eighth straight beat drew only ~0.5–2% after-hours; a 15bp gross-margin guide miss stalled the rally. Capex escalation: $25B in H1 FY27, higher in H2 — reinvesting the cycle's cash at the peak. Memory cycle history (the 2022–23 DDR crash). CXMT/Chinese supply. ITC patent case pending (per ad-hoc-news preview). Inventory days 131 (up from 122).

**7. Shortage score: 9/10.** One-sentence verdict: The deepest-mofed shortage book in semiconductors ($150B RPO, 75% of CY27 output sold) — but after a 277% YTD run it's a expectations-management trade, not a surprise trade.

**MU earnings-quality flag — RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN — verdict:** **Benign business-model artifact, not channel stuffing.** Receivables rose to $36.2B from $9.27B YoY (~+290%) while revenue grew +379% YoY — receivables actually grew *slower* than revenue. OCF was $43.97B in Q4 with $33.2B adjusted FCF; customers simultaneously *prepaid* $12.3B in Q4 cash deposits (booked as financing cash flow, not revenue) under take-or-pay agreements — the opposite of stuffing. Watch receivables days vs. revenue growth in Q1 FY27 as the canary.

## LLY — Eli Lilly

**1. Shortage thesis.** The acute shortage is over; the structural demand surge is not. Zepbound/Mounjaro came off FDA shortage lists (2024–25). But management says demand still exceeds supply and capacity remains tight "in the near to mid-term" — manufacturing is now the strategic bottleneck. Evidence: $55B manufacturing build-out since 2020 ($7.8B capex in 2025 alone); Lebanon, IN tirzepatide-API site more than doubled to $9B, begins production end-2026, scaling through 2028; $6.5B Houston site (small-molecule/Foundayo) operational by 2030. GLP-1 market projected $62.2B (2025) → $157.5B (2035), 9.7% CAGR. ~700K new Medicare seniors started GLP-1s under the July 2026 Bridge program; Lilly supplies ~70% of that flow.

**2. Backlog / order book.** No order backlog — pharma sells through channels, not contracts. The analogue of "booked demand": formulary coverage. CVS Caremark (May 28, 2026) put Foundayo on formularies and restored Zepbound as preferred (Oct 1) — Lilly now covered by all three major PBMs. Medicare Bridge ($50/month program, started July 2026) is the volume engine.

**3. Category position.** #1 globally and widening. U.S. GLP-1 share ~60% vs Novo Nordisk ~39–40% (Q1 2026, IQVIA); international ~53–60% vs Novo ~47%. Q1 2026: Mounjaro $8.66B, Zepbound $4.16B. Incretins = ~65% of Lilly revenue (H1 2026). Oral GLP-1: Foundayo (orforglipron, FDA approved April 1, 2026) holds ~1/3 of new oral GLP-1 starts, but Novo's Wegovy pill is launching faster (week-6: 175K scripts vs Foundayo's ~36.6K).

**4. Irreplaceability.** Patent protection on tirzepatide into the back half of the 2030s; compounders (the grey-market substitute) were forced out when the shortage ended — genuine regulatory moat. No customer can in-source a drug. Real threats are horizontal: Novo's amycretin (Phase 3) and next-gen triple agonists; Pfizer/Merck oral contenders; IRAs/price negotiation. Lilly's answer is pipeline depth: 36 Phase III programs, $20B+ in acquisitions since 2025, retatrutide. **Irreplaceability score: 8/10.**

**5. Recent catalysts.** Q2 2026 (Aug 2026): beat on Mounjaro/Zepbound, raised FY2026 guide to $85–87B (from $82–85B), raised non-GAAP EPS ~$2.78 at midpoint; stock +4% on the day. CVS formulary victory (May 28). Medicare Bridge program (July 1) driving ~700K new senior starts. Foundayo launch tracking to expectations (45% of early volume via LillyDirect). +$4.5B new Indiana capex for Foundayo/retatrutide capacity.

**6. Risks.** Concentration: incretins are ~65% of revenue — a single-franchise company on a drug-price-sensitive market. Wegovy pill is out-executing Foundayo's launch; Novo's amycretin is Phase 3. Prices already declining: overall price −13%, international −36% (China reimbursement). Wholesaler destocking caused Q3 (2025 vintage report) sales misses — Mounjaro $3.11B vs $3.7B expected, Zepbound $1.26B vs $1.76B — showing even giant demand doesn't guarantee smooth quarters. Tirzepatide patent cliff in the 2030s; compounding/regulatory overhangs.

**7. Shortage score: 5/10.** One-sentence verdict: A dominant #1 in a secularly growing market with real manufacturing moat — but the shortage phase is over, so it's a compounding story, not an explosive shortage squeeze.

## ANET — Arista Networks

**1. Shortage thesis.** Networking is the *beneficiary* of someone else's shortage, not the shortage owner. AI clusters are supply-constrained on accelerators and memory; Arista monetizes the connectivity layer. Arista itself faced component constraints: purchase commitments surged to **$9.6B** (from ~$3.6B three quarters earlier) to secure chip supply through 2026–27; management described component costs as "horrendous." The durable demand signal: AI networking revenue target raised twice — $2.75B → $3.25B (Feb) → $3.5–3.6B (May 2026) — with execs saying the AI infrastructure cycle could run "for years" across training/inference/scale-up/scale-across.

**2. Backlog / order book.** Order backlog >$3.6B (Q1 2026). Product deferred revenue rising as AI deployments get more complex. The $9.6B in purchase commitments is Arista's own supply-side commitment, signaling how far out it sees demand.

**3. Category position.** Lost the #1 crown in data-center Ethernet switching: IDC Q1 2026 — **Nvidia 21.5% ($2.1B, +193% YoY), Arista 20.7%, Cisco 17.8%**. Nvidia went from <4% share two years ago to #1 via Spectrum-X. Arista still leads in overall cloud/data-center networking historically and won the EOS-software war against Cisco; AI backend "back-end" market expected >$15B annually by end-2026, Ethernet at >65% of new AI back-end deployments.

**4. Irreplaceability.** Moat = EOS software (single binary, telemetry, automation) — hyperscalers use white-box hardware (Celestica: ~27% of AI backend networking revenue; Google's Virgo in-house fabric; Meta/Microsoft white-box builds) for simpler layers while keeping Arista for complex layers. But the disintermediation threat is concrete and growing: **Nvidia sells the GPU+switch+cable+software as one pre-optimized AI factory** — when the customer buys clusters, not switches, Arista gets bypassed on the highest-value deals. Top 2 customers = ~42% of revenue. **Irreplaceability score: 6/10.**

**5. Recent catalysts.** Q2 2026 (Aug 2026): first-ever $3B quarter — $3.036B revenue (+37.7% YoY, beat $2.83B consensus), EPS $1.02 (beat $0.88); non-GAAP operating margin 49.9%; raised FY2026 guide to ~$12.6B (~40% growth, from $11.5B). Etherlink AI fabrics: >100 cumulative customers. Launched XPO 12.8T liquid-cooled pluggable optics ("breakthrough for AI data centers," Dell'Oro). Morgan Stanley target $180 (Overweight); Evercore $200 (Outperform); Evercore called Google Virgo alignment "incremental positive."

**6. Risks.** Nvidia is now the #1 DC-Ethernet vendor and sells the integrated stack Arista can't match — existential to the AI-narrative premium (~46x forward earnings). Customer concentration (42% from two hyperscalers). Margin dilution as large customers negotiate volume pricing (Q2 gross margin 63.4% on mix/component costs). Supply-chain component constraints ("horrendous" costs). White-box/commodity pressure on boxes. Valuation already prices the AI-platform story off a business that is still mostly switches.

**7. Shortage score: 6/10.** One-sentence verdict: The best software moat in networking riding the AI buildout at ~40% growth — but Nvidia just took its crown in its core segment, making this a share-defense story with a rich multiple, not a shortage squeeze.
