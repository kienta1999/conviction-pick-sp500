# Ballot C — QUALITY/MOAT & IRREPLACEABILITY lens — 2026-09-28

## SINGLE-PICK BALLOT

- **Top pick:** KLAC
- **Runner-up:** LRCX
- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** KLA is the only name in this panel that a hyperscaler, a foundry, or a GPU maker fundamentally *cannot* build around. TSMC — the world's most advanced chipmaker — cannot replicate KLA's defect-classification algorithms trained on ~25 years of process data; every wafer passes through KLA metrology/inspection tools repeatedly, and yield entitlement lives in that data moat. The shortage is supply-side and self-inflicted: KLA's own shareholder letter says "customer lead times for our products are increasing due to supply constraints," and CFO Higgins guides H2 CY2026 ~20% above H1 purely as supply availability improves. Backlog is $12.57B, up 60% YoY, with visibility into late 2026 and 2027 — and the stock sits 37.6% below its 52-week high despite record $3.66B quarterly revenue. You get a 62–74% share of process control, an algorithmic moat with no substitute, sold-out shipments, and a drawdown — the disintermediation trap cannot touch it.
- **Key shortage/backlog evidence:** $12.57B backlog as of 6/30/2026, +60% YoY (from $7.86B), with lead times actively extending due to KLA's own supply constraints — demand is rationed by the supplier, not by customers, and management sees "continued sequential growth into calendar 2027."
- **Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk:** LOW. No fab risks its yield to swap metrology vendors — re-qualification means re-qualifying yield models mid-production, which no one does. Tool-OEM bundling (AMAT/ASML) and Onto Innovation are credible threats at the margin, but the 25-year process-data moat cannot be rebuilt on any useful timescale; not even TSMC can in-source it. Export controls (China 28% of 2025 revenue, TSMC >10% concentration) are the real exogenous risk, not displacement.
- **12-18 month return scenario:** Base: ~+40–50% as the $12.57B backlog converts through improving H2 supply and 2027 visibility; bull: ~+80–100% if advanced-packaging process control (+70% YoY guided, ~$1.1B) becomes the market's new growth story and the 52-week-high discount (currently −37.6%) closes.
- **Top risk:** Export-control tightening on China (28% of 2025 revenue) or a WFE cycle rollover; the backlog can be "subject to timing shifts, modifications and cancellations" per the 10-K.
- **Conviction (1-10):** 9

### Top 3 deep dives

**KLAC (#1).** The single most compelling data point is the combination of $12.57B backlog (+60% YoY) with KLA's own admission that *lead times are extending due to supply constraints* — orders are rationing shipments, the classic sold-out compounder setup, and CFO Higgins expects H2 CY2026 ~20% above H1 purely from improving supply availability. The moat is the 25-year algorithmic defect-classification data set: yield entitlement lives in KLA software, and no competitor — not even TSMC — can re-create it on a useful timescale. Base 12–18mo: +40–50% as backlog converts; bull: +80–100% if advanced-packaging control becomes the headline growth story.

**LRCX (#2).** CFO Bettinger's own words — "The industry is fundamentally under-supplying demand, and clean room is a constraint" — is the cleanest demand-outruns-supply statement in the panel, backed by a record $6.72B quarter and $8.10B±$400M September guidance (+20%+ Q/Q) well above consensus ($7.13B). Etch is process-of-record: once qualified in a fab's flow, swapping vendors means re-qualifying yields — no fab does that mid-production — and the installed base is monetizing at a third consecutive record in customer support ($2.47B, +42.6% YoY). Base: +30–40% on the guided ramp; bull: +60–75% if the WFE cycle extends into 2027 at the low-$150B level Lam now projects.

**NVDA (#3).** The most compelling figure is the backlog depth: supply commitments nearly doubled to $95.2B through calendar 2027, with $500B of Blackwell+Rubin revenue visibility through end-2026 and $1T in combined purchase orders expected through 2027 — the deepest pre-committed demand on earth. The honest in-sourcing threat (J.P. Morgan: ASICs surpass GPUs at 53% share in 2027; TPUs 1.4–2x more cost-efficient for inference) is real but marginal: hyperscalers building ASICs remain Nvidia's largest customers, and frontier training still defaults to the CUDA full stack. Base: +30–40% as Rubin ramps; bull: +70–90% if the China datacenter-compute option (assumed zero in guidance) reopens.

**Deliberately left out of my top 10:** APH (ranked 11) — #2 connectors in a second-sourced market, D/E 133% after CommScope, priced at ~40x with no lead-time moat; SNDK (12) — #5 NAND, irreplaceability 4/10, a substitutable commodity vendor riding a cyclical pricing wave priced for perfection (+1,731% 1-yr); CF (13) — fungible commodity, pricing power that collapses to ~4/10 on a Hormuz ceasefire, a war-duration trade not a moat.
