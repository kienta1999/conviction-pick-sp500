## (1) SINGLE-PICK BALLOT

- Top pick: MU
- Runner-up: SNDK
- Thesis (3-5 sentences): Micron is the only name where the shortage is fully contracted, not just described — calendar-2026 HBM is sold out under fixed-price/volume contracts and the vast majority of calendar-2027 HBM supply is already agreed at significantly higher prices, with supply constraints guided through 2027 and 2028. That visibility sits in a 3-firm HBM/DRAM oligopoly where qualification, not capacity, is the gate, so demand converts to revenue rather than leaking to substitutes. The screen still prices a collapse — fwd P/E 4.5 on TTM revenue growth of 256.3% and analyst upside of +67.6% — while Q1 FY2027 was guided to $61.5B ±$1.5B revenue at ~86.25% adjusted gross margin. If the contracted book merely delivers, the earnings base the multiple is applied to keeps stepping up, giving explosive upside from repricing of duration, not just of price.
- Key shortage/backlog evidence: ~$150B of remaining performance obligations across 26 signed Strategic Customer Agreements, all take-or-pay at committed volumes and minimum pricing, with >75% of FY2027 output already committed and SCAs covering >35% of revenue through 2030 — corroborated by $32B of customer financial commitments and $12.3B of deposits received in Q4 alone.
- Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk: medium — irreplaceability 7/10 in the dossier. Hyperscalers cannot in-source HBM (only SK hynix, Samsung and Micron make it at scale, and Samsung lost a whole Blackwell cycle on qualification), but Nvidia/AMD can and do reallocate share between the three qualified vendors at each generation, so Micron's slice is contestable even though the bottleneck itself is not.
- 12-18 month return scenario: rough base / bull case — Base: +35% to +45% as the contracted FY2027 book converts; Bull: +67.6% or more, anchored to the screen's analyst upside, if 2027 pricing and HBM4 share for Vera Rubin hold.
- Top risk: Peak-cycle arithmetic — today's ~86-87% gross margins are what the 4.5x forward multiple says will collapse, and >$50B of FY2027 industry capex is tomorrow's oversupply arriving after late 2028; HBM share can also flip in a single generation.

### Top 3 — shortage/backlog detail and scenario

**1. MU:** The single most compelling point is the $150B RPO across 26 take-or-pay SCAs — management called it inherently conservative, counting only agreements with a determined pricing framework at minimum pricing — backed by customers wiring $12.7B of prepayment deposits in FY2026, the opposite of channel-stuffing. With >75% of FY2027 output committed and 2026 HBM sold out, revenue visibility extends further than for any other cyclical in the set. Rough 12-18mo scenario: base +35% to +45%; bull +67.6% or more (screen analyst upside).

**2. SNDK:** $93.9B of minimum revenue under signed New Business Model agreements with 8 data-centre/edge customers, weighted-average duration >4 years, covering >50% of FY2027 bits and ~two-thirds of FY2028 bits at floor prices, while management says NAND bits remain on allocation beyond calendar 2027. That contracted floor is why a name at ~84.6% Q4 gross margin and only ~11.4% NAND share still ranks second on this lens — the book, not the share, carries the visibility. Rough 12-18mo scenario: base +25% to +35%; bull +55.8% (screen analyst upside) if NBM floors hold and pricing does not normalise early.

**3. GOOGL:** Google Cloud backlog was $514B in Q2 2026, up >$50B sequentially, with management saying just more than 50% should convert to revenue over the next 24 months — the largest disclosed backlog in the triage in absolute dollars, from a supply-constrained business whose customers exceeded commitments by more than 50%. The drag on this lens is that conversion must outrun $195-205B of guided 2026 capex and a negative-FCF quarter. Rough 12-18mo scenario: base +10% to +15%; bull +23.2% (screen analyst upside) if Cloud growth and backlog conversion persist.
