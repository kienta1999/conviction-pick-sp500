## Single-pick ballot

- **Top pick:** APH
- **Runner-up:** WST
- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** Amphenol is the only name in the 13-name dossier that satisfies all three legs of the doctrine at once. It is the #1 interconnect manufacturer globally (>15% share of industrial connectors; 13.0% of cable assemblies), it sits on a genuine physical bottleneck — at 224 Gb/s per lane, signals degrade on PCB traces and only ~3 firms on earth (Amphenol, TE Connectivity, Molex) have the decades of process knowledge and rack-level qualification to supply the cable assemblies AI training runs depend on — and orders have exceeded shipments for four straight quarters while the order book keeps growing. The AI buildout is converting directly into revenue: Q3 2026 was the first-ever $10B quarter (sales $10.23B, +65%, +37% organic), IT Datacom grew 100% in dollars, and FY2026 guidance implies +60% sales and +70% EPS. At 21.4x forward earnings with +38.2% analyst upside, it is also the cheapest of the AI shortage names — the market has not yet priced the backlog as a multi-year annuity.
- **Key shortage/backlog evidence:** Book-to-bill above 1.0 for four straight quarters (1.31 in Q4 2025, 1.24 in Q1 2026, 1.23 in Q2 2026, 1.19 in Q3 2026) on record Q3 orders of $12.17B (+99% YoY, +13% QoQ); backlog grew $1.94B in the quarter and $5.7B year-to-date, and Q3 orders were 17% above the midpoint of Q4 sales guidance — more than a full quarter of shipments already booked. High-speed assembly lead times exceed 12 weeks, with procurement advised to lock multi-year volume agreements for 2027 delivery.
- **Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk:** Low. Hyperscalers cannot realistically in-source this: it is precision manufacturing plus signal-integrity process knowledge plus rack-level qualification, not software — and a failed link can kill an entire training run, so nobody chases the cheapest cable. The real risks are share loss to dual-sourcing (TE/Molex) and technology migration (copper → fiber/CPO at 1.6T), which Amphenol hedges via the CommScope acquisition ($10.5B, adds fiber/structured cabling) and its own optical portfolio (XPO OverPass, 448G co-packaged copper demos). Irreplaceability 8/10 per the dossier — the highest in the AI names.
- **12-18 month return scenario:** Base +25–35% (orders convert into shipments through 2027, FY2026 guidance of $36.9–37.1B sales / $2.82–2.84 EPS delivered, multiple holds near current ~21x forward as the backlog de-risks). Bull +50–70% (book-to-bill stays above 1.2 through 2027, AI interconnect demand stays ahead of qualified supply, and the multiple re-rates toward the high-20s as the market treats the backlog as a multi-year annuity — analyst target of $106.97 already implies +38.2% from the $69.72 close).
- **Top risk:** AI concentration — ~36% of revenue is now AI-linked. A datacenter capex pause would hit hard, and the torrid pace is already moderating: Q4 IT Datacom is guided to only mid-single-digit sequential growth after +30% in Q3.
- **Conviction (1-10):** 8

### Top 3 detail

**1. APH —** The single most compelling data point is the book-to-bill streak: 1.31 / 1.24 / 1.23 / 1.19 over four consecutive quarters on record orders of $12.17B (+99% YoY), with backlog up $5.7B year-to-date — orders are not just strong, they are compounding faster than shipments can clear them, and only three qualified suppliers exist at 224G. Base +25–35% / bull +50–70% over 12–18 months (see single-pick ballot above).

**2. WST —** The most compelling data point is the capacity lead time: new finishing capacity takes 18–24 months to come online, while GLP-1-linked HVP revenue grew "well into the 30% range" organically in Q3 and pricing contributed just over 4 points of growth — above West's 2–3% long-term planning assumption — the classic signature of demand exceeding qualified supply. West is the near-monopoly elastomer supplier specified into Lilly and Novo pens, and a stopper is written into the drug's approved filing, so switching supplier means FDA/EMA re-validation. Base +15–25% / bull +35–45% over 12–18 months if HVP keeps compounding near 20% organically (analyst target $471.00 implies +18.0% from the $390.30 close); the bull case needs the Q3 beat/raise momentum to survive the Feb 2027 Q4 print, including DSO normalisation after the receivables flag.

**3. ANET —** The most compelling data point is the demand side: Q3 revenue $3.66B (+58.5% YoY, beating $3.33B consensus), 2026 guidance raised for the 4th time this year to $13.3B (+48%), and a 2027 outlook of ~$18.5B (+38–40%) with AI fabrics revenue doubling to ~$7B — plus purchase commitments of $11.4B (+17.5% QoQ) to secure component availability. But the disintermediation flag is live and is why it ranks third, not first: NVIDIA now leads datacenter Ethernet (21.5% share vs Arista's 20.7% in 1Q26 per IDC) and bundles Spectrum-X switches into the GPU sale, while Arista rents its silicon from Broadcom (gross margin fell 65.6% → 63.4% YoY) and depends on Microsoft (~26%) and Meta (~16%) for revenue. Base +20–30% / bull +45–60% over 12–18 months (analyst target $249.65 implies +40.0% from $167.62) if the 2027 outlook holds and NVIDIA share loss stalls; the bear case is the trap — networking becomes a cost centre of the AI factory, not a standalone purchase.
