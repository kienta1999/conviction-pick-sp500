## VEEV — Veeva Systems (life-sciences industry cloud)

### 1. Shortage thesis
No physical shortage — this is software. The demand-surge analogue is a **forced migration
window**: Veeva is ripping its flagship CRM off Salesforce's platform onto its own Vault CRM
(GA April 2024), and support for the joint Salesforce-based CRM ends December 2029 (pulled
forward from September 2030). Deloitte's warning, quoted in coverage: "Nearly every
pharmaceutical company will have to redeploy their CRM in the next few years" — a redeployment
costing "tens of millions of dollars and tens of thousands of hours" per customer
(Diginomica, Nov 2025). That is a multi-year, non-deferrable buying wave, not a capacity crunch.
Second driver: industry-specific AI — CEO Peter Gassner raised TAM to $30bn from $20bn at the
Nov 5, 2026 Investor Day, "largely because of opportunities in industry-specific AI."
No evidence of sold-out capacity or lengthening lead times; the constraint is customer
migration bandwidth, and Veeva itself hired to "address increased CRM migration activity."

### 2. Backlog / order book
Veeva does not disclose RPO. Its backlog proxy is **normalized billings**: Q2 FY2027
(quarter ended Jul 31, 2026) normalized billings $768m, +19% YoY (company prepared remarks,
Aug 26, 2026). FY2027 normalized-billings guidance ~$3.87bn, +~14% YoY (raised Aug 26).
Subscription revenue is ~83% of total ($767m of $928m in Q2), so the book is annuity-like
rather than a dated backlog — no "booked out to date X" figure exists. Not found: RPO.

### 3. Category position
#1 in life-sciences CRM: ~80% market share, "serves over 1,500 customers including virtually
all of the top 20 pharmaceutical companies" (RightClick Research, May 2026). On a broader
life-sciences-software definition, analyst stats cited by Diginomica put Veeva largest at
26.8% vs Salesforce 16.4%, Oracle 11.2%, SAP 5.7%. Vault CRM commitments: 12 of top 20
biopharma at Q2 (incl. Eli Lilly, Biogen, Regeneron), 14 of top 20 by the Nov 5 Investor Day;
>200 customers live on Vault CRM including 5 top-20 pharmas; management expects 14 of top 20
to commit and is "optimistic" on the remaining two decisions this year.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk — score 8/10
Moat: validated-system lock-in. Every clinical/regulatory/quality document must live in a
system with a tamper-proof audit trail regulators can inspect; re-validating a replacement is
a multi-year, tens-of-millions project, and Veeva's Vault is the operating system across
clinical, regulatory, safety, quality and commercial. Customers cannot realistically
in-source this — no pharma builds its own validated eTMF/EDC/safety stack.
Threat is real but bounded: Salesforce is now a direct competitor with Life Sciences Cloud,
claims "100+ Life Sciences customers that have selected Life Sciences Cloud and are moving
off Veeva" and 6 of the top 10 pharmas (Salesforce CRO Miguel Milano, via Diginomica,
Jan 2026). Veeva expects to keep 14 of top 20, implying ~6 top-20 losses to Salesforce.
Score 8, not 10, because the #2 is a $300bn platform company actively winning defections
during the forced migration — the moat is switching cost, not impossibility.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- Q2 FY2027 beat, Aug 26, 2026: revenue $928m +18% YoY vs ~$904m consensus; non-GAAP EPS
  $2.35 vs ~$2.22 expected; non-GAAP op margin 45%; FY2027 guidance raised to
  $3.682–3.687bn (+15%), +$42m at the high end.
- Investor Day, Nov 5, 2026: reaffirmed $6bn revenue run-rate by calendar 2030 (built from
  existing products only — excludes Falcon/Vault AI/Aspen, so AI is pure upside); TAM $30bn.
  Vault AI priced at 10% of the applicable Vault subscription (unlimited use); Falcon agents
  have "six- and seven-figure deals in [the] pipeline," revenue expected next year, no AI
  revenue booked yet.
- Q3 FY2027 (ended Oct 31, 2026) guidance $932–935m revenue, non-GAAP EPS $2.33–2.34;
  Nov 6 8-K: CFO said results are expected to "meet or exceed" guidance. Earnings Dec 3, 2026.
- Analyst target raises Nov 6: Jefferies to $375, RBC $365, BTIG/Needham $360, UBS/Evercore
  $325; Wells Fargo raised to $400 on Nov 11 (from $330).
- $2bn buyback initiated Jan 2026; ~$1.4bn remained at Jul 31, 2026; $246m repurchased in Q2.

### 6. Risks
- Migration execution: if Vault CRM disappoints, the forced window hands share to Salesforce;
  CRM is ~20% of revenue today, targeted ~10% by 2030, but a top-20 loss damages the halo.
- Stock already gave back the run: 52-week high $325.99 (Oct 22) → $268.65 close Nov 23
  (−18% in a month), despite the Investor Day — the market is discounting the migration/AI
  story, not chasing it.
- AI monetisation unproven and capped by design: Vault AI is a flat 10% uplift, Falcon
  unpriced at scale; compute cost sits with Veeva.
- Life-sciences budgets are resilient but not immune to pharma cost-cutting; services
  revenue ($150–153m guided Q3) is the cyclical slice.

### 7. Shortage score: 4/10
**Verdict:** No physical shortage, but a forced, dated migration wave plus a free AI option
on a $6bn-by-2030 plan — a quality compounder with a genuine near-term trigger (Dec 3
earnings), not an explosive shortage bet.

## VRTX — Vertex Pharmaceuticals (cystic fibrosis + rare disease)

### 1. Shortage thesis
Weak as a shortage story; strong as a monopoly story. Vertex's CF drugs treat a **fixed
prevalence population** — there is no demand surge supply can't meet, because the patient
pool is capped (~31,000 addressable CF patients in Alyftrek's approved US/UK/EU label
regions). The "surge" is instead: (a) Alyftrek converting Trikafta patients onto a
once-daily, higher-priced successor (Q3 sales $706m, +186% YoY); (b) Casgevy, the first
CRISPR medicine, where the constraint is treatment-centre throughput, not drug supply
(~200 infusions in Q3, 750+ patients initiated since launch — each patient needs months of
hospital care); (c) Journavx, first new non-opioid pain mechanism in 20+ years, 750,000+
prescriptions and $58m in Q3. No quote of sold-out capacity or extending lead times found.
The explosive angle is pipeline breadth, not scarcity.

### 2. Backlog / order book
Not applicable in the industrial sense — no backlog disclosed; revenue is recurring
prescription/patient-based. Nearest equivalents: Alyftrek >18,000 patients globally and
reimbursement in 31 countries ex-US (Q3 call, Nov 2, 2026); Casgevy 750+ patients initiated;
Journavx 750,000+ prescriptions; Palsonify ~500 endocrinologists prescribing. 9M 2026
revenue $9.81bn vs $8.81bn a year earlier (+11%).

### 3. Category position
Unchallenged #1 in CF — effectively 100% of the CFTR-modulator market; "no serious rival"
in CF (Cryptensive review, Nov 2026). CF is ~95% of revenue (global CF revenue ~$3.3bn of
$3.44bn in Q3, +8% YoY). In its newer markets it is the challenger: Casgevy vs transplants
and other gene therapies; Journavx vs cheap generics/opioids; povetacicept vs first-mover
IgAN drugs. Trikafta/Kaftrio Q3 $2.44bn (−8% YoY as patients convert to Alyftrek);
Alyftrek $706m; Casgevy $99m (+30% QoQ, +487% YoY); Journavx $58m.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk — score 9/10
Moat: patents plus disease biology. Trikafta patent to 2037, Alyftrek to 2039 (RMCM
Research, Nov 5, 2026); CFTR modulators require deep structural biology and a validated
manufacturing chain no customer can in-source — patients/payers cannot build a CF drug,
and no competitor has a modulator. Vertex is also researching the genetic cure itself
(CRISPR-licensed CFTR correction, Moderna mRNA partnership), hedging the only long-term
substitution threat. Score 9, not 10, because the CF franchise is a wasting asset on a
~10-year clock and the new franchises (pain, sickle cell, renal, endocrine) face real
competition. Note: one secondary source (AktienSensor) claims Trikafta exclusivity ends
2031 — inconsistent with the 2037 patent figure; treat 2031 as unverified.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- Q3 2026 results, Nov 2, 2026: revenue $3.44bn +12% YoY (US +10% to $2.17bn, ex-US +16%
  to $1.27bn); FY2026 guidance raised to $13.15–13.25bn (from $13.1–13.2bn). But non-GAAP
  EPS $4.76 was flat vs $4.80 a year ago and GAAP net income fell to $654m from $1,083m —
  costs +44% to $2.73bn incl. ~$500m Crinetics compensation/transaction costs; shares fell
  ~4.1% on the print.
- Crinetics acquisition closed Sep 1, 2026 (~$10.0bn equity value, ~$8.8bn net of cash;
  $4.5bn term loan drawn same day) — creates 4th commercial pillar (rare endocrine);
  Palsonify $10.1m revenue in September alone; FY2026 Palsonify guidance $40–50m.
- **Povetacicept PDUFA date Nov 30, 2026** (6 days after this run) for IgA nephropathy —
  approval would create the 5th franchise (renal); Phase 3 RAINIER showed ~52% proteinuria
  reduction; at-home once-every-4-weeks auto-injector.
- Casgevy label expansions: approved ages 2–11 in US/UAE/Bahrain, 5–11 in UK (Oct 2026).
- Inaxaplin (APOL1 kidney disease): AMPLITUDE fully enrolled; Week-48 interim analysis for
  possible US accelerated approval expected late 2026/early 2027.
- VX-017 (blood-type-agnostic stem-cell islet therapy, T1D) Fast Track, pivotal development
  in 2027 — doubles the T1D addressable pool vs zimislecel (blood types A/A-B only).

### 6. Risks
- Concentration: CF still ~80% of sales projected by 2030; any pricing/political attack on
  $350k/yr Trikafta / ~$375k/yr Alyftrek list prices hits the whole thesis.
- Cost explosion: combined non-GAAP opex guidance raised to ~$6.0bn (from $5.65–5.75bn);
  leverage taken on for Crinetics ($4.5bn term loan, tranches due 2027/2028/2029); cash +
  marketable securities fell to $9.8bn from $12.3bn at Dec 31, 2025.
- PDUFA binary Nov 30: a rejection/CRL would hit the renal narrative hard.
- Casgevy/Journavx are slow ramps (hospital throughput; generic/opioid substitution);
  combined Casgevy+Journavx FY2026 guidance is only $500m+.

### 7. Shortage score: 3/10
**Verdict:** Not a shortage play — a patented monopoly with a dated binary catalyst
(povetacicept, Nov 30) and a pipeline that could re-rate the stock, but the "shortage"
itself is absent and costs are eating the growth.

### Earnings-quality flag — INVENTORY_BUILD: BENIGN, largely acquisition accounting
10-Q for quarter ended Sep 30, 2026 (filed Nov 3, 2026), Note I: inventories $2,245.3m vs
$1,686.8m at Dec 31, 2025 (+$558.5m, +33.1%). Breakdown: raw materials $218.2m (vs $259.8m,
**down** 16%), work-in-process $1,632.7m (vs $1,196.9m, +36%), finished goods $394.4m (vs
$230.1m, +71%). The 10-Q states verbatim: "we acquired a total fair value of $461.8 million
PALSONIFY inventory as part of the Crinetics Acquisition in the third quarter of 2026" —
**$461.8m of the $558.5m increase (83%) is acquired inventory at fair value, not organic
stockpiling**; the residual organic build is ~$97m (+5.7%). Finished goods ($394m) are only
~10 days of quarterly product sales ($3.44bn) — no glut. And the cash-flow statement
contradicts the value-trap signature: 9M 2026 operating cash flow $3,196.6m **exceeds** net
income $2,784.8m, while the inventory cash outflow was only $159.6m (vs $446.8m in 9M 2025).
One-line verdict: **INVENTORY_BUILD is benign — 83% is Crinetics purchase accounting, cash
flow still covers earnings; not earnings running ahead of cash.**

## UNP — Union Pacific (western freight railroad)

### 1. Shortage thesis
The strongest genuine shortage in this batch — but cyclical, not structural. US intermodal
demand is outrunning truck and rail capacity in late 2026: domestic intermodal averaged
~300,000 units/week in early October, +12.6% YoY, and "demand then increased sharply over
the last two weeks of October" (C.H. Robinson, Nov 5, 2026); one late-October week likely
ranks among the highest outbound demand periods of peak season. Drivers: diesel +70% YoY
(US avg on-highway diesel; $5.65/gal week of Aug 24 vs ~$3.71 a year earlier), truckload
spot rates forecast +30% in 2026, and ACT Research reporting the US trucking industry
"re-entered a driver shortage for the first time in approximately 3.5 years." C.H. Robinson,
Nov 5: "Union Pacific has identified several capacity-constrained markets and is actively
managing container reservations, giving priority to committed contract customers. Shippers
seeking committed capacity out of California; Texas; Salt Lake City, Utah; and Chicago
should expect limited availability" — with higher rates and tighter capacity; "new
long-term agreements are unlikely to begin before early 2027" (Sep update). Intermodal spot
rates +4.3% YoY net of fuel, with "larger rate increases" expected in Q1 2027 contract renewals.
Railway Age (Nov 2026): rail-bound containers at LA/Long Beach waited 7.19 days in
September, up from 6.34 in July.

### 2. Backlog / order book
No order book disclosed (railroads don't carry backlog). Proxies: intermodal volumes +11%
to 956,000 carloads in Q3; domestic intermodal "fifth consecutive record quarter in volume
and revenue" (EVP Kenny Rocker, Q3 call); intermodal accounted for ~75% of Q3 volume
growth; international intermodal +8% on West Coast imports. The capacity-constrained
markets plus "priority to committed contract customers" is the functional equivalent of a
booked-out book — but no dated backlog figure exists. Not found: backlog $.

### 3. Category position
Largest publicly traded US railroad and one half of the western duopoly (with BNSF; the
other four Class Is are eastern or Canadian). Q3 freight revenue ex-fuel $5.6bn (record).
Intermodal revenue +27% YoY to ~$1.52bn in Q3 — the fastest growth among North American
Class Is that quarter (CSX intermodal +20%, CPKC +22%). If the Norfolk Southern merger
closes, the combined company would be the largest railroad in US history: 50,000+ route
miles, 43 states, ~100 ports.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk — score 7/10
Moat: the physical network itself — nobody can build a second transcontinental western
railroad; capital intensity and land/rights-of-way are prohibitive, and only two western
Class Is exist. Shippers cannot in-source rail; the substitute is trucking, which is
currently *more* expensive and capacity-constrained — the shortage itself proves the moat.
Score 7, not higher, because (a) trucks cap rail pricing power on most lanes (intermodal
only wins where it undercuts trucking), (b) ~75% of the volume surge is intermodal, the
most truck-substitutable segment, and (c) the merger that would entrench the moat is
unapproved and opposed.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- Q3 2026 results, Oct 22, 2026: record revenue $7.2bn +15% YoY; freight revenue ex-fuel
  +6% to record $5.6bn; carloads +6%; net income $2.1bn +16%; reported EPS $3.49 (+16%),
  adjusted EPS $3.53 (+15%, beat ~$3.47 consensus by ~1.7%); revenue beat ~$7.12–7.16bn
  consensus. Adjusted operating ratio 58.8% — core ratio improved 110bps ex-fuel and
  merger costs, but diesel at $4.21/gal (+64%) was a 140bps drag.
- Intermodal momentum: revenue +27% YoY, volumes +11%; domestic 5th straight record quarter.
- Merger: $85bn Norfolk Southern deal (announced Jul 29, 2025): 1 UP share + $88.82 cash
  per NS share (~$320 implied, ~25% premium); $2.75bn/yr synergies; $3.5bn/yr shipper
  savings; ~2.1m truckloads/yr off highways; 10,000 interline lanes become single-line.
  STB Decision No. 47 on Nov 18, 2026 (procedural — completeness/comments, not approval);
  public comments due Nov 18, competing-application comments Nov 27, replies Dec 1; final
  STB decision expected late Aug 2027 (Aug 28, 2027 deadline per Aardvark); UP expects
  close H2 2027 and "first earnings as a merged company about a year after" the Q3 call.
  500+ customer support letters; ~30 customers assisting integration.
- Capex raised to $3.5bn for 2026, pulling forward work planned for 2027; building cash
  reserves ahead of the merger close.

### 6. Risks
- Merger is a binary regulatory event: BNSF, CPKC and CSX seek broad trackage rights;
  Stop the Rail Merger Coalition (farmers, labour, manufacturers) argues consolidation
  raises prices; STB must find the deal *enhances* competition under the stricter
  post-2001 framework — the first major rail merger tested under it. A rejection or
  heavy conditions would unwind the +33% 12-month run's merger premium.
- Cyclicality: the intermodal surge is driven by diesel prices and truck capacity — both
  mean-revert; auto and coal volumes were soft in Q3 even as intermodal boomed.
- Fuel: diesel +64% YoY already cost 140bps of operating ratio; fuel surcharges flatter
  revenue (+15% headline vs +6% ex-fuel) and can reverse.
- Valuation already pricing it in: +33.3% in 12 months, fwd PE 20.1 — the cheapest of
  this batch, but the merger premium is in the price.

### 7. Shortage score: 5/10
**Verdict:** A real, quotable capacity crunch with pricing power — but it is a fuel/trucking
cycle, not a structural shortage, and the stock's upside is hostage to an STB decision a
year away.

## VRSN — VeriSign (.com/.net registry)

### 1. Shortage thesis
No shortage at all — this is a regulated toll road, and the "surge" is a demand
re-acceleration, not scarcity. Management cites a "synergistic convergence" of demand:
new business starts, registrar marketing programs, and an AI tailwind — "AI makes sites
faster to build, and creators want their content found by AI" (CEO Jim Bidzos, Q3 call,
Oct 22, 2026). New registrations hit a record 13.1m in Q3 (vs 12.7m Q2, 10.6m year-ago);
the domain base grew 6.1% YoY after years of ~1–3% growth. There is no capacity
constraint — VeriSign can register unlimited names; the constraint is demand, and demand
is a flow, not a stock. The pricing lever is contractual, not market-driven (see §5).

### 2. Backlog / order book
No backlog. The annuity analogue: .com + .net domain base 182.5m names at Sep 30, 2026
(169.8m .com + 12.6m .net), +6.1% YoY, +3.4m QoQ; net additions 3.4m in Q3 (vs 3.05m Q2,
2.54m Q1). Renewal rate is the retention metric: expected Q3 renewal 74.3% vs 75.4% a year
ago — down, because first-time renewals are a larger share of the base; the record 2026
cohort faces its first renewal test in 2027. Deferred-revenue style visibility is high
(annual renewals), but no multi-year booked figure is disclosed. Not found: backlog $.

### 3. Category position
Literal monopolist: sole operator of the .com registry — "No other entity can provide .com
registry services, and no registrar or registrant can obtain .com domains from any source
other than Verisign" (Kessler v. VeriSign complaint, filed Sep 4, 2026, C.D. Cal.).
.com is ~41% of all domain registrations worldwide (~166m names at Q2 2026). #2 is not a
competitor but a different TLD: .net (also VeriSign), then country-code and new gTLDs run
by others. Also operates 2 of the 13 global internet root servers.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk — score 10/10
The highest score in this batch and the easiest to justify: the moat is a government
contract, not technology. The .com Registry Agreement with ICANN and the Cooperative
Agreement with the US Department of Commerce (NTIA) run through Nov 30, 2030; no customer
— not Google, not a hyperscaler — can route around VeriSign for a .com name, and no
substitute TLD carries .com's default status. Hyperscalers cannot in-source the registry;
the only threats are regulatory (price control, re-bid) or a slow shift in how people
establish online presence (apps, social profiles, AI agents answering without a domain).
The Sep 2026 antitrust suit alleging monopoly pricing (claiming ~$3.10 cost vs $10.26
wholesale price, ~232% markup, 67%+ operating margins) is the concrete form of that
regulatory risk, but it is a private suit, not a government action.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- Q3 2026 results, Oct 22, 2026: revenue $443m +5.7% YoY (beat ~$441.8m); operating
  income $296m +4.2% (~67% margin, after a $7.5m asset charge); non-GAAP EPS $2.41 vs
  $2.42 consensus — a 1-cent miss. Record 13.1m new registrations.
- Guidance raised for the **third straight quarter**: domain-base growth 6.5–7.0% for 2026
  (initial guide was 1.5–3.5%); revenue $1.755–1.760bn; operating income $1.188–1.192bn.
- Contractual price increases: .com wholesale $10.26 → **$10.97 effective Nov 1, 2026**
  (+6.9%); .net $10.91 → $12.00 effective Mar 1, 2027 (+10.0%, announced Aug 27, 2026).
  Contract permits +7% in 4 of 6 years; ~half the .com increase reaches 2027 revenue, most
  of the rest in 2028.
- .web launch: entered the root zone July 2026 after a 10-year dispute (VeriSign paid
  $135m at auction in 2016); sunrise period for trademark holders starts Dec 10, 2026,
  limited registration mid-Jan 2027, general availability Apr 6, 2027 — a new registry
  revenue stream in 2027.
- Dividend: board declared $0.81/share quarterly dividend (record Nov 17, payable
  Nov 24, 2026; ~$3.24 annualised, ~1.0% yield, 34.8% payout); company "intends to
  continue to pay a cash dividend on a quarterly basis."
- Analyst upgrades post-earnings: Baird raised target $355 → $380 (Oct 23, Outperform);
  Wedbush $351 → $367 (Oct 26, Outperform); Citi $325 → $345 (Oct 23, Buy); consensus
  ~$352. Stock hit a 52-week high $324.63 on Nov 5, 2026.
- New product: VeriSign Informed (certificate-revocation check on DNS-grade
  infrastructure) in beta; pricing by year-end 2026, service in H1 2027.

### 6. Risks
- The renewal-rate slide (74.3% vs 75.4%) and the untested 2026 cohort: if the record
  registrations were AI-generated bulk or low-quality names, 2027 renewals could disappoint.
- Regulated pricing is the whole growth story — any NTIA/ICANN pushback or the antitrust
  suit gaining traction caps the escalator.
- Growth is still only ~6%: at 28.6x forward PE and +27.9% in 12 months, the market has
  already paid for the re-acceleration; there is no explosive operating lever — revenue
  growth is arithmetic (base growth + ~7% price), not exponential.
- .web could flop (most new gTLDs have); Verisign Informed is unproven and immaterial.

### 7. Shortage score: 2/10
**Verdict:** The best moat in the batch and the worst explosive-return setup — a 10/10
irreplaceable monopoly growing 6% at 29x earnings; own it for quality, not for a breakout.
