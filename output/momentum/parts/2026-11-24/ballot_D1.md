- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** Amphenol is the cleanest fit for the doctrine in this dossier: the #1 interconnect manufacturer globally, riding a genuine structural shortage in AI datacenter interconnect where only about three firms on earth can supply qualified 224G assemblies at scale. Orders have exceeded shipments for four straight quarters, lead times exceed 12 weeks, and the stock is actually *cheaper* than its growth peers at 21.4x forward earnings despite 65% sales growth in Q3 — the market is doubting "what's next for 2027 profit" (shares off 5.7% Nov 10–16) while the order book keeps growing. Unlike the flashier AI names, the bottleneck is precision manufacturing and qualification, not software a customer can clone, so the explosive upside is not hostage to a single customer's in-sourcing decision.
- **Key shortage/backlog evidence:** Record orders of $12.17B in Q3 2026 (+99% YoY), book-to-bill 1.19:1 — the fourth consecutive quarter above 1.2/1.19 — with backlog up $1.94B in the quarter and $5.7B year-to-date; orders were 17% above the midpoint of Q4 sales guidance ($10.3–10.5B), i.e. more than a full quarter of shipments already booked.

The live risks are dual-sourcing to TE/Molex and copper-to-fiber/CPO migration at 1.6T, both of which Amphenol hedges via the CommScope acquisition ($10.5B, adds fiber/structured cabling) and its own optical portfolio. Irreplaceability scored 8/10 in the dossier — the highest of the genuine shortage names.

### Top 3 detail

**1. APH —** The single most compelling data point is the order book: record Q3 orders of $12.17B (+99% YoY), book-to-bill 1.19 for a fourth straight quarter, backlog up $1.94B in one quarter — while high-speed assembly lead times exceed 12 weeks and procurement is advised to "lock multi-year volume agreements with Amphenol or TE Connectivity for 2027 delivery." Only ~3 firms can supply qualified 224G interconnect, and Amphenol is the largest of them with >15% of industrial connectors and 13.0% of cable assemblies. Base +25–40% over 12–18 months (toward the $106.97 analyst target); bull +50–65% if the book-to-bill holds above 1.1 and 2027 AI interconnect demand matches the order flow.

**2. WST —** The most compelling moat data point is the capacity lead time: new finishing capacity takes 18–24 months to come online, while GLP-1-linked HVP revenue grew "well into the 30% range" organically in Q3 and pricing contributed just over 4 percentage points of growth — above West's 2–3% long-term assumption, the classic signature of demand exceeding qualified supply. The stopper is specified into each drug's approved filing, so switching supplier means FDA/EMA re-validation; Lilly and Novo are expanding their own fill-finish, which *increases* West's component demand rather than bypassing it. Base +15–25% (analyst target $471.00, +18.0% from $390.30); bull +35–45% if HVP keeps compounding near 20% organically. Valuation is the skeptic's caveat: +55.4% in 12 months at 38.4x forward earnings, plus an unresolved receivables flag (receivables +30.3% vs 9M sales +14.2%).

**3. VEEV —** The most compelling data point is the forced, dated migration wave: support for the Salesforce-based CRM ends December 2029, and "nearly every pharmaceutical company will have to redeploy their CRM in the next few years" at tens of millions of dollars each — while Veeva already holds ~80% of life-sciences CRM and 14 of the top 20 biopharma have committed to Vault CRM. The contrarian tell: the stock fell from its $325.99 52-week high (Oct 22) to $268.65 by Nov 23 (−18% in a month) *despite* the Nov 5 Investor Day raising TAM to $30bn and reaffirming a $6bn revenue run-rate by 2030 from existing products alone — the market is discounting the migration story, not chasing it. Base +15–25% (analyst target $325.87, +21.3% from $266.22); bull +35–45% if Dec 3 earnings confirm migration momentum and the remaining top-20 decisions land with Veeva.

**Left out of my top 10:** ANET (disintermediation trap — NVIDIA now leads datacenter Ethernet at 21.5% vs Arista's 20.7%, and Arista rents its silicon from Broadcom; the biggest customer is now the biggest competitor), V (duopoly toll facing its first credible bypass — stablecoins, Wero, CCCA — plus a 2027 renewal cycle covering ~25% of volume), MA (same bypass threat as Visa, and the challenger's thinner scale means less pricing power in a fee-compression world).
