### Top 3 deep-dives

**AVGO.** The single most compelling data point is the velocity of the AI revenue ramp: $8.2B (Q1 FY26 guide, +100% y/y) → $10.8B (Q2, +143% y/y) → ~$16B (Q3 guide, +200% y/y), funded by a $73B backlog with an 18-month ship window. OpenAI's 10 GW partnership (deployments H2 2026–end 2029) means the design-win pipeline is now expanding beyond the original five customers. 12–18mo: base ~+35–45% as the FY27 >$100B line-of-sight materializes; bull ~+70–90% if OpenAI volume pulls forward or a sixth hyperscaler converts.

**MU.** The most compelling shortage data point is that demand exceeds capacity by ~50% (CEO Sanjay Mehrotra) with HBM sold out through 2027 — and most 2027 supply already agreed at higher prices, with ~$100B+ in binding multi-year take-or-pay contracts per dossier batch research. AI memory has flipped from cyclical commodity to contracted strategic input: >35% of revenue through 2030 under take-or-pay. 12–18mo: base ~+30% as contracted revenue rolls in and forward multiples re-rate; bull ~+80–100% if HBM share reaches the 20–25% target by late 2026 and shortage persists into 2028.

**LRCX.** The most compelling data point is that 2026 wafer-fab equipment spend has been raised to the low-$150B range *twice*, and Lam owns ~45% of plasma etch — the process bottleneck for the memory capacity build everyone now agrees must happen. Fabs' capacity constraints (Micron's FY27 factory spend tops $50B; SK Hynix $38B through 2031) convert directly into tool orders, and lead times push visibility into 2027–28. 12–18mo: base ~+30% as memory-capex orders accelerate; bull ~+60% if China revenue holds and the HBM/DRAM buildout front-loads equipment orders.
