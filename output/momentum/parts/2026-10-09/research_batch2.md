## LLY — Eli Lilly

**1. Shortage thesis.** Structural GLP-1 demand surge. Tirzepatide demand has outpaced supply for two years; Lilly CEO Dave Ricks (Q3-2025 earnings call, Nov 2025): "Is there a demand problem? No," blaming channel-stock lumpiness — and the company had "yet to begin" demand-stimulating activities (advertising/sampling) for Zepbound, with the push starting Nov 2025. FDA removed tirzepatide from its shortage list in 2025, but compounding pharmacies sued to keep it listed, claiming it is still short. Lilly has committed a $55B manufacturing build-out since 2020 (1.6x more incretin doses in H1 2025 vs H1 2024) and doubled its Lebanon, IN API plant from $3.7B to $9B (manufacturing begins late 2026, ramping through 2028). Management expects supply "tight in the near to mid-term."

**2. Backlog / order book.** Not a backlog company (pharma), but the demand proxy is violent: Q2 2026 revenue $23.0B; Mounjaro $9.94B (+91% YoY, beat consensus $8.94B); Zepbound $4.93B (+46% YoY); volumes +60% YoY while realized prices fell 13%. International Mounjaro sales +172% YoY ($5.2B) as it joined China's National Reimbursed Drug List. Lilly's own Q2 earnings deck (Aug 2026) put its US incretin share at 60.9% vs Novo Nordisk's 38.8%. Goldman raised the 2030 global anti-obesity market forecast to $114B (June 2026); Morgan Stanley sees the GLP-1 market at $190B by 2035.

**3. Category position.** #1 in incretins by a widening margin — 60.9% US share vs 38.8% for #2 Novo Nordisk (Lilly Q2 2026 earnings presentation, Aug 2026). Mounjaro/Zepbound were a $12.8B quarter in Q1 2026 (~65% of company revenue).

**4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk.** Patents + manufacturing scale are the moat: injectable peptides at this scale require sterile fill-finish capacity and cold-chain distribution that takes years and tens of billions to build. Customers can't in-source (patients/payers, not hyperscalers). Real substitute threat: compounders (Lilly beat them in court — Strive Specialties' antitrust suit dismissed Aug 3, 2026, W.D. Texas, and mass compounding ruled illegal), and oral alternatives where Novo leads today (Wegovy pill ~$497M Q2 sales vs Lilly's new Foundayo ~$98M). **Irreplaceability score: 7/10.**

**5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months).** Q2 2026 (Aug 6, 2026): revenue beat +11%, EPS beat +39%, guidance raised (Q1 had already gone to $82–85B revenue / $35.50–37.00 EPS); a third raise is expected at Q3 on Oct 29, 2026. Foundayo (orforglipron), the first oral small-molecule GLP-1 with no food/water restrictions, got FDA approval Apr 1, 2026; $98M first partial quarter. $3.35B InnoCare licensing deal announced Oct 7, 2026. Antitrust win vs compounding pharmacy Strive (Aug 2026); Novo's misleading-advertising suit vs Lilly (Jul 21, 2026) is defensive noise. 53% incretin share ex-US (Q1 2026).

**6. Risks.** Realized prices fell 13% in Q2 — price erosion is the structural tax on volume; incretins = 65% of revenue (concentration). Oral race is genuinely contested: Novo's Wegovy pill did ~$497M in Q2 vs Foundayo's $98M. LillyDirect/cash-pay and the Medicare GLP-1 Bridge ($50/mo from Jul 2026) expand volume at lower margin. P/E ~41, market cap ~$1.1T — expectations are priced. Litigation overhang (Novo suit, compounder enforcement).

**Shortage score: 8/10** — demand structurally exceeds supply; the constraint is manufacturing, which is also the moat.
**Verdict: The purest demand-surge name of the four — #1 in a $100B+ market Lilly itself can't yet fully supply, with volume (+60%) overwhelming price decay (-13%).**

## ANET — Arista Networks

**1. Shortage thesis.** AI datacenter networking demand surge rather than a physical shortage. AI back-end network market expected >$15B/yr by end-2026; 800G switches alone were 35.8% of DC segment revenue in Q1 2026 (IDC). Arista guided AI fabrics revenue of at least $3.5–3.6B in 2026 within ~$12.6B total — AI is ~28% of the guide. Hyperscalers (MSFT, AMZN, GOOG, META) are all raising AI infra capex; Arista sells Ethernet regardless of whose AI chips sit in the racks ("picks and shovels," less GPU-concentration risk).

**2. Backlog / order book.** Deferred revenue: $5.1B short-term + $1.8B long-term as of Q2 2026 (will convert to realized sales over time). Total backlog cited at ~$4.7B entering 2026. Q2 2026 (Aug 2026): revenue $3.04B (+37.7% YoY, +12% QoQ), EPS $1.02 beat consensus. 2026 guidance: ~$12.6B revenue, Q3 EPS guide $1.06–1.08. ~$13.3B cash, zero debt.

**3. Category position.** Arista was #2 in DC Ethernet at 18.7–20.7% share (IDC Q1–Q2 2026) — behind NVIDIA, which surged from <4% to 21.5% (Q1) / 20.4% (Q2) on Spectrum-X bundles. Cisco holds ~28.7% of the *total* Ethernet market but only 17.8% of DC. Arista is the #1 pure-play/market-share independent systems vendor in high-speed (400G/800G) DC networking, and the Ethernet standard-bearer vs InfiniBand/Spectrum-X: Ethernet captured >65% of new AI back-end deployments by early 2026.

**4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk.** Moat = EOS (extensible Linux-based network OS) + hyperscaler qualification depth + 1.6T product cycle leadership. Concrete in-sourcing threats: (a) NVIDIA bundling Spectrum-X with GPU clusters so networking is no longer a standalone purchase — the #1 DC vendor now; (b) white-box/merchant-silicon (Arista is 100% dependent on Broadcom merchant silicon); (c) hyperscalers building SONiC-based white-box fabrics in-house. Counter: Meta/Microsoft are "increasingly wary of vendor lock-in," favoring Arista's open multi-vendor Ethernet over closed stacks. **Irreplaceability score: 7/10** — switching is costly/complex and Ethernet is winning, but the bundling threat is real and priced as the bear case.

**5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months).** Q2 beat (Aug 2026, +37.7% revenue). Bernstein initiated coverage at Outperform (Oct 2026); consensus Buy, ~$231.70 average target vs ~$211 (Oct 6). New open Ethernet rack-scale portfolio for AI launched with AMD, Broadcom, Meta, Microsoft, Qualcomm partnerships (Oct 2026). Q3 2026 earnings Nov 3 (defined near-term catalyst). Zacks downgrade to Hold after the +65% YTD rally is a contrarian caution flag, not a fundamental break.

**6. Risks.** Customer concentration: Microsoft >25% of 2025 revenue; ~42% from two cloud titans — one capex pause would be violent. Margin pressure as buyers scale (48% op margin is the peak to defend). Valuation: ~64x trailing P/E, +65% YTD — the NVIDIA-disintermediation scenario is the bear case at exactly this multiple. Taiwan Strait/supply exposure for merchant silicon and optics.

**Shortage score: 7/10** — no physical shortage, but a demand wave with multi-quarter contracted visibility (deferred revenue + backlog) and the AI-buildout standard shifting to its home turf (open Ethernet).
**Verdict: The highest-quality systems-layer AI-infra bet — 38% growth with 48% operating margins and a balance sheet fortress, but concentration + Nvidia bundling + a 64x multiple make it a momentum rather than a value trade.**

## APH — Amphenol

**1. Shortage thesis.** Genuine order-book demand surge: customers are ordering 23% more interconnect than Amphenol can ship. CEO Adam Norwitt: interconnect is "the central nervous system of AI" — a $3M server rack is worthless without the connectors/cables. IT datacom is ~4x its size two years ago; AI-related IT datacom grew 63% organically YoY and is 43% of total sales. Rising power density expands content-per-datacenter; inference workloads "require an unprecedented level of interconnect intensity." Multiyear capacity commitments from hyperscalers (Meta–Corning $6B, Prysmian–Molex €5.5B, July 2026) signal the whole chain is capacity-constrained.

**2. Backlog / order book.** Q2 2026 (July 2026): orders $10.732B (+94% YoY), book-to-bill 1.23 — 23% above $8.8B shipped. Q1 2026: orders $9.435B (+78% YoY, +12% QoQ), b/b 1.24. Both quarters showed positive book-to-bill in *every* end market. Sales $8.8B (+55% YoY, +30% organic), adjusted EPS $1.35 (+67%), adjusted op margin ~28% (+380bp YoY). CommScope's IT datacom business (acquired Jan 2026) nearly doubled YoY and will be ~half of CommScope's 2026 sales.

**3. Category position.** #1 or co-#1 global interconnect maker alongside TE Connectivity. The $10.5B CommScope CCS acquisition (closed Jan 2026, largest in company history) added ~$3.6B sales and 26% EBITDA margins, materially widening its fiber-optic and high-density interconnect footprint — the exact bottleneck layer for AI datacenters.

**4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk.** Moat = manufacturing scale + breadth (tens of thousands of SKUs) + customer qualification cycles; interconnect is the layer hyperscalers genuinely cannot in-source at scale (precision stamping/molding at hyperscale volumes). But connectors are the most commoditizable layer of the stack: rivals TE Connectivity (DDN +34% YoY, bookings +70% YTD) and private Molex compete on most sockets; Marvell/Credo compete in active copper/DSP. The moat is share-of-socket breadth and execution, not patents. **Irreplaceability score: 6/10.**

**5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months).** Q2 blowout (July 2026): +55% sales, +67% EPS, b/b 1.23, stock ~3% off all-time high (Oct 1, 2026), +312% over 3 years. CommScope integration exceeding plan ($1.2B+ Q2 contribution). AI datacom mix shifting to 43% of sales. Analysts model $50.7B revenue / $10.8B earnings by 2029 (~20.5% CAGR).

**6. Risks.** 43% of sales now tied to IT datacom = a leveraged AI-capex bet; any hyperscaler pause hits disproportionately. Record orders could include double-ordering. Debt from the $10.5B CommScope deal (pro-forma net leverage ~1.8x) and TE Connectivity as a genuinely formidable #2. Valuation embeds the compounding — fair value implies only ~18% upside.

**Shortage score: 8/10** — book-to-bill >1.2 two quarters running with every end market positive is the closest thing to a shortage print in this batch.
**Verdict: The purest order-book momentum name — customers can't unorder cable assemblies, and b/b 1.23 means next quarter's growth is already contracted; just know 43% of revenue now rides the AI-capex cycle.**

## CDNS — Cadence Design Systems

**1. Shortage thesis.** No physical shortage — it's a contractual chokepoint business. Record $8.1B backlog = ~1.3x annual revenue, with $4.2B contracted to land within 12 months (Q2 2026, July 27, 2026). The driver: AI-accelerator and custom-silicon design starts surging; agentic AI tools (AgentStack, ChipStack, AuraStack, ViraStack/InnoStack) run hundreds of parallel iterations, *increasing* EDA engine consumption rather than cannibalizing licenses. Revenue +24.2% in Q2 with double-digit growth across all product groups; IP segment +40%+.

**2. Backlog / order book.** Record $8.1B at Q2 2026 (beat Zacks backlog consensus $7.68B); Q1 was $8B. ~$4.2B recognized within 12 months vs $6.26–6.34B 2026 revenue guide — ~two-thirds of the guide is contracted. ~80% recurring revenue; 45.5% adjusted op margins; ~$2B operating cash flow.

**3. Category position.** EDA duopoly: Synopsys ~31%, Cadence ~30%, Siemens ~13% globally. Cadence holds ~60%+ of leading-edge design flows by some counts; its Spectre AI/Genus stack is standard in 70% of 5nm+ designs. TSMC certified Cadence for A14 (1.4nm) and N2P (Sep 23, 2026) — albeit alongside Synopsys and Siemens, so not exclusive. Customers: NVIDIA, Apple.

**4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk.** The strongest moat in the batch: no modern chip exists without Synopsys/Cadence/Siemens software; ~3,200+ patents; multi-year engineer retraining makes switching prohibitive. Hyperscalers designing their own chips (Google TPU, AWS Trainium) still *must* buy EDA licenses — custom-silicon growth is a tailwind, not a disintermediation threat. Real threats are geopolitical, not technological. **Irreplaceability score: 10/10.**

**5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months).** Guidance raised twice in 2026 (to $6.26–6.34B revenue, $8.05–8.15 EPS). TSMC A14/N2P certification + silicon-proven UALink IP on N3P/N2P (Sep 23, 2026). ChipStack RTL Generation Agent launch (Sep 22, 2026): 24% area reduction, 18% power reduction, 100% functional accuracy vs foundation-model code. AuraStack AI super agent (July 2026). Intel IP agreement; FBN Outperform (Sep 11, 2026). Hexagon Design & Engineering integration driving +37% YoY System Design & Analysis revenue. Q3 2026 earnings Oct 26, 2026.

**6. Risks.** China export controls are the live overhang: 12–16% of revenue exposed; Cadence pleaded guilty to export-control conspiracy in July 2025 ($140M penalties, 3 years' federal probation to ~July 2028). Domestic Chinese EDA (Empyrean, UniVista) could erode long-term share. Hexagon acquisition is EPS-dilutive (-$0.28) until 2027 accretive. Premium valuation; insider selling noted.

**Shortage score: 7/10** — not a supply shortage, but a contracted demand backlog at 1.3x revenue with AI-design-start growth accelerating.
**Verdict: The highest-moat name in the batch — a legal duopoly tollbooth on every AI chip ever designed, with the record backlog to prove it; the risk is entirely geopolitical (China/export controls), not competitive.**
