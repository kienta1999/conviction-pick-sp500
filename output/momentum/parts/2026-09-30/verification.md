## Claim 1 — BACKLOG/DEPOSITS: ~16 long-term customer agreements covering ~20% of DRAM bits / ~30% of NAND bits, tied to ~$22B in customer cash deposits and commitments (FQ3 FY26 earnings call, June 2026)

**Verdict: CONFIRMED**

Multiple independent sources report the same figures from Micron's FQ3 FY26 earnings call (reported June 24, 2026): 16 strategic customer agreements covering ~20% of DRAM bit supply and ~30% of NAND bit supply, tied to ~$22B in customer deposits/commitments. Two sources add that 14 of the 16 agreements carry ~$100B in cumulative minimum contract revenue (through calendar 2030), with ~$18B of the $22B being cash deposits and the remainder financial commitments; the 10-Q confirms the agreements are take-or-pay.

Sources:
- Binance Square (Binance News), "Micron Technology's Preview of Its Q4 FY2026 Earnings Report," 2026-09-28: "16 strategic customer agreements covering roughly 20% of DRAM bit supply and about 30% of NAND bit supply, corresponding to at least $100 billion in minimum contract revenue and $22 billion in customer deposits and commitments."
- MEXC earnings preview (republishing call details), crawled 2026-09-29: "sixteen such agreements covering roughly 20% of DRAM volume and about 30% of NAND volume through calendar 2030, with fourteen of them carrying cumulative minimum revenue near $100 billion and approximately $22 billion in customer financial commitments, including about $18 billion of cash deposits." The 10-Q confirms take-or-pay structure.
- Baptista Research, "Micron Earnings Preview: Why Forward Gross Margin Matters Most," 2026-09-28: "16 SCAs... covering roughly 20% of DRAM volume and one-third of NAND volume over their terms. Fourteen represented approximately $100 billion of cumulative minimum contractual revenue, while the agreements signed so far carried approximately $22 billion of cash deposits and related financial commitments."

No contradiction found. All figures match the dossier.

## Claim 4 — MOAT: Only ~3 firms on earth make leading-edge DRAM (Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron); HBM consumes ~3x the fab capacity per bit of DDR5

**Verdict: CONFIRMED**

- Three-firm oligopoly: CONFIRMED. TechTimes (2026-09-03, citing TrendForce Q2 2026 data): "Samsung, SK hynix, and Micron redirecting advanced manufacturing capacity toward high-bandwidth memory." TechTimes (2026-06-24): "Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron — which control over 95% of global DRAM production." Caveat worth noting: China's CXMT reached ~10% DRAM revenue share in Q2 2026 (TrendForce, via TechTimes 2026-09-03), but in commodity/legacy DRAM — it is not a leading-edge DRAM/HBM supplier, so the "only ~3 firms make leading-edge DRAM" claim stands.
- HBM ~3x wafer capacity: CONFIRMED, attributed to Micron itself. Third-party research note citing Micron: "Micron estimates that HBM3E consumes roughly 3× the wafer supply of DDR5 to produce a given number of bits at the same process node." TechTimes (2026-06-24): "producing one gigabyte of high-bandwidth memory consumes roughly three times the wafer capacity of producing one gigabyte of DDR5." TechTimes (2026-09-03): "Micron noted publicly that its HBM ramp required a 3-to-1 conversion ratio of wafer capacity — meaning every wafer redirected to HBM production removed three DDR5 wafers from the commodity market."

No contradiction found.
