## SNDK — Sandisk (NAND flash + enterprise SSD)

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Business | NAND flash memory and SSDs; spun off from Western Digital Feb 2025. Datacenter segment is the growth engine (enterprise SSDs for AI data centers). |
| FY26 scale | FY2026 revenue USD 20.25B (+175% YoY); FQ4 FY26 revenue USD 8.965B (+51% QoQ, +372% YoY); non-GAAP gross margin 84.6% in FQ4. |

**1. Shortage thesis.** Yes — structural, AI-driven. Management says customer demand is outpacing supply and "NAND bits will remain on allocation beyond calendar year 2027" (FQ4 FY26 call, Aug 5, 2026). Two-thirds of sequential FQ4 revenue growth came from higher pricing, one-third from volume — price is doing the work. TrendForce: AI servers will account for 44% of total NAND demand in 2026; enterprise HDDs were on 2-year backorder (Nov 2025) and enterprise SSDs filled the gap. Sandisk estimates the NAND market grows from over USD 300B in 2026 toward USD 500B in 2027. Gartner projects NAND market revenue up 372% this year (per Fool, Sep 2026). CEO David Goeckeler on the FQ4 call: "AI is fundamentally a memory-centric storage-intensive problem... reshaping the demand equation for NAND."

**2. Backlog / order book.** Sandisk finalized 8 "New Business Model" (NBM) multi-year agreements securing a minimum of USD 93.9B in future revenue, with supply visibility of 4+ years (FQ4 call). NBMs cover over 50% of bits in FY2027 and ~two-thirds of bits in FY2028, with variable pricing bounded by floors and ceilings. Datacenter revenue up 437% YoY in FY2026, now 38% of bit portfolio (was 12% a year ago).

**3. Category position.** Not #1 — #5 in NAND. Q2 2026 revenue shares (TrendForce/Counterpoint, Sep 2026): Samsung ~29%, SK hynix ~18%, Micron ~13-15%, Kioxia ~14%, Sandisk ~11-14%, YMTC ~13-14%. Samsung's share is falling (prioritizing higher-margin DRAM) and Chinese YMTC is rising.

**4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk.** NAND is a commodity — suppliers compete on price, products are interchangeable. The moat is structural, not product: a 25-year JV with Kioxia (fabs in Japan), BiCS process tech (BiCS 8 = 218 layers in majority production; BiCS 10 = 332 layers sampling since Jul 3, 2026, mass production targeted 2027), and brutal capital intensity (Sandisk+Kioxia announced a combined ¥5T / USD 31B+ investment through 2032 on Aug 27, 2026, including a new Kitakami fab for FY2029). Only ~6 firms on earth make leading NAND; hyperscalers cannot realistically in-source it. But no customer is locked to Sandisk on technology — it wins on allocation availability, not indispensability. **Irreplaceability score: 5/10.**

**5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months).** Record FQ4 FY26 beat (Aug 5, 2026); Analyst Day (Aug 13, 2026) centered on durable AI NAND economics; FQ1 FY27 guidance: revenue USD 10.30-10.80B, gross margin 83-85%, non-GAAP EPS USD 44.00-46.00; promotion to the S&P 100; USD 4.5B Q4 buybacks + new USD 14B authorization; QLC Stargate platform shipping for revenue (AI data-lake workloads); USD 31B+ joint JV capacity commitment with Kioxia (Aug 27).

**6. Risks.** Classic memory cyclicality — trading at ~10x annualized run-rate EPS means the market prices a cycle peak, not a growth business (digitaltradinglab, Sep 25, 2026). NBM contracts cap the upside too: prices bounded by ceilings on 2/3 of FY28 bits, so further tightening doesn't flow to Sandisk. TrendForce price momentum is decelerating (NAND contract prices +70-75% QoQ in spring vs +10-15% now). Consumer revenue fell 32% QoQ to USD 556M — bits are being diverted from consumer to datacenter, so any AI demand wobble hits volume and price at once. China: YMTC tied Sandisk at ~13% share and targets 15%; CXMT entering NAND. Stock is ~24% below its June high of USD 2,354 after the mid-summer pullback.

**Shortage score: 9/10.** Bits rationed past CY2027; price-driven 372% YoY quarter; NAND market revenue projected +372% this year.

**Verdict:** The purest NAND-shortage vehicle, with allocation through 2027 and record margins — but price ceilings on most future bits cap the upside, and the whole thesis is one price cycle, so it earns momentum points only while NAND prices are still rising.

## MU — Micron Technology (DRAM incl. HBM, NAND)

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Business | DRAM (incl. HBM for AI accelerators) and NAND. DRAM = 73% of revenue; NAND = 26%. |
| FY26 scale | FY2026 revenue USD 133.19B (+256%); GAAP net income USD 84.97B; adjusted FCF USD 62.3B; net cash USD 68.3B. FQ4 FY26 (reported Sep 30, 2026): revenue USD 54.23B (+31% QoQ, +379% YoY), adj. EPS USD 33.42, non-GAAP gross margin 87.0%. |

**1. Shortage thesis.** The strongest of the three. Management described "one of the most severe memory shortages in industry history" (FQ4 call). HBM supply fully contracted — agreements on price and volume for all of CY2026 HBM completed, and Micron can meet only ~50-66% of core-customer HBM demand into 2027 (TrendForce). Capacity is ~75% booked through end-2027; management expects supply-demand "much tighter in 2027 and 2028" and has "no line of sight to when supply catches demand." The FQ1 FY27 guide (USD 61.5B ± 1.5B) was raised well above consensus. New cleanroom capacity won't add meaningful output until after late-2028 (FY27 capex > USD 50B).

**2. Backlog / order book.** 26 Strategic Customer Agreements (SCAs), take-or-pay, up from 16 in June; remaining performance obligations ~USD 150B measured at minimum contracted prices (majority have price floors; management says actuals run "far above"). Customer financial commitments rose to USD 32B (from USD 22B), the vast majority cash deposits — USD 12.3B received in FQ4 alone. SCAs cover >35% of revenue through 2030, several extending into 2031. >75% of FY2027 output already committed.

**3. Category position.** DRAM: #3 and closing — Q2 2026 revenue shares: Samsung ~39%, SK hynix ~25%, Micron ~24% (TrendForce/Counterpoint). HBM: #3 — SK hynix 50%, Samsung 33%, Micron 18% (Counterpoint Q2 2026). NAND: #3-4 at ~13-15%. Micron is qualified at Nvidia for HBM4 alongside SK hynix (HBM4 ramp 2x faster than prior generation; >USD 1B shipped). DRAM share is up ~3pp YoY while SK hynix lost ~13pp.

**4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk.** Only 3 firms make DRAM at scale (~88% of the market); HBM is qualified, leading-edge tech (1-gamma DRAM; HBM4 >11 Gbps; HBM4E on track for 2027 Nvidia's post-Vera-Rubin platform). Hyperscalers cannot build or in-source DRAM/HBM — no substitute routes around memory; custom AI ASICs still need HBM attached. The SCA deposit structure adds qualification lock-in: customers prepay billions for capacity priority. Threats: CXMT's DRAM share rose to ~7-10% in Q2 2026 (direction unsettled, but China's entry is the real structural risk), and memory remains a commodity at the contract level. **Irreplaceability score: 8/10.**

**5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months).** FQ4 FY26 beat-and-raise (Sep 30, 2026): all metrics beat, FQ1 FY27 guide well above consensus (EPS USD 38.15 ± 1.00 vs ~USD 35.4 consensus). 10 new SCAs signed in one quarter. Citi raised target to USD 1,300 (Aug quarter) on firm DRAM prices; post-earnings Rosenblatt raised to USD 1,900 (Buy), D.A. Davidson and Mizuho also raised. FY27 capex guide >USD 50B for cleanroom build. HBM4 volume shipments aligned with Nvidia Vera Rubin. BofA estimates buybacks could reach USD 60-100B in FY28-29.

**6. Risks.** The stock is up ~707% in a year — perfection is priced; after-hours reaction to the FQ4 beat was only +1.7% vs 6.3% options-implied. Deeply cyclical industry: FY2025 gross margin was 40.9%, now 81%+ — the multiple is lowest exactly when earnings peak (the memory value-trap pattern). Consumer end markets (phones/PCs) may see double-digit unit declines. New capacity arriving late-2028/2029 sets up the classic bust if AI demand pauses. China (CXMT DRAM, YMTC NAND) is the structural supply threat.

**Shortage score: 10/10.** HBM sold out through CY2026, 75% of 2027 capacity booked, USD 150B take-or-pay RPO backed by USD 32B in customer cash — the shortage is so structural that customers are pre-financing Micron's fabs.

**Verdict:** The deepest structural-shortage bet in the set — contracted revenue with price floors through 2030 de-cyclicalizes a cyclical business — but the +707% run means the momentum trade is betting the shortage outlasts the multiple compression, and memory cycles have always ended eventually.

### MU earnings-quality note: RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN

Receivables rose to USD 36.2B from USD 9.27B a year earlier (+291% vs +379% revenue), using USD 25.2B of operating cash flow in FY2026. But this is **not** the value-trap signature: FQ4 operating cash flow was USD 44.0B (81% of revenue), adjusted FCF USD 33.2B, CFO/NI = 1.02, accrual ratio = -0.009 — cash is being collected faster than earnings are booked. Benign explanation: FQ4 was a 14-week quarter with back-loaded shipments during a 379% YoY revenue ramp, so quarter-end receivables mechanically spiked; meanwhile customers wired USD 12.3B in cash deposits the same quarter (the SCA model means customers prepay, the opposite of a collection problem). **One-line verdict: benign timing artifact of the revenue ramp plus SCA prepay structure — cash confirms the earnings, not contradicts them.**

## ANET — Arista Networks (datacenter Ethernet switching)

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Business | High-speed Ethernet switching + EOS software for hyperscale datacenters; AI back-end (GPU-to-GPU) fabrics are the growth engine. |
| Q2 2026 | Revenue USD 3.036B (+37.7% YoY, first USD 3B quarter); non-GAAP EPS USD 1.02 (+39.7%); FY26 guidance raised to ~USD 12.6B (from USD 11.5B; third raise this year). Q3 EPS guide USD 1.06-1.08. |

**1. Shortage thesis.** Indirect but real. The "Year of the Network": after two years of GPU buying, hyperscaler networks are the bottleneck, driving an 800G-to-1.6T Ethernet upgrade cycle. Arista's own supply chain went from semiconductor decommits and 52-week lead times (Q1) to a secured position — it nearly tripled multiyear purchase commitments to USD 9.7B (vs USD 3.6B a year ago), locking in memory and networking components with visibility into 2027. The Ultra Ethernet Consortium (UEC), which Arista co-founded, published its 1.0 spec in mid-2025, standardizing AI networking on Ethernet and eroding Nvidia's InfiniBand advantage; Dell'Oro: Ethernet was >2/3 of AI back-end switch sales in Q3 2025, up from <1/2 the year before. Advanced optical transceivers/DSP chips for 1.6T were in short supply as of early 2026. Arista's AI fabrics target: USD 3.5B for 2026 (2x+ YoY); Etherlink customers >100 (up from a handful in 2024).

**2. Backlog / order book.** Arista doesn't publish a classic backlog; the proxy is multiyear purchase commitments: USD 9.7B (up from USD 8.9B in Q1, USD 3.6B a year ago), extending into 2027 — supply locked to deliver booked AI demand. Management also signaled one, possibly two, additional 10%+ customers beyond Microsoft and Meta.

**3. Category position.** Not #1 overall — ~19-22% of total datacenter switching (Cisco leads overall). But #1 where it matters for AI: >40% share in high-speed 400G/800G with cloud titans. Founded the UEC; 7060XE7 (1.6T, Broadcom Tomahawk 6, 100 Tbps) endorsed by Meta and Microsoft, shipping Q4 2026.

**4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk.** The moat is EOS software: deep integration and operational lock-in with hyperscalers, embedded in their automation stacks. But the in-sourcing threat is concrete, not hypothetical: (a) Microsoft and Meta are ~40% of revenue and both have run white-box/Sonic programs on front-end networks — the back-end could follow; (b) merchant silicon is Broadcom's (Tomahawk 6) — anyone can buy the chip; the value-add is EOS and integration; (c) Nvidia pushes InfiniBand and its own Spectrum-X Ethernet. Standardization via UEC helps Ethernet adoption but commoditizes the hardware layer over time. The scale-across routing push (USD 15-20B TAM by 2030) expands the moat into new territory. **Irreplaceability score: 6/10.**

**5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months).** Q2 2026 beat + guidance raise (Aug 4, 2026): revenue USD 3.04B vs USD 2.83B expected, EPS USD 1.02 vs USD 0.89; stock +10%+ on the report, hit USD 214.89 high. Bernstein initiated coverage at Outperform with USD 250 target (Sep 30, 2026). 1.6T 7060XE7 shipping Q4 2026 with Meta/Microsoft endorsement. USD 9.7B supply commitments (near-triple YoY). Stock +56% YTD; next earnings Nov 2.

**6. Risks.** Valuation is the headline risk: ~64x P/E (Sep 30), priced for perfection — the stock pulled back after the August beat despite everything firing. Customer concentration: Microsoft and Meta = ~40% of revenue; any capex pause or white-box shift is existential. Insider sales of USD 714.9M over the prior 90 days (per Sep 30 MarketBeat report). Gross margin ticked down; if AI cluster build plans shift, the USD 9.7B purchase commitments become inventory risk. Broadcom dependence for silicon. The growth acceleration was partly core front-end/enterprise, not pure AI — the "AI growth" narrative is more mixed than the headline.

**Shortage score: 5/10.** Arista is a demand beneficiary, not a shortage asset — its own product isn't rationed; the shortage shows up in components it must secure and demand it can't quite build fast enough to meet.

**Verdict:** The AI-networking standard-bearer with accelerating 40% growth and booked demand into 2027 — but at ~64x earnings and 40% revenue concentration in two hyperscalers who know how to build their own switches, it's priced for a flawless decade, so momentum here is pure multiple risk.

## Summary comparison

| Ticker | Shortage (0-10) | Irreplaceable (0-10) | #1 in category? | YTD run | Core bet |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SNDK | 9 | 5 | No — #5 NAND (~11-14%) | ~+553% | NAND price/allocation through 2027, capped by contract ceilings |
| MU | 10 | 8 | No — #3 DRAM (~24%), #3 HBM (~18%) | ~+250% | HBM sold out; USD 150B take-or-pay RPO; gaining DRAM share |
| ANET | 5 | 6 | Segment #1 (400G/800G cloud), not overall | ~+56% | AI-networking upgrade cycle; booked demand into 2027 |

**Net:** MU has the strongest combined profile (10/8) and the most structurally defensible shortage — customers prepaying USD 32B is evidence money can't fabricate. SNDK is the purest price-leverage play on the same shortage but commodity-capped on both ends (interchangeable product, price ceilings). ANET is the weakest "explosive" candidate here — a great company whose shortage thesis is borrowed from its customers' demand, not its own scarcity, and the in-sourcing vector (white-box/Sonic from its two biggest customers) is the single biggest disintermediation risk in the batch.
