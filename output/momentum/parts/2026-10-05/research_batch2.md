# Momentum research batch 2 — run date 2026-10-05

Candidates: LRCX (Lam Research), AMAT (Applied Materials), KLAC (KLA Corporation), KEYS (Keysight Technologies).

Sources: company earnings calls/PRs via secondary transcripts and GlobeNewswire, TrendForce/ETNews industry reporting, Bernstein research notes cited in press, reputable financial outlets. Dates noted inline. Where a figure could not be found it is marked "not found".

**Cross-cutting shortage backdrop (September 2026):** Per ETNews via TrendForce (Sep 18, 2026), lead times for essential semiconductor-equipment components have more than doubled — deposition-equipment parts that took 4 months now take up to 10 months; a laser-processing equipment maker faces up to 40-month lead times on key Japanese components. The same report cites a July ETNews finding that major-equipment lead times from Applied Materials, ASML, Lam Research, Tokyo Electron, and KLA have stretched 1.5-2x — tools that took ~6 months now take up to a year. In short, the supply chain is currently the industry's constraint, which is a shortage thesis and a shipment-execution risk at once.

## LRCX — Lam Research (wafer fab equipment: etch + deposition)

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Business | #1 player in plasma etch; major in deposition (CVD/ALD/ECD). Products: Sense.i, Akara, Kiyo (etch); ALTUS, Striker, VECTOR (deposition); SABRE 3D (ECD for advanced packaging/HBM). |
| FQ4 FY26 scale (ended Jun 28, 2026; reported Jul 29, 2026) | Revenue USD 6.72B (+15.1% QoQ, +30% YoY); EPS USD 1.82 vs USD 1.69 est.; GAAP op margin 37.4%; net margin 31.27%; deferred revenue up ~9.5% to USD 2.43B. September-quarter guidance: revenue USD 8.10B, EPS USD 2.00-2.30. |

**1. Shortage thesis.** Yes — Lam's tools sit at the bottleneck of the AI buildout. Drivers: (a) GAA/2nm transitions require ~30% more etch and deposition steps vs prior nodes; (b) 3D NAND layer stacking and advanced packaging (HBM attach via SABRE 3D) are etch/deposition-intensive; (c) backside power delivery needs through-wafer etch. Evidence: Lam's leading-edge shipments are >50% of total; equipment lead times for Lam (per ETNews July 2026) stretched from ~6 months to up to a year; Korea contributed 20% of sales on memory capex; fab utilization at leading-edge fabs >90% supporting higher ASPs. Micron's customer commitments reaching USD 32B (cash deposits, reported Oct 1, 2026) directly foreshadows memory equipment orders.

**2. Backlog / order book.** Lam does not disclose a formal backlog figure; the proxy is deferred revenue (up ~9.5% to USD 2.43B at FQ4 end, ~Jul 2026) plus USD 490M of inventory held at cost in Japan pending customer acceptance (revenue not yet recognized). Management's September-quarter guidance of USD 8.10B revenue (~20% above FQ4) signals the order book is booked well beyond current shipments.

**3. Category position.** #1 in etch: ~40-45% total etch market share, >80% of sub-5nm etch, ~70% of critical NAND etch (multiple 2026 industry analyses). Installed base: 102,000+ chambers. Applied Materials is the broad portfolio #2/bundling challenger; Tokyo Electron challenges in cryo etch.

**4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk.** Moat: plasma-physics process know-how (decades of recipes co-developed with TSMC/Samsung/Intel), installed-base lock-in (chambers can't be swapped mid-fab without re-qualification), Lam Cryo early-mover lead vs TEL's Nautilus, R&D >10% of revenue. TSMC, Samsung, Intel cannot credibly in-source etch tools — no foundry builds its own etch fleet. Substitute risk is low; only threat is a share-shift to AMAT's integrated Centura platform. **Irreplaceability score: 9/10.**

**5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months).** FQ4 FY26 record beat (Jul 29, 2026: revenue USD 6.72B +30% YoY, EPS USD 1.82 beat); dividend raised USD 0.26 → USD 0.33/quarter (~+27%); aggressive September guidance (USD 8.10B revenue, implies ~2x sequential revenue growth); Bernstein raised target to USD 385 (Aug 12, 2026); Micron's USD 32B customer-commitment disclosure (Oct 1, 2026) lifting equipment demand outlook. Stock YTD +~103%, 52-wk high USD 438.50.

**6. Risks.** (a) China = 26% of revenue — export-control tightening is the single biggest cliff; (b) extreme cyclicality — equipment is the most elastic link of the memory/logic cycle; (c) valuation: at ~60x trailing P/E (Finnhub, Oct 2026) and 112.92% above the USD 156.44 GF Value estimate per GuruFocus (Oct 1), the AI supercycle is fully priced; (d) Bernstein notes KLA's longer lead times will outgrow Lam into 2027+; (e) geographic rotation (China→Taiwan/Korea/US) is already compressing gross margins (-80 bps QoQ).

**Shortage score: 9/10.** Etch is the binding constraint on 2nm/GAA and 3D NAND ramps; lead times doubled; deferred revenue and acceptance-inventory are at records.

**Verdict:** The #1 etch monopoly on the critical path of every AI fab ramp, with record orders and the cleanest leading-edge mix — but at ~60x earnings the market has already discounted a multi-year supercycle, so the explosive edge depends on the memory capex wave converting to shipments.

**Earnings-quality flags — one-line verdict each:** HIGH_ACCRUALS (0.0627) + RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN (divergence 0.2808; CFO/NI 0.806): **benign, business-model timing** — Lam holds shipped systems at cost in inventory until customer acceptance (notably Japan, USD 490M pending) and deferred revenue grew 9.5%; with revenue up 15% QoQ, receivables and working-capital builds mechanically outrun cash collection into the steep September-quarter ramp. No evidence of channel-stuffing; the order book is real.

## AMAT — Applied Materials (wafer fab equipment: deposition/etch/CMP + services)

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Business | Largest semiconductor-equipment maker (broadest portfolio: deposition incl. ALD/CVD, etch, CMP, metrology/inspection-lite, advanced packaging tools, services). |
| FQ3 FY26 scale (reported Aug 13, 2026) | Revenue USD 9.12B (+24.8-25% YoY, beat vs USD 8.99B est.); EPS USD 3.50 vs USD 3.40 est.; net margin 30.05%; ROE 38.02%. FQ4 guidance: revenue USD 10.25B (+51% YoY), EPS USD 3.82-4.22 (mid USD 4.02, +85% YoY) — the first USD 10B+ quarter in company history. |

**1. Shortage thesis.** Yes — and AMAT is expanding capacity to chase it, which is itself the tell: the company announced a new Singapore campus and plans to roughly double quarterly system output by 2028 (ainvest, Sep 2026). Demand drivers: DRAM equipment +52% YoY in FQ3; advanced-packaging equipment expected +70% in 2026 (doubling to ~USD 3B); 2nm GAA nanosheets require AMAT's proprietary ALD; equipment lead times stretched 1.5-2x per ETNews (July 2026). CFO Brice Hill cited "eight-quarter visibility backlog" — customers have forecast demand two years out (Barchart, Aug 2026).

**2. Backlog / order book.** AMAT does not publish a dollar backlog; management's eight-quarter visibility statement plus a revenue staircase of USD 7.0B (Q1) → USD 7.9B (Q2) → USD 9.1B (Q3) → USD 10.25B guided (Q4) is the order-book evidence. Not found: a formal backlog dollar figure.

**3. Category position.** #1 overall equipment vendor by revenue. Dominant in deposition (ALD/CVD); projected to capture >50% of the GAA and backside-power-delivery components market in the current cycle (2026 industry analyses). #2 in etch behind Lam; competes with KLA in inspection/metrology through the bundle.

**4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk.** Moat: breadth (the "integration" strategy — Centura platforms bundling deposition+etch in one tool), decades of materials-science IP, EPIC Center ecosystem (Samsung as founding member, USD 5B facility; Kioxia joined Sep 2026; collaboration with Synopsys/Nvidia on AI/quantum-chemistry research). Customers (TSMC, Samsung, Intel, Micron) could not replicate this portfolio — only ASML, Lam, TEL, KLA compete in fragments, none across the full materials-engineering stack. **Irreplaceability score: 9/10.**

**5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months).** Record FQ3 FY26 (Aug 13, 2026); first-ever USD 10B+ guided quarter (+51% YoY revenue, +85% EPS); Kioxia joined the EPIC Center (Sep 2026); ASMPT NEXX advanced-packaging acquisition (agreed May 2026); new DRAM/HBM tool introductions (June 2026); Bernstein raised target to USD 675 (Aug 12, 2026, "most exposure to leading-edge logic, DRAM and packaging"); Morgan Stanley raised 2027 revenue forecast to USD 50.7B/EPS USD 20.92 while trimming target to USD 563. Stock YTD +~110-115%.

**6. Risks.** (a) Valuation is the loudest risk: stock fell after the record Aug 13 print — at YTD +~110%, "the stock is no longer a discovery... it is a bet on cycle duration" (ainvest, Sep 2026); (b) memory capex has "supply discipline" that caps equipment orders — AMAT is winning more in foundry logic, so any logic pause hurts most; (c) no disclosed backlog figure means the eight-quarter visibility claim is hard to verify; (d) China exposure (not quantified in latest print) and export controls; (e) ainvest's caution: 2026 is proven, but "it does not support the assumption that 2027 looks the same."

**Shortage score: 9/10.** Eight-quarter order visibility, equipment lead times 1.5-2x stretched, AMAT racing to double output by 2028 — the industry's capacity problem is AMAT's pricing power.

**Verdict:** The broadest, best-positioned equipment franchise in the AI cycle — first USD 10B quarter, record DRAM/packaging demand — but the market already prices multi-year 30%+ growth, and the stock sold off on its own beat, so the explosive leg requires 2027 to look like 2026, which management has not yet promised.

## KLAC — KLA Corporation (process control: inspection + metrology)

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Business | #1 in process control (wafer/reticle inspection, metrology, e-beam); services ~23% of revenue; specialty semi-process + PCB/component inspection. |
| FY2026 scale (year ended Jun 30, 2026; FQ4 reported late Jul 2026) | FY26 revenue USD 13.58B, net profit USD 4.83B; FQ4 revenue USD 3.66B (+15% YoY, record), gross margin 62.4%. September-quarter guidance: revenue ~USD 4.0B (±USD 200M), gross margin ~62.5%, non-GAAP EPS USD 1.16 (±USD 0.10). |

**1. Shortage thesis.** Yes — process control is a derived bottleneck of the AI buildout: 2nm/GAA transitions raise process-control intensity, EUV-in-DRAM creates new inspection requirements, and hybrid bonding/CoWoS for AI accelerators and HBM demand precision inspection. Evidence: KLA raised its calendar-2026 WFE expectation (incl. advanced packaging) to the low-USD 150B range from >USD 140B, vs ~USD 120B in 2025; KLA's advanced-packaging process-control revenue is guided to ~USD 1.1B in 2026 (+70%+ YoY), ~2x the pace of the broader advanced-packaging market; 2H 2026 revenue expected ~20% above 1H.

**2. Backlog / order book.** Backlog USD 12.57B at June 30, 2026, up from USD 7.86B a year earlier (~+60%), "primarily driven by demand tied to the AI infrastructure buildout" (FY2026 10-K, filed Aug 5, 2026; reiterated at September investor conferences). Management: backlog "near USD 12.5B" supporting 2H 2026 and 2027 visibility. Note per industry research (404k, Sep 2026): customer rolling forecasts "remain subject to revision and must not be conflated with non-cancellable backlog."

**3. Category position.** #1 in process control with ~55% market share (2026 chokepoint analysis); #1 in advanced wafer-level-packaging process control per management. Competitors: Applied Materials and ASML expanding inspection/metrology; Onto Innovation in niche diagnostics.

**4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk.** Moat: physics-level IP (deep-UV/e-beam optics, defect-classification algorithms), decade-plus customer co-development embedded in fab yield recipes, enormous installed base; process-control is the "eyes" of the fab — at 2nm yields, fabs literally cannot run without it. Customers cannot build this (nobody's IDM makes its own e-beam inspectors); substitute tech exists only at the margins. The real check is that AMAT/ASML are pushing into KLA's turf — but with 55% share and the highest switching costs in the fab (re-qualifying a new inspection tool on a live line is prohibitive). **Irreplaceability score: 9/10.**

**5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months).** FQ4 FY26 record revenue + 2026 WFE raise to low-USD 150Bs (late Jul 2026); September-quarter guidance implying ~9% sequential revenue growth; advanced-packaging process-control outlook raised to ~USD 1.1B; September investor conferences reiterating "2026 a year of accelerating momentum... 2027 growth could be at least as strong"; Bernstein raised target to USD 250 (Aug 12, 2026); advanced-packaging business growing >25% (~2x market pace). Dividend USD 0.23/quarter; USD 876M returned in FQ4 (USD 571M buybacks + USD 305M dividends).

**6. Risks.** (a) Customer concentration: TSMC >10% of revenue in each of FY24-26 (10-K); (b) 87% of revenue international; ~30% China — export-control risk; (c) tariff impact ~100 bps on gross margin; (d) KLA's longer lead times mean orders convert to revenue more slowly — Bernstein flags it "growing a bit slower than the others this year given longer leadtimes"; (e) the Jan 2026 sell-off showed the market punishes stretched lead times and conservative WFE definitions.

**Shortage score: 8/10.** A 60% backlog surge on AI buildout with 2027 visibility, and 2nm yield complexity is a genuine secular driver — but KLA's own long lead times are the brake, converting orders to cash slower than peers.

**Verdict:** The inspection monopoly the AI node transitions can't proceed without — record backlog, 70%+ packaging growth, and the highest gross margins of the four — but its longer delivery cycle means it monetizes the shortage last, so explosive upside needs the 2027 WFE view to hold.

**Earnings-quality flags — one-line verdict each:** RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN (divergence 0.1241; CFO/NI 0.858): **benign-to-watch** — with backlog up ~60% YoY and 2H revenue guided ~20% above 1H, receivables building ahead of cash is the mechanical signature of a ramping shipment book, not aggressive recognition; only becomes a concern if CFO/NI stays below 1 through the 2H delivery wave.

## KEYS — Keysight Technologies (electronic test & measurement)

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Business | #1 electronic test & measurement vendor (oscilloscopes, network analyzers, signal generators, protocol/validation software). Customers include Nvidia, Marvell, hyperscalers, defense. |
| FQ3 FY26 scale (reported Aug 18, 2026) | Revenue USD 1.846B (+36% YoY, 31% core); orders USD 2.091B (+56% YoY, 52% core) — second consecutive quarter above USD 2B; non-GAAP EPS USD 3.07 (+79%); op margin 33.2% (above 31-32% long-term target). FQ4 guidance: revenue USD 1.93-1.95B (+37% YoY), EPS USD 3.34-3.40. |

**1. Shortage thesis.** Partially — but the shortage is Keysight's, not its customers': management said on the FQ3 call that "demand is no longer the constraint. Component supply is" — revenue conversion is gated by component availability while orders outrun revenue by USD 245M in the quarter (ainvest, Aug 2026; Zacks, Sep 16, 2026). Demand drivers are structural: for the first time in company history, wireline orders and revenue exceeded wireless — AI datacenter interconnect (800G → 1.6T → 3.2T optical, silicon photonics, GPU-cluster fabric validation); AI-specific customer count doubled in a year; backlog at record levels (USD ~2.8B+). Orders running ahead of revenue for two straight quarters implies reported sales understate underlying demand.

**2. Backlog / order book.** Record backlog of ~USD 2.8B at FQ1 (Feb 2026); orders exceeded revenue by USD 245M in FQ3 alone, with two consecutive USD 2B+ order quarters. Pipeline at an all-time high per management (FQ3 call, Aug 18, 2026). Not found: a precise current backlog dollar figure.

**3. Category position.** Broad T&M #1 overall. But the DOJ's 2025 antitrust action is the qualifier: the combined Keysight+Spirent would have held 85% of high-speed Ethernet testing, 60% of network-security testing, and 50% of RF channel emulation — so DOJ forced divestiture of those three Spirent units (40% of Spirent's revenue) to VIAVI as the deal closed (Oct 2025). Net: Keysight kept the lion's share of Spirent (network automation, positioning) but the pure high-speed Ethernet testing crown went to VIAVI. Key competitors: VIAVI, Rohde & Schwarz, Anritsu, EXFO, Teradyne/LitePoint, Tektronix (Fortive).

**4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk.** Moat: metrology-grade precision, protocol/software depth embedded in customer validation workflows, long product cycles (a flagship scope family lasts a decade). Customers (Nvidia, Marvell, hyperscalers) cannot credibly build metrology-grade test gear themselves — validation requires vendor-neutral calibration. BUT: this is a multi-vendor market with real substitutes — Rohde & Schwarz, Anritsu, and now a strengthened VIAVI in the divested segments; competition is on price, features, and release cadence, not a single physics bottleneck like EUV or HAR etch. **Irreplaceability score: 7/10.**

**5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months).** FQ3 FY26 blowout + guidance raise (Aug 18, 2026: revenue +36%, orders +56%, op margin 33.2% record); Q4 guide raised (revenue USD 1.93-1.95B, EPS USD 3.34-3.40); first-ever USD 1B commercial-communications quarter; wireline overtaking wireless for the first time (generational mix shift); defense segment +14% (electromagnetic-spectrum warfare, satnav resilience); nearly 3,000 new customers added in FQ3; management "on track to achieve record operating cash flow in fiscal 2026"; CEO says the 6G opportunity will exceed 5G. Analyst consensus Moderate Buy, avg target USD 392.83 vs ~USD 353 price (Sep 23, 2026).

**6. Risks.** (a) The stock FELL ~7.2% (~USD 4B market value) on the blowout print — management admitted component supply, not demand, caps shipments, and at a full valuation "already assumes flawless execution" (ainvest, Aug 2026); (b) test equipment is cyclical T&M, not a fab bottleneck — customers can defer validation purchases; (c) wireless/5G heritage is still the other half of the business and 5G capex is maturing; (d) VIAVI is now a strengthened competitor in the divested high-speed segments; (e) tariffs/supreme-court tariff-power uncertainty overhang on supply chains.

**Shortage score: 7/10.** Orders outrun revenue by a wide margin and the pipeline is at an all-time high — but the gating factor is Keysight's own component supply, and test equipment is deferrable capex, not a hard fab bottleneck.

**Verdict:** A genuine AI-infrastructure beneficiary at an inflection point (wireline eclipsing wireless, two USD 2B+ order quarters) — but the demand-side story is already fully priced while revenue is supply-capped, so explosive returns need the component crunch to resolve into a shipment surge, not just more orders.

**Earnings-quality flags — one-line verdict each:** RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN (divergence 0.1765; CFO/NI 1.273): **benign** — CFO/NI well above 1 with record operating cash flow on track for FY2026, so cash conversion is strong; the receivables divergence is acquisition noise (Spirent closed Oct 2025) plus mechanical buildup from 52-56% order growth against supply-capped shipments, not aggressive revenue booking.
