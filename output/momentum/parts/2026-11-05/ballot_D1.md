## SINGLE-PICK BALLOT

- Top pick: CF
- Runner-up: MU
- Thesis (3-5 sentences): CF is the panel's tightest *physical* shortage, not a demand-surge story, and the market sold it 4.4% on the day it printed a strong Q3 — the classic contrarian setup this lens hunts for. Nitrogen has no substitute at scale, CF ran at 97% ammonia utilisation year-to-date — effectively sold out — while ~37% of European ammonia capacity is offline or reduced and only about half of average Middle East urea exports have left the Persian Gulf since the Iran conflict. At a $111.68 verified Nov 4 close and a forward P/E of 10.3, the market is pricing a normal fertiliser cycle, not a supply-constrained one. If the supply disruption persists into the next application season, pricing power flows almost straight to earnings, and the company has already raised its 2030 mid-cycle adjusted EBITDA outlook to ~$3.3B from ~$2.9B.
- Key shortage/backlog evidence: CF ran at 97% utilisation of available ammonia capacity year-to-date, with ~37% of Europe's nameplate ammonia capacity offline/reduced (Oct 2026) and channel inventories low — a sold-out producer in a market where supply, not demand, is the constraint. No quantified backlog was found in the dossier (fertiliser sells spot/seasonal forward, not booked backlog).
- Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk: low — nitrogen fertiliser has no substitute at scale (ammonia feeds ~half the world's food production, per the dossier), customers cannot in-source it (a world-scale plant costs billions; the ~$3.7B Blue Point JV does not produce until 2029), and CF is the world's largest ammonia producer with 39% of US domestic capacity. The offset is not disintermediation but commoditisation: farmers buy the cheapest tonne, so resumed Middle East/Russian supply or higher Chinese urea exports (+2–4M mt projected) can undercut price fast.
- 12-18 month return scenario: rough base / bull case — Base, roughly +20–35% (to ~$134–151): supply stays tight through the next season, ammonia pricing holds, and the stock re-rates modestly off a 10.3x forward P/E as Q3's earnings strength ($436M net earnings / $2.87 diluted, revenue $1.86B, +12.1% YoY; adjusted EBITDA $833M vs $667M) repeats rather than reverses. Bull, roughly +50–70% (to ~$168–190): the European/Middle East outage persists, channel inventories stay low, and nitrogen prices spike into a second season, with earnings leverage on a 97%-utilised plant. These are my rough scenario estimates, not dossier forecasts.
- Top risk: The classic commodity reversal — supply restarts (Middle East/Russian shipments resume, Chinese urea exports rise) while CF's own Yazoo City complex stays offline until at least H1 2027 (2026 ammonia production ~9.5M tons vs 10.1M in 2025), collapsing the price premium that is the whole thesis; natural gas at $2.88/MMBtu in Q3 is the swing feedstock cost, and the March 2026 class action alleging nitrogen price-fixing since 2021 (allegation, not a finding) is an overhang.
- Conviction (1-10): 7

### Top 3 — most compelling shortage/backlog/moat point and 12–18mo scenario

**1. CF ($111.68 verified Nov 4 close):** The single most compelling point is 97% ammonia utilisation year-to-date against ~37% of European capacity offline and only ~half of average Middle East urea exports leaving the Gulf — a producer that cannot make more of a product the world cannot do without, selling into low channel inventories. Base roughly +20–35% (to ~$134–151) if tightness holds one more season; bull roughly +50–70% (to ~$168–190) if the outage persists and nitrogen prices spike again. Downside is a supply-restart price collapse, not a demand failure.

**2. **3.
