## MU — Micron Technology

### 1. RPO ~$150B at fiscal Q4 2026 (reported Sep 30, 2026), up from ~$100B the prior quarter
**Verdict: CONFIRMED**

- Source: Micron fiscal Q4 2026 earnings release / prepared remarks, Sep 30, 2026, as reported by Zacks (Oct 1, 2026) and Carbon Finance (Oct 9, 2026): CFO Mark Murphy said "remaining performance obligations are about $150 billion for agreements with determined pricing frameworks."
- Prior quarter: Axis Intelligence (Oct 20, 2026), citing Micron's filings: "RPO grew 50% from about $100 billion in June"; MEXC (Oct 1, 2026): one quarter earlier 14 agreements carried "roughly $100 billion in cumulative revenue at minimum contract pricing."
- Precision note: the $150B is a minimum-pricing figure covering only strategic customer agreements (SCAs) with a determined pricing framework; management says actual revenue from those agreements is expected to exceed it.

### 2. Customer financial commitments of $32B under 26 strategic customer agreements, mostly cash deposits; >75% of fiscal 2027 output already committed; HBM sold out / supply constrained through 2028 per CEO Sanjay Mehrotra on the fiscal Q4 2026 call
**Verdict: CONFIRMED, with a precision note on the HBM wording**

- Source: Micron fiscal Q4 2026 earnings call, Sep 30, 2026, via Zacks (Oct 1, 2026): "Financial commitments tied to the agreements have increased to $32 billion, mostly in cash deposits"; "During Q&A, Mehrotra clarified that more than 75% of Micron's 2027 output is already committed across SCA and non-SCA customers, while discussions increasingly center on 2028 demand." 26 SCAs covering "more than 35% of estimated revenue through 2030."
- Cash deposits: ConvexityOS (Oct 2026), from Micron's prepared remarks: customers committed "$32 billion, 'the vast majority of which are cash deposits'"; "$12.3 billion of those deposits in the fourth quarter alone."
- Precision note: the "through 2028" language applies to overall DRAM/NAND supply constraint, not literally to HBM being sold out through 2028. Zacks: "Micron expects DRAM and NAND markets to remain supply constrained through calendar 2028" and Mehrotra said supply-demand should be "much tighter in calendar 2027 and 2028 than in 2026" with "no line of sight to when supply and demand will return to balance." On HBM specifically, The Cheap Investor (Oct 27, 2026) reports Mehrotra said "HBM capacity is sold out through calendar 2026" with demand outpacing supply in 2027–2028, and "the vast majority of Micron's calendar 2027 HBM bit supply is covered by agreements" (Zacks). The claim's substance — contracted, cash-backed, supply-constrained through 2028 — survives; the literal "HBM sold out through 2028" phrasing overstates what was said.

### 3. Kioxia's Oct 30, 2026 warning: Dec-quarter operating profit guided to ~¥2T vs ~¥2.35T consensus, and NAND ASP growth decelerating from +70% QoQ to mid-20s — the claimed first fundamental crack in memory pricing
**Verdict: CONFIRMED**

- Source: Kioxia Q2 FY2026 results filing, Oct 30, 2026, via VLSI (Oct 31, 2026) and FinanceFeeds (Nov 2, 2026): for October–December, Kioxia guides "operating profit of about JPY 1,995 billion, flat against the record quarter," which is "15.1 percent below the JPY 2,350 billion consensus operating profit"; revenue guided ¥2,500.0B, "up just 0.8 percent."
- ASP: Bloomberg via FinanceFeeds (Nov 2, 2026): "Kioxia said its blended NAND average selling price increased in the mid-20% range sequentially in the September quarter, compared with roughly 70% in the previous period." VLSI puts it as "in the 20 percent range quarter on quarter in dollar terms, after a rise of about 70 percent in the previous quarter" — the "mid-20s" figure matches Bloomberg's reporting; the "20% range" variant is the same deceleration rounded differently.
- Caveats (from the same sources, and relevant to the "crack" interpretation): ~¥55B of the guided shortfall is a yen assumption (¥157/$ vs ¥161/$ in the reported quarter), not demand; some December-quarter shipments were pulled forward into September; management expects stronger shipment growth in the March quarter and says 2027 capacity is already allocated. Prices are still rising — the rate of increase has slowed, not reversed. Kioxia shares fell 15.8% in Tokyo on Nov 2. The dossier's characterisation of this as a deceleration / first crack in the *rate of price gains* is accurate; it is not evidence of falling NAND prices.

### 4. Micron's fiscal Q4 2026 print (Sep 30, 2026): revenue $54.23B and non-GAAP gross margin 87.0% as reported in secondary sources — flag any inconsistency between sources on the revenue/margin figures
**Verdict: CONFIRMED — no inconsistency found between sources on revenue or margin**

- Source: Micron's own earnings release, Sep 30, 2026 (investors.micron.com): "Revenue of $54.23 billion versus $41.46 billion for the prior quarter and $11.32 billion for the same period last year"; the release's financial table shows revenue $54,229M and gross margin $47,204M (non-GAAP) — i.e. 87.0% non-GAAP, 86.8% GAAP ($47,047M).
- Corroboration is unanimous across the secondary sources checked: HeyTheo (Oct 1, 2026): "Gross margin was 86.8% on a GAAP basis and 87.0% adjusted"; TradingKey: "GAAP gross margin expanded to 86.8%"; Leverage Shares: "On a non-GAAP basis, gross margin reached 87%"; ConvexityOS: "Gross margin was 86.8% on Micron's reported basis and 87.0% on its adjusted one." Non-GAAP EPS $33.42 and Q1 FY27 guidance revenue ~$61.5B / non-GAAP gross margin ~86.25% also agree across sources.
- Inconsistency flagged: the dossier itself notes "one source warns FQ1 FY27 gross margin may struggle to stay in the 48–50% range." That figure is irreconcilable with Micron's own guided non-GAAP gross margin of ~86.25% for FQ1 FY27 and with the 87.0% Q4 print; it appears to be a garbled figure (different margin definition, different company, or an error) and should be disregarded, not treated as a margin-direction signal. No other source I checked reports a margin figure inconsistent with $54.23B / 87.0% non-GAAP / 86.8% GAAP.

### 8. Microsoft + Meta together were 42% of Arista's 2025 revenue (26% + 16%, per the 10-K filed Feb 17, 2026)
**Verdict: CONFIRMED in substance — with an attribution caveat on the names**

- The 42% total and the 26%/16% split are confirmed in the 10-K itself. Quartr copy of Arista's Form 10-K for the fiscal year ended Dec 31, 2025: "sales to one end customer represented 16%, 15%, and 21% of our total revenue, and sales to the other end customer represented 26%, 20%, and 18% of our total revenue for the years ended December 31, 2025, 2024, and 2023, respectively." 26% + 16% = 42% for 2025. MTN Consulting (Substack) likewise: "Arista's 10-K filed 17 February 2026 shows two customers took 26% and 16% of 2025 revenue of $9.0B. Together that is 42%."
- Caveat: the 10-K does **not name** the two customers — it calls them "one end customer" and "the other end customer." The identification as Microsoft (26%) and Meta (16%) comes from CEO Jayshree Ullal's earnings-call remarks that Microsoft and Meta have been "10% and greater customers for over a decade" and from secondary reporting (e.g. Trefis, Jun 30, 2026: "top 2 customers, identified by the CEO as long-term partners Microsoft and Meta"). The dossier's "per the 10-K" attribution is therefore imprecise for the *names*, though the percentages and the 42% total are exactly as the 10-K states.

## Overall verdict

The MU pick's headline evidence survived verification essentially intact. The two load-bearing pillars — the ~$150B of remaining performance obligations (minimum-pricing, only for agreements with a defined pricing framework) and the $32B of mostly-cash customer commitments under 26 strategic agreements, with >75% of fiscal 2027 output already committed — are confirmed verbatim from Micron's own September 30, 2026 disclosures, and the Q4 print itself ($54.23B revenue, 87.0% non-GAAP / 86.8% GAAP gross margin) is unanimously corroborated with no inter-source inconsistency. The one genuine crack the dossier flags, Kioxia's October 30 warning (December-quarter operating profit ~¥2T vs ~¥2.35T consensus; NAND ASP growth slowing from ~+70% to the mid-20s QoQ), is also confirmed — but with its own mitigations on the record: roughly ¥55B of the shortfall is a yen assumption, some shipments were pulled forward into September, and prices are still rising, just more slowly. The supporting cast checked out too: all four ANET claims and all four CF claims are confirmed, subject only to small precision notes (the 10-K never names Microsoft and Meta; CF's 37% European figure is CF's own "reportedly" estimate; the CF $3.3B outlook raise was first communicated in August and reiterated in the November 4 8-K; and "HBM sold out through 2028" overstates the call — the through-2028 language covers overall DRAM/NAND supply constraint, with HBM specifically sold out through 2026 and largely contracted for 2027). Nothing among the 12 claims was contradicted or left unverified. The verification therefore supports the dossier's own net assessment: MU's contracted, cash-backed shortage thesis is real, while the Kioxia deceleration is a legitimate watch-item on the rate of price gains rather than a refutation of the thesis.
