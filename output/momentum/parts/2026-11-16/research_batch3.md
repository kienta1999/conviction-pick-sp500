## WST — West Pharmaceutical Services (Health Care Supplies; mcap ~$29.0B; rev growth +11.3%; op margin 22.9%; ret 12m +52.5%; fwd PE 40.1; analyst upside +13.1%; next earnings 2027-02-18)

HVP Components (stoppers, seals, plungers for injectable drugs) grew ~20% organically; GLP-1-related HVP grew well into the 30s% organically and is ~12% of revenue. Biologics +23.2% organic. 5. **Recent catalysts:** Q3 beat + guidance raise (Oct 29); Asia-Pacific +41.9% organic (India/China GLP-1 introductions); generic GLP-1 launches in multiple countries.
6. **Risks:** Valuation rich (fwd PE 40.1, TTM P/E ~45); West Vantage contract manufacturing −6.6% organic (CGM program wind-down, trough in Q3); SmartDose 3.5mL divestiture weighs on 2027 reported growth; GLP-1 growth-rate comps get harder.
7. **Shortage score: 8/10. Verdict:** The cleanest "sell shovels into the shortage" name after MU/APH; qualification lock-in is a real moat.

## GE — GE Aerospace (Aerospace & Defense; mcap ~$342.9B; rev growth +16.9%; op margin 19.7%; ret 12m +8.0%; fwd PE 35.6; analyst upside +14.0%; next earnings 2027-01-21)

**Earnings-quality flag (screen): RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN — explain:** Orders +49% and revenue +18% in Q3 with FCF conversion 105% YTD — cash is converting ahead of earnings, the opposite of the flag's concern. Receivables scale with the $19B quarterly order intake. Verdict: **benign — order-driven, cash conversion strong.**

1. **Shortage thesis:** Commercial aviation supply chain is capacity-constrained: airframer order books full through end of decade; engine deliveries lag demand. GE's constraint is on the supply side (deliveries), demand side is a multi-year backlog. LEAP deliveries +26% YTD; LEAP installed base expected to more than double 2025–2030.
2. **Backlog / order book:** Commercial services backlog >$170B. Q3 2026 (2026-10-20): orders +49% to ~$19B (CES +58%); revenue +18% (total $14.1B vs $12.2B); adj EPS $2.25 (+36%, vs ~$2.02 consensus); operating profit $3.0B (+30%), margin 22.5% (+220bps); FCF >$2B. FY2026 guidance raised 4th consecutive quarter: adj EPS $8.00–8.10 (from $7.65–7.85), revenue growth 19–20%, FCF $9.1–9.4B. 2027: commercial services growth low-double-digits; LEAP shop visits +25–26%.
3. **Category position:** #1 commercial aircraft engine maker (via CFM JV with Safran); installed base >80,000 engines; services = annuity.
4. **Irreplaceability:** Very high. Engines are certified, safety-critical, sole-sourced per airframe; switching costs are prohibitive; aftermarket is locked by design. Threat: Boeing/Airbus delivery delays cap new-engine revenue (but boost services — airlines fly older engines longer). **Irreplaceability 9/10.**
5. **Recent catalysts:** Farnborough: commitments for >1,800 engines incl. largest-ever LEAP deal (>1,000 LEAP-1A, IndiGo); Turkish Airlines >300 LEAP-1B + services for 600+ engine fleet (Oct 2026); China agreements >$12.9B incl. China Eastern 202 LEAP-1A (Nov 6, 2026); GE9X certification targeted year-end 2026.
6. **Risks:** Boeing delays; GE9X ramp losses flatten 2027 margins (expansion resumes 2028); valuation 35.6x forward; defense exposure to budget cycles; supply-chain (castings) capacity — acquiring Consolidated Precision Products.
7. **Shortage score: 8/10. Verdict:** The largest hard backlog in the field ($170B+) with a certification moat; growth is steadier than explosive, but the visibility is unmatched.

1. **Shortage thesis:** Weak on shortage — this is a procedure-growth compounder, not a supply-constrained story. Demand is strong (procedures +15%), but there is no scarcity of da Vinci systems; the constraint is hospital adoption pace.
2. **Backlog / order book:** Installed-base annuity: 12,094 da Vinci systems (+12% YoY), 1,152 Ion systems; 84% recurring revenue. Q3 2026 (2026-10-20): revenue $3.05B (+22%, vs ~$2.90B consensus); non-GAAP EPS $3.00 (+25%, vs ~$2.65); procedures +15% (da Vinci +14%, Ion +31%); 546 systems placed (+28%), incl. 315 da Vinci 5; instruments/accessories $1.80B (+18%). FY2026 da Vinci procedure growth guided 14–15% (near midpoint). GAAP EPS fell to $1.49 on a $450M pre-tax charge for proposed antitrust settlement (third-party instrument remanufacturing/servicing class action; court decision not expected until 2027). Buybacks: $2.94B in Q3 at ~$377 avg.
3. **Category position:** Dominant #1 in surgical robotics; >10,000 procedures/day; da Vinci 5 ramp.
4. **Irreplaceability:** High. Installed base + surgeon training + instruments ecosystem; hospitals don't rip out robots. Threat: competition (Medtronic Hugo, J&J Ottava) and the antitrust settlement's injunctive relief opening servicing/instruments to third parties — that directly touches the moat. **Irreplaceability 7/10.**
5. **Recent catalysts:** Q3 beat on both lines; da Vinci 5 momentum (315 placements); 18 installs at US ambulatory surgery centers.
6. **Risks:** US da Vinci procedure growth 10% — lowest since COVID (tough 16% comp); pricing headwind: extended-life EndoWrist + volume pricing from H1 2027 and China price cut from Nov 2026 expected to cut instrument/accessory revenue per procedure 3–5%; antitrust settlement overhang; valuation ~56x P/E, ~14.5x sales; stock −22.5% over 12m.
7. **Shortage score: 4/10. Verdict:** Great compounder with a real installed-base moat, but no shortage thesis and the antitrust settlement nibbles at the moat; momentum is negative.

7. **Shortage score: 4/10. Verdict:** A monopoly with a gene-editing kicker, but no shortage and modest growth; the Nov 30 catalyst is the near-term swing factor.
