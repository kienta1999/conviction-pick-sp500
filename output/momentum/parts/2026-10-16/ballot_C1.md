# Panelist C — Quality / Moat & Irreplaceability

## Ballot 1 — Single pick

- **Top pick:** LLY
- **Runner-up:** VRTX
- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** Lilly is the one name where category dominance, margin quality and irreplaceability all point the same way: #1 in the US incretin market at 60.9% share vs Novo Nordisk's 38.8%, and widening, on an 83.4% gross margin and >100% screen ROE. No payer, patient or hyperscaler-style customer can in-source a patented dual GIP/GLP-1 molecule — the only bypass is a rival molecule, and Lilly owns the most likely displacers itself (Foundayo, the first oral small-molecule GLP-1, plus retatrutide behind it). The explosive leg is conversion, not hope: Q2 2026 revenue rose 48% YoY as new capacity finally shipped into unmet demand, and guidance was raised twice to $85–87B. Even the disintermediation scenario is self-cannibalisation on Lilly's terms.
- **Key shortage/backlog evidence:** Q2 2026 worldwide volume rose ~60% while realised prices fell ~13% — growth is coming from finally shipping more product into demand that supply had capped, with only ~2% of the US population on pharmacological obesity treatment in 2025 against a $114B 2030 global anti-obesity market forecast.
- **Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk:** low — patented molecule no customer can build or in-source; compounded copies are shrinking as branded supply and formulary wins (all three large US PBMs) close them off, and the successor stack is Lilly's own. Dossier irreplaceability 8/10, docked only because Novo is a credible, well-resourced #2.
- **12-18 month return scenario:** base ~+20% (toward JPMorgan's $1,500 target vs $1,251.61 screen price) on continued volume-led growth; bull ~+40% if Foundayo converts oral starts at scale and retatrutide's Q1 2027 filing lands cleanly.
- **Top risk:** Concentration — Mounjaro plus Zepbound were ~65% of Q2 revenue on one molecule, so any tirzepatide safety, supply or pricing shock is a company shock, and realised-price compression (~9–13% in Q2) must keep being outrun by volume.
- **Conviction (1-10):** 8

**Top 3 — moat/shortage detail and scenarios:**

**1. LLY** — The most compelling data point is the Q2 2026 mix: volume up ~60% with prices down ~13%, Mounjaro at $9.94B (+91% YoY) and Zepbound at $4.93B, proving the prior constraint was Lilly's own capacity, not demand — and the treated pool is still only ~2% of the US population. Because the molecule is patented and the next two products in the class are also Lilly's, no substitute technology routes around this moat in the investment window. 12–18mo scenario: base ~+20% (JPMorgan $1,500 target), bull ~+40% on Foundayo oral conversion plus retatrutide filing momentum.

**2. VRTX** — Vertex is the purest irreplaceability in the field: its CFTR modulators are the only root-cause therapy for ~90% of cystic fibrosis patients (~90,000 worldwide), with no meaningful #2 found, key patents running to the late 2030s, adherence above 90%, and $13.6B of cash and investments behind it. Casgevy adds a second sole-source franchise whose bottleneck is authorised-treatment-centre throughput (>500 patient initiations vs only $76M of Q2 revenue), not demand. 12–18mo scenario: base ~+15–20%, bull ~+31% (BofA's $667 target vs $508.75 screen price) if the povetacicept PDUFA on 30 Nov 2026 delivers the first non-CF franchise.

**3. PH** — Parker's moat is certification: flight-critical content is qualified into an aircraft's type certificate, so replacing Parker means re-certification, and the installed base then generates proprietary spares demand for decades — scored 9/10 for irreplaceability in the dossier. The shortage evidence is the queue behind it: a record $12.8B total backlog ($8.5B aerospace, both records) sitting behind a combined Airbus/Boeing backlog above 17,000 aircraft, over 10 years of production. 12–18mo scenario: base ~+10–20% (screen analyst upside +20.1%), bull ~+28% (Citi's $1,234 target vs $961.75) if Filtration Group/CIRCOR deleveraging tracks from ~2.7x toward ~2.0x.

**Deliberately left out of the top 10:** SNDK, EW, CSCO and ANET — SNDK is a #5, single-product commodity NAND producer (11.4% share) that rivals and YMTC can substitute unit-for-unit; EW faces a credible #2 (Medtronic Evolut) already dual-sourced in the same cath lab; CSCO (5/10) is already partly replaced in AI data-centre switching; and ANET (4/10) fails this lens outright — merchant-silicon sameness, Nvidia Spectrum-X bundling and hyperscaler white-box/SONiC mean its own customers and its GPU supplier can design it out.
