# Panelist A — Supply-chain analyst — Momentum ballot 2026-10-22

Lens: weight shortage/backlog evidence and multi-year revenue visibility above all.

## 1. Single-pick ballot

- Top pick: ANET
- Runner-up: AME
- Thesis (3-5 sentences): ANET is the only name in the 14 where demand is explicitly supply-constrained for 1-2 more years, with customers booking a year-plus out rather than Arista rationing discretionary demand. That converts directly into revenue visibility: deferred revenue is already shipped-but-unaccepted product and FY2026 guidance was raised to ~$12.6B (+40% YoY), with AI Fabrics at least $3.5B. AME is the clear runner-up on the same lens — a hard, record $4.11B backlog — but its backlog is ~2x one quarter's sales and largely ships within 12 months, whereas ANET's constraint, deferred balance and purchase commitments point to a longer AI-fabric build-out. On shortage + backlog + visibility combined, ANET screens first by a wide margin.
- Key shortage/backlog evidence: Dell'Oro (2Q 2026): AI back-end networks to remain "supply-constrained, rather than demand-constrained, for at least the next one-to-two years"; CEO Jayshree Ullal Q1 2026: demand "best I have ever seen," capacity "constrained for the next couple of years," wafer fab shortages; COO Todd Nightingale: "52-week lead times pretty reliably with reservation needs beyond that"; deferred revenue ~$6.9B at end-Q2 2026, up from $6.2B in Q1, majority product-related; Arista's own purchase commitments $9.7B at end-Q2, up from $8.9B in Q1 and $3.6B a year earlier (+169%), with Citi noting purchase commitments tripled in three quarters and deferred product taking 18-24 months / 6-8 quarters to realise through acceptance.
- Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk: medium — EOS single-image software and hyperscaler design-in make rip-out prohibitively expensive, and Arista is the neutral / Ultra Ethernet Consortium alternative hyperscalers want versus NVIDIA's proprietary stack, but bypass is already live: Celestica white-box/ODM is #1 in Ethernet AI back-end (Dell'Oro 1Q/2Q 2026), NVIDIA Spectrum-X datacenter Ethernet grew +181% YoY, Arista designs no ASICs and depends wholly on Broadcom Tomahawk/Jericho silicon, and Microsoft/Meta concentration gives the largest customers in-sourcing leverage.
- 12-18 month return scenario: rough base / bull: Base +25% to +35% (from screen $172.145, toward the +41.5% analyst-upside zone if FY2026 +40% guidance is delivered and Q3/Q4 acceptance converts deferred revenue on schedule) / Bull +50% to +65% if supply stays constrained through 2027, 1.6T/Tomahawk 6 validates broaden the fabric share, and hyperscaler capex carries networking spend 12-18 months past the GPU peak as Deutsche Bank argued 19 Oct 2026.
- Top risk: Customer concentration plus silicon dependency — a pause or in-sourcing shift by Microsoft/Meta, or NVIDIA/Celestica share capture, leaves the $9.7B purchase-commitment inventory overhang exposed, with gross margin already compressing (63.4% in Q2 vs 65.6% a year earlier) on memory/silicon costs, at a 33.0x forward P/E priced for perfection.

### Top 3 — detail

**1. ANET.** Most compelling point is the combination, not any single figure: $6.9B deferred revenue (mostly product, +$0.6B sequentially) alongside $9.7B of Arista's own purchase commitments and 52-week lead times — both sides of the chain are booked a year-plus out, and Dell'Oro independently calls the market supply-constrained for at least 1-2 years. That is multi-year visibility no other name matches. 12-18mo: base +25% to +35% / bull +50% to +65% (screen $172.145).

**2. AME.** The only other hard, quantified industrial backlog in the set: record orders $2.3B in Q2 (+28% YoY, +25% organic, second straight quarter >20% organic order growth), record backlog $4.11B (+~21% since end-2025, ~80% expected to ship within 12 months, ~2.0x Q2 sales of $2.04B, book-to-bill ~1.13x), feeding what CEO Dave Zapico called "the beginning stages of a multiyear infrastructure build-out" in AI semiconductor metrology, power grid and defence, with design-in/validation lock-in (irreplaceability 8/10). 12-18mo: base +15% to +20% (screen $237.405, +18.8% analyst upside, FY2026 sales ~+10% and EPS guide raised to $8.20-$8.30) / bull +35% to +40% if orders stay >20% and the $5.0B Indicor deal (~$1.1B annual sales) integrates without leverage drag.

**3. CF.** The shortage is quantified in physical tons: management estimates the Iran conflict removed ~4.0-4.5Mt of traded urea and ~1Mt of ammonia from a Middle East bloc that normally supplies 25-30% of traded ammonia and 35-40% of traded urea, lifting Q2 granular urea to $593/ton vs $460 a year earlier, while CF holds 39% of US ammonia capacity (vs Nutrien 16%) at Henry Hub ~$3.31-3.50/MMBtu vs $12-15 TTF for marginal rivals. Visibility is weaker than ANET/AME — no disclosed backlog, UAN fill program at ~$300 extending only into Q4, and prices had already retraced to pre-conflict levels by end-Q2 — which is why it ranks third, not second. 12-18mo: base +10% to +20% (screen $117.72, 10.4x forward P/E, mid-cycle EBITDA baseline raised to $2.9B) / bull +40% to +50% if Hormuz/Middle East supply stays impaired into 2027 and nitrogen re-spikes.

Deliberately left out of top 10: CPAY, AAPL, ABNB, BMY — CPAY and ABNB have no shortage and no disclosed backlog (CPAY bookings +30% is share-gain in a $560-600B TAM it holds <1% of; ABNB lodging supply is elastic-to-oversupplied and bookings are ~41-day lead, not an order book), AAPL is the shortage's victim not its beneficiary (Loop Capital 20 Oct: ~13M iPhone units cut on memory/processor shortage, not recoverable in 2027, at 36.0x with -4.7% analyst upside), and BMY has no structural shortage or backlog with the Eliquis generic cliff on 1 Apr 2028 capping visibility.
