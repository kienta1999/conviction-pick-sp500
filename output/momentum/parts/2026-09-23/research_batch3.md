# Momentum research — batch 3 of 4 (2026-09-23)

## GOOGL (Alphabet)

1. **Shortage thesis.** AI demand exceeds supply — literally capacity-constrained. On the Q2 2026 call (7/22/26) management's message was that "demand is not the problem. Capacity is." Google Cloud revenue accelerated to +82% yoy ($24.8B, vs ~$22.3B consensus and vs +63% in Q1), and Alphabet raised 2026 capex guidance to $195–205B to chase the demand. Gemini models are processing 22B API tokens/minute; nearly 90% of the Fortune 100 use Gemini Enterprise; cloud marketplace transactions up 7x. AI Overviews/AI Mode now top 1B monthly users and are driving incremental query growth — AI is expanding Search, not cannibalizing it (Search +17% to $63.3B).
2. **Backlog.** Google Cloud backlog $514B, up $50B+ sequentially (Q2 2026 call); CFO said just over 50% should convert to revenue over the next 24 months.
3. **Category position.** #1 in Search (~90% share), YouTube #1 in video, Google Cloud #3 in cloud infra (~12–15% share per Synergy, June 2026) but posting the fastest growth of the big three.
4. **Irreplaceability.** Full-stack AI moat: proprietary TPUs (TPU v5p ~2–3x price-performance vs GPU alternatives), Gemini models, private fiber network, and distribution through Search/Android/Workspace. Customers can multi-cloud, but the integrated stack is not replicable by any single competitor. Disintermediation risk: open-source models and AI changing search monetization — but the Q2 print shows AI expanding queries, not shrinking them. **Irreplaceability: 8/10.**
5. **Recent catalysts.** Q2 2026 (7/22/26): revenue $119.8B (+24%, beat $116.9B consensus), Cloud op income tripled to $8.8B (35.6% margin, from ~10% a year ago). Caveat: headline EPS $9.11 was flattered by ~$99B unrealized gains on SpaceX/Anthropic stakes; operational EPS ~$2.87.
6. **Risks.** Capex intensity ($195–205B) is compressing free cash flow; the stock fell after-hours on the print despite the beat — expectations are sky-high; regulatory overhang (DOJ remedies); cloud margin pressure if AI demand outruns capacity too far.
7. **Shortage score: 9/10. Verdict:** the cleanest AI-demand-exceeds-supply print in large caps, but it's the most consensus long in the market.

## MSFT (Microsoft)

1. **Shortage thesis.** Azure demand is outrunning even Microsoft's aggressive infrastructure buildout: Azure +43% yoy in Q4 FY26 (ended 6/30/26), accelerating from 40% in Q3, with management guiding ~45% for the current quarter. Commercial bookings ex-OpenAI grew 18%; the buildout is the constraint, not demand.
2. **Backlog.** Commercial remaining performance obligation (RPO) +84% yoy to $678B; ~30% recognized in the next 12 months (+37% yoy); ex-OpenAI RPO still +25%. Azure crossed $100B in annual revenue for the first time; Microsoft Cloud FY26 revenue >$214B (+27%).
3. **Category position.** #2 in cloud infra (~20–24% share) but the fastest-growing in absolute revenue terms; #1 in enterprise software (M365, Windows) with 30M+ paid Copilot seats.
4. **Irreplaceability.** Enterprise entrenchment is the moat: M365/Windows/Azure are the corporate standard, $678B of contracted backlog locks customers in, and the OpenAI partnership gives exclusive enterprise-grade GPT access. A customer cannot realistically in-source a hyperscale cloud; the threat is workload migration to a rival cloud, not in-sourcing. **Irreplaceability: 9/10.**
5. **Recent catalysts.** Q4 FY26 (7/29/26): revenue $90B (+18%, +17% cc), EPS $4.74 (+23%, ~11.5% beat), operating margin 45.1% (above high-end guide). FY27 Q1 guide slightly above consensus ($90.4B revenue, 48.5% op margin). Caveat: ~$3.2B of the quarter's income came from the Anthropic investment.
6. **Risks.** Capex guided at $50B/quarter-equivalent pace is compressing FCF (quarterly FCF $19.6B vs $25.6B prior year); trades ~26x forward (below 5-yr average, but not cheap); the OpenAI relationship concentration.
7. **Shortage score: 9/10. Verdict:** the highest-quality backlog in the panel — $678B of contracted revenue is a floor, not a forecast — but the market has had a year to price it.

## EXPE (Expedia)

1. **Shortage thesis.** No genuine supply shortage — this is a demand/market-share momentum story, not a shortage one. The durable driver is the B2B platform: 20 consecutive quarters of double-digit B2B growth (+21% bookings in Q2), plus the fastest U.S. consumer growth in 15 quarters (+8% B2C bookings).
2. **Backlog.** Closest analogue is forward bookings: Q2 gross bookings $33.9B (+12%), room nights 111.5M (+6%); FY26 guidance raised to bookings $129.5–130.8B (+8–9%) and revenue $16.05–16.22B (+9–10%). TTM free cash flow $4.5B.
3. **Category position.** #2 global OTA behind Booking Holdings; #1 in B2B travel distribution (the segment growing 21%).
4. **Irreplaceability.** Two-sided marketplace with supply-side stickiness (hotels need the distribution), but the disintermediation risk is real and rising: Google's AI travel features and agentic AI could route around OTAs over time. Booking is a fierce direct competitor. **Irreplaceability: 5/10.**
5. **Recent catalysts.** Q2 2026 (8/5/26): beat the high end of guidance — revenue +14% ($4.3B), adj EBITDA +23% ($1.1B, 196bps margin expansion), adj EPS +36%; raised full-year guidance; $900M buybacks YTD; $0.48 quarterly dividend.
6. **Risks.** Consumer travel slowdown would hit B2C first; AI-agent disintermediation of travel search; valuation has re-rated on the beat — the easy multiple expansion may be done.
7. **Shortage score: 5/10. Verdict:** the best-executing OTA in the screen, but it's an execution compounder, not a shortage play — the momentum is real, the explosiveness is not.

## NEM (Newmont)

1. **Shortage thesis.** The shortage is price, not physical: gold is in a historic bid (central-bank buying, elevated prices) and Newmont is the purest large-cap lever to it. Realized leverage is extreme — AISC $1,621/oz against multi-thousand-dollar gold prints record cash flow.
2. **Backlog.** Production guidance is the analogue: FY26 ~5.3M attributable gold ounces reaffirmed (Q2: ~1.3M oz gold, 7M oz silver, 17kt copper). Record Q2 free cash flow of $2.2B; $9.0B cash, $3.4B net cash position.
3. **Category position.** World's largest gold producer by a wide margin; scale + reserve base no junior can replicate.
4. **Irreplaceability.** For a commodity producer this means cost position, not product: AISC $1,621/oz with year-to-date costs tracking well below full-year guidance, and 100M+ shares (~9%) retired since Feb 2024. Gold itself is fungible — there is no product moat, only scale and cost discipline. **Irreplaceability: 6/10.**
5. **Recent catalysts.** Q2 2026 (7/23/26): adj EPS $2.10 (beat $2.05), record Q2 FCF $2.2B, $1.9B returned to shareholders since last call; Red Chris Block Cave got key BC regulatory approvals.
6. **Risks.** Pure gold-price beta — a gold reversal unwinds the whole thesis; Cadia seismic events cut copper output 43% (operational risk is real); revenue missed consensus ($6.12B vs $6.35B).
7. **Shortage score: 7/10. Verdict:** the defensive momentum diversifier — if tech momentum cracks, this is the name that keeps working; but it's a commodity lever, not a compounder.

## FCX (Freeport-McMoRan)

1. **Shortage thesis.** Copper's structural deficit (electrification, AI datacenters, grid buildout) meets constrained supply — and Freeport is living the constraint: realized copper $6.17/lb in Q2 (+36% yoy), Comex futures ~$6.46/lb recently, with record Comex inventories that could flip to surplus only if the refined-copper tariff decision goes against bulls.
2. **Backlog.** Production guidance is the analogue: FY26 copper sales 3.1B lbs (H2 guided 26.8% above H1, Q4 near 983M lbs); 2027 copper sales guided >20% higher. Grasberg output doubled from 34k to 69k tons/day April→June. Q2 unit net cash costs $1.97/lb — 27¢ below April estimate.
3. **Category position.** World's largest publicly traded copper producer; Grasberg is the world's second-largest copper mine and largest gold mine.
4. **Irreplaceability.** Nobody builds another Grasberg: the asset is irreplaceable, and only a handful of firms on earth operate at this scale. But: the Indonesian government is the majority owner, the operating permit extension beyond 2041 is still pending (decision hoped by end-2026), and copper itself is fungible. **Irreplaceability: 7/10.**
5. **Recent catalysts.** Q2 2026 (7/23/26): adj EPS $0.74 (+25% beat), revenue $7.03B (+4.8% beat), gold realizations $4,520/oz (+37% yoy); applied for Grasberg permit extension.
6. **Risks.** Grasberg is the bottleneck: only ~65% of capacity expected restored by H2 (down from an 85% target); 2026 Grasberg copper cut to ~800M lbs from 1.1B lbs; the stock fell on the Q2 print despite the beat because attention is all on Grasberg execution. Refined-copper tariff decision unfinalized — a "no tariff" outcome unwinds the Comex premium fast. Trades 34x trailing earnings.
7. **Shortage score: 8/10. Verdict:** the purest copper torque in the screen with a real asset, but the market has decided Grasberg execution — not copper price — is the stock, and the ramp keeps slipping.
