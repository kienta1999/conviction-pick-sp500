Lens: hunt for the thesis that is *real but still underappreciated by the market* — avoid what's already fully priced; weigh valuation, downside, and disintermediation risk. Judged only on the consolidated dossier; no new research.

## Ballot 1 — single pick

- **Top pick:** AME
- **Runner-up:** FCX
- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** AME is the skeptic's pick because the shortage evidence is the most honestly durable in the field — record orders for two consecutive quarters ($2.3B, +28% yoy / organic +25%), a record $4.11B backlog up 21% since year-end, and a CEO calling the "beginning stages of a multiyear infrastructure build-out" across AI, semiconductors, power grid, defense, and aerospace — while the market still prices it as a sleepy industrial compounder, not an AI-infrastructure name. The diversification that the growth lens calls a "drag" is the skeptic's cushion: no single hyperscaler customer, no single architecture shift that strands it, and qualification-locked instruments across the portfolio. Valuation is not an AI-bubble multiple, the balance sheet compounds (111% FCF conversion), and the order book is the thing the market hasn't caught up to.
- **Key shortage/backlog evidence:** record Q2 orders $2.3B (+28% yoy, organic +25%) and record backlog $4.11B (+21% since year-end), book-to-bill 1.12 with ~80% shipping within 12 months — plus June was the all-time monthly orders record and July orders were "very good."
- **Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk:** low — precision instruments and metrology are qualification-locked into semiconductor-fab and aerospace customer processes; switching is slow and expensive, and no single hyperscaler can bypass the portfolio the way it can whitebox a network switch.
- **Top risk:** the orders are lumpy by management's own admission — two great quarters could be pull-forward — and the pending $5B Indicor acquisition is a big integration bet that could dilute the story if it stumbles.
- **Conviction (1-10):** 7
