# Momentum research — batch 2 (financials): MS, SCHW, JPM, HOOD, V

Run date: 2026-09-23. All figures from Q2 2026 (calendar) or fiscal Q3 2026 (Visa) prints, July 2026. Sources dated per item.

---

## MS — Morgan Stanley (Q2 reported 2026-07-15)

**1. Shortage thesis.** The "shortage" is durable capital-markets demand: investment-banking revenue +58% yoy to $2.44B on completed M&A, IPOs and debt issuance; equities trading a record $6.3B (+69% yoy, ~$1.9B above StreetAccount) with "notable strength in Asia." Management frames AI capex as a multi-year fee driver — data-center CapEx projected $1.3T for 2027, potentially $1.5T for 2028 (Q2 call).

**2. Backlog / order book.** No disclosed IB backlog figure; the analogue is the asset-accumulation flywheel: record net new assets $148.1B in the quarter (just over half from IPO flows of late-stage private workplace clients), total client assets across wealth + investment management at the $10T milestone, fee-based client assets above $3T.

**3. Category position.** Top-2 global investment bank; #1 equities franchise by revenue in the quarter; wealth management scale ($8T+ client assets in the segment) second to none among wirehouses.

**4. Irreplaceability.** Relationships, balance sheet, and the workplace channel (capturing IPO wealth at the source) are the moat; a fintech cannot replicate M&A advisory trust or a $10T-asset wealth funnel. **Irreplaceability: 8/10.**

**5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months).** Record quarter across the board (revenue $21.35B +27%, EPS $3.46 +62%, ROTCE 26.6%); dividend raised 15% to $1.15; new $20B multiyear buyback authorization; wealth pre-tax margin 30.5%.

**6. Risks.** Equities trading is volatility-dependent and mean-reverts; IB fees are cyclical; compensation and investment spend rise with revenue; the stock already reflects a strong print.

**7. Shortage score: 7/10.** Verdict: the durable capital-markets cycle plus the asset-gathering flywheel makes MS a quality momentum compounder, but the trading spike is the cyclical part.

## SCHW — Charles Schwab (Q2 reported 2026-07-21)

**1. Shortage thesis.** The "shortage" is retail investor engagement at historic intensity: record daily average trades 11.9M (+57% yoy), record core net new assets $119.8B (+49% yoy; June alone a record $62.7B, 5.8% annualized organic growth), 1.4M new accounts in the quarter. Rate cuts are the second driver — NIM expanded 12bps q/q to 3.00%.

**2. Backlog / order book.** Client assets $13.08T (+22% yoy); margin loan balances $165.1B (+30% q/q; $169.9B at July month-end, +92% yoy); bank loan balances $67.0B (+33% yoy); managed-investing net flows +53% yoy; July added another $58.1B in core net new assets (+24% yoy).

**3. Category position.** Largest US retail brokerage by client assets (~$13T); dominant RIA custody platform.

**4. Irreplaceability.** Scale and switching costs (tax lots, account aggregation, advisor workflows) are real, but the threat is concrete: HOOD and fintechs compete directly for the active-trader cohort, and Schwab just launched Schwab Crypto (spot BTC/ETH at 75bps) in explicit response. **Irreplaceability: 7/10.**

**5. Recent catalysts.** Record revenue $7.07B (+21%) and record GAAP EPS $1.54 (+43%); management raised the 2026 revenue growth outlook; Schwab Crypto launch; Portfolio Insights (GenAI analytics); trading revenue +28% to $1.2B.

**6. Risks.** NII is rate-sensitive in both directions; revenue per trade fell to $1.64 from $2.03 (mix shift toward lower-value flow); margin-loan balances nearly doubling yoy is leverage on the way up and credit risk on the way down.

**7. Shortage score: 7/10.** Verdict: the asset-gathering machine is firing on all cylinders with a crypto kicker, but the momentum is engagement-cycle leverage, not a supply shortage.

## JPM — JPMorgan Chase (Q2 reported 2026-07-14)

**1. Shortage thesis.** Scale flywheel on every cycle driver at once: IB fees $3.21B (+30% yoy, led by equity underwriting), markets revenue $12.08B (+35%, equities +86% to $6.03B), NII $25.51B (+10% on loan/deposit growth), payments revenue +12% to $5.30B. Management raised the FY2026 NII outlook to ~$105.5B.

**2. Backlog / order book.** AUM $5.1T (+18% yoy) with $50B long-term net inflows in the quarter; client assets $7.7T (+19%); average loans +18% yoy; card services NCO rate expected ~3.2% for FY2026 (credit still benign).

**3. Category position.** #1 US bank by assets, deposits, IB fees, and card outstandings — the largest lender is simply the biggest beneficiary of the capital-markets rebound.

**4. Irreplaceability.** Fortress balance sheet (CET1 ~14.8% standardized at MS; JPM similarly fortress), deposit franchise, and payments scale; the threats are regulatory (Basel III endgame capital demands) and fintech unbundling at the edges, not the core. **Irreplaceability: 8/10.**

**5. Recent catalysts.** Headline net income $21.2B (+41%) — note $4.55B pretax Visa-share gain + $1.03B equity-investment gains flattered it; ex-items profit +13%. Quarterly dividend raised to $1.65 from Q3; raised NII guide.

**6. Risks.** Management also raised the 2026 adjusted expense outlook to ~$107.5B (stock fell 2% on the print); credit normalization ahead; valuation already reflects best-in-class status; the Visa gain is non-recurring.

**7. Shortage score: 6/10.** Verdict: the highest-quality bank compounder in the screen, but the least "shortage-like" — this is scale compounding, not explosive-cycle torque.

## HOOD — Robinhood (Q2 reported 2026-07-29)

**1. Shortage thesis.** The closest thing to a true demand surge in this batch: event-contracts revenue $156M (up 10x+ yoy) — prediction markets are a genuinely new, supply-constrained product category; options $342M (+29%), equities $129M (+95%). ARPU +24% to $187 while funded customers grew only 7%: monetization depth, not just user growth.

**2. Backlog / order book.** Funded customers 28.4M (+7% yoy); total platform assets $369B (double-digit growth); net deposits $21.7B in Q2 and $75.7B over twelve months; Gold subscribers 4.8M (+39%).

**3. Category position.** #1 US retail options/event-contracts flow; challenger brokerage vs Schwab/Fidelity; emerging crypto stack (Bitstamp, WonderFi acquisition completed, Robinhood Chain mainnet launched, tokenized US stocks in 120+ countries).

**4. Irreplaceability.** Low switching costs for plain brokerage — Schwab Crypto (75bps) is a direct competitive response. The moat, such as it is, is network effects in prediction markets and first-mover crypto/tokenization infrastructure. Unproven at scale. **Irreplaceability: 5/10.**

**5. Recent catalysts.** Record revenue $1.31B (+32%), net income $573M (+48%) — though $129M was a one-time RVI deconsolidation gain ($0.14 of the $0.62 EPS). Robinhood Chain mainnet + tokenized stocks launch; Robinhood Earn (decentralized lending); agentic trading ~100k accounts, $100M+ AUM. June 2026 workforce reduction (restructuring charge in opex).

**6. Risks.** Crypto revenue −38% yoy ($100M) shows the cyclicality even as volume grew (fee/mix compression); opex +33%; prediction-market and crypto regulatory overhang; valuation prices a lot of the tokenization story.

**7. Shortage score: 8/10.** Verdict: the most explosive-cycle name in the batch — event contracts and tokenization are real demand surges — paired with the weakest moat; momentum with fragility.

## V — Visa (fiscal Q3 reported 2026-07-28)

**1. Shortage thesis.** Consumer-spend durability, not a supply shortage: payments volume crossed $4T in a quarter for the first time (+10% constant dollars); US volume +10% (fastest since FY19 ex-pandemic recovery); cross-border ex-intra-Europe +12%; processed transactions 71.7B (+10%). Value-added services +34% to $3.8B (1,200 consulting projects in the quarter); Visa Direct transactions +21% to $4B.

**2. Backlog / order book.** No backlog analogue; the metrics are volume-based. Raised full-year outlook: revenue growth low end of low-teens, EPS growth low end of mid-teens.

**3. Category position.** Largest global card network (~$4T quarterly payments volume); duopoly with Mastercard.

**4. Irreplaceability.** Two-sided network effects (issuers/merchants/consumers), bank-issuer rails, and settlement infrastructure; the long-term threat is stablecoin disintermediation of card rails — which Visa is co-opting via the newly launched Visa Stablecoin Platform and OpenUSD/Open Standard consortium membership. **Irreplaceability: 9/10.**

**5. Recent catalysts.** Raised FY outlook; FIFA World Cup volume boost (June inbound North America/LatAm); $4.9B buybacks + $1.3B dividends in the quarter; stablecoin platform launch; layoffs announced ($563M severance) as an efficiency drive.

**6. Risks.** DOJ/antitrust scrutiny of the network (ongoing overhang); consumer-spending slowdown would hit volume directly; opex +17% outpaced revenue; stablecoins could bypass card rails long-term if Visa's co-option fails.

**7. Shortage score: 7/10.** Verdict: the highest-quality payments compounder with genuine volume acceleration, but the momentum is consumer-cycle leverage at a premium multiple, not a shortage.
