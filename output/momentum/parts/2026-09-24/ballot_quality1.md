## Single-pick ballot

- **Top pick:** AMAT
- **Runner-up:** MSFT
- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** Applied Materials sits on the one bottleneck every AI build must pass through — the semiconductor supply chain's binding constraint has migrated from foundry capacity to equipment supply itself. Its moat is the deepest in this slate: fab qualification lock-in means a customer cannot swap a tool without risking yield on a $20B fab, and its "integrated materials solutions" (dep+etch+treatment under one vacuum) plus the GAA transition multiplying process steps give it compounding content per wafer. With lead times stretching 1.5–2x and component shortages into the dozens of months, that lock-in converts straight into pricing power that memory and NAND names can never claim.
- **Key shortage/backlog evidence:** ETNews/TrendForce (2026-09-18): major-equipment lead times stretched 1.5–2x, deposition-tool parts went from 4 to 10 months, and some Japanese components now stretch to 40 months; AMAT opened a $500M Singapore campus in June 2026 because it "cannot meet current demand from its existing footprint," and CEO Gary Dickerson is investing in capacity "through the end of the decade" — management raised the calendar-2026 Semiconductor Systems outlook to >30% WFE growth with "stronger customer visibility" into another strong 2027.
- **Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk:** low — hyperscalers don't build wafer-fab equipment; the only in-sourcing whisper is TSMC/Samsung exploring in-house tool development, which has never materialized at scale and faces decades of process-recipe IP and yield-qualification barriers.
- **12-18 month return scenario:** base ~+35–50% as 2026–27 equipment visibility converts toward the ~$640 analyst mean; bull ~+80–100% if the equipment shortage persists into 2027 (Susquehanna's $900 PT implies roughly a double from current levels).
- **Top risk:** concentration — roughly 80% of 2026–27 WFE growth runs through AI-linked segments (leading-edge foundry, DRAM/HBM, advanced packaging); a hyperscaler capex pause, or further US export-curb hits on the ~30% China revenue slice, unwinds the cycle.
- **Conviction (1-10):** 8

Base: ~+15–20% steady toll-booth compounding; bull: ~+30–40% if cross-border (+13%) and Visa Direct/value-added services (+34%) re-accelerate on macro resilience.

**MSFT.** The most compelling data point: commercial backlog of **$678 billion** (Sep 2026) with RPO +51% YoY — demand booked years out that literally cannot be delivered yet, while the stock is flat over the trailing 12 months (ret12m −0.02), so the capacity ramp is unpriced. Base: ~+25–35% as the ~1 GW/quarter capacity additions convert backlog to revenue. Bull: ~+50–70% if Azure re-accelerates to 40%+ and the market reprices capex spend as capacity delivery rather than cost burn.
