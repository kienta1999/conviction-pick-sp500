# Supply-Chain Analyst Ballot — Momentum — 2026-09-24

Lens: shortage strength first, then backlog size/visibility, then demand durability. Explosive upside = price can't clear the market yet, and someone has already paid to lock supply in.

## Single-pick ballot

- **Top pick:** MU
- **Runner-up:** SNDK
- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** Micron is selling into the tightest memory market in ~15 years with take-or-pay contracts that convert a cyclical boom into contracted cash flow. Sixteen strategic 5-year take-or-pay agreements (14 carrying ~$100B cumulative minimum contracted revenue at price floors, backed by $22B in customer deposits) mean a meaningful slice of the cycle is locked, not spot-exposed. HBM is fully booked through CY2026 with price/volume locked, next-gen HBM "completely sold out through calendar 2027," and Micron can meet only ~50–66% of core-customer HBM demand into 2027 — customers want more than the factory can make. Dell's COO confirmed on the August call that AI server demand is being held back by memory ("DRAM, DRAM, DRAM, followed by NAND, NAND, NAND"). Earnings land September 30 — six days out — as the immediate re-rating catalyst.
- **Key shortage/backlog evidence:** $22 billion in customer deposits and financial commitments standing behind ~$100B of minimum contracted revenue at price floors, on top of HBM being completely sold out through calendar 2027 — booked demand that exceeds the factory's capacity to deliver it.
- **Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk:** medium (i.e., favorable) — DRAM is a 3-player oligopoly behind $10B+ leading-edge fabs, and HBM adds a stacking/packaging layer only ~3–4 firms can do at yield; hyperscalers can design their own ASICs but cannot make DRAM bits — memory is purchased, never in-sourced. The risk is not substitution but collective capacity discipline: Samsung/SK Hynix capex decisions move the price for everyone.
- **12-18 month return scenario:** base — contract-price escalation holds through the expected ~2Q2027 pricing peak and FY2027 contracts re-price up; stock re-rates off its current ~6.8x forward P/E as spot-cycle EPS compounds; roughly +30–50%. Bull — HBM4 ramps 2x faster than planned, DRAM stays tight past the expected peak as AI server builds accelerate, and the contracted-revenue floor gets the market pricing Micron like an infrastructure utility rather than a commodity cyclical; roughly +75–100%.
- **Top risk:** the cycle turns — Citi expects price increases to decelerate over the next four quarters and peak in 2Q 2027, and only ~20% of DRAM volume is floor-protected, so the rest reprices spot; the stock is also up ~700% in 12 months, so much of the shortage is priced in.
- **Conviction (1-10):** 8

## Top-3 detail (supply-chain lens)

**1. MU** — The single most compelling point is the $22B in customer deposits securing ~$100B of minimum contracted revenue at price floors on 5-year take-or-pay terms: customers are pre-paying cash for memory they can't get elsewhere, and next-gen HBM is completely sold out through calendar 2027 with Micron able to meet only ~50–66% of core-customer demand into 2027 — that is demand rationing, the textbook shortage. Base ~+30–50% as price escalation compounds through the expected 2Q2027 peak; bull ~+75–100% if HBM stays sold out past the expected pricing peak and the market re-rates contracted memory revenue.

**2. SNDK** — Purest shortage instrument in the dossier: a genuine double squeeze — Samsung/SK Hynix actively cutting NAND wafer output ~10% to fund HBM while AI data centers hoard high-capacity enterprise SSDs — with Samsung raising NAND supply prices >100% QoQ and TrendForce projecting +70–75% QoQ contract prices. The backlog visibility is striking: 8 take-or-pay customers locking >50% of FY2027 bit supply and ~2/3 of FY2028 capacity, with $16.5B in financial guarantees and ~$93.9B minimum contracted revenue (treat the exact figures as approximate). Base ~+25–40% on continued price pass-through; bull ~+70–100% if the AI-SSD hoarding persists into 2027 before new supply arrives.

**3. AMAT** — The bottleneck has migrated up the stack: equipment lead times stretched 1.5–2x (tools that took ~6 months now take up to a year; deposition-tool parts from 4 to 10 months, some Japanese components to 40 months), and AMAT "cannot meet current demand from its existing footprint," opening a $500M Singapore campus and investing through end-of-decade. Moat is the strongest in the dossier (9/10): fab qualification lock-in, integrated process recipes, and GAA-transition step-count growth multiplying content per wafer — hyperscalers don't build WFE and never have at scale. Base ~+25–40% as raised 2027 visibility converts; bull ~+60–90% if 2027 WFE growth holds above 30% and AMAT captures share of the HBM/advanced-packaging tool buildout.
