## SINGLE-PICK BALLOT

- **Top pick:** GE
- **Runner-up:** MSFT
- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** GE Aerospace is the only name in the set where supply, not demand, is visibly capping output while the order book stretches years out: LEAP delivery growth slowed to ~5% in Q3 (535 units) after +26% year-to-date, explicitly on castings, while Q3 orders jumped 49% to $19.0bn and RPO stands at $180.4bn. The LEAP installed base is expected to more than double from 2025 to 2030, and ~70% of revenue is services on an installed base of ~50,000 commercial plus ~30,000 military engines — a multi-year, annuity-like pull-through that no other name matches. GE is buying the bottleneck itself with the $11.75bn CPP acquisition (close expected H2 2027), so easing the constraint directly converts backlog into revenue. That combination — a certified, sole-source engine franchise with a decade-long aftermarket tail — is the closest this set gets to the doctrine's structural shortage plus irreplaceable technology.
- **Key shortage/backlog evidence:** RPO $180.4bn at Sep 30, 2026 (commercial services $170.2bn, +10% vs end-2025), Q3 orders $19.0bn (+49% YoY), and ~95% of Q4 revenue already in backlog — against LEAP deliveries capped at ~5% growth by castings, with airfoil demand +30% by 2030 vs 2026.
- **Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk:** low — airlines/airframers cannot in-source a jet engine; FAA/EASA certification takes a decade, single-crystal casting is mastered by a handful of firms, LEAP-1B is the sole engine on every 737 MAX (100%) and GE9X is sole-source on the 777X. The moat is shared with Safran via the 50/50 CFM joint venture, which dilutes sole ownership but does not create a disintermediation path for customers.
- **12-18 month return scenario:** Base +12–18% — toward the $378.43 target (+14.0% from $331.81), driven by services growth and gradual LEAP delivery recovery. Bull +30–40% — if CPP integration and casting capacity let LEAP deliveries re-accelerate toward the +26% YTD pace while the $180.4bn RPO converts, the market re-rates the aftermarket annuity rather than just the next quarter's deliveries.
- **Top risk:** Valuation — ~35.6x forward P/E, PEG ~2.3–3.0, and the stock fell ~3.7% on Oct 20 despite an EPS beat, so years of aftermarket growth are already partly priced in; if the casting bottleneck does not ease, delivery growth stays capped and the re-rating stalls.
- **Conviction (1-10):** 8

### Top 3 in detail

**1. GE —** The single most compelling data point is the supply cap itself: LEAP delivery growth slowed to ~5% in Q3 (535 units) after +26% year-to-date, while Q3 orders rose 49% to $19.0bn and RPO reached $180.4bn with ~95% of Q4 revenue already in backlog — demand is running far ahead of what the casting bottleneck can deliver, and the LEAP installed base is expected to more than double 2025–2030. Base +12–18% toward the $378.43 target; bull +30–40% if casting capacity (aided by the $11.75bn CPP acquisition) lets deliveries re-accelerate and the services annuity re-rates.

**2. MSFT —** The single most compelling data point is commercial RPO of $677bn (+73% YoY, 2.3-year weighted-average duration, ~$203bn recognised in the next 12 months), with CFO Amy Hood stating on the Q1 FY27 call that "customer demand continues to exceed available supply" and Azure at $33.165bn (+48% YoY). Base +15–20% toward the $636.12 target (+21.4% from $533.16); bull +30% if delayed GPU capacity deliveries (some third-party GPU-as-a-service contracts slipped to Jan–Jun 2027) catch up and Azure sustains ~+49–50% constant-currency growth. Tempering note: RPO was flat sequentially ($678bn in Q4 FY26 to $677bn), and Microsoft must spend >$50bn/quarter in capex to deliver it.

**3. APH —** The single most compelling data point is the cleanest shortage print in the set: record Q3 orders of $12.17bn (+99% YoY, +13% sequentially), book-to-bill 1.19, with sales of $10.23bn (+65%, +37% organic) and IT datacom at 48% of sales, about three-quarters of it AI. Base +25–35% toward the $106.97 target (+36.7% from $78.23); bull +50%+ if the AI capex cycle lasts and order growth stays near triple digits. Tempering note: irreplaceability is only 5/10 — a standards-based, multi-source business where TE, Molex/Koch, Foxconn and Luxshare make equivalents, hyperscalers dual-source deliberately, total backlog $ and booked-out horizon were not disclosed, and backlog is cancellable.

Left out of top 10: META, ISRG, VRTX, APP — META is the buyer of scarce compute, not the seller (shortage 3/10, Q3 FCF -$2.12bn on the buildout); ISRG has no factory shortage (shortage 3/10 — the constraint is surgeon training/OR capacity); VRTX is a monopoly-pricing story, not a supply squeeze (shortage 2/10, CF is a fixed ~90,000-patient population); APP is software with no physical scarcity (shortage 2/10 — the story is Axon model gains, not a shortage/backlog).
