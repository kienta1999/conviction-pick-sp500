## GE AEROSPACE

### Claim 1 — RPO $180.4bn at Sep 30, 2026, incl. $170.2bn commercial services (+10% vs end-2025)
**Verdict: CONFIRMED (with scope clarification)**
- GE Aerospace Q3 2026 10-Q summary (via Minichart, Oct 20, 2026): "RPO at CES increased $15.9 billion, or 10%, from December 31, 2025, to $180.4 billion, driven by commercial actions and increases to existing long-term service agreements." Total-company RPO was $212.1bn (up 11% from $190.6bn at year-end 2025).
- Top1Markets (Oct 2026, summarising the 10-Q): "Remaining performance obligations reached $180.4 billion at September 30, 2026, including $170.2 billion in services, up 10% from the end of 2025" and "services remaining performance obligations were $170.2 billion at September 30, 2026, part of a $180.4 billion total."
- CEO Larry Culp on the Oct 20, 2026 release: 2027 commercial services growth supported by "our over $170 billion commercial services backlog."
- Clarification: $180.4bn is the **Commercial Engines & Services (CES) segment** RPO, not total-company RPO ($212.1bn). The +10% is the CES RPO change vs Dec 31, 2025; the claim's parenthetical attaches it to the services figure, which is imprecise but the numbers themselves match the filing.

### Claim 2 — Q3 2026 orders $19.0bn, +49% year-on-year
**Verdict: CONFIRMED**
- GE Aerospace press release, "GE Aerospace Announces Third Quarter 2026 Results," Oct 20, 2026 (geaerospace.com / SEC 8-K Ex. 99.1): "Total orders of $19.0B, +49%." CES orders $15.3bn, +58%; services orders +23%.

### Claim 3 — LEAP-1B is the sole engine on every Boeing 737 MAX (100%)
**Verdict: CONFIRMED**
- Simple Flying, Jan 14, 2026: "the LEAP-1B serving as the exclusive engine for the Boeing 737 MAX" / "Sole engine option"; and Oct 24, 2025: "the CFM LEAP-1B is the only option on the 737 MAX."
- Simple Flying, Nov 12, 2026: table lists "Boeing 737 MAX | CFM International | LEAP-1B" as an exclusive-engine programme; "the LEAP-1B was developed over a six-year program specifically for the airframe."
- Simple Flying, Jan 6, 2026: the LEAP "powers … 100% of the 737 MAX fleet."
- Note: LEAP is built by CFM International, the 50/50 GE Aerospace / Safran joint venture — i.e. a GE JV engine, not a wholly GE-owned engine. The sole-source fact itself is confirmed.

### Claim 4 — GE agreed to acquire Precision Castparts (CPP) for $11.75bn, announced Sep 8, 2026, close expected H2 2027
**Verdict: CONTRADICTED — on the target's name (price, date and close timing all confirmed)**
- GE Aerospace press release / SEC Form 8-K Ex. 99.1, Sep 8, 2026: "GE Aerospace to acquire Consolidated Precision Products (CPP)"; "Purchase price of $11.75B, expect transaction closes in the second half of 2027." Funded with $7bn cash and ~$4.75bn new debt; sellers Warburg Pincus and Berkshire Partners; subject to regulatory approvals.
- The target is **Consolidated Precision Products (CPP)** of Cleveland — a castings maker (~6,600 employees, 20+ facilities, ~$2.0bn 2027E revenue). **Precision Castparts Corp. (PCC) is a different company** (Berkshire Hathaway-owned) and is NOT the target. The claim's use of "Precision Castparts (CPP)" conflates the two. Every other element — $11.75bn, Sep 8, 2026 announcement, H2 2027 expected close — is confirmed by the primary source.

### Claim 5 — LEAP delivery growth slowed to ~5% in Q3 2026 (535 units) after +26% year-to-date, on casting supply constraints
**Verdict: CONFIRMED (quantitative part; causal clause is contextual, not a verbatim management attribution)**
- Motley Fool Q3 2026 earnings-call transcript takeaways, Oct 20, 2026: "LEAP Engine Deliveries — Growing 5% in the third quarter and 26% year to date, with five consecutive quarters of at least 500 deliveries."
- Top1Markets (Oct 2026, from the 10-Q): "LEAP deliveries were 535 units in Q3 versus 511 a year earlier, against 1,565 versus 1,240 year-to-date. That is roughly 5% growth in the quarter after a 26% pace through nine months."
- GE Q3 release: "LEAP growing 26%" year-to-date; priority-supplier material input up double-digits YoY for a 10th consecutive quarter.
- On causation: the call/release describe supply/material-input progress rather than blaming Q3 LEAP specifically on castings in a single quote I could verify. The castings link is strongly supported as context — the CPP acquisition (Claim 4) is explicitly "to build adequate capacity of mission-critical castings," and coverage (e.g. Tikr, Oct 29, 2026) states "GE Aerospace's LEAP engine deliveries are up 26% this year but grew just 5% in the third quarter. GE agreed to buy Consolidated Precision Products to add castings capacity." Treat "on casting supply constraints" as a well-supported inference, not a direct Q3 management quote.

### Claim 6 — FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance raised to $8.00–$8.10 in the Q3 2026 print (Oct 20, 2026)
**Verdict: CONFIRMED**
- GE Aerospace Q3 2026 press release, Oct 20, 2026: full-year 2026 guidance raised across the board; adjusted EPS $8.00–$8.10 (prior $7.65–$7.85, per TickerGrove/MoneyCheck coverage of the same release). Adjusted revenue growth ~19–20%, operating profit $10.9–$11.0bn, FCF $9.1–$9.4bn.

## MICROSOFT

### Claim 7 — Commercial RPO $677bn at Sep 30, 2026, +73% year-on-year, weighted-average duration 2.3 years
**Verdict: CONFIRMED**
- Microsoft Q1 FY2027 earnings release, Oct 28, 2026 (quarter ended Sep 30, 2026), as reported by Reuters/Yonhap (Oct 28, 2026) and multiple summaries of the release: "Commercial remaining performance obligations (RPO) surged by 73% to $677 billion."
- UseLuminix summary of the release/call: "Commercial remaining performance obligation was $677 billion, up 73%, with a weighted-average duration of 2.3 years; about 30% is due in the next 12 months."
- Cash & Chaos (citing the 10-Q): "Commercial remaining performance obligations were $677bn, up 73%, with approximately 30% expected to become revenue over the next twelve months. The 10-Q separately reports $683bn of total-company obligations; the commercial and total figures have different scopes." (Use the $677bn commercial figure, not the $683bn total.)

### Claim 8 — Azure revenue $33.165bn in Q1 FY27, +48% year-on-year; CFO Amy Hood said on the Oct 28, 2026 call that customer demand continues to exceed available supply
**Verdict: CONFIRMED**
- Microsoft Q1 FY2027 release, Oct 28, 2026: Azure revenue $33.165bn, up 48% (from $22.384bn a year earlier) — the first quarter Microsoft disclosed Azure revenue in dollars. Total revenue $92.279bn, +19%; Microsoft Cloud $63.9bn, +30%.
- Motley Fool Q1 FY2027 earnings-call transcript, Oct 28, 2026, Amy Hood: "In Azure, we expect revenue growth of 49% to 50% in constant currency … Customer demand continues to exceed available supply."
- GeekWire, Oct 28, 2026: "Customer demand continues to exceed available supply," she said — quoting Hood on the call. Hood also noted some third-party GPU-as-a-service capacity deliveries slipped to H2 FY27, which is why the demand-exceeds-supply statement matters (supply, not demand, is the constraint).

### Claim 9 — A global Microsoft 365 outage occurred Nov 12–13, 2026, degrading Outlook/Teams/Copilot for 20+ hours
**Verdict: CONFIRMED**
- Ookla/Downdetector analysis (Nov 2026): "Microsoft's email service began having problems on November 12, 2026, and outage reports surged worldwide from about 17:30 UTC (12:30 p.m. EST) as Outlook went down and Microsoft Teams, Microsoft Copilot, and other Microsoft 365 apps were disrupted." "Outage reports stayed high for about 20 hours"; "more than 7 million reports about Microsoft's services by 16:00 UTC on November 13" — the largest outage on Downdetector in 12 months.
- BusinessDay, Nov 14, 2026: problems with "Outlook, Teams, Copilot and other Microsoft 365 services persisted for about 20 hours across different regions"; Microsoft attributed it to email servers where a process for secure connections became overwhelmed, after a recent network change; no evidence of a cyberattack.
- Microsoft's own status updates (via Digit, Nov 13, 2026, 11:35 PM update): "This was a technical issue not related to cyber or AI/agents. The incident was introduced by an issue during an earlier network capacity upgrade that surfaced following a recent network change."
- Precision note: Downdetector's "~20 hours" is the period reports stayed elevated; Microsoft's incident start (EX1550543, ~11:17 AM ET Nov 12) to its final "restored service to the remaining impacted customers" notice (early Sat Nov 14, ~1:09 AM ET) spans ~38 hours. The claim's "20+ hours" is supported either way.
