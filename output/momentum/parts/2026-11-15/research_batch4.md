## APP — AppLovin Corp (NASDAQ: APP)

### 1. Shortage thesis
**No real supply shortage — say so plainly.** AppLovin sells AI-optimized mobile advertising (Axon engine + MAX mediation), a software service, not a physical product. There is no capacity sold out, no lead-time extension, no price increase driven by scarcity. What exists instead is a **demand surge for its AI ad-targeting performance**: growth is driven by model improvements, not constrained supply. Q3 2026: net revenue per install +55% YoY while install volume rose only ~2% (SEC-filing analysis of 10-Q/8-K, Nov 4, 2026) — i.e. advertisers pay more per install because Axon's predictions got better, not because inventory is scarce. E-commerce advertiser spend grew triple digits YoY (Q3 2026 call, per The Market MBA, Nov 5, 2026). The closest thing to "shortage" is advertiser demand for Axon-optimized ROAS campaigns, which are restricted to MAX publishers only — but that is a deliberate bundling choice, not a supply constraint. **Verdict on shortage: none.**

### 2. Backlog / order book
**Not disclosed.** AppLovin is a transactional ad platform; it reports no backlog, bookings, or contracted order book. Revenue is recognized per auction/install. Not applicable as a backlog name.

### 3. Category position
**#1 independent player in mobile in-app performance advertising; top-3 overall behind Google and Meta.** No audited share series exists (AppLovin does not disclose share). Third-party evidence: MAX mediation is used by 73.1% of top-downloaded games and 55% of top-grossing games (GameBiz Consulting, 368-game sample, Mar 2025 data) vs Unity LevelPlay 5.7% / 25.0% and AdMob 11.4% / 13.3%. In GameBiz's 2026 client data, AppLovin took 61.4% of iOS ad revenue and 39.5% of Android ad revenue vs Unity Ads ~10%. AppLovin serves ~50% of impressions on MAX. Caveat: Google (AdMob) and Meta remain larger in overall mobile advertising; APP is #1 only in the independent performance-ad niche and in mediation.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk — score 6/10
**Moat:** (a) data flywheel — Axon trains on auctions across 1B+ devices / 2M+ auctions per second; (b) MAX mediation lock-in — ROAS-optimized campaigns are MAX-only, so publishers that want AppLovin's best demand must run MAX, which in turn feeds Axon more data; (c) switching costs — moving mediation is a 6–12 week instrumented test (MonetizationGuy). **In-sourcing threat is real but bounded:** the biggest "customers" are game publishers (advertisers) and app developers (publishers), not a single concentrated buyer; Google and Meta already have their own ad stacks and are the structural competitors, not in-sourcers. The genuine bypass risk: a large publisher (e.g. a top game studio) could build direct-sold ads or shift spend to Google/Meta; and Apple/Google privacy changes (ATT-style) have historically degraded mobile ad targeting — Axon's edge depends on signal access it does not fully control. Unity is the direct rival but is losing share (ironSource direct traffic ended May 1, 2026). Score 6: strong network-effects moat in its niche, but it is a software intermediary whose edge is a model, not a physical/capital barrier — a better model or a platform policy change can route around it.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- **Q3 2026 earnings, Nov 4, 2026 — blowout:** revenue $2.185B, +55% YoY (beat ~$2.07B consensus); net income $1.439B, +72%; diluted EPS $4.30 vs ~$4.04 expected; adj. EBITDA $1.84B at 84.3% margin; FCF $1.42B. Stock +31.04% on Nov 5 to $356.10 (even as Nasdaq fell 5.52% that day).
- **Q4 2026 guidance raised above Street:** revenue $2.365–2.395B (+43–44% YoY) vs ~$2.25B consensus; adj. EBITDA $1.995–2.025B.
- **Analyst upgrades Nov 5–6, 2026:** Morgan Stanley raised PT $400→$450 (Overweight; gaming ad revenue +~50% YoY on model upgrade, e-commerce +100%+ YoY); RBC $500→$520 Outperform; Evercore ISI $510→$540 Outperform; Oppenheimer $490→$520 Outperform; Wedbush $600. Scotiabank cut $515→$450 (kept Sector Outperform) on valuation. Avg target ~$497 (MarketBeat, 28 analysts: 3 Strong Buy / 17 Buy / 8 Hold).
- **S&P 500 inclusion** during Q3 2025 (institutional flow tailwind, noted in Q3 2025 recap).
- **AppLovin Ads self-serve opened to all advertisers late June 2026** (referral requirement ended); consumer-brand spend growing triple digits YoY. CEO: new-generation model rollout was only partial in Q3 — "the transition is still in front of us."

### 6. Risks
- **Valuation/momentum whiplash:** stock fell from 52w high $738.01 (Dec 22, 2025) to a 52w low $225.19 (Oct 30, 2026) — down ~65% in 2026 before the Nov 5 pop; still −39% YTD at Nov 13 close despite the +31% day. Beta 2.53.
- **Model-dependence:** growth is entirely "model gains" (revenue per install, not volume); CEO warned "model gains won't land on a perfect quarterly schedule."
- **Customer/advertiser cyclicality:** ad budgets are discretionary; gaming-ad demand (core) is cyclical and e-commerce expansion is unproven at scale.
- **Platform risk:** Apple/Google privacy/policy changes can degrade targeting signal.
- **Short-seller overhang (2025):** short reports (Fuzzy Panda, Culper) alleged aggressive accounting/ad practices in 2025; not re-litigated here, but the controversy contributed to the 2026 derating and can recur.
- **Concentration of the thesis in one engine:** Axon is the whole story; a competitive model leap (Google/Meta/Unity Vector) would compress pricing power fast.

### 7. Shortage score: 2/10 — Verdict
No supply shortage exists; this is a demand/AI-model-performance story, not a scarcity bet. Explosive-return case rests on Axon model gains + e-commerce expansion compounding at 80%+ EBITDA margins, but the Nov 5 pop already re-rated it off the lows and the thesis is model-cadence-dependent.

## AME — AMETEK, Inc. (NYSE: AME)

### 1. Shortage thesis
**No classic structural shortage, but genuine demand-outstrips-supply pockets in its highest-tech niches.** AMETEK is a diversified precision-instrument/electromechanical conglomerate (EIG + EMG segments), not a single scarce product. Evidence of tight supply/demand in specific lines:
- **Semiconductor (Cameca, Zygo):** orders +>30% YoY in Q3 2026 vs sales +mid-teens — orders outpacing sales. CEO Dave Zapico, Q3 2026 call (Nov 3, 2026): "if you're trying to make yield improvements with the latest technology and you want to find defects in your process, you're going to buy instruments from CAMECA. These are multimillion-dollar instruments and they're kind of unique and just about every expanding fab has one and wants more." On Zygo EUV optics: "we are very unique."
- **Power:** orders up double digits vs sales up mid-single digits, driven by grid expansion, data-center infrastructure, nuclear and natural-gas build-out — order intake running ahead of shipments.
- **Aerospace & defense:** organic sales +mid-teens, "not seeing a slowdown," broad platform exposure.
However: no company statement of sold-out capacity, extended lead times, or price increases was found. The "shortage" is best described as **order-book strength in secular niches (semi capex, power grid, aero/defense)**, not a structural supply shortfall. **Verdict on shortage: partial — real demand surge in niches, no demonstrated supply constraint.**

### 2. Backlog / order book
**Record $4.54 billion at Sep 30, 2026 (Q3 2026 earnings, Nov 3, 2026), up 27% from the start of 2026 ($3.58B at year-end 2025).** Q3 2026 orders $2.65B, +35% YoY (organic orders +17%; ~18pp of the 35% was the acquired Indicor backlog). Organic order growth decelerating from 25% (Q2) and 22% (Q1) on tougher comps, per Earnings Whispers summary. ~80% of backlog shippable within 12 months. Book-to-bill: orders $2.65B vs sales $2.22B = ~1.19x. EIG orders +37%, EMG orders +31% YoY. September 2026 was an all-time monthly orders record; October started on plan.

### 3. Category position
**Not a single-category #1 — a portfolio of niche #1s.** AMETEK's model is owning mission-critical, highly differentiated niches (its stated strategy). Documented niche leadership: Cameca defect-detection instruments (CEO: "just about every expanding fab has one"); Zygo EUV optics ("very unique"); Abaco rugged electronics for semi capex equipment. No consolidated market-share figure vs a #2 exists — the company is a diversified acquirer of niche instrument businesses, not a single-market leader. Overall scale: ~$8.5B+ annualized revenue, $55B market cap.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk — score 7/10
**Moat:** qualification/certification lock-in and process-tech uniqueness in niches. Cameca instruments are multimillion-dollar, fab-qualified tools — a fab cannot swap metrology suppliers without requalification and yield risk. Zygo EUV optics sit in the ASML EUV supply chain, one of the most concentrated, hardest-to-replicate technology stacks on earth. Aerospace/defense products are platform-qualified across multiple aircraft/defense programs. **In-sourcing threat: low.** Customers (fabs, aerospace OEMs, utilities) do not build their own analytical instruments or EUV optics — the capital intensity, precision optics know-how, and installed-base qualification make in-sourcing unrealistic. The realistic bypass is a competing instrument vendor, but these are oligopolistic niches with few qualified suppliers. Score 7, not higher, because AMETEK is a portfolio — most of its businesses are solid niche franchises, not un-replicable monopolies, and the conglomerate average dilutes the crown-jewel irreplaceability. Also, semi capex is cyclical: today's >30% order growth can reverse.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- **Q3 2026 earnings, Nov 3, 2026 — record quarter, third straight beat-and-raise:** sales $2.22B, +17% YoY (organic +10%, acquisitions +7pp), record; adj. EPS $2.21, +17% YoY, beat $2.12 consensus by 4.2% and top of $2.08–2.10 guide; operating margin ~26.8%; record orders and backlog (see §2).
- **Guidance raised (3rd consecutive raise):** FY2026 adj. EPS $8.53–8.57 (from $8.20–8.30; initial $7.87–8.07), ~+15% YoY; FY2026 sales +mid-teens. Includes ~$0.08 from Indicor.
- **Indicor acquisition completed Aug 26, 2026 — $5B all-cash, largest deal in company history:** ~$1.1B annual revenue portfolio of instrumentation businesses, ~30% EBITDA margin, 10–12% synergy target over 3 years; contributed >$100M revenue in ~5 weeks of Q3 ownership. M&A pipeline remains active.
- **End-market momentum:** semi orders +>30% (Cameca/Zygo/Abaco); power orders double-digit on grid/data-center/nuclear; aero/defense mid-teens organic growth; Asia organic growth mid-20s, China above regional average.

### 6. Risks
- **Leverage from Indicor:** total debt $6.7B at Sep 30, 2026 vs $2.3B at end-2025; net leverage 2.1–2.3x EBITDA (from 0.6x in Q2). Integration risk on the largest deal ever; $5B at ~14x EBITDA is a full price.
- **Cyclicality:** semi capex, aerospace, and industrial automation are cyclical; order growth already decelerating (organic orders 25%→22%→17% across Q1–Q3 2026).
- **Not an explosive-return profile:** this is a steady compounder (+17.9% YTD, +25% over 1y); backlog converts over 12 months at mid-teens growth — no step-change scarcity payoff.
- **Acquisition dependence:** growth model relies on serial M&A at disciplined prices; a rich deal or integration stumble compresses the multiple (fwd PE 25.1x).
- **Geopolitics/export controls:** semi exposure (~6% of sales) sits in the US–China tech-controls crosshairs; China was a growth driver this quarter.

### 7. Shortage score: 4/10 — Verdict
Real order-book strength and genuinely hard-to-replicate niches (Cameca, Zygo EUV optics) with orders outrunning sales, but no demonstrated supply shortage and a diversified industrial profile that caps explosiveness — a quality compounder with a record backlog, not a shortage squeeze.
