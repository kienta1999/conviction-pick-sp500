Lens: category dominance, margins, returns on capital, balance-sheet durability, and above all irreplaceability. Evidence is from the consolidated dossier only.

## SINGLE-PICK BALLOT

- **Top pick:** GE
- **Runner-up:** MSFT
- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** GE Aerospace is the only name in the set that scores 9/10 on both shortage and irreplaceability, and it earns both. Through CFM it is sole-source on every 737 MAX (LEAP-1B, 100%) and sole-source with the GE9X on the 777X, with an installed base of ~50,000 commercial plus ~30,000 military engines and services at ~70% of revenue — a decades-long, high-margin aftermarket annuity, not a one-off equipment sale. The LEAP installed base is expected to more than double from 2025 to 2030 while castings cap output, so demand is rationed by supply rather than by GE's ability to sell. GE is now buying the bottleneck itself with the $11.75bn CPP acquisition, and guidance was raised again in Q3. That combination — a certification-locked monopoly, a supply-capped ramp, and a services annuity — is the closest this set comes to a moat nobody can copy.
- **Key shortage/backlog evidence:** RPO of $180.4bn at Sep 30, 2026 (commercial services $170.2bn, +10% vs end-2025), with Q3 orders of $19.0bn (+49% YoY) and ~95% of Q4 revenue already in backlog.
- **Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk:** low — airlines and airframers cannot in-source a jet engine; FAA/EASA certification takes a decade, single-crystal casting is mastered by only a handful of firms, and GE is sole-source on the 737 MAX and 777X. The moat is shared with Safran via the 50/50 CFM joint venture, which dilutes control but does not create a substitute.
- **12-18 month return scenario:** base roughly +15–20% (toward ~$380–400, in line with the $378.43 target, on continued services growth and backlog conversion); bull roughly +35–45% (toward ~$450–480) if LEAP deliveries re-accelerate as casting capacity eases and the CPP deal closes in H2 2027, with the aftermarket compounding on the doubling installed base.
- **Top risk:** Valuation — ~35.6x forward P/E and a PEG of ~2.3–3.0 already price years of aftermarket growth, and the stock fell ~3.7% on Oct 20 despite beating; a casting-constrained delivery miss or a softening air-travel cycle would hit a fully priced name hard.
- **Conviction (1-10):** 8
