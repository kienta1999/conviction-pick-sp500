## 1. CBOE — Cboe Global Markets ($353.27)

### 1. Shortage thesis
No physical shortage — this is a **demand-surge / exclusive-supply** thesis, not a capacity-constrained one. The driver is the structural explosion in index-options trading, above all **0DTE (zero-days-to-expiration) SPX options**: Q3 2026 SPX 0DTE average daily volume hit a **record 3.2 million contracts, +34% YoY**, and 0DTE made up a **record 65% of all SPX activity** (Cboe Q3 2026 earnings call, 30 Oct 2026). SPX options ADV rose 26% YoY to 4.9m contracts; total options ADV +14% to 21.3m contracts. Retail is the engine: SPX 0DTE retail volume +46% YoY vs +20% institutional; retail now ~58% of SPX 0DTE volume (up from 53% a year ago). Mini-SPX (XSP) volume more than doubled YoY, averaging 251,000 contracts/day, "driven largely by retail following the June repeal of the Pattern Day Trader rule" (Q3 call). Global Trading Hours volume jumped >130%, Asia now >30% of GTH volume. There is no "sold out" capacity — the exchange scales electronically — but demand growth is real and compounding, and Cboe is the *only* venue that can supply SPX/VIX options (see §4).

### 2. Backlog / order book
**Not applicable in the backlog sense — no order book exists for an exchange.** The closest forward indicator is trading volume momentum plus guidance: Cboe raised 2026 organic net revenue growth guidance to the **high teens** (from mid-to-high teens) on 30 Oct 2026. No deferred-revenue/backlog figure was reported. **Not found** as a backlog number.

### 3. Category position
**#1, by a wide margin, in its proprietary niche.** Cboe's own monthly volume statistics (updated 4 Nov 2026) show **index-options market share ~98.3–98.5%** through 2026, total options share ~30% (multi-listed options ~23–24%, where it competes with Nasdaq/NYSE/ICE venues), and US equities exchange share ~9%. In plain terms: essentially 100% of US index options trade on Cboe; in fungible multi-listed options it is one of several venues at ~23%.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk — score 9/10
The moat is **contractual exclusivity, not technology**: on 30 Oct 2026 Cboe announced a **25-year extension of its exclusive licensing agreement with S&P Dow Jones Indices, through 2051**, covering SPX and VIX options/futures. No customer can in-source this — a broker or hyperscaler cannot legally list SPX options; only Cboe can. The only theoretical bypass is a substitute index (e.g. Nasdaq-100 options on Nasdaq, or CME's S&P futures/options complex), but SPX is the benchmark for US equity hedging and 0DTE liquidity is self-reinforcing (deepest pool attracts the next trade). Risks to the moat: the licence could in principle not be renewed in 2051 (a quarter-century away); S&P raised royalty terms (management: "de minimis" impact on 2027 net revenue growth); and regulators could in theory challenge exclusivity. No hyperscaler in-sourcing threat exists — this is not a manufactured input. **Irreplaceability: 9/10** (1 point off for the licence being a contract, not a law of physics, and for substitute indices existing).

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- **Q3 2026 earnings, 30 Oct 2026:** net revenue $712.2m, +18% YoY (beat ~$700m consensus); adjusted diluted EPS $3.63, +36% YoY (beat $3.43 consensus by 5.8%); GAAP EPS $4.08, +43%, a record; options net revenue +23% to $469.5m; derivatives net revenue +22% to $402m; DataVantage net revenue $179.1m, +13% (record); Global FX +23%. Guidance raised to high-teens organic revenue growth; expense forecast lowered.
- **S&P licence extended through 2051** (announced with Q3 results, 30 Oct 2026).
- **Cboe Predicts / KPI event contracts:** SEC approved listing of company-KPI binary options (13 Oct 2026, Release No. 34-106659) and temporary clearing via Cboe Clear U.S.; launch expected **November 2026**, covering 23 companies, with **Robinhood and Interactive Brokers as launch partners** (Schwab also interested); fees waived through 31 Dec 2026. First product (Mini-S&P 500 binaries) launched June 2026.
- **Share price:** +28.7% in the month to early Nov 2026, +40.8% YTD (Simply Wall St, 3 Nov 2026) — momentum is already strong.
- Divestiture: sale of Cboe Australia closed 31 Jul 2026 ($71.5m net gain in Q3).

### 6. Risks
- **Cyclicality of volumes:** options volumes are volatility- and sentiment-driven; VIX options ADV rose only +3% YoY in Q3 ("a stable volatility environment limited monetization opportunities"). A prolonged calm market or a retail-trading cool-off (0DTE is 58% retail) would hit the growth engine.
- **Valuation already pricing it in:** ~25x P/E (Multiples.vc, 8 Nov 2026: 25.3x; Finnhub ~26.7x); Simply Wall St's most-followed narrative pegs fair value at $241.95 vs ~$349 close (3 Nov 2026) — i.e. the market may already have paid for the 2051 licence. Stock +41% over 1 year.
- **Regulatory:** event contracts face an unsettled SEC/CFTC turf question (Kalshi/Rothera asked the SEC to wait; declined) — Cboe's CEO himself warned about event contracts trading outside securities law creating a "shadow securities market."
- **Royalty drag:** higher S&P royalty terms under the new licence (small near-term, per management).
- **Concentration:** proprietary index products are the profit engine (index RPC $0.952 vs $0.307 blended); any shift of hedging flow to futures or rival indices would hurt disproportionately.

### 7. Shortage score & verdict
**Shortage score: 6/10.** No physical shortage, but a genuine demand surge in a product only Cboe can legally supply, with record volumes and pricing power (options RPC +9% YoY).
**Verdict:** The strongest moat in this batch — a legal monopoly on the market's favourite hedging instrument, extended to 2051, riding a retail 0DTE boom — but the explosive-return case is weakened by a +41% one-year run and a full valuation; the bet is on volumes staying hot, not on a supply crunch.

## 2. VRSN — VeriSign ($308.67)

### 1. Shortage thesis
No physical shortage — this is a **regulated-monopoly pricing-power** thesis with a demand *re-acceleration*, not a supply crunch. The driver is AI: CEO Jim Bidzos on the Q3 2026 call (22 Oct 2026): "AI tools are making content and website creation faster and easier," contributing to record registrations; management cited AI and agentic AI as accelerating tailwinds, with new security challenges from person-to-machine and machine-to-machine interaction. Evidence: **record 13.1m new .com/.net registrations in Q3 2026, +23% YoY** (10.6m a year ago; 12.7m in Q2). The domain base grew 3.4m in the quarter to 182.5m. This is demand growth, not scarcity — anyone can register a domain, but every .com registration must pass through VeriSign's registry. The "shortage" analogue is price: the **.com wholesale fee rises $0.71 (+7%, to $10.97) effective 1 Nov 2026**, under the NTIA agreement permitting up to 7%/yr rises — a contractual price escalator, not a market-clearing shortage.

### 2. Backlog / order book
**No backlog in the order-book sense.** The registry is a flow business: domains are registered/renewed continuously. The closest forward indicator is the domain base itself: **182.5m .com/.net names at 30 Sep 2026, +6.1% YoY (+3.4m QoQ)**; FY2026 base-growth guidance raised for the third straight time to **6.5–7.0%** (initial guidance was 1.5–3.5%). Renewal rate is the retention metric: expected Q3 renewal rate **74.3% vs 75.4%** a year ago — down, because first-time renewals (mid-40% renewal rate) are a larger share of the base after the registration surge. No booked-revenue backlog figure disclosed. **Not found** as a backlog number.

### 3. Category position
**#1 — in fact, the only player.** VeriSign operates the authoritative registry for .com and .net with **100% market share of .com** (DOJ has acknowledged it "possesses significant market power"). .com is ~42.7% of all observed hostnames on the internet (1.36bn hostnames, DomainsProject June 2026 dataset). There is no #2 for .com — the "competition" is other TLDs (.ai, .xyz, .shop, country codes): new gTLDs totalled 52.9m registrations at end-Q2 2026, growing +34% YoY vs +5.1% for .com/.net (Domain Name Industry Brief, via MoatExplorer). The .com/.net base had *declined* for six straight quarters through Q3 2024 (to 169.0m at end-2024) before re-accelerating in 2025–26 — so the monopoly does not guarantee unit growth.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk — score 9/10
The moat is **a government-sanctioned contract**: VeriSign is the sole operator of the .com/.net registries under its ICANN/NTIA cooperative agreement (runs to 2030; auto-renewal structure). Nobody can build a competing .com — the namespace is singular, and switching costs are total (a business's .com *is* its identity). ~900 employees run it at ~67% operating margin (Q3 2026: operating income $296m on $443m revenue). The bypass threats are slow and indirect: users discovering businesses via social media/apps/AI assistants without ever resolving a hostname (named in VeriSign's own risk factors), and cheap new gTLDs taking marginal share. Hyperscalers cannot in-source the registry — it is a contracted public function, not a component they buy. The real (low-probability, high-impact) risk is regulatory: the .com agreement renewal and pricing terms are set by NTIA/ICANN, and an antitrust suit over .com pricing exists (noted as a key risk by Tickeron, Oct 2026). **Irreplaceability: 9/10** (1 point off for the contract/regulatory tail risk and the slow AI-assistant disintermediation of the domain habit).

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- **Q3 2026 earnings, 22 Oct 2026:** revenue $443m, +5.7% YoY (beat ~$441.75m); GAAP EPS $2.35 vs $2.27 (adjusted $2.41 vs $2.42 consensus — a 1-cent miss ex-items, per Earnings Whispers); operating income $296m, +4.2%; **record 13.1m new registrations**; guidance raised: FY2026 revenue $1.755–1.760bn (from $1.745–1.755bn), base growth 6.5–7.0% (third raise this year).
- **.com price rise +7% effective 1 Nov 2026** — ~50% of the benefit flows to 2027 revenue, most of the rest in 2028 (management). Registrars had raised retail prices only ~1% since the announcement, with no early-renewal pull-forward observed.
- **.web launch:** 30-day sunrise period from mid-Dec 2026, limited registration period mid-Jan to early Apr 2027, general availability ~Apr 2027. Pricing being finalised; no .web revenue in 2026 guidance.
- **VeriSign Informed** (certificate-revocation security service): pricing due by year-end 2026, production in H1 2027.
- **Analyst action:** Wedbush raised target to $367 (27 Oct 2026, Outperform reiterated); Citi raised to $345 (Buy, Oct 2026); Baird $380 Outperform; JPMorgan $316 Neutral. Consensus ~$339.60, ~+10% upside (5 analysts). Buybacks: $189m in Q3; diluted shares 90.4m vs 93.6m a year ago.
- Stock +28.8% over 1 year (RockFlow), near 52-week high ($312.48) at ~$307–309.

### 6. Risks
- **Valuation already pricing it in:** trailing P/E ~33x, forward ~29x (StockAnalysis, 6 Nov 2026); P/S ~15.8x FY2026. Consensus upside only ~+10% — the market already pays a premium for the monopoly.
- **Renewal quality:** renewal rate slipped to 74.3%; the record 2026 cohort faces its first renewal test in 2027 and first-time renewal rates are only mid-40% — if the AI-driven registration surge is speculative (domain-flipping/AI-generated sites), churn could spike.
- **Regulatory tail risk:** .com agreement runs to 2030; pricing flexibility (7%/yr) is a negotiated term, not a right; antitrust scrutiny of .com pricing is live.
- **Slow growth ceiling:** even in a record quarter, revenue grew only 5.7%; this is a mid-single-digit grower with a price escalator, not an explosive compounder, unless .web or VeriSign Informed becomes material (both unproven, no revenue guided).
- **Structural demand risk:** AI assistants answering questions without resolving hostnames, and identity via social/apps, could erode the need for domains over a decade (company's own risk factor).

### 7. Shortage score & verdict
**Shortage score: 4/10.** No shortage at all — infinite supply of names at a regulated price — but record AI-driven registration demand plus a contractual +7%/yr price escalator is a real, durable pricing-power story.
**Verdict:** The purest monopoly in the batch (100% of .com, ~67% operating margin, 47% FCF margin) with a genuine AI tailwind and a fresh price rise — but it is a slow, fully-priced annuity (33x P/E, ~+10% consensus upside), not an explosive-return bet; upside needs .web or a renewal surprise.

## 3. ABNB — Airbnb ($189.99)

### 1. Shortage thesis
**Honest answer: Airbnb has no shortage thesis.** It is a marketplace for travel stays, not a scarce physical input. Supply (hosts/listings) is ample and growing — >9m active listings across 220 countries (Nasdaq, 21 Oct 2026; >7.7m per NerdWallet May 2026) — and demand is cyclical travel demand, not a structural supply crunch. The closest analogue to "shortage" is *demand re-acceleration*: Q3 2026 was the fastest revenue growth in 3+ years, with nights booked in North America growing at the fastest pace in four years. But there are no sold-out capacity constraints, no extending lead times, no input price spike — Airbnb's constraint, if any, is regulatory (city short-term-rental caps), which *restricts* its supply rather than creating a shortage it can monetise. Any shortage framing for ABNB would be a stretch; the real thesis here is a growth re-acceleration + platform expansion story.

### 2. Backlog / order book
**No backlog.** Bookings are short-dated by nature (nights booked, then consumed within weeks/months). The forward indicators are: **Gross Booking Value $27.7bn in Q3 2026, +21% YoY** (5pp faster than Q2); **Nights and Seats Booked 155m, +16% YoY** (~13% excluding the Reserve Now, Pay Later lift, which added ~3pp); Q4 2026 guidance: revenue $3.22–3.28bn (+16–18% YoY), GBV growth mid-teens, nights growth at least low-double-digit. Note the quality caveat: Reserve Now, Pay Later also lifted cancellations to ~19% (from ~16%), and management expects its net benefit to shrink in Q4. No deferred backlog figure exists. **Not found** as a backlog number.

### 3. Category position
**#1 in short-term rentals, but not a monopoly.** Skift Research 2026: Airbnb ~**44% of global short-term-rental market share**, vs Booking.com ~18% and Vrbo ~9% (top three = 71%). An alternative industry estimate puts Airbnb ~24.6%, Booking.com ~21.3%, Expedia ~19.7% (Boostly, 2026) — the ranking is consistent (#1) but the gap depends on market definition (pure STR vs all online travel). Listings: >9m active listings (Airbnb) vs >2m Vrbo; Booking.com has ~28m total listings but mostly hotels. In the US, Vrbo is stronger (20–25% of domestic booking volume, Hostfully survey). Airbnb's edge is brand/network effects and entire-home inventory, not exclusivity.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk — score 3/10
Low. Airbnb is a two-sided marketplace with **no proprietary supply**: hosts multi-list on Vrbo/Booking.com, guests compare across platforms, and direct booking is a live threat — guests who book direct return 28.3% of the time vs 8.9% through Airbnb (hostAI 2026 report), and property managers increasingly push direct channels. There is no process-tech lead, patent wall, certification lock-in, or capital intensity preventing replication — Booking.com and Expedia already replicate it at scale, and Google Travel aggregates demand above it. The moat is brand + network effects + review history, which is real but porous: Airbnb charges hosts ~15.5% (vs Vrbo ~8%), giving hosts a standing incentive to steer repeat guests direct. Hyperscaler in-sourcing is not the threat; **host/guest disintermediation and Booking's distribution** are. **Irreplaceability: 3/10.**

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- **Q3 2026 earnings, 5 Nov 2026 (the big one):** revenue $4.87bn, **+19% YoY — fastest in 3+ years**, beating ~$4.75bn consensus; EPS $3.16, +43% YoY (beat ~$2.85); net income $1.9bn; adjusted EBITDA $2.5bn, +22%, 52% margin; GBV $27.7bn, +21%; nights 155m, +16%. Stock jumped ~11% after hours. First-time booker growth accelerated to 13% YoY (highest since early 2022), Gen Z fastest-growing; app now 66% of nights booked (+29% YoY).
- **Guidance raised:** FY2026 revenue growth to high-teens (from at least mid-teens); adjusted EBITDA margin ~36% (from ≥35.5%); Q4 revenue $3.22–3.28bn, above ~$3.16bn consensus.
- **$12bn new buyback authorisation** announced 5 Nov 2026 (total available $14.7bn incl. prior unused); $0.7bn repurchased in Q3.
- **Platform expansion:** hotels — thousands of boutique/independent hotels across 60+ destinations, bookings accelerating; ~35% of guests whose first booking is a hotel return to book a home within a year; Services expanded 30 Sep 2026 (meal delivery, laundry, baby gear, ski gear, boat rentals; grocery delivery expanded US/Canada and into Europe); car rentals (launched summer 2026) now one of the most popular services. **Amex partnership** launched 22 Oct 2026 — exclusive, bringing 1m+ listings to Amex Travel. **AI travel agent** planned for H1 2027 rollout; >50 products/features shipped in Q3 (+70% YoY) with AI help; ~half of customer-service tickets now handled by AI.
- **Stock:** +55–57% over 12 months (Finnhub: 1-yr +57.17%), near 52-week high ($193.45); +11.7% on the Q3 print alone.

### 6. Risks
- **No shortage/backlog thesis — the panel's core lens simply doesn't apply.** The bet would rest on cyclical travel demand and execution in hotels/services, not scarcity.
- **Cyclicality:** travel is discretionary and macro-sensitive; a recession or airfare/hotel-price shock hits nights quickly. Q4 guide already implies slower nights growth (low-double-digit vs 16%).
- **Valuation already pricing it in:** P/E ~37x trailing, forward P/E ~33x vs Booking ~15.5x (Nasdaq, 21 Oct 2026); P/S 8.0x vs Booking 4.3x. Stock +57% in 12m and near its high *after* the Q3 beat — the re-acceleration is now consensus.
- **Quality of the Q3 beat:** ~3pp of nights growth came from Reserve Now, Pay Later, which also raised cancellations to ~19%; underlying nights growth ~13% and the benefit fades in Q4.
- **Competition/disintermediation:** Booking.com (~18% STR share, far larger overall travel scale, cheaper stock), Vrbo/Expedia, direct booking, and Google as gatekeeper; hosts pay ~15.5% vs Vrbo ~8%.
- **Regulation:** city-level STR caps/restrictions remain a structural overhang on supply growth in major markets.
- **New verticals unproven:** hotels/services/AI agent are small today; CEO's "multiple billions in hotels revenue in coming years" is aspiration, not guidance.

### 7. Shortage score & verdict
**Shortage score: 1/10.** No shortage, no backlog, no scarcity — a demand re-acceleration in an abundant-supply marketplace.
**Verdict:** A genuine growth re-acceleration (fastest revenue growth in 3+ years, guidance raised, $12bn buyback, hotels/services optionality) and the #1 STR brand — but it fails the panel's shortage lens outright, is easily substituted, and at 37x earnings after a +57% run the explosive upside is mostly already in the price.
