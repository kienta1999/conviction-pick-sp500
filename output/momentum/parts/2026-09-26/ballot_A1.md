## Single-pick ballot

- **Top pick:** SNDK
- **Runner-up:** MU
- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** NAND is in a structural, AI-driven shortage with contract prices up 80% in a single quarter and all 2026 industry production sold out — and SanDisk has pre-sold the cycle: eight NBM framework agreements covering >50% of FY2027 bits and ~two-thirds of FY2028 bits at >4-year average duration, representing ≥$93.9B in expected revenue plus $16.5B in financial guarantees. Citi estimates average NAND prices +186% YoY in 2026 and TrendForce sees a −4% to −5% bit supply gap through 1H 2027, so the contracted book converts against rising pricing, with management targeting 80% non-GAAP gross margin. Bits "to remain allocated beyond calendar 2027" means demand visibly exceeds supply for 15+ months. The guide miss (FQ1 FY27 guide below consensus) knocked it off its peak — this lens reads that as a pricing entry into a sold-out cycle, not a demand problem.
- **Key shortage/backlog evidence:** The ≥$93.9B of signed agreements + $16.5B of financial guarantees, locking >50% of FY2027 and ~2/3 of FY2028 bits under >4-year take-or-pay-style frameworks — the hardest contracted-revenue figure in the dossier.
- **Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk:** Medium (6/10 per dossier). One of only ~5–6 NAND makers on earth; fabs cost $10B+, take 12–18 months to qualify, and the Flash Ventures JV with Kioxia splits capex — hyperscalers cannot realistically build NAND. But NAND is a commodity at wafer level (pricing power, not product uniqueness, is the moat), and YMTC just overtook SanDisk to #3 (14%) with CXMT entering NAND — capacity overhang is the real flank.
- **12-18 month return scenario:** Base +30–40% as contracted bits convert at elevated pricing and the 80% GM target progresses; bull +80–120% if the shortfall persists through 2028 (Citi's case) and peak-cycle pricing holds through the full contracted book.
- **Top risk:** Memory cyclicality — TrendForce already sees NAND contract-price hike deceleration (70–75% → 10–15% QoQ); if pricing rolls, the 6.7x forward P/E proves to be peak-earnings pricing and a bust compresses both earnings and multiple.
- **Conviction (1-10):** 8

### Top 3 — the single most compelling data point each

**SNDK.** The ≥$93.9B in expected revenue + $16.5B in financial guarantees under >4-year framework agreements covering >50% of FY2027 bits and ~2/3 of FY2028 bits is the dossier's single hardest contracted book — pre-sold, take-or-pay-adjacent revenue while management says bits "remain allocated beyond calendar 2027" and Citi estimates NAND prices +186% YoY in 2026. Base +30–40% as the contracted book converts at peak pricing toward the 80% GM target; bull +80–120% if the shortfall runs through 2028 (Citi) with no YMTC supply relief.

**MU.** The CBO's "we're sold out for 2026" plus "can meet only 50–66% of medium-term customer bit demand," backed by $22B of customer prepayments and multi-year Strategic Customer Agreements — cash-in-hand proof the shortage is real, with HBM4 already >$1B and ramping 2x HBM3E. Base +25–35% on the FQ4 guide of ~$50B revenue at ~86% GM converting against prepayments; bull +60–100% if supply constraints persist beyond 2026 (Mehrotra's "unprecedented" call) — near-term binary is Sept 30 earnings, 4 days out.

**MSFT.** The $678B commercial RPO (+84% YoY, +$53B in one quarter), with ~30% converting within 12 months (>$200B of contracted FY27 revenue), growing while Nadella says customer demand exceeds available capacity ("I don't have warm shells to plug into") — the largest and fastest-growing contracted backlog in the dossier, with Azure accelerating to +43%. Base +15–25% as backlog converts and Q1 FY27 Azure guides ~45%; bull +40–60% if the $255–260B capex program closes the power gap without margin collapse.

### Left out of the top 10

ANET, CSCO, LRCX. ANET is left out because its $9.7B is purchase commitments (its own purchases, not customer backlog) and NVIDIA's Spectrum-X has already flipped DC Ethernet share (20.4% vs 18.7%, +181% YoY) — the shortage upside accrues to the competitor. CSCO's $9.3B of AI orders is real but small next to MSFT/GOOGL backlogs, AI-hardware mix is diluting product gross margin (64.8% vs 67.5%), and NVIDIA now leads its key growth segment. LRCX's shortage is purely indirect derived demand with only $2.43B of deferred revenue against KLAC's ~$13B backlog and AMAT's cancellation-charge-backed forecasts.
