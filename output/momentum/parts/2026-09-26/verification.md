## MU (Micron Technology)

### Claim 1 — HBM "sold out for 2026", can meet only 50–66% of medium-term customer bit demand (Sadana / Mehrotra, "unprecedented" shortage call)
**Verdict: CONFIRMED** — substance and attribution both hold.
- Prism News (covering January 2026 FQ1 earnings/CES): EVP & CBO **Sumit Sadana told CNBC at CES** that "we're sold out for 2026," adding that "at most, Micron can only meet two-thirds of the medium-term memory requirements for some customers."
- WebProNews (Jul 2026): "**CEO Sanjay Mehrotra told investors the AI-driven memory shortfall is 'unprecedented.'** It will persist beyond 2026. That call came in January."
- Mobile World Live (FQ2 2026 call): Mehrotra on "the unprecedented gap" between supply and demand.
- aiweekly (Jun 24, 2026): Mehrotra said the company can fulfill "only between half and two-thirds" of customer demand, entire 2026 HBM supply sold out under multi-year contracts.
- BigGo Finance (Jun 24, 2026): entire 2026 HBM4 supply "sold out under long-term, fixed-price contracts"; can fulfill only 50–65% of key customers' medium-term demand.

### Claim 2 — ~$22B of customer prepayments (Q3 FY26 earnings, Jun 24, 2026) backing SCAs
**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**
- Zacks (Jun 25, 2026): 16 SCAs "backed by more than $22 billion in cash and related financial commitments, including nearly $18 billion in cash deposits" (CBO Sadana on Q3 call).
- BigGo Finance (Jun 24, 2026): "$22 billion in cash deposits and financial commitments from customers."
- aiweekly (Jun 24, 2026): "Micron has collected $22 billion in customer prepayments," used to justify raising capex target to >$25B.
- mitrade (Jun 2026): "financial commitments of $22 billion for these deals, with $18 billion in cash deposits."

### Claim 3 — FQ4 FY2026 guide ~$50B revenue at ~86% gross margin; FQ4 print scheduled ~Sept 30, 2026
**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**
- financefeeds: "In its June 24 release Micron guided revenue of $50.0 billion plus or minus $1.0 billion, gross margin of about 86%"; FQ4 report **Wednesday, September 30, 2026** (after close, 2:30 p.m. MT call; date announced in an Aug 26 press release); 14-week quarter closing 53-week FY2026.
- CoinCentral: "Micron itself guided for Q4 revenue of $50 billion, plus or minus $1 billion, with a non-GAAP gross margin near 86%"; reports Sept 30.
- stockmoguls / insiderfinance.io (Sept 2026): confirm $50B ± $1B guide, ~86% non-GAAP GM, Sept 30 print.

## MSFT (Microsoft)

### Claim 4 — Commercial RPO ~$678B, +84% YoY, ~$200B+ (~30%) converting to revenue in next 12 months (Q4 FY2026, Jul 29, 2026)
**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**
- Barchart (Jul 29 report, recapped Sept 2026): commercial RPO "soared 84% to $678 billion."
- Tickeron: RPO "jumped 84% year over year to $678 billion"; Azure +43% YoY in the same quarter.
- tradingnews: "Roughly 30% of the backlog will be recognized as revenue over the next 12 months... That implies more than $200 billion of already-contracted revenue landing in fiscal 2027"; weighted-average RPO duration 2.3 years (incl. OpenAI).
- tech-insider: Q4 FY2026 table — $678B RPO, +84% YoY; Azure 43%.

### Claim 5 — Azure +43% in the quarter, Q1 FY2027 guide ~45%; Nadella's "no warm shells to plug into" comment (demand > physical capacity)
**Verdict: CONFIRMED** — with one venue nuance on the quote.
- Azure +43% YoY in Q4 FY2026: confirmed by barchart, tickeron, alphastreet ("accelerating 43% YoY"), tech-insider.
- Q1 FY2027 Azure guide ~45% constant currency: ainvest (Sept 6, 2026) — "Microsoft guided Azure to grow 45% in constant currency"; ainvest (Aug 2026) — "Microsoft guides for 45% Azure growth in the first quarter of fiscal 2027."
- The "warm shells" quote is real and verbatim: Nadella on the **BG2Pod podcast (Nov 2025)** with Brad Gerstner, Bill Gurley and Sam Altman — "It's not a supply issue of chips. It's the fact that I don't have warm shells to plug into." (reported by Guardian via aiweekly; techtimes Sept 11, 2026). Nuance: it was a podcast interview, **not** a Q4 FY2026 earnings call; the dossier's phrasing implies it generically. Substance is corroborated independently — techtimes/Guardian report Microsoft turning away paying customers (incl. Temu) in 2025–26 because physical/power-ready shells, not chips, were the constraint; CFO Hood said capacity was "a little short, a little tight." The claim's interpretation (power/physical shells as binding constraint) is supported.

### Claim 6 — FY2027 capex of ~$255–260B planned
**Verdict: CONTRADICTED-or-nuanced.** Microsoft's official guided figure for FY2027 capex is ~**$175B**, not $255–260B.
- investinglive (post-Q4 call, Jul 2026): "Calendar 2026 capex will be approximately $175 billion and FY27 capex the same"; the lower number vs the earlier $190B is a lease-accounting reclassification, not a cut. Q1 capex >$50B.
- ad-hoc-news (Sept 2026): "guidance pointing to roughly USD 175 billion in spending across the year" per CFO Hood.
- nova.kapualabs: "Microsoft's fiscal-2027 capital-expenditure figure is approximately $175 billion... The flat $175 billion headline reflects reporting rather than reduced activity, since lease reclassification lowered reported capital expenditures from $190 billion to $175 billion."
- The $255–260B figure appears only in secondary commentary (ainvest, Sept 6, 2026: "For fiscal 2027, Microsoft guided capital expenditures to $255–$260 billion, up roughly 35% from the near-$190 billion it spent in fiscal 2026"; nova.kapualabs calls it a "larger physical narrative" tied to Azure demand). This is not Microsoft's stated guidance. The dossier claim should read **~$175B official FY2027 capex guide** (Hood, Q4 FY2026 call, Jul 29, 2026).
