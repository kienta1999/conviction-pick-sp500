## (a) SINGLE-PICK ballot

- Top pick: MU

- Thesis (3-5 sentences): MU has the fastest revenue acceleration in the entire 13-name set, with TTM revenue growth of 148% and an operating margin of 80.7% in the screen, capped by a fiscal Q3 2026 print of $41.5bn revenue at a record 84.9% non-GAAP gross margin. - Key shortage/backlog evidence: Micron had already completed price-and-volume agreements for its entire calendar 2026 HBM supply, including HBM4, as of December 2025 — and management was reported as saying it may meet only half to two-thirds of key customer demand in the medium term.
- Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk: medium — the memory category itself is irreplaceable (a hyperscaler cannot realistically build leading-edge DRAM/HBM in-house on a relevant timescale, needing fabs, EUV, packaging and years of yield learning), but Micron as a vendor is replaceable: it is #3 in HBM at about 18% share in Q2 2026 versus SK Hynix at about 50% and Samsung at about 33%, and Nvidia qualifies all three, so share can shift in a single quarter as Samsung's HBM4 ramp showed.
- 12-18 month return scenario: base — roughly +30% to +50%, driven by continued HBM/DRAM pricing strength and the analyst upside gap closing part-way as the shortage persists through 2027; bull — roughly +75% to +100%+, if HBM stays sold out into 2027, Micron closes the DRAM gap to #2 and holds near-80%+ gross margins, with the market re-rating the 4.26x forward P/E in the screen toward a normal growth multiple. These are rough scenario judgments anchored to the dossier's 76.5% analyst upside, not price targets from the dossier.
- Top risk: Cyclicality — memory has repeatedly mean-reverted, 80%+ gross margins are a duration bet not a given, and a July 2026 drawdown of about 28% peak-to-trough already showed how fast this reprices on plateauing hyperscaler capex, efficiency gains reducing memory-per-GPU, or CXMT share gains in commodity DRAM.
