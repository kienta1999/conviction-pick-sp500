## (a) SINGLE-PICK ballot

- Top pick: NVDA
- Runner-up: MU
- Thesis (3-5 sentences): Nvidia is rationing by capacity, not by price — CEO Jensen Huang said on the Q2 FY2027 call that "Blackwell sales are off the charts, and cloud GPUs are sold out" and that "capacity is the constraint". That constraint is now downstream in TSMC CoWoS packaging, substrates and interposers, and Nvidia is locking in multi-year capacity deals targeting >300% more interposer wafer capacity by end-2028, which extends visibility well beyond a single quarter. With an estimated 80–90% AI accelerator share by revenue and the CUDA/NVLink full-stack lock-in, Nvidia — not its customers — controls allocation of the scarcest AI input. The explosive case is therefore a multi-year, capacity-gated revenue ramp, not a one-quarter beat.
- Key shortage/backlog evidence: CFO Colette Kress's statement on the August 2026 call of "visibility to $0.5 trillion in Blackwell and Rubin revenue from the start of this year through the end of calendar year 2026" — the largest forward-visibility figure in the dossier, even though the dossier notes it is visibility, not a formal backlog figure.
- Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk: medium — and why: low in general-purpose training today (CUDA, NVLink, full-stack systems and priority CoWoS allocation; dossier irreplaceability 9/10 for training), but medium and rising overall because inference is far less CUDA-dependent and Google TPU, Amazon Trainium/Inferentia, Microsoft Maia, Meta MTIA and AMD Instinct are all gaining, with the dossier scoring irreplaceability falling toward ~7/10 over time as inference grows.
- 12-18 month return scenario: rough base / bull case: Base — Blackwell/Rubin shipments convert the $0.5 trillion visibility and the $279 billion of supply/purchase commitments (as of 26 July 2026, mostly FY2027–FY2029) into sustained growth, with the share moving roughly toward the screen's analyst mean target of $328.95 versus the $204.64 screen price (screen upside 60.7%). Bull — Rubin ramps on schedule, the interposer capacity build lands, and Vera Rubin demand from deals such as the Anthropic–Nscale $44.6 billion six-year deal pushes revenue and pricing beyond that target path; the dossier gives no separate bull-case price target, so no higher figure is asserted here.
- Top risk: The financing loop behind the visibility — accounts receivable rose $22.3 billion in one quarter to $63.1 billion (DSO stretched from 45 to ~60 days), five direct customers were 70% of receivables, and the dossier cites up to $105 billion of off-balance-sheet lease/power guarantees; if a concentrated, debt-funded customer falters, booked visibility becomes credit exposure rather than revenue.

### Top 3 in detail

**1. NVDA —** The single most compelling data point is the $0.5 trillion Blackwell/Rubin revenue visibility through end-calendar 2026, backed by $279 billion of supply/purchase commitments (up from $119 billion one quarter earlier) and $15.6 billion of customer advances in H1 FY2027. No other name in the dossier pairs a sold-out product with a multi-year, customer-funded commitment book of this scale. Rough 12–18 month scenario: base — steady conversion of that visibility toward the $328.95 analyst mean target (60.7% screen upside from $204.64); bull — Rubin plus the >300% interposer capacity expansion by end-2028 keeps Nvidia allocation-constrained into 2027 and revenue runs ahead of the target path (no separate bull price is given in the dossier).

**2. MU —** The single most compelling data point is that Micron had already completed price-and-volume agreements for its entire calendar 2026 HBM supply, including HBM4, as of December 2025, while management was reported as saying it may meet only "half to two-thirds of key customer demand in the medium term" — a textbook sold-out rationing signal, reinforced by Samsung's memory chief flagging shortages continuing "through at least 2027". The offset, judged through this lens, is vendor substitutability: Micron is #3 in HBM (~18% Q2 2026 share, down from 21% in Q1) behind SK Hynix (~50%) and Samsung (~33%), so the shortage is a category tailwind, not a Micron-only moat. Rough 12–18 month scenario: base — HBM4 volume for Nvidia's Vera Rubin platform plus sustained DRAM pricing carries the share toward the $1,561.91 analyst mean target (76.5% screen upside from $884.74); bull — the shortage extends through 2027 as Samsung's chief flagged, HBM share stabilises or recovers, and pricing stays at Q2 2026's sharply higher levels (no separate bull price is given in the dossier).

**3. APH —** The single most compelling data point is Q3 2026 orders of a record $12.17 billion, +99% year on year and +13% sequentially, for a 1.19:1 book-to-bill — orders exceeded $10.23 billion of quarterly sales by ~$1.94 billion in a single quarter, with IT Datacom up 63% organically and now roughly four times its size of two years ago. Unlike a price-only surge, this is unit/content growth: AI racks require rising interconnect content across copper, optics and power as computational intensity rises. Rough 12–18 month scenario: base — the order book covers well into 2027 at current run rates and the share moves toward the $105.00 analyst mean target (32.4% screen upside from $82.08); bull — CommScope CCS optics (now guided to $5.1 billion 2026 sales) plus continued AI interconnect content gains keep book-to-bill above 1 and growth above the FY2026 guidance of $36.9–$37.1 billion in sales (no separate bull price is given in the dossier).

Their moats are strong (dossier irreplaceability 8/10, 8/10 and 9/10 respectively), but this lens weights shortage/backlog and revenue visibility above moat alone, so they do not displace the ten names above.
