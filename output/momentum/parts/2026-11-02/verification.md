## CDNS (Cadence Design Systems)

### 1. Record $8.2bn backlog at 30 Sep 2026, of which $4.2bn of remaining performance obligations is expected as revenue in the next 12 months.
**Verdict: CONFIRMED**

- Cadence Q3 2026 results, reported 26 Oct 2026: "Quarter-end backlog was $8.2 billion and revenue expected to be recognized in the next 12 months from remaining performance obligations was $4.2 billion." Q3 revenue was $1.633bn vs $1.339bn a year earlier.
- Corroborated by Zacks, 27 Oct 2026: "Backlog reached a record $8.2 billion, including $4.2 billion of remaining performance obligations expected to convert into revenues over the next 12 months."
- Sources: Cadence Q3 2026 press release as reproduced 26 Oct 2026 (https://investingnews.com/cadence-reports-third-quarter-2026-financial-results/); Zacks, 27 Oct 2026 (https://www.zacks.com/stock/news/3016087/cadence-q3-earnings-call-highlights-ai-expansion-and-higher-outlook).

### 2. FY2026 revenue guidance raised to $6.387–$6.417bn (about 21% growth) after a Q3 2026 beat-and-raise.
**Verdict: CONFIRMED**

- Q3 2026: revenue $1.633bn (+22% YoY) beat consensus ~$1.61bn; non-GAAP EPS $2.11 beat ~$2.04–$2.05. CFO John Wall raised FY2026 guidance to $6.387–$6.417bn, "reflecting approximately 21% year-over-year growth at the midpoint," up from $6.260–$6.340bn. This was described as the third raise in 2026.
- Sources: Motley Fool Q3 2026 earnings-call transcript, 26 Oct 2026 (https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/10/26/cadence-cdns-q3-2026-earnings-call-transcript/); Zacks, 27 Oct 2026 (https://www.zacks.com/stock/news/3016297/cadence-q3-earnings-beat-estimates-on-ai-demand-outlook-raised).

### 3. EDA duopoly with Synopsys: Cadence about 36% share vs Synopsys about 38%, near-100% retention for the duopoly.
**Verdict: UNVERIFIED (as a precise, primary-sourced claim)**

- The exact figures do appear verbatim in one secondary source: Arvy, mid/late Oct 2026 ("Cadence & Synopsys: The duopoly that never loses a client"): "The EDA market is dominated by two giants controlling 74% of it: Synopsys holds a 38% share, and Cadence 36%. It's a duopoly. This dominance is backed by near-100% retention rates and an 80–85% recurring revenue model."
- That is NOT a primary source, and other reputable estimates conflict materially depending on market definition (EDA-only vs EDA+IP vs EDA+simulation): e.g. SemiAnalysis EDA Market Primer (May 2026) puts the Big-3 (Synopsys, Cadence, Siemens EDA) at over 85% combined, with Synopsys $8bn CY2025 revenue (incl. Ansys) and Cadence $5.30bn — a different implied split; other market-research summaries give roughly 30%/30%, 35–37%/26–27%, or ~20%/18%. No Cadence or Synopsys filing or earnings call found in this check discloses a 36%/38% share or a near-100% retention rate.
- Directionally, duopoly dominance and very high retention / recurring revenue are widely supported; the precise 36%/38% split and the near-100% retention figure are not primary-confirmed and should not be cited as filing-grade facts.
- Sources checked: Arvy (https://arvy.ch/en/cadence-and-synopsys-the-duopoly-that-never-loses-a-client/); SemiAnalysis EDA Market Primer (https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/eda-market-primer); Dataintelo / TalkMarkets summaries giving divergent splits.

## NVDA (Nvidia)

### 4. CFO visibility to about $0.5 trillion in Blackwell and Rubin revenue from start of 2026 through end of calendar 2026.
**Verdict: CONTRADICTED on the stated window; amount and end-date CONFIRMED for the correct window**

- The $0.5tn figure itself is real CFO language. On the Q3 FY2026 earnings call, 19 Nov 2025, CFO Colette Kress said: "We currently have visibility to a half a trillion dollars in Blackwell and Rubin revenue, from the start of this year through the end of calendar year 2026." "This year" on that call was 2025, not 2026.
- The same 2025–2026 window is confirmed by later reporting: CNBC via Webull, 6 Jan 2026 (Kress: the $500bn visibility "has definitely gotten larger" since GTC in October, with Rubin orders already being placed); and a March 2026 summary noting the $500bn was "for 2025–2026" (about $184bn in 2025 + ~$320bn in 2026).
- Correction for the panel: the window is start of calendar 2025 through end of calendar 2026, not start of 2026. Note also that by March 2026 Nvidia had superseded this with visibility to over $1tn in Blackwell + Rubin revenue from CY2025 through CY2027, so the $500bn figure is stale as a current (Nov 2026) visibility statement.
- Sources: Nvidia Q3 FY2026 earnings-call transcript, 19 Nov 2025 (https://s201.q4cdn.com/141608511/files/doc_financials/2026/q3/NVDA-Q3-2026-Earnings-Call-19-November-2025-5_00-PM-ET.pdf); Data Center Dynamics earnings report (https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/nvidia-reports-blowout-earnings-ai-data-center-boom-continues-despite-bubble-fears/).

### 5. About $279bn of supply/purchase commitments at 26 July 2026.
**Verdict: CONFIRMED**

- Nvidia's 10-Q for the quarter ended 26 July 2026 (filed 26 Aug 2026), Note 10, lists "Supply and capacity" future commitments totalling $279bn (remainder of FY2027 $92bn, 2028 $87bn, 2029 $88bn, 2030 $6bn, 2031 $5bn, thereafter $1bn), and states supply commitments rose "from $119 billion last quarter to $279 billion as of July 26, 2026," primarily for memory and manufacturing facilities. Total future commitments across all categories were $366bn; the $279bn is the supply-and-capacity line only.
- Caveat from the filing itself: some supplier agreements may be cancellable, rescheduled or adjustable before firm orders are placed, sometimes at additional cost — so $279bn is commitments, not irrevocable purchases.
- Sources: Nvidia 10-Q for period ended 26 July 2026, Note 10 (via SEC filing copy); FourWeekMBA summary of Note 10 (https://fourweekmba.com/ai-nvidia-10-q-lists-530b-in-commitments-and-guarantees/).

### 6. Cloud GPUs sold out / capacity is the constraint (CEO language).
**Verdict: CONFIRMED (as CEO language, not as a quantified capacity figure)**

- On the Q3 FY2026 earnings call / reporting of it (Nov 2025), CEO Jensen Huang said "Blackwell sales are off the charts, and cloud GPUs are sold out" (Data Center Dynamics summary of the Nov 2025 earnings).
- More recently, at GTC Berlin on 22 Oct 2026, Huang told CNBC neocloud demand is "off the charts" and operators were described as "completely sold out"; on the August 2026 earnings call management said growth guidance was limited by supply while customer forecasts pointed higher (reported Oct 2026).
- Note the nuance: by Oct 2026 Huang was also framing the binding constraint for neoclouds as working capital / financing, not only silicon supply. The "sold out" language itself is confirmed; it does not quantify how much capacity is short.
- Sources: Data Center Dynamics (https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/nvidia-reports-blowout-earnings-ai-data-center-boom-continues-despite-bubble-fears/); Tesla News Wire, 22 Oct 2026 (https://teslanewswire.com/articles/jensen-huang-neocloud-demand-off-the-charts-nvidia-chips-financeable-asset-gtc-berlin-oct-22-2026).
