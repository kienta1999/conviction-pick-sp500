# PANELIST A — Supply-chain analyst — Momentum Panel, RUN_DATE 2026-10-25

## SINGLE-PICK BALLOT

- **Top pick:** MU
- **Runner-up:** SNDK
- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** At the frozen $877.55, Micron is the only name where a three-way shortage — HBM, conventional DRAM and NAND — has been converted into contracted, deposit-backed revenue rather than spot pricing hope. HBM is sold out for 2026 and 2027 with 75% of fiscal 2027 shipments already committed, while hyperscalers pre-finance the fab build that cannot add bits until late 2028. That gives multi-year earnings visibility rare for memory, and if shortage pricing holds toward 2028 the forward multiple at this price still capitalises a rollover that the contracts argue against. SNDK at $1238.03 is purer and cheaper on the same logic, but it is #5 in NAND and easier for customers to leave at renewal.
- **Key shortage/backlog evidence:** ~$150 billion of remaining performance obligations from 26 take-or-pay Strategic Customer Agreements covering >35% of revenue through 2030, backed by ~$32 billion in customer financial commitments, disclosed 30 Sept 2026.
- **Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk:** medium — hyperscalers cannot in-source a leading-edge HBM/DRAM fab (decade-scale, deep process IP, per-platform HBM4E co-design with Nvidia creates design-in lock-in), but they can reallocate share among the only three suppliers, Micron lost HBM share 21% to 18% in 2Q26, and CXMT threatens conventional DRAM where recent margin gains came from.
- **12-18 month return scenario:** Base +60% to +80% toward ~$1,400–$1,562 (screen target mean $1561.91) if SCA pricing and sold-out HBM hold; bull +110% to +130% toward ~$1,850–$2,020 if HBM share converges to its 20–25% DRAM share and the shortage extends toward 2030 as SK hynix has warned.
- **Top risk:** Cyclicality repriced late — DRAM ASP momentum is already moderating and new Micron capacity arrives from late 2028, precisely when today's shortage may have ended; a hyperscaler capex digestion or CXMT conventional-DRAM break hits earnings faster than the multiple can expand.
- **Conviction (1-10):** 9

**Top 3 — shortage/backlog detail and scenarios:**

**1. MU ($877.55):** The most compelling point is the contract stack itself — ~$150B RPO at minimum prices across 26 take-or-pay SCAs, ~$32B in customer commitments (mostly cash deposits, $12.3B arriving in fiscal Q4 alone), HBM sold out through 2027 and no line of sight to supply-demand balance per COO Bhatia. Base 12-18mo: +60% to +80% toward the $1561.91 target mean; bull: +110% to +130% if HBM4/HBM4E share recovers and DRAM/NAND stay tight simultaneously.

**2. SNDK ($1238.03):** All 2026 capacity is pre-sold, bits are on allocation beyond calendar 2027, and the New Business Models programme locks a $93.9 billion minimum at floor pricing across 8 customers over a weighted-average >4 years — 4.6× FY2026 revenue of $20.25B, backed by $16.5B in deposits/guarantees, covering >50% of FY2027 bits and ~two-thirds of FY2028 bits. Base 12-18mo: +55% to +75% toward ~$1,920–$2,173 (target mean $2173.0); bull: +90% to +110% on a retest/extension of shortage pricing, capped in part by the NBM floor-and-ceiling structure and by customers' ability to shift NAND share to Samsung, SK hynix or Micron at renewal — the disintermediation-by-substitution risk that keeps it behind MU.

**3. FCX ($65.18):** FCX has no order book, but its backlog is geological — ~25 years of reserves against an IEA-flagged ~30% copper supply shortfall by 2035, a ~330kt refined deficit projected for 2026, 16–17-year discovery-to-production lead times, and price-inelastic data-centre demand where copper is <0.5% of project cost; every $0.10/lb is ~$390M of annual EBITDA, with realised prices guided above $6.50/lb for Q3 2026 and 2027 volumes guided up >20% as Grasberg recovers. Base 12-18mo: +20% to +35% toward ~$78–$88 (target mean $79.20455); bull: +55% to +75% if Grasberg reaches near-full rates by end-2027 into a widening deficit. No customer can in-source an ore body — the risk is commodity cyclicality and Grasberg execution, not disintermediation.

**Deliberately left out of top 10:** ANET, META, VRTX and FANG — ANET discloses no customer backlog and is a victim, not seller, of the component shortage (its $9.7B is its own purchase commitments); META has no backlog and is itself the in-sourcer (MTIA/Broadcom) destroying merchant-silicon demand; VRTX (shortage 4/10) is a CF monopoly annuity, not a shortage, with growth dependent on a back-loaded non-CF ramp; FANG has no backlog, sells a fungible barrel, and shale supply — FANG itself via DUCs — can respond within quarters, so its tightness is a squeeze, not a structural shortage.
