# Panelist C — Quality / Moat & Irreplaceability — Momentum Ballot, 2026-10-25

Lens: category dominance, margins, returns on capital, balance-sheet durability, and above all irreplaceability. I reject any name whose customers — especially hyperscalers — could realistically build or in-source the product, or that a substitute technology could route around. Frozen prices cited are from the screen only.

## SINGLE-PICK BALLOT

- **Top pick:** LLY
- **Runner-up:** ISRG
- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** At $1273.46, Lilly is the only name in the 14 that pairs a 9/10 shortage with a 9/10 irreplaceability score, category dominance, and elite quality economics — 60.9% of U.S. incretin prescriptions, 54% operating margin and 83% gross margin on the screen, with revenue growing ~30% TTM. No patient, payer or PBM can in-source a patented dual GIP/GLP-1 molecule, its KwikPen delivery, or the API capacity behind it, and no substitute can route around tirzepatide's clinical superiority data, which is actively taking share from Novo Nordisk. The moat then compounds: the ~$55B manufacturing build, Foundayo's oral entry (~one-third of new oral GLP-1 starts already), and retatrutide (triple-agonist, up to 20.8% weight loss, FDA filing planned Q1 2027) extend the franchise well past the current injectables. This is explosive upside carried by a moat nobody can copy, not by a commodity price spike that can mean-revert.
- **Key shortage/backlog evidence:** Mounjaro + Zepbound generated $27.7B in H1 2026, up ~88% YoY and ~65% of total revenue, while only 11% of U.S. adults were taking a GLP-1 for weight management (Gallup, June 2026) against a market projected toward ~$100B by 2030 — demand still outrunning a $55B capacity build, with FY2026 guidance raised to $85–87B.
- **Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk:** low — patients and payers cannot build a patented molecule, sterile injectable capacity, or Lilly's clinical/PBM position themselves; the only bypass is a rival molecule (Novo, Amgen), which is competition, not in-sourcing, and Lilly is currently winning that contest on share and data.
- **12-18 month return scenario:** base +25–35% (to roughly $1,590–$1,720) on volume-led GLP-1 growth and Foundayo ramp at falling realised prices; bull +50–60% (to roughly $1,910–$2,040) if retatrutide filing, Lebanon capacity ramp and continued Novo share gains re-rate the duration of the franchise.
- **Top risk:** Concentration and valuation — ~65% of revenue rides on one molecule, realised prices are guided down low-to-mid-teens, and at $1273.46 (analyst target only +4.6% on the screen) a merely-good Q3 on 2026-10-29 can de-rate it hard.
- **Conviction (1-10):** 9

### Top 3 — moat / shortage deep dive

**1. LLY ($1273.46).** The single most compelling data point is the combination of 60.9% U.S. incretin prescription share with H1 2026 Mounjaro/Zepbound sales of $27.7B (+88% YoY) against only 11% GLP-1 penetration of U.S. adults — a category leader still supply-building ($55B programme, Lebanon ramp, $6.5B Houston plant) into unmet demand, protected by patents no customer can work around. 12-18mo scenario: base +25–35%, bull +50–60% (see single-pick ballot above).

**2. ISRG ($452.29).** The moat data point is the installed base itself: 12,094 da Vinci systems (+12% YoY, including 2,023 da Vinci 5) plus 1,152 Ion systems, >20M cumulative procedures, and ~81–85% recurring revenue from proprietary instruments and service — a hospital cannot swap platforms without retraining its surgeons and re-credentialing, and it certainly cannot in-source a surgical robot, while robotic surgery is still only ~5% of procedures worldwide. 12-18mo scenario: base +15–25% (to roughly $520–$565) on 14–15% procedure growth and da Vinci 5 placements (+28% in Q3 2026); bull +40–50% (to roughly $633–$678) if U.S. procedure growth re-accelerates off Q3's +10% and the guided 3–5% instrument revenue-per-procedure cut from 2027 is more than offset by volume.

**3. NVDA ($210.96).** The compelling data point is order visibility at system scale — ~$500B of Blackwell + Rubin revenue visibility through end-2026 and ~$1T of purchase orders through 2027 (management statements, GTC March 2026) — sitting on CUDA, NVLink and an annual architecture cadence that frontier training still cannot leave, at 66% operating margin on the screen. I rank it third, not first, because the in-sourcing trap is real here: all four top customers ship their own inference silicon (TPU, Trainium, Maia, MTIA), which caps share of wallet even though it does not displace Nvidia in training or the merchant market. 12-18mo scenario: base +35–45% (to roughly $285–$305) on the guided ~70% FY2028 revenue growth; bull +60–75% (to roughly $338–$369) if Rubin supply (CoWoS/HBM4) loosens and inference share holds better than the feared fall toward 20–30%.

**Deliberately left out of my top 10:** SNDK (irreplaceability 5/10 — #5 NAND share at ~11.4%, an interchangeable standards-based input hyperscalers can multi-source and leave at renewal, despite its $93.9B contracted floor), CSCO (4/10 — #3 in AI data-centre switching behind Nvidia and Arista, bypassable by Spectrum-X bundling and white-box/SONiC), META (3/10 shortage, 5/10 blended irreplaceability — it sells no scarce product, its AI infrastructure is commodity capacity, and it is itself the in-sourcer via MTIA destroying merchant theses), and FANG (5/10 — a fungible barrel with zero customer lock-in or pricing power; Tier-1 acreage is scarce but the product is the most substitutable on the list).
