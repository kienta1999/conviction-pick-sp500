## Macro context — crude oil (applies to EOG and COP)

- IEA Oil Market Report, published Nov 13, 2026: global oil supply rose 2.3 mb/d in October to 105.4 mb/d, surpassing demand for the first time since the Iran war began. The IEA now expects a Q4 2026 surplus of ~1.7–2.0 mb/d (vs a deficit of similar size projected a month earlier) and a 2027 surplus of ~3.8 mb/d (2027 supply 108.1 mb/d vs demand ~104.3 mb/d). 2026 demand is forecast to fall 2.7 mb/d to 102.3 mb/d.
- Caveats inside the same report: Gulf exports were still 26% below pre-war levels, on-land stocks were at their lowest since at least 2016 (most of October's build sat aboard ships), and diesel remains the IEA's "overriding concern" — European diesel ~77% above where it started the year. The market is loose on paper, tight in the tanks.
- OPEC+: the seven voluntary-cut countries held December production at September levels (statement Nov 1); the full OPEC+ ministerial meets Nov 29, 2026 to set the 2027 framework, with a capacity review under way that will set 2027 baselines. Iraq is already pumping above target, at its highest output since 2019.
- Bottom line for the panel: there is NO crude-supply shortage. Both EOG and COP are riding a war-driven price spike (realized prices +30%+ YoY), not a structural supply deficit, and the IEA now calls a surplus. Any momentum case for either name is a price/cost/return-of-capital case, not a shortage case. The Nov 29 OPEC+ decision is the binary event: deeper cuts support price; a let-it-ride outcome pressures it.

## EOG — EOG Resources — $154.91, fwd PE 9.1x, ret 12m +44.8%, target $168.27 (+8.6%)

### 1. Shortage thesis
No structural shortage in EOG's core product. Crude is moving into surplus per the IEA (see above). EOG's own management guides only low-single-digit oil growth in 2027 "if current oil supply-demand fundamentals persist" — i.e. it is deliberately not chasing volume into a surplus. The genuine tightness angle is in EOG's gas business, not oil: US LNG export demand is growing, and EOG is positioning gas volumes into premium international pricing (see §5). But gas is ~12–15% of revenue; oil/condensate is ~68–72%. The shortage thesis for EOG as a whole does NOT hold — it is a price-taker in a loosening crude market.

### 2. Backlog / order book
No order book — EOG is a commodity producer selling into spot/contract crude, NGL and gas markets. Substitute metrics:
- Production: record 1,433.1 MBoe/d in Q3 2026 (+10% YoY); oil 550.4 MBbl/d, NGL 357.1 MBbl/d, gas 3,153 MMcf/d — all above guidance midpoints (Q3 2026 earnings release, Nov 5, 2026).
- Realized prices Q3: crude $86.72/bbl (vs $65.95 a year ago), NGL $26.13, natural gas $3.74/Mcf (vs $2.80). The YoY earnings surge is price-driven, not volume-driven (oil volumes only +3% YoY).
- Proved reserves 5,514 MMBoe at YE2025 (+16% YoY); 4.67M net acres; ~$50 WTI breakeven; plan funds growth + dividend at WTI below $50/bbl.
- No inventory/utilization figure reported for Q3; the Janus gas plant ran above 99% utilization YTD (see §5).

### 3. Category position
One of the largest independent US E&Ps; market cap ~$78.7B at ~$152 (Nov 10, 2026) — #2 independent behind ConocoPhillips (~$147–165B), ahead of Diamondback (~$56.5B) and Devon (~$54.4B) (May 2026 market caps). Was the largest Lower 48 oil producer in 2013–14. Not #1 in any single basin by production, but arguably the best-run large-cap independent: lowest-cost large US independent, 28-year unbroken dividend record. Multi-basin footprint (Delaware 395k acres, Eagle Ford/Dorado 1,313k, Utica 1,736k post-Encino, Rockies 748k) vs single-basin pure plays.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk
Moat: low-cost premium-acreage position + operating efficiency, not a technology lock-in. Customers are refiners and gas buyers on open markets — nobody needs EOG specifically; crude is fungible. No customer can "in-source" oil production without becoming an E&P itself, but substitution threats are real and structural: EVs/energy transition cap long-run oil demand, and the IEA's 2026 demand decline (−2.7 mb/d) is the live version of that threat. The durable edge is cost: sub-$50 WTI breakeven and $10.49/Boe cash operating costs (below $10.70 guidance midpoint) mean EOG survives price troughs that kill higher-cost shale. **Irreplaceability score: 3/10** — excellent operator of a fully replicable, fungible commodity; the moat is cost position and inventory quality, which erodes as Tier-1 Delaware acreage is harvested (per-well productivity fell in 2025; Delaware Tier-1 thinning is a flagged concern).

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- Q3 2026 beat (Nov 5–6, 2026): adjusted EPS $4.87 vs $4.45 consensus (+9.4%), revenue $8.55B (+46% YoY) vs $7.54B consensus; GAAP net income $2.78B (+89% YoY); FCF $2.2B (+57% YoY).
- Dividend raised 8% to indicated $4.40/share for 2027 (quarterly $1.10, payable Jan 29, 2027); >$1.5B returned in Q3 ($540M dividends + $1B buybacks); ≥70% of 2026 FCF to be returned.
- ENGIE deal (announced on Q3 call, Nov 6): new 10-year gas supply agreement, 140,000 MMBtu/d linked to JKM pricing, starting by 2030, lifting premium-priced LNG feed-gas exposure to >1 Bcf/d by 2030. Also in talks with data centers/hyperscalers on gas supply.
- Janus gas processing plant expansion approved: $300M, doubling Delaware Basin capacity to 600 MMcf/d, completion H2 2028 (existing plant >99% utilized YTD, netback uplift >$0.65/Mcf).
- FY2026 guidance unchanged: 5% oil growth, 14% total production growth, $6.5B capex. No analyst upgrade found in the last ~3 months (no upgrade reported in searches; not found).

### 6. Risks
- Crude surplus: IEA Q4 surplus ~2 mb/d and 2027 surplus ~3.8 mb/d; if OPEC+ does not cut on Nov 29, realized prices (the entire earnings story — oil +3% volume vs +32% price) roll over. Q4 production guidance already moderates (midpoint 1,408.8 MBoe/d, −1.7% vs Q3).
- Tier-1 inventory depletion in the Delaware; Encino/Utica acquisition pushed EOG into secondary Utica zones.
- Gas pivot is small and late-dated: ENGIE volumes start 2030; Janus completes H2 2028.
- Valuation: +44.8% in 12m on a price spike; fwd PE 9.1x looks cheap only if $86 oil persists.
- Takeover-target status (last standalone prize among large independents) is a double-edged catalyst, not a fundamental.

### 7. Shortage score: 2/10
**Verdict:** A superbly run, low-cost shale producer riding a war-inflated oil price into an IEA-called surplus — no shortage, no order book, no moat beyond cost; the momentum is price momentum, and it inverts the moment crude does.

## COP — ConocoPhillips — $139.48, fwd PE 12.1x, ret 12m +58.5%, target $152.97 (+9.7%)

### 1. Shortage thesis
No structural shortage — same IEA surplus picture as EOG, and COP is even more exposed to it: COP's own Q3 production FELL 2% YoY on a pro-forma basis (2,302 MBoe/d vs 2,399 a year ago) because Middle East conflict effects on Qatar offset Lower 48 organic growth. The war that lifted prices is simultaneously suppressing COP's volumes. The one genuine tightness angle is diesel/distillates (IEA's "overriding concern") and LNG: COP's 13 mtpa LNG offtake portfolio stands to benefit from tight global gas/LNG balances, but LNG is a minority of the business and first material volumes from Port Arthur Phase 1 / North Field East only arrive in 2027. No crude shortage angle.

### 2. Backlog / order book
No order book — commodity producer. Substitute metrics:
- Production Q3 2026: 2,302 MBoe/d (Lower 48 1,517 MBoe/d: Delaware 757, Midland 197, Eagle Ford 369, Bakken 191); Q4 guided 2,320–2,360 MBoe/d; FY2026 ~2.3 MMBoe/d (Q3 2026 release, Nov 5, 2026).
- Realized price $61.40/Boe, +32% YoY — again, the earnings story is price, not volume (volumes down, price up).
- Qatar production "remains below pre-conflict levels" (Q3 call, Nov 5) though up sequentially vs Q2; Q4 guidance explicitly bakes in Qatar uncertainty.
- Proved reserves 7.6 Bboe at YE2025; market cap ~$164B (early Sep 2026).
- Willow (Alaska): module fabrication >75% complete, first oil early 2029, ~180,000 bbl/d peak. North Field East first gas H1 2027 (per QatarEnergy).

### 3. Category position
Yes — the world's largest independent E&P by production (~2.3 MMBoe/d) and reserves, ~$147–165B market cap, roughly 2x EOG and ~3x Diamondback. Largest oil producer in Alaska (Prudhoe Bay, Kuparuk, Western North Slope; 199 MBOED Alaska production, 1.7M net acres). One of the largest Permian producers (Lower 48 1.5 MMBoe/d). Scale advantage is real but it is scale in a commodity, not category dominance with pricing power — COP is a price-taker like everyone else.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk
Moat: scale, low cost of supply, portfolio diversity (14 countries, Alaska conventional + Lower 48 shale + LNG + Canada oil sands), and a long-dated project pipeline (Willow, Port Arthur LNG 30% stake, North Field East/South). No customer lock-in — crude and LNG are sold into global markets; no certification moat. Substitution threat is the same as EOG's (energy transition, EVs) plus COP-specific geopolitical concentration: Qatar is a live, unresolved volume drag, and a $5.2B environmental fine in Kazakhstan is under legal challenge. **Irreplaceability score: 3/10** — largest independent, but its product is fungible and its customers can buy the same barrel from anyone; the moat is cost, scale and project pipeline, not irreplaceability.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- Q3 2026 beat (Nov 5, 2026): adjusted EPS $3.27 vs ~$2.97–3.05 consensus (+7–10%), net income $4.0B (+142% YoY), revenue $20.1B vs $18.6B expected; CFO $7.2B, FCF ~$4.0B after $3.1B capex.
- Dividend raised 11% to $0.93/quarter (payable Dec 1, 2026); $3.4B returned in Q3 ($2.4B buybacks + $1.0B dividends); buybacks to rise in Q4 toward 45% of CFO.
- Preliminary 2027 framework: modestly lower capex, low-single-digit underlying production growth, lower unit costs, ~45% of CFO returned. Long-term target: +$7B FCF by 2029 at $70 WTI (~doubling 2025 FCF), with reinvestment rate falling well below 50%.
- Operational: record Permian completion efficiency (+15% pumping hours YoY); Surmont Pad 104W-B first production ahead of schedule and under budget; LNG offtake expanded to 13 mtpa.
- No specific analyst upgrade found in the last ~3 months (not found); consensus target ~$153–156.

### 6. Risks
- The IEA surplus is the central risk: COP's +58.5% 12m return and +142% earnings growth are price effects on FLAT-TO-DOWN volumes. If crude reverts toward the surplus-implied level, earnings fall faster than EOG's (COP has higher absolute production and more international exposure).
- Qatar: production still below pre-conflict levels with a wide range of Q4 outcomes; Strait of Hormuz flow risk cuts both ways (price up, COP's own volumes down).
- Willow is $8.5–9B of capital with first oil in 2029 — no near-term cash contribution; execution risk in the Arctic.
- Kazakhstan $5.2B fine overhang; Surmont royalty drag on Canadian volumes.
- Valuation already prices the price spike: fwd PE 12.1x is the highest of the energy names here, and the 12m return (+58.5%) is the largest in the batch.

### 7. Shortage score: 2/10
**Verdict:** The biggest independent in the world, but the biggest price-taker too — record cash returns built on $61/Boe realizations into an IEA surplus, with its own Qatar volumes still impaired by the war that lifted the price; momentum with no shortage underneath.

## CF — CF Industries — $119.34, fwd PE 10.1x, ret 12m +43.1%, target $130.08 (+9.0%)

### 1. Shortage thesis — RE-CHECKED, figures still hold
Yes — this is the one genuine shortage in the batch, and the figures from yesterday's panel check were confirmed on CF's own Q3 2026 earnings call (Nov 5, 2026, transcript via Nasdaq/Motley Fool):
- **~9M metric tons of urea supply lost since hostilities began in the Middle East in late February 2026** — CEO Chris Bohn / CCO Bert Frost, Q3 call. Middle East urea exports running ~800,000 t/month vs a normal 1.5–1.7M t/month — roughly halved. Management said normalization would take 4–6 months even if hostilities ended immediately.
- **>35% of European ammonia capacity curtailed** on high natural gas costs — same call; trade press (AgNavigator, Nov 5) puts it at 37% offline or at reduced rates in October. Management now expects some European shutdowns 12–18 months earlier than previously expected (~2030 → ~2028–29) because extended curtailments raise maintenance/restart/fixed-cost burdens.
- **Trinidad: 35% of ammonia capacity permanently shutting down** (AgNavigator, Nov 5; Nutrien's Trinidad nitrogen facility shut indefinitely).
- **CF's own utilization: 97%** of available ammonia capacity YTD through Q3 (7.2M tons produced in 9M 2026; ~9.5M tons expected for FY2026). Fall ammonia program participation "very strong," volumes potentially among the highest in 10 years. UAN shipments nearly 2M tons in Q3 — highest quarterly UAN volume this decade.
- Inventories low across North America, South America, Europe and Southeast Asia; India to import >10M mt of urea in 2026; Chinese urea exports filling only part of the gap (export authorizations ~9M mt vs ~5M mt last year — still not loosening the balance).
- Management: "nitrogen capacity currently under construction is not sufficient to meet global nitrogen demand growth and expected permanent shutdowns through 2030"; pricing expected on a "positive trajectory through at least H1 2027."
- One nuance: the shortage is uneven by product — Q3 showed stronger ammonia economics but weaker urea/UAN realizations (FactorsToday, Nov 6), so the "scarcity narrative" is not uniform across the product slate.

### 2. Backlog / order book
No formal order book (commodity fertilizer sold via fill programs and seasonal contracts), but the closest equivalents are strong and current:
- Fall ammonia fill program: participation "very strong"; weather permitting, fall volumes among the highest in 10 years (Q3 call, Nov 5).
- UAN fill program drove record Q3 UAN shipments (~2M tons).
- Low-carbon ammonia: ~10% of H1 2026 volumes sold as low-carbon at an average premium of >$20/ton — a contracted, premium-priced book that is growing.
- Forward demand signal: US corn acreage 95–96M acres for the 2027 planting season (management, Q3 call).
- Utilization 97% is itself the backlog proxy: the plants are effectively sold out.

### 3. Category position
Yes — #1. World's largest producer of anhydrous ammonia and North America's largest pure-play nitrogen manufacturer. Controls ~40% of North American ammonia capacity, ~41% of granular urea, ~44% of UAN, ~19% of ammonium nitrate (company/industry data, 2025). In the broader US agricultural macronutrients market, CF leads with 16–19% share vs Nutrien 14–17% and Koch 11–14% (BIS Research, 2025). Donaldsonville alone is ~40% of its ammonia capacity. Scale + lowest-cost North American gas feedstock is the position.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk
Moat: (a) lowest-cost position on the global nitrogen cost curve — cheap Henry Hub gas vs European producers paying LNG-linked prices (the very dynamic curtailing >35% of European capacity); (b) world-scale plants + distribution/logistics network that is effectively unreplicable at reasonable cost; (c) Haber-Bosch is mature chemistry — no patent moat — but the capital intensity (Blue Point JV ~$4B) and the 2M t/yr CO2 sequestration at Donaldsonville (with ExxonMobil, since Jul 2025) plus North America's first commercial-scale green ammonia plant (20MW electrolysis) give CF a differentiated low-carbon product with a >$20/t premium that competitors cannot quickly match.
Substitution threats: biological nitrogen / nitrogen-use-efficiency tech and precision ag reduce nitrogen intensity over time, but nitrogen is indispensable to high-yield corn/wheat — there is no substitute for the nitrogen atom in fertilizer at scale. Customers (CHS ~13% of sales, co-ops, distributors) cannot in-source ammonia production — it requires a world-scale chemical plant and gas supply. The real threat is not substitution but price normalization: if Middle East supply returns and China exports more, the scarcity premium evaporates. **Irreplaceability score: 7/10** — the product is a commodity, but CF's cost position, scale and low-carbon differentiation make it the hardest nitrogen producer in the world to displace; the score reflects cost moat + logistics, not product uniqueness.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- Q3 2026 (Nov 4–5, 2026): net earnings $436M / $2.87 per diluted share; adjusted EBITDA $833M; 9M adjusted EBITDA ~$3.0B; 9M net earnings ~$1.8B / $11.60 per share. Returned $1.3B to shareholders in 9M 2026.
- Guidance raised: mid-cycle EBITDA $2.9B (raised Aug 2026, citing structurally higher global capital costs); growth projects to add $400M, taking mid-cycle EBITDA to $3.3B by 2030; nitrogen per share +15% by 2030 via growth projects + buybacks ($1.7B repurchase plan in progress).
- Analyst action: RBC raised target to $125 from $115 (Sector Perform) post-Q3, citing nitrogen fundamentals + Yazoo City restart; Freedom Broker raised to $133 from $129 (Hold); Scotiabank reaffirmed Buy, $130 target. Consensus EPS revised up 8.9% in 90 days to $11.72 (TickerNerd, Nov 16–17).
- Blue Point One JV (with JERA/Mitsui, ~$3.7–4.0B total cost) began site construction in August 2026; completion 2029; 1.5M tons nitrogen-equivalent capacity; CF funds its 40% share from FCF.
- thyssenkrupp Uhde won FEED contract for a urea-liquor upgrade at Courtright, Ontario (DEF capacity +400,000+ short tons/yr).

### 6. Risks
- The shortage is war-driven and reversible: Middle East normalization (4–6 month lag even if hostilities end now) plus rising Chinese exports (~9M mt authorizations) would loosen the balance fast; nitrogen prices can fall as fast as they rose.
- Uneven product economics: urea/UAN realizations were weaker in Q3 even as ammonia strengthened — the thesis is not uniformly strong across the slate.
- Yazoo City outage delayed further — full production now expected H2 2027 (was earlier); 9M consolidated volumes below prior year partly because of Yazoo.
- Brazil demand down ~1M tons urea (credit constraints); overall Brazilian fertilizer demand −7 to −9M tons.
- FTC investigation into fertilizer prices (reported Nov 2026) — regulatory wildcard on domestic pricing.
- Farm economics: nitrogen demand ultimately depends on corn/wheat prices and farmer affordability; a crop-price downturn cuts application rates.
- Blue Point is a $3.7B+ multi-year build (2029) funded from FCF — execution and capital-allocation risk.

### 7. Shortage score: 8/10
**Verdict:** The only real shortage in this batch, verified on the company's own call — ~9M tons of urea gone, >35% of European ammonia curtailed, CF running at 97% with record UAN volumes — but it is a war-driven, reversible shortage, so the score reflects tightness today, not permanence.

## PKG — Packaging Corporation of America — $244.45, fwd PE 17.0x, ret 12m +27.1%, target $269.31 (+10.1%)

### 1. Shortage thesis
No — judged honestly, there is no shortage angle. What exists is pricing power in a tight-but-not-short market, and management's own language confirms the distinction: on the Q3 call (Oct 21–22, 2026) management said it is "operating in a tight containerboard market that appears to be tightening further as seasonal fourth-quarter demand picks up" — but legacy corrugated volumes actually FELL 0.5% per day YoY (weakness in agricultural end markets, notably lettuce), total corrugated shipments rose 11% only because of the Greif acquisition, and systemwide containerboard inventories ROSE 34,000 tons in Q3. A market with rising inventories and falling legacy volumes is not a shortage. The price increases are being pushed through on industry discipline/consolidation, not on customers being unable to get boxes. Doctrine 3 verdict: weakest keep — correctly identified.

### 2. Backlog / order book
No order book — containerboard is sold on short-cycle orders to converters and box plants, much of it internally consumed (PCA is vertically integrated: mills feed its own ~90 converting plants). Substitute metrics:
- Legacy mills produced 1.286M tons of containerboard in Q3 (+77k tons vs Q2, +31k vs Q3 2025).
- Systemwide containerboard inventories +34,000 tons vs end-Q2 — rising, not drawing down.
- Q4 outlook: "solid corrugated products demand with typical seasonal improvement," total shipments above Q3 despite fewer shipping days; management expects volumes "roughly flat" for Q4 and to recover in Q4 2026 or Q1 2027. No backlog figure disclosed (not found).

### 3. Category position
No — #3. Third-largest containerboard producer in North America (~10% market share per Morningstar), behind Smurfit Westrock (global ~$31B revenue, 500+ plants, 40 countries) and International Paper (~$23.6B revenue, DS Smith acquired early 2025). PCA revenue ~$8–9B (FY2025), 10 mills / ~90 converting plants, ~4.7M tons annual containerboard capacity. It is the most efficient operator of the three (industry-leading EBITDA margins; packaging segment EBITDA margin 23.9% in Q3, up from 23.1%), but it is a clear #3 by scale and has no pricing leadership — it follows industry price moves rather than setting them.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk
Moat: vertical integration (mills → converting plants), mill efficiency, and domestic focus — not a technology or certification moat. Corrugated boxes are a commodity: any customer (Amazon, food producers, e-commerce shippers) can buy from Smurfit Westrock, International Paper, Pratt, or regional converters; switching costs are low. Substitution threats are mild but real: plastic/reusable packaging in some niches, and e-commerce mix shift toward lighter-weight packaging. No customer can realistically in-source a containerboard mill (capital intensity is the one barrier), but they don't need to — there are two bigger suppliers. **Irreplaceability score: 2/10** — efficient commodity producer in a consolidated industry; the pricing power comes from industry structure, not from anything PKG-specific that a customer cannot route around.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- Q3 2026 beat (Oct 21–22, 2026): adjusted EPS $3.04 vs $2.91 guidance and ~$2.93 consensus (+11.4% YoY); net income $262M / $2.93 per share; net sales $2.6B (+12.5–13% YoY); adjusted EBITDA $577M (+14.7% YoY).
- Pricing: domestic containerboard/corrugated price & mix +$0.75/share YoY and +$1.11/share sequentially. Realized substantially all of the first $50/ton increase and ~half of the second $50/ton increase; remainder of the second expected in Q4; the third increase ($70/ton, recognized by Fastmarkets RISI in September; PCA had initially announced $140/ton that round) to be realized split Q4/Q1, skewed to Q1 2027. Management: pricing is "our supreme focus" and "driving our results at the moment" (President Tom Hassfurther).
- Greif integration: acquired containerboard business (Sep 2025) contributing better than expected (+$0.24/share YoY); +45k tons pushed into the Greif system; acquired mills producing ~70k more tons on a run-rate basis than at acquisition.
- Q4 guidance: adjusted EPS $2.81 (down sequentially, incl. $0.72/share maintenance outage expense). Full-year capex raised to $865–890M.
- No analyst upgrade found in the last ~3 months (not found).

### 6. Risks
- The entire thesis is three rounds of price increases ($50 + $50 + $70/ton) landing in a market where legacy volumes are DOWN and inventories are UP — if demand softens further, realization stalls and the increases reverse or get competed away (Morningstar notes increased competitive intensity from legacy paper producers has already limited pricing power in recent years).
- Cost headwinds: labor + operating costs $0.28/share, freight $0.26, fiber $0.12 in Q3 — $0.54/share combined — eating much of the pricing gain.
- Customer concentration in cyclical end markets (agriculture already weak; e-commerce mix is dilutive to mix).
- Q4 EPS guidance ($2.81) is a sequential deceleration; the $70 increase's payoff is back-loaded to Q1 2027 and unproven.
- Valuation: fwd PE 17.0x is the highest in this batch by a wide margin for a no-growth commodity box maker; +27.1% in 12m with volumes flat-to-down.
- Palmyra, PA sheet-feeder fire kept the Greif corrugated plant down most of September — operational disruption risk in an aging mill system.

### 7. Shortage score: 1/10
**Verdict:** No shortage — a #3 commodity box maker pushing through industry-wide price hikes while its own volumes fall and inventories rise; the pricing power is real but borrowed from industry discipline, and at 17x forward earnings it is the weakest name in the batch.
