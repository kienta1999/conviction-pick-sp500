- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** GE Aerospace is the one name in this field whose moat nobody can copy: LEAP is sole-source on the Boeing 737 MAX, GE9X is the exclusive engine for the 777X, and an engine change requires re-certifying the airframe — a multi-year, multi-billion-dollar undertaking no airline will attempt. The moat is not a market share number but a certification wall plus a $170.2B contracted commercial services backlog that converts into decades of high-margin aftermarket revenue. The shortage is genuine and management-admitted: services growth is constrained by supply, not demand, with spare-parts delinquency rising and more than 95% of Q4 spares already in backlog. At $331.81 the market is paying 35.6x forward for that backlog, but it is the only name where the shortage, the backlog and the moat are all contracted facts rather than price forecasts.
- **Shortage / backlog evidence:** $170.2B commercial services backlog (remaining performance obligations at Sep 30, 2026, +10% from end-2025); Q3 orders $19.0B (+49%), commercial services orders +23% in Q3 and +30% YTD; more than 95% of Q4 spare parts already in backlog and more than 100% of engines for internal shop visits already in the shop; spare-parts delinquency increased sequentially in Q3 despite improving material availability because demand keeps pushing unfilled orders higher.
- **Irreplaceability:** low risk — airlines cannot in-source jet-engine manufacturing, and the only scaled rivals (Pratt & Whitney, Rolls-Royce) sit behind the same FAA type-certification wall and the same supply constraints. The aftermarket is the lock-in: engines generate decades of high-margin services revenue, and GE's $170B services backlog is contracted, not hoped for. The credible threat is not a customer building its own engine; it is GE failing to convert the backlog fast enough.

- **Top risk (disintermediation case):** There is no in-sourcing threat — the disintermediation case here is conversion failure: if castings/material input growth stalls, the $170B backlog converts slower, not faster, and the CPP acquisition is itself an admission of the bottleneck. Commercial services revenue growth already slowed to ~17% in Q3 from 32% in H1, LEAP delivery growth nearly stalled (+5% in Q3), FCF fell 14% in the quarter, and GE9X is a ~$300M additional YoY loss in 2027 with margin expansion only from 2028.
