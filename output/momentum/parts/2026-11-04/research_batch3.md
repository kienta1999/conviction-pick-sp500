## LLY — Eli Lilly and Company

### 1. Shortage thesis
Demand-surge thesis, but the acute shortage phase has largely passed. Tirzepatide (Mounjaro/Zepbound) was removed from the FDA shortage list in December 2024. In 2026 the constraint is narrower: periodic pharmacy gaps at maintenance doses, "especially the 12.5 mg and 15 mg" strengths, which share fill-finish lines with Mounjaro (TrimRx 2026 availability review, citing IQVIA dispensing data). The volume evidence is the surge itself: Q3 2026 worldwide revenue rose 39% to $24.4bn "driven by a 53% increase in volume, partially offset by a 15% decrease in realized prices" (Lilly Q3 2026 press release, 29 Oct 2026). Q2 2026 was the same pattern: revenue +48% to $23.0bn, volume +60%, realized prices −13% (Lilly Q2 2026 10-Q summary). CEO David Ricks's framing, reported in 2026 coverage, is global GLP-1 use rising from ~20m patients at end-2025 to ~30m by end-2026. Medicare began covering obesity drugs in July 2026 (Medicare GLP-1 Bridge Program, $50/month out-of-pocket cap to Dec 2027, per Q1 2026 call coverage), a further demand tailwind. So: not "sold out" in 2026, but demand growth still outrunning comfortable supply at the high-dose end, and Lilly is cutting price to convert volume rather than rationing it.

### 2. Backlog / order book
**Not found** — Lilly does not report a formal order backlog for its drug franchise. Closest proxies: Q3 2026 Mounjaro $10.7bn (+65% y/y) and Zepbound $5.4bn in the US (+49%) / $5.3bn US per Q3 summaries (29–30 Oct 2026); Mounjaro + Zepbound + Foundayo = 66% of total revenue for the nine months ended 30 Sep 2026 (Lilly 10-Q, via Q3 coverage). Foundayo monthly prescriptions rose from 222,000 in July to 527,000 in September 2026, and its "share of new patient starts exited Q3 at ~40%" (Lilly Q3 slides, via coverage). No "booked X months out" figure exists in public filings.

### 3. Category position
#1 in incretins, ahead of Novo Nordisk (#2). Lilly slides using IQVIA data put its US incretin prescription share at 63.0% in the four weeks to 25 Sep 2026 vs 36.6% for Novo; outside the US, 58.4% by sales over Jul–Aug 2026 (Q3 2026 coverage). Earlier: Mounjaro alone captured 51% of US incretin-class prescriptions in Q1 2026, up from 39% in Q1 2025 (Lilly Q1 2026 presentation). On revenue, Axis Intelligence's duopoly index (company filings) put Lilly at 61.8% of combined Lilly–Novo incretin revenue in Q2 2026, up from 36.0% in 2024. In obesity specifically, Zepbound led for a fifth consecutive quarter in Q1 2026 with >60% of total prescriptions (TRx).

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk
Moat: peptide API synthesis + fill-finish scale that "takes years and billions of dollars to build" (industry coverage, Oct 2026), plus tirzepatide patent protection and a pipeline ladder (oral Foundayo, triple-agonist retatrutide). Customers (patients/payers) cannot in-source a prescription molecule; the realistic bypass is a rival molecule, not self-manufacture. Substitution threats are real and named: Novo's semaglutide (Ozempic/Wegovy, plus Wegovy pill), generic semaglutide entry in India from March 2026 (which actually expanded the market — Mounjaro volume +10% after entry, per Q1 call), compounders (largely shut out after the FDA shortage resolution), and future oral competitors. **Irreplaceability score: 7/10** — the molecule class is replicable by one credible rival, but Lilly's scale, efficacy edge and pipeline make it very hard to displace as #1.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- **29 Oct 2026 — Q3 beat + guidance raise (third raise of 2026):** revenue $24.4bn (+39% y/y, vs ~$22.1–22.6bn expected), non-GAAP EPS $7.48 (vs $5.38 Zacks consensus, +39%; +7% y/y despite $4.57/share acquired IPR&D charges). 2026 revenue guidance raised to $91–92bn from $85–87bn (midpoint +$5.5bn); performance-margin outlook raised to 52–52.5% from 49–50.5%. ~$900m of Q3 revenue was non-recurring (incl. $410m Jardiance milestones + rebate adjustments) — ex-items revenue still ~$23.5bn, still a beat.
- **28 Oct 2026 — Retatrutide head-to-head win:** TRANSCEND-T2D-2 (1,298 adults, 80 weeks) — retatrutide cut A1C 2.2 pts at both 9 mg and 12 mg vs 1.8 pts for Ozempic 2 mg, and weight −19.5% / −20.9% vs −9.4% for semaglutide (Lilly press release). First Phase 3 head-to-head vs another incretin; tolerability gap noted (discontinuations for adverse events 4.8%/7.9% vs 2.7%).
- **Apr–May 2026 (just outside 3 months, still driving Q3):** FDA approval of Foundayo (orforglipron) April 2026, retail/telehealth launch 9 Apr; +$4.5bn additional Indiana (Lebanon) manufacturing investment announced 6 May 2026, taking Indiana commitments since 2020 to >$21bn and US commitments to >$50bn; Lebanon API site opens 2027, billed as the largest API site in US history.
- Acquisitions closed in Q3 (Orna, Ajax, Centessa, Kelonia among others) — the source of the $4.57/share IPR&D charge.

### 6. Risks
Single-molecule concentration (tirzepatide franchise = ~2/3 of revenue); realized prices falling 13–15% as Lilly cuts price to drive volume and as China NRDL / Medicare negotiation bite; $2bn of 2026 one-time milestone/rebate benefits not recurring in 2027 (CFO, Q3 call); retatrutide still unapproved with tolerability questions; Novo oral pill traction; valuation already reflecting a >$1tn market cap (first pharma to pass $1tn, Nov 2025; ~$1.1tn mid-Oct 2026 per Forbes via coverage).

### 7. Shortage score and verdict
**Shortage score: 6/10. Verdict: Demand surge, not a shortage — volume (+53%) at falling prices is a land-grab, and the explosive leg now depends on retatrutide/Foundayo extending the lead rather than on scarcity pricing.**

## WST — West Pharmaceutical Services

### 1. Shortage thesis
Indirect shortage thesis: West does not sell a scarce drug, it sells the scarce *containment* for scarce drugs. High-Value Product (HVP) Components — coated/ready-to-use elastomer stoppers and plungers for injectables — are capacity-constrained by qualification, not just by plant space. Q3 2026 call: "HVP Components grew 20% organically, including GLP-1 growth well into the 30% range and high-teens growth outside GLP-1" and "more than 200 HVP upgrade projects" added in 2026 (211 through Q3 per TradingKey summary). GLP-1-related business was 21% of Q3 sales (12% elastomers + 9% West Vantage). Management is "investing to support future growth through HVP finishing-capacity expansions in Europe and the U.S." with new capacity "expected to come online over roughly 18–24 months, with existing facilities expected to support near-term demand" (Q3 2026 call summary) — i.e. demand is being met today only by stretching existing finishing capacity while the expansion is built. A second structural driver: EU Annex 1 contamination-control rules pushing standard-to-HVP conversions — "only a small fraction of the estimated 6 billion addressable components has converted" (Finviz/Zacks coverage of prior guidance). No "sold out / lead times extended to X weeks" quote was found; the evidence is growth + capacity investment, not a published stock-out.

### 2. Backlog / order book
**Not found** — West does not disclose a formal backlog/order-book figure in its Q3 2026 release or 10-Q. Closest proxies: 211 new HVP upgrade projects added in 2026 through Q3; HVP Components $466.7m in Q3 (+20.3% organic, 53% of total sales); biologics market group 43% of Q3 sales ($379.3m, +23.2% organic). No "booked through [date]" figure is published.

### 3. Category position
#1 in elastomeric closures. Dataintelo (2025–2033 market report, verified May 2026): West ~24% of the global elastomeric closures market, vs Datwyler ~18% (#2) and AptarGroup ~14% (#3); the three together ~56%. West also claims "90%+ participation in biologics and biosimilars" (prior management commentary via Finviz). Scale: >41bn components/devices per year, 26 manufacturing facilities, 50 sites, >10,000 employees; FY2025 net sales $3.07bn.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk
Moat: regulatory qualification lock-in. A closure is part of the drug's approved container-closure system: supplier qualification "typically requiring 18–36 months of development before commercial supply begins," plus 12–24 months of stability studies and extractables/leachables data packages (Dataintelo, citing FDA 21 CFR Part 11 / USP Class VI practice). Switching a stopper on an approved biologic means re-validation and a regulatory filing change — pharma customers do not in-source elastomer formulation lightly, and a GLP-1 maker cannot ship vials/cartridges without a qualified closure. Substitution threat: Datwyler and Aptar are fully qualified alternatives for *new* programs, and for standard (non-HVP) components price competition is real; the lock-in is strongest at the HVP end (FluroTec/NovaPure coatings) and weakest for commodity stoppers. **Irreplaceability score: 8/10** for HVP/biologics containment (qualification + formulation + global sterile finishing network); lower (~5/10) for standard products.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- **29 Oct 2026 — Q3 beat + guidance raise:** net sales $874.5m (+8.7% reported, +11.9% organic) vs ~$827m consensus; adjusted diluted EPS $2.66 (+35.7% y/y) vs ~$2.20 consensus (+20–21% beat); GAAP EPS $3.02 (+57.3%). Gross margin 39.7% (+310 bps), adjusted operating margin 25.3% (+420 bps). Full-year 2026 guidance raised: net sales $3.41–3.42bn (from $3.345–3.38bn), adjusted EPS $9.38–9.48 (from $8.85–9.05). Dividend raised 18% to $0.26/quarter (declared 19 Oct 2026). $231m buybacks in Q3 ($685m YTD).
- **1 Jul 2026 — SmartDose 3.5mL divestiture to AbbVie completed** (~$136m per prior coverage); cleans up the mix but creates a ~290 bps headwind to reported growth in 2027 (Q3 call).
- **Q3 2026 — HVP momentum:** HVP Components +20.3% organic; HVP Delivery Devices +26.4% organic ex-SmartDose; 211 upgrade projects YTD. Dublin drug-handling program ramping toward a targeted >$60m revenue at >30% gross margin (prior call commentary).
- Overhangs in the same print: West Vantage −6.6% organic (planned CGM contract roll-off; Q3 described as the trough), and a May 2026 cyber incident that caused production downtime.

### 6. Risks
Customer concentration in practice (GLP-1 = 21% of sales; biologics = 43% — a GLP-1 demand or pricing shock flows straight through); West Vantage transition (CGM exit, SmartDose removal = 2027 growth headwind); capacity timing (18–24 months for new HVP finishing — if GLP-1 growth cools, the capex lands into softer demand); working-capital build (see flag below); the Feb 2025 guidance-cut precedent (−38% in one session) shows how fast the multiple reprices on a guide-down.

### EARNINGS-QUALITY FLAG — RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN (divergence 0.110)
**What it is:** receivables grew faster than revenue. Balance sheet: accounts receivable, net $574.4m at 31 Dec 2025 → $712.0m at 30 Jun 2026 (+24% in six months) (Q2 2026 10-Q), while H1 revenue grew far more slowly; Q3 call confirms the mechanism — 9M 2026 free cash flow $278m, down ~5% y/y, "due primarily to increases in customers accounts receivable, as well as increased inventory levels to support our operations, partially offset by lower capital spending," with management adding "this is an improvement from Q2's results, and we expect continued improvement in Q4."
**Benign or value-trap?** Lean benign, with a verification condition: the build coincides with +20% HVP volume growth, new GLP-1/biologics programs and deliberate inventory build "to support operations" — the classic growth-working-capital pattern, not channel-stuffing (revenue itself beat consensus and margins expanded 310–420 bps, which a stuffing story would not produce). But it is not yet cleared: FCF fell while EPS rose 36%, so earnings are running ahead of cash until Q4 delivers the promised receivables/inventory unwind.
**One-line verdict: Benign-leaning growth working capital, not yet a value-trap signature — but the flag only clears if Q4 free cash flow recovers as management guided.**

### 7. Shortage score and verdict
**Shortage score: 7/10. Verdict: The purest picks-and-shovels shortage proxy in the batch — GLP-1/biologics volumes are pulling HVP capacity faster than West can finish it, and qualification lock-in makes that demand sticky, but West reports no backlog and the 2027 SmartDose headwind caps the explosive leg.**

## VRTX — Vertex Pharmaceuticals

### 1. Shortage thesis
Weak on a strict shortage reading — this is a monopoly thesis, not a scarcity thesis. Cystic fibrosis (CF) is a fixed, genetically defined patient population; there is no demand surge that supply cannot meet, and Vertex does not report capacity constraints, stock-outs or lead-time extension for Trikafta/Alyftrek. The "surge" is a *switching* surge inside its own franchise: patients moving from twice-daily Trikafta to once-daily Alyftrek, plus label expansions and new geographies (Alyftrek reimbursed in 7 additional countries in Q3, total 32). The adjacent shortage-adjacent angle is the non-CF pipeline: Casgevy (gene therapy, capacity-constrained by treatment-centre throughput in principle) grew to $99.2m in Q3, but no capacity/sold-out quote was found for it either. Score accordingly low on shortage, high on pricing power (see §4).

### 2. Backlog / order book
**Not found / not applicable** — Vertex, as a chronic-therapy seller, does not report an order backlog. Demand proxies: CF product revenue $3,273.2m in Q3 2026 (+8% y/y); Alyftrek patients "over 18 thousand… around the world" (Q3 2026 call); Journavx >750,000 prescriptions cumulative, +40% sequentially in Q3. No booked-out horizon is published.

### 3. Category position
#1 by a distance that is effectively a monopoly in disease-modifying CF therapy. "Vertex makes essentially all of the drugs that treat [CF's] underlying cause" (AInvest, Oct 2026); CF = $3.27bn of $3.44bn Q3 total revenue (~95–96%). Trikafta/Kaftrio is "the first and only therapy to treat the underlying cause of cystic fibrosis in about 90% of the global patient population." Trailing revenue through Jun 2026: Trikafta/Kaftrio ~$10.08bn (~80% of consolidated revenue), Alyftrek ~$1.63bn (~13%), together ~93% (Intickers, Oct 2026). There is no credible #2 in CFTR modulators — the "competition" cited in low-quality summaries (Astellas/Pfizer selling Trikafta) is erroneous; the real #2 threat is future generics/biosimilars after patent expiry, not a current rival. In the new arenas (IgAN, acute pain) Vertex is a challenger, not #1 (Vera's Tru... — Vera Therapeutics' TRUTAKNA is already accelerated-approved in IgAN; Journavx competes with generic opioids/NSAIDs on price).

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk
Moat for CF: proprietary CFTR modulator chemistry + patents + a decade of regulator/payer/patient infrastructure. Patients cannot in-source a CFTR corrector/potentiator combination, and no substitute technology (gene therapy for CF is pre-commercial; mRNA approaches are early) routes around small-molecule modulators today. The moat's expiry is dated, not hypothetical: Trikafta exclusivity ends ~2031, which is precisely why Vertex is forcing the switch to Alyftrek, "expected to extend the company's dominance until 2039 or later." **Irreplaceability score: 9/10 for the CF franchise through the Alyftrek transition; 5/10 for the company as a whole, because the non-CF pipeline (kidney, pain, sickle cell) faces direct, credible substitutes.**

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- **2 Nov 2026 — Q3 beat on revenue, guidance raised (slightly):** total revenue $3.44bn (+12% y/y, modestly above consensus ~$3.40bn); CF $3.27bn (+8%); Alyftrek $705.8m (from $247.0m y/y, +186%) vs Trikafta/Kaftrio $2,436.9m (−8% y/y — the switch, not share loss); Casgevy $99.2m (+487% y/y, +30% sequentially); Journavx $58.3m (+197% y/y, +18% sequentially, >750k prescriptions); Palsonify $10.1m in its first month post-Crinetics close (1 Sep 2026, ~$9bn acquisition). GAAP EPS $2.56 (from $4.20) and non-GAAP EPS $4.76 (from $4.80) fell on Crinetics costs and +44% total costs/expenses (~$2.7bn). FY2026 revenue guidance raised to $13.15–13.25bn from $13.1–13.2bn (still below ~$13.28bn consensus); Casgevy + Journavx still guided ≥$500m combined for 2026. Cash/securities fell to $9.8bn from $12.3bn at end-2025 (acquisition + buybacks + borrowing).
- **23 Oct 2026 — Povetacicept Phase 3 RAINIER published in NEJM + presented at ASN Kidney Week:** Week-36 interim — proteinuria −52.0% from baseline (−49.8% vs placebo, p<0.0001), 42.2% of treated patients reaching UPCR <0.5 g/g vs 6.2% placebo; **PDUFA target action date 30 Nov 2026** for accelerated approval in IgA nephropathy (BLA accepted Jun 2026, Breakthrough Therapy designation). If approved, first commercial product in Vertex's nephrology franchise, dosed as a low-volume at-home autoinjector every 4 weeks (vs Vera's weekly TRUTAKNA). Modelled approval odds ~78% (BiopharmaWatch FDA calendar, 20 Oct 2026).
- **Q3 2026 — Next-gen CF:** VX-828 data (sweat chloride −6.6 mmol/L at day 28 vs Trikafta baseline; −9.4 mmol through day 28) reported on the Q3 call, supporting the post-Alyftrek CF ladder.
- Analyst stance (aggregated, Nov 2026): broadly "Buy," average ~$571 target (range ~$350–667); UBS raised to $615, Goldman ~$654, Morgan Stanley ~$665 (Tickeron aggregation).

### 6. Risks
Concentration: ~95% of revenue from one disease; the Alyftrek switch cannibalises Trikafta (Q3 Trikafta −8%) so headline CF growth (+8%) understates franchise churn; Trikafta patent cliff ~2031 if Alyftrek transition stalls; non-CF bets are expensive and unproven at scale — Crinetics (~$9bn) cut GAAP profit and cash in Q3, Journavx ($58m/quarter) remains below some expectations against cheap generic painkillers, Casgevy's gene-therapy logistics cap its ramp; povetacicept faces an approved direct competitor (Vera) and accelerated approval still requires a Week-104 kidney-function confirmatory readout; valuation ~30× trailing earnings / ~31× 2026 expected EPS at ~$524 (24 Oct 2026) leaves little room for a PDUFA miss.

### 7. Shortage score and verdict
**Shortage score: 3/10. Verdict: Not a shortage bet at all — a monopoly-plus-catalyst bet: the explosive leg is the 30 Nov 2026 povetacicept PDUFA decision and the Alyftrek switch sustaining CF pricing power, not scarcity.**
