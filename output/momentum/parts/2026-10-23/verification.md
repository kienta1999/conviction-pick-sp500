## WINNER — MU (Micron)

### 1. ~$150B RPO under 26 SCAs at minimum prices through 2030/31, backed by ~$32B in customer financial commitments (mostly cash deposits)

**Verdict: CONFIRMED — with the definitional difference the brief asked about, and it is material.**

- Q4 FY26 call / prepared remarks, 30 Sep 2026: Micron has signed 26 multi-year take-or-pay Strategic Customer Agreements, estimated at >35% of revenue through 2030, with some terms extending into 2031. CFO Mark Murphy: remaining performance obligations associated with agreements that have determined pricing frameworks totalled approximately $150 billion, based on committed volumes and minimum (floor) pricing — described on the call as inherently conservative. Customer financial commitments rose to $32 billion (from $22 billion in June), the vast majority / mostly in the form of cash deposits; $12.3 billion of deposits were received in Q4 alone. More than 75% of FY2027 output is already committed. Sources: MarketBeat Q4 call highlights, 30 Sep 2026; The Elec, 30 Sep–1 Oct 2026; AI Stock Wire summary of the release, 30 Sep 2026.
- Definitional difference vs the 10-K — exactly as flagged in the brief: Micron's Form 10-K filed 9 Oct 2026 (period ended 3 Sep 2026, SEC filing mu-20260903) states: "As of September 3, 2026, the transaction price allocated to our remaining performance obligations was approximately $134 billion, of which $13 billion has been recognized as contract liabilities," primarily customer deposits under SCAs, nearly all deposits scheduled to be repaid 2029–2031, approximately one-fourth of the RPO expected to be recognised as revenue over the next twelve months and the remainder fully within five years. The 10-K explicitly (i) bases RPO on expected purchases to satisfy committed volumes and minimum pricing, (ii) says it is "not expected to be indicative of future revenue under these contracts," and (iii) excludes agreements without fixed pricing or price bands (variable consideration at inception) and, as a practical expedient, contracts of ≤1 year.
- So $150B (call, agreements with determined pricing frameworks, at minimum prices) and $134B (10-K accounting RPO, narrower accounting scope / as-of balance-sheet date) are not the same measure and must not be used interchangeably. The panel claim is accurate as a statement of what management said on the call; the filing-grade backlog figure is ~$134B.

Backlog check (doctrine): PASS, qualified — real take-or-pay backlog, but cite $134B 10-K alongside the $150B call figure.

Q4 FY26 prepared remarks / call, 30 Sep 2026, Mehrotra: "We have completed agreements for the vast majority of our calendar 2027 HBM bit supply with significant price increases year over year, narrowing the gross margin gap with conventional DRAM" (transcript via Longbridge; Korea JoongAng Daily and The Next Platform, 1–2 Oct 2026, quoting the same line). - Correction for the memo: replace "HBM supply/capacity is sold out through calendar 2027" with "calendar 2026 HBM was sold out; agreements are completed for the vast majority of calendar 2027 HBM bit supply at significantly higher prices." The shortage driver itself — demand exceeding supply in 2027 and 2028, tightness greater than in 2026, >75% of FY2027 output committed — is CONFIRMED on the same call.

Shortage-driver check (doctrine): PASS, qualified — shortage is real and management-sourced; the 2027 "sold out" wording overstates "vast majority."

## TOP-3 — NVDA (Nvidia)

### 4. $500B in booked demand for Blackwell + Rubin through end-2026 (CFO Colette Kress, Nov 2025), and ~80–85% of AI accelerator revenue share (~10x AMD)

**Verdict: CONFIRMED — with two definitional caveats: "visibility to revenue," not a formal backlog, and share is a third-party estimate, not a company disclosure.**

- $500B figure and attribution: CONFIRMED as to Kress / Nov 2025. Q3 FY26 earnings call, 19 Nov 2025, CFO Colette Kress: "We currently have visibility to $500 billion in Blackwell and Rubin revenue from the start of this year through the end of Calendar Year 2026" (transcript via MarketBeat, 19 Nov 2025). In Q&A the same day Kress said the company is on track for that $500B through end-CY2026 and the number will grow.
- Caveats that change the meaning: (i) CEO Jensen Huang first announced the $500B at GTC Washington in late Oct 2025; Kress's November statement was a reaffirmation / CFO-commentary version, not the origin. (ii) Nvidia's own word is "visibility to … revenue," not booked orders / backlog — post-GTC clarification reported at the time was that ~30% had already shipped and the figure includes networking products, so the remaining forward portion was materially less than $500B (secondary summaries put the trued-up remaining backlog near ~$307B). Calling it "$500B in booked demand" overstates its contractual status.
- Share 80–85%: CONFIRMED as a current third-party estimate, UNVERIFIED as a primary/company figure — no company publishes merchant AI-chip share. Presenc AI (May 2026, via Blockonomi AI Chip Market Statistics 2026): Nvidia holds 80% to 85% of data-centre AI accelerator revenue in 2026, down from ~92% in 2023, with AMD at ~5% to 7%. Other 2026 trackers put Nvidia lower (Silicon Analysts / IDC-based summaries: ~75–81% in 2026, ~75% projected). The 80–85% range is within the published estimate range, at its top half.
- "~10x AMD": order-of-magnitude CONFIRMED, not a sourced exact ratio. On the same Presenc shares (80–85% vs 5–7%) the implied ratio is roughly 12–17x; on reported data-centre revenue in the most recent comparable 2026 quarters (Nvidia ~$75.2B for the quarter ended 26 Apr 2026 vs AMD data-centre segment ~$5.8B) it is ~13x. "~10x" is if anything conservative, but it is arithmetic on estimates, not a disclosed figure.

Backlog check (doctrine): QUALIFIED PASS — $500B is management visibility including already-shipped and networking revenue; it is not an RPO/backlog in the Micron sense. Memo must not equate the two.

Moat central claim (doctrine): dominance is real on every 2026 estimator, but share is eroding (92% in 2023 → 75–85% in 2026 depending on estimator) as custom ASICs (Google TPU ~6–8%) and AMD grow — the moat claim should be stated as current share, not a static ~85%.

### 5. Management guided / preliminarily indicated FY28 revenue growth of roughly 70%, described as supply-constrained

**Verdict: CONFIRMED — and the brief's "guided / preliminarily indicated" hedge is the correct characterisation.**

- This was the first time Nvidia gave a full-year forward look of this kind; it is a preliminary expectation, not formal guidance in an earnings release. Do not upgrade it to "guided FY28" without the word "preliminary."

Catalyst / date check (doctrine): PASS — catalyst is the August 2026 Q2 FY27 call; the ~70% FY28 preliminary outlook and its supply-constrained framing are correctly dated and attributed (Kress for the number, Huang for the demand > supply framing).
