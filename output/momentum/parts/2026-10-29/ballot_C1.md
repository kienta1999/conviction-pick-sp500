## 1. SINGLE-PICK ballot

- Top pick: MU
- Runner-up: NVDA
- Thesis (3-5 sentences): Micron is one of only three vertically integrated memory makers on earth at scale, alongside Samsung and SK Hynix, and that oligopoly — not a single product cycle — is what makes the shortage bankable. Its HBM supply is sold out, its pricing is locked in multi-year contracts rather than spot hope, and its fiscal Q4 FY26 gross margin was ~87% with revenue of $54.23B, up 379% year over year. Unlike a commodity name that merely rides a price spike, Micron's fab capital intensity, process lead (1-gamma DRAM, HBM3E/HBM4 roadmap) and multi-year GPU-platform qualification mean a hyperscaler cannot simply replace it or build around it. That combination — a genuine shortage plus a moat that takes tens of billions and years to replicate — is the explosive upside case.
- Key shortage/backlog evidence: Remaining performance obligations of ~$150B across 26 signed multi-year strategic customer agreements, with $32B in customer financial commitments (mostly cash deposits), covering ~35% of revenue through 2030 and with more than 75% of fiscal 2027 output committed (Micron fiscal Q4 FY26 release, 30 Sep 2026).
- Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk: medium — and why: no hyperscaler can realistically build DRAM/HBM fabs itself, so true in-sourcing risk is low, but supplier substitution is real — SK Hynix, not Micron, is the HBM leader at ~50% share versus Micron's ~18%, so customers can shift volume between the three makers, which stops this being a low-risk call.
- 12-18 month return scenario: rough base / bull case: Base (my scenario judgment, anchored to the shortlist's 58.9% analyst upside to the $1,561.91 mean target from the $983.23 screen price): +35–55% as contracted HBM and DRAM/NAND pricing flows through fiscal 2027. Bull (my scenario judgment): +60–90% if calendar-2027 HBM supply stays fully allocated at significantly higher prices and no capacity glut is priced in before 2028.
