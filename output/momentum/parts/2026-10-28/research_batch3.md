- **Long-run gap:** IEA's 2026 critical minerals outlook: base-case output from existing + announced mines falls **~25% short of primary supply requirements in 2035** (stated policies); BNEF (Oct 20, 2026 outlook) sees a **$332B investment gap** and notes **average new-mine development takes ~17 years** — supply cannot respond within any relevant investment horizon.

- **Grasberg disruption is both cause and casualty:** the Sep 2025 mud-rush incident cut one of the world's largest copper sources, tightening the market FCX sells into while temporarily reducing FCX's own volumes (see §2 and the inventory flag below).

**One-line verdict: BENIGN — the build is disclosed, guided smelter-feed/concentrate and leach-stockpile accumulation during the Grasberg restart (production deliberately running ahead of sales, with deferred gold shipments), not the unsold-finished-goods signature of a value trap.**

## CF — CF Industries (nitrogen fertilizer)

### 1. Shortage thesis — Hormuz-driven nitrogen tightness on an already-constrained balance
- **Primary source (CF Q2 2026 10-Q, filed Aug 2026):** "Global nitrogen prices increased from mid-February 2026 into the second quarter of 2026 as the conflict with Iran further tightened an already constrained global supply-demand balance during the peak Northern Hemisphere application season."
- **Quantified supply loss:** "CF Industries estimates that the conflict with Iran has reduced Middle East traded nitrogen supply by approximately **4.0 to 4.5 million metric tons of urea** and approximately **1 million metric tons of ammonia** to date." The Middle East supplies **25–30% of globally traded ammonia and 35–40% of globally traded urea**, and CF expects regional supply to "remain below pre-conflict levels due to continued hostilities, uncertainty around transit through the Strait of Hormuz, and the time required to restore production facilities," with higher shipping costs persisting "over the longer term."

Russia production also flagged at risk from the Ukraine war; CF expects an "enduring geopolitical risk premium" for Middle East/Russian supply.
