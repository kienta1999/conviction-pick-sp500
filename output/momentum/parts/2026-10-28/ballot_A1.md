- Thesis (3-5 sentences): MU is the cleanest conversion of a physical shortage into contracted revenue in the field: the entire calendar 2026 HBM supply is sold out, more than 75% of 2027 output is already committed, and management says 2027-28 will be tighter than 2026. That scarcity is no longer spot-priced — 26 take-or-pay Strategic Customer Agreements, mostly floor-and-ceiling priced for 3-5 years into 2031, have replaced the old spot cycle. With only three firms on earth able to make leading-edge DRAM/HBM at scale, customers cannot in-source or lightly dual-source away, so the shortage rent is deposit-backed and multi-year. Explosive upside comes if those committed volumes reprice and extend into 2028 while the shares still trade on peak-cycle scepticism.
- Key shortage/backlog evidence: ~$150B remaining performance obligations backed by 26 take-or-pay SCAs and $32B of customer financial commitments, the vast majority cash deposits.
- Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk: low — and why: only Samsung, SK hynix and Micron make leading-edge DRAM/HBM at scale; hyperscalers operate no DRAM fabs, and their custom ASICs increase rather than replace HBM demand. Displacement is possible only by the other two oligopolists, not by in-sourcing.
- 12-18 month return scenario: rough base / bull case: base +35-50% as SCA-covered 2027 volumes ship at contracted higher prices; bull +70% or more, toward the $1,561.91 analyst mean target, if 2028 commitments lock in at similar pricing and supply stays tighter than 2026.
- Top risk: Memory cyclicality and new supply — CXMT's rising conventional DRAM share and the industry's own 2027-28 fab capex could erode the scarcity premium, and floor/ceiling SCA pricing caps upside on covered revenue.
