# Ballot A1 — Supply-Chain Analyst lens — momentum 2026-11-13

## Single-pick ballot

- **Top pick:** MU
- **Runner-up:** CF
- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** Micron is the purest shortage in the 12-name set, and the only one where the shortage is both priced into the product (DRAM prices up high-teens and NAND up ~30% in a single quarter) and contracted years forward. HBM and DRAM are effectively sold out into 2027–28, with the CEO guiding to conditions "much tighter in calendar 2027 and 2028 than they were in 2026" and SK Hynix warning the shortage persists through 2030. At $811.30 (Nov 12 screen) the stock still sits well below its $1,213.37 June record close despite the FQ4 blowout, while the analyst target of $1,561.90 implies +92.5% — the gap between a sold-out order book and a price that has not reclaimed its peak is the explosive setup. The risk is cycle duration, not demand: this is a bet that the shortage outlasts the market's scepticism about 80%+ gross margins.
- **Key shortage/backlog evidence:** ~$100bn in binding multi-year AI memory contracts, backed by $32bn in customer financial commitments (mostly cash prepayments) and 26 strategic agreements covering >35% of revenue through 2030 — customers are paying cash years in advance for supply that does not yet exist.
- **Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk:** low — hyperscalers cannot realistically in-source memory: only three firms on earth make HBM at scale, fabs cost tens of billions and take years (Micron's New York fab delivers its first wafer only in 2030), and HBM requires Nvidia/AMD/Google qualification that locks in suppliers for years. The caveat is that the moat protects the oligopoly, not Micron specifically: Micron is #3 in HBM (18% share, down from 21%) behind SK Hynix (50%) and Samsung (33%), so customers can shift share among the three — but they cannot bypass the three.
- **12-18 month return scenario:** Base ~+40–50% (reclaiming the $1,213 June high as 2027 HBM contracts reprice higher and the sold-out position holds through 2027). Bull ~+90–120% (toward the $1,561.90 analyst target) if the shortage extends into 2028 as guided, DRAM/HBM prices keep rising, and Micron holds or regains HBM share on its HBM4 ramp for Nvidia's Vera Rubin platform.
- **Top risk:** Cyclicality / duration — panic buying and double-ordering flip a severe shortage into oversupply, or the datacentre power wall caps AI buildout; Samsung's HBM4 share gains (15% → 33% in a year) compound the risk if Micron keeps losing HBM share inside the oligopoly.
- **Conviction (1-10):** 7
