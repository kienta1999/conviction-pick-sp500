## MU — Micron Technology (tentative winner)

### 1. "~$100bn in binding multi-year AI memory contracts" (reported Aug 2026)
**Verdict: CONFIRMED (substance; date attribution slightly off).**
Micron disclosed 16 strategic customer agreements (SCAs), all take-or-pay, with its fiscal Q3 2026 results on **June 24, 2026** — not first disclosed in August. Fourteen of the 16 carry about **$100 billion of cumulative revenue at minimum contracted prices**, typically five-year terms running calendar 2026 through end-2030, covering ~20% of DRAM volume and ~33% of NAND volume. The figure was widely reported in August 2026 (e.g. WebProNews, Aug 1, 2026; CEO Sanjay Mehrotra reiterated on Aug 20, 2026 in Boise that more SCAs had been signed since the 16 disclosed in June, and that datacenter customers wanted ~50% more memory than Micron could commit). By the FQ4 call (Sep 30, 2026) the count had grown to 26 SCAs covering >35% of estimated revenue through 2030.
Sources: Micron FQ3 2026 prepared remarks, Jun 24, 2026; Trefis, Aug 27, 2026; BroadBrowse, Sep 2026; DilutionWatch, Oct 17, 2026.

### 2. "$32bn in customer financial commitments, mostly cash prepayments" (FQ4 2026 call, Sep 30, 2026)
**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**
CFO Mark Murphy disclosed on the FQ4 2026 call (Sep 30, 2026) that financial commitments from customers under the strategic customer agreements had risen to **$32 billion, up from $22 billion a quarter earlier, with the vast majority sitting on Micron's balance sheet as cash deposits**. (One secondary source, MEXC, Oct 2026, states this explicitly; the $22bn prior-quarter figure matches Micron's own FQ3 disclosure.)
Sources: Micron FQ4 2026 earnings call, Sep 30, 2026 (via MEXC Crypto Pulse summary; Convexity, Oct 2026, notes $12.3bn of customer cash deposits received in the quarter).

### 3. HBM sold out through 2026 and into 2027; "much tighter in calendar 2027 and 2028 than in 2026"; shortages persist "well beyond 2028"
**Verdict: CONFIRMED (core quotes verbatim; "well beyond 2028" is a paraphrase of management's actual wording, not a verbatim quote).**
- "Much tighter": CEO Sanjay Mehrotra, prepared remarks, FQ4 2026 call, Sep 30, 2026: **"We expect memory and storage supply-demand conditions to be much tighter in calendar 2027 and 2028 than they were in 2026"** — verbatim, confirmed by Network World (Oct 1, 2026), Zacks (Oct 1, 2026), Computerworld/PCMag via multiple outlets.
- HBM sold out: Mehrotra on the same call: **"for 2027, a large part of the volume is already sold out for 2027 for HBM, and the prices are much higher than 2026 prices"**; more than 75% of Micron's total 2027 output is already committed, and most customer discussions have moved to 2028 supply (Ars Technica / The Register / Computerworld, Oct 1, 2026).
- "Well beyond 2028": Mehrotra's actual words were **"We do not have line of sight to when supply and demand will return to balance"** and that Micron expects both DRAM and NAND markets to remain supply constrained through calendar 2028. That supports a shortage with no visible end date, but the dossier's phrase "shortages persist well beyond 2028" is a paraphrase, not a verbatim CEO quote. SK Hynix independently titled a section of its Oct 28, 2026 release "Memory supply shortage to persist through 2030".
Sources: Micron FQ4 2026 earnings call, Sep 30, 2026; Network World, Oct 1, 2026; Zacks, Oct 1, 2026; Traictory, Oct 6, 2026; SK Hynix Q3 2026 release, Oct 28, 2026.

### 4. HBM revenue share Q2 2026: SK Hynix 50%, Samsung 33%, Micron 18% (Counterpoint Research)
**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**
Counterpoint Research's quarterly HBM tracker, published **September 2–3, 2026**: SK Hynix **50%**, Samsung **33%**, Micron **18%** of global HBM **revenue** (shares sum to 101% due to rounding). Prior quarters: Q1 2026 was 58/21/21; Q2 2025 was 64/15/21. Same release put Q2 2026 overall DRAM revenue share at Samsung 38%, SK Hynix 25%, Micron 24%, CXMT 10%. Multiple independent summaries (asiaai.fyi, safeguard.sh, pacifictape.com, sourcebyspec.com, indmoney.com) reproduce the identical figures.
Sources: Counterpoint Research Memory Tracker, Sep 2–3, 2026 (via Edaily, Sep 3, 2026, and multiple secondary summaries, Oct 2026).

### 5. SANITY CHECK — FQ4 2026 revenue $54.23bn (+379% y/y) and FY2026 revenue $133.19bn
**Verdict: CONFIRMED — the dossier's figures are correct as reported. They are NOT wrong, and they are NOT a typo.**
Micron's own earnings release, **September 30, 2026** (GlobeNewswire / investors.micron.com; Form 8-K Exhibit 99.1): **FQ4 2026 revenue $54.23 billion** (vs $41.46bn in FQ3 2026 and $11.32bn in FQ4 2025, i.e. +379% y/y), GAAP net income $37.70bn ($32.87/diluted share), non-GAAP EPS $33.42, GAAP gross margin 86.8% (87.0% non-GAAP). **Full fiscal 2026 revenue $133.19 billion** vs $37.38bn in fiscal 2025 (+256%), GAAP net income $84.97bn. DRAM revenue $39.8bn in the quarter (73% of total); FQ1 2027 guidance ~$61.5bn revenue. The figures are an order of magnitude above Micron's pre-2026 scale because the scale itself changed: FQ4 2025 revenue was $11.32bn, and DRAM/NAND prices rose triple digits through 2026 on the AI memory shortage. One legitimate context note: fiscal 2026 was a 53-week year and Q4 ran 14 weeks vs 13 in Q3, so the sequential +31% overstates the run-rate slightly (per-week revenue growth was ~21.5%, per Convexity's arithmetic). The dossier's flag was prudent, but the numbers check out against the primary source.
Sources: Micron Technology, "Reports Record Fiscal Fourth-Quarter and Full-Year 2026 Results," Sep 30, 2026 (investors.micron.com); CNBC/LSEG consensus $51.07bn (via MEXC, Leveraged Shares, Oct 2026).

## Overall verdict

MU's headline evidence survives verification essentially intact. The two figures the dossier itself flagged as implausible — FQ4 2026 revenue of $54.23bn (+379% y/y) and FY2026 revenue of $133.19bn — are exactly what Micron reported in its own September 30, 2026 earnings release, so the sanity check resolves in the dossier's favour: the numbers are real, and the "order of magnitude above pre-2026 scale" reflects a genuine repricing of the memory market (FQ4 2025 revenue was $11.32bn), not a data error. The $100bn contract floor is confirmed, though its first disclosure was June 24, 2026 rather than August; the $32bn of customer financial commitments (mostly cash deposits, up from $22bn) is confirmed; the "much tighter in calendar 2027 and 2028" CEO guidance is verbatim, and the sold-out HBM position is confirmed with >75% of 2027 output already committed — the only softening is that "shortages persist well beyond 2028" is a paraphrase of Mehrotra's "no line of sight to when supply and demand will return to balance," not a direct quote. The HBM share figures (SK Hynix 50%, Samsung 33%, Micron 18%) are confirmed by Counterpoint Research, which also confirms the dossier's own caveat that Micron is the #3 HBM supplier, not the leader. The CDNS and CF claims all check out against primary sources as well, with no contradictions anywhere in the set. The panel may weight MU's shortage thesis as verified; the remaining debate is the one the dossier already names — cyclicality and duration risk, and Micron's #3 HBM position — not the factual basis of the evidence.
