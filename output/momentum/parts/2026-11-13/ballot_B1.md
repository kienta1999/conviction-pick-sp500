# Panelist B — Growth / Momentum Investor — Momentum 2026-11-13

Lens: revenue acceleration, earnings revisions, and price momentum — the fastest compounder. All figures from the 2026-11-13 research dossier and the Nov 12 screen snapshot.

## Single-pick ballot

- **Top pick:** MU
- **Runner-up:** ANET
- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** MU is the fastest compounder in the set by a wide margin: TTM revenue growth +135.6%, 12-month return +220.7%, and FQ4 2026 revenue of $54.23bn (+379% y/y) with non-GAAP gross margin 87% in Q4, on a forward P/E of just 3.9x at the $811.30 Nov 12 price — still well below the $1,213.37 June record close. The driver is a genuine sold-out shortage, not a demand story: HBM consumes ~3x the wafer capacity per GB of ordinary DRAM, AI servers + HBM now consume close to 70% of total DRAM output, and Micron expects supply-demand to be much tighter in calendar 2027 and 2028 than in 2026. FQ1 2027 guidance (revenue ~$61.5bn, adjusted EPS ~$38.15) points to sequential growth every quarter of fiscal 2027, and the analyst mean target of $1,561.90 (+92.5%) is the highest upside in the dossier.
- **Key shortage/backlog evidence:** ~$100bn in binding multi-year AI memory contracts, backed by $32bn in customer financial commitments, mostly cash prepayments — customers are paying cash years in advance for HBM supply already sold out through 2026 and into 2027, with calendar 2027 HBM agreed "with significant price increases year over year."
- **Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk:** low — hyperscalers cannot realistically in-source memory: only three firms on earth make HBM at scale, fabs cost tens of billions and take years (Micron's New York fab first wafer not until 2030), and HBM requires Nvidia/AMD/Google qualification that locks in supply for years. The caveat is that the moat protects the oligopoly, not Micron specifically — Micron is #3 in HBM (18% share, down from 21%) behind SK Hynix (50%) and Samsung (33%), so customers dual-/triple-source among the three.
- **12-18 month return scenario:** base +40–60% (to roughly $1,130–$1,300, reclaiming the June $1,213 peak) if the 2027–28 shortage persists as Micron, SK Hynix and Samsung all guide; bull +90–120% (to roughly $1,550–$1,800, around the $1,561.90 analyst mean target) if HBM price increases hold through 2027 and FQ1 guidance converts into the guided sequential growth every quarter.
- **Top risk:** Cyclicality / duration risk — the market is re-pricing how long 80%+ gross margins last, not current earnings; panic buying / double-ordering could flip severe shortage into oversupply, and Micron is already losing HBM share to Samsung (15% → 33% in a year).
- **Conviction (1-10):** 8
