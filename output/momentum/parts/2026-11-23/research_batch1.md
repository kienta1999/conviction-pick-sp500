## LLY — Eli Lilly and Company

### 1. Shortage thesis
Yes — the cleanest structural-shortage story in the S&P 500. Demand for GLP-1/incretin drugs (tirzepatide: Mounjaro for diabetes, Zepbound for obesity) has exceeded supply since the Zepbound launch in late 2023. Penetration is still below 5% of eligible patients in the US and below 1% globally, against a market projected to exceed $130bn by 2030 (DataToBrief). Lilly's own Q3 2026 release (29 Oct 2026) says revenue growth was "driven by broad global consumer demand for Mounjaro and Zepbound" plus expanded US coverage for obesity care; volume rose 53% YoY while realized prices fell 15% — i.e. the constraint is how much Lilly can make, not how much it can sell. One analysis puts it bluntly: "Lilly's revenue trajectory through 2027 is largely a function of capacity, not demand" (TickerXray). Capacity build: $18bn+ committed since 2023, including Lebanon, Indiana ($9bn total after a further $5.3bn add-on; API production starting late 2026, scaling through 2028), Concord NC ($2.5bn), Limerick Ireland ($1.8bn), Research Triangle Park NC ($4.5bn). New GLP-1 plants take 3–4 years from groundbreaking to commercial production.

### 2. Backlog / order book
Pharma has no order backlog in the industrial sense — **not applicable / not found** as a reported figure. The closest proxy is the capacity ramp vs demand: 2025 capacity supported ~$30bn incretin revenue; the 2026 ramp targets $45–50bn supportable revenue and 2027 above $60bn (TickerXray). Q3 2026 incretins: Mounjaro $10.72bn (+64.6% YoY), Zepbound $5.35bn (+49.2%), oral Foundayo (orforglipron) $184m in its second quarter; incretins were 66.6% of the $24.4bn quarter (SEC filing analysis, 10-Q). FY2026 revenue guidance raised three times during the year, to $91–92bn (from $80–83bn in Feb).

### 3. Category position
#1 and pulling away. Lilly and Novo Nordisk together control ~90% of the GLP-1 market; Lilly is gaining share on superior efficacy (tirzepatide ~22.5% average weight loss vs ~15% for semaglutide) and manufacturing scale. The divergence is stark in Q3 2026: Lilly incretins +65%/+49% while Novo's obesity-care sales grew only +15% in Q2 2026 (Wegovy injectable +1%) and Novo guided 2026 adjusted sales growth of 0% to −6% at constant exchange rates. Market cap ~$1.2tn vs Novo far behind; within the screen's Pharmaceuticals sub-industry LLY is rank #1, 1.84x the #2 name by market cap.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk
Moat: patents on tirzepatide/retatrutide/orforglipron molecules, plus the manufacturing moat — sterile fill-finish and synthetic-peptide API capacity that takes 3–4 years and billions to build, FDA-qualified site by site. No customer can in-source this: patients/payers don't manufacture drugs; the threat is competitor supply, not customer in-sourcing. Real threats: Novo (Wegovy pill launched in the US 5 Jan 2026, 265k+ weekly prescriptions by Jul 2026), compounding pharmacies, and patent cliffs in the 2030s. **Irreplaceability score: 8/10** — the molecule + capacity combination is very hard to replicate, but it is a two-player race, not a monopoly.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- Q3 2026 (29 Oct 2026): revenue $24.4bn, +39% YoY, beat consensus by ~8%; non-GAAP EPS $7.48 (incl. $4.57 acquired IPR&D charges); FY26 revenue guidance raised $5.5bn at the midpoint to $91–92bn; underlying non-GAAP EPS raised $4.07 at the midpoint.
- Retatrutide beat Ozempic 2 mg head-to-head in Phase 3 (20–21% weight loss vs 9.4%); US BLA planned Q1 2027.
- Orforglipron (oral GLP-1, brand Foundayo) met its cardiovascular outcomes endpoint (57% lower all-cause death risk vs insulin glargine in ACHIEVE-4); submitted in US/Japan, positive CHMP opinion in EU. Foundayo launched in the US in Q2 2026.
- EloraTZP (eloralintide + tirzepatide) Phase 2: 23.3% weight loss at 48 weeks, 57% better than tirzepatide alone; Phase 3 starting Q4 2026.
- FDA approvals in Q3: Mounjaro for cardiovascular-risk reduction, Onswik once-weekly basal insulin, Inluriyo + Verzenio in breast cancer, expanded Jaypirca indication.
- Medicare GLP-1 coverage expansion ("Medicare GLP-1 bridge") cited on the call as a driver of the guidance raise.

### 6. Risks
- Concentration: incretins are two-thirds of revenue; a safety signal or price event hits the whole company.
- Pricing: realized prices fell 15% in Q3 (US payer pressure; Mounjaro added to China's National Reimbursement Drug List). Volume is outrunning price erosion for now, but the trend is adverse.
- Novo competition, especially the oral Wegovy pill, and compounded tirzepatide.
- Valuation: ~46x TTM EPS / ~40x FY26 non-GAAP EPS guidance midpoint (30 Oct 2026); the market already prices years of flawless execution.
- IPR&D charges are recurring and large ($4.57/share in Q3 alone).

### 7. Shortage score: 9/10
**Verdict:** The purest supply-constrained explosive-growth story in this batch — demand vastly exceeds what Lilly can produce, capacity is the only governor, and the pipeline (retatrutide, oral) extends the lead; the open question is price, not volume.

## EOG — EOG Resources, Inc.

### 1. Shortage thesis
Weak fit. EOG sells a commodity (oil, NGLs, gas) into a global market — there is no product-level shortage it uniquely captures, and it is a price-taker. The nearest "shortage" angle is management's own framing on the Q3 call (5 Nov 2026): oil demand resilient, global inventories "well below their five-year average," and "the supply recovery remains fragile," with Middle East disruption risk — i.e. a tight physical market supporting prices above mid-cycle. The real thesis is cost, not scarcity: EOG's sub-$50 WTI breakeven and premium-inventory discipline let it profit where peers cannot. The screen's +28.7% TTM revenue growth is mostly a price/volume rebound (Q3 revenue $8.55bn, +49% YoY per TipRanks; note the shortlist's revenue-growth field is +42.7% on a different basis — the +28.7% TTM figure is the screen metric).

### 2. Backlog / order book
No order backlog — E&P companies do not take orders; **not applicable**. Closest equivalents: proved reserves 5,514 MMBoe at YE2025 (+16% YoY), a ~12.3-year reserve life at current production; EOG "consistently claims 10+ years of premium inventory" (Double Premium standard: ≥60% after-tax rate of return at $50 WTI; tightened in May 2026 to ~30% returns at $45 oil / $2.50 gas on a bottom-cycle basis). Dorado dry-gas play: 400+ drilling locations, 8–10 years of inventory at Q1 2026 pace. 2026 program: 24 rigs, 585 net wells; oil +5%, total production +14% guided for 2026; low-single-digit oil growth expected for 2027.

### 3. Category position
Not #1 by size — ConocoPhillips is the sub-industry leader (EOG is #2 in Oil & Gas E&P by market cap in this screen, 0.48x COP). But EOG is widely regarded as the lowest-cost large US independent and the benchmark operator: 28-year unbroken dividend, ROCE 19% in 2025 (3-yr avg 24%), finding & development costs among the lowest in the Delaware Basin. Its edge is quality of inventory, not scale.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk
Low irreplaceability in the product sense — a barrel of EOG oil is identical to anyone else's; customers (refiners) can buy from anyone, and the energy transition is a slow structural substitute. The moat is economic, not technological: premium acreage positions (Delaware, Eagle Ford, Utica via the ~$5.6bn Encino acquisition), vertically integrated inputs (own sand, water, chemicals), and the capital-allocation standard that keeps breakevens below $50 WTI. Nobody needs EOG specifically, but few can match its returns at low prices. **Irreplaceability score: 3/10.**

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- Q3 2026 (5 Nov 2026): record oil, NGL and gas production, all above guidance midpoints; adjusted EPS $4.87 vs $4.47 consensus; revenue $8.20bn vs $7.26bn expected (+43% YoY); $2.2bn FCF in the quarter.
- Dividend raised 8% to $4.40/share indicated annual rate; returned >$1.5bn in Q3 ($540m dividends + $1.0bn buybacks); $10.6bn buyback authorization remaining at 30 Sep 2026; share count down 13% since buybacks began in 2023.
- Janus gas processing plant expansion approved (~$300m, doubles capacity to 600 MMcf/d, completion H2 2028, >30% return).
- 2026 FCF guided at $9.7bn at strip pricing; commitment to return ≥70% of FCF to investors.
- Dorado targeting 1 Bcf/d by end-2026, timed to feed LNG export demand via the Verde pipeline.

### 6. Risks
- Pure commodity cyclicality: an OPEC+ supply return, demand slowdown or recession cuts the oil price and EOG's earnings with it — the "shortage" can vanish in months. This is the dominant risk.
- Service-cost inflation in 2027 (only ~60% of services contracted, ~half of casing/tubular secured; higher steel and diesel costs).
- International exploration (Bahrain intermittent operations, UAE commerciality unresolved) is unproven capital at risk.
- Energy transition / ESG pressure on long-term oil demand; reserve value is price-sensitive (PV-10 $38bn vs market cap ~$77bn — the market already pays well above proved-reserve NAV).
- Valuation is cheap (fwd PE 8.74) precisely because the market prices cyclicality, not because it is mispriced.

### 7. Shortage score: 3/10
**Verdict:** A best-in-class low-cost shale operator riding a tight oil market and returning most of its cash — a quality cyclical value play, not a structural-shortage explosive bet; the upside is capped by the oil price, which EOG does not control.

## MA — Mastercard Incorporated

### 1. Shortage thesis
No structural shortage — card networks have no capacity constraint; the "surge" is secular: global shift from cash to electronic payments, cross-border travel recovery, and value-added services. The demand-side evidence is volume growth, not scarcity: Q3 2026 (29 Oct 2026) net revenue $9.81bn, +14% currency-neutral; GDV +7% local currency; cross-border volume +11% (though decelerating: 15% → 14% → 13% → 12% over four quarters per pre-earnings analysis); switched transactions +9%; value-added services +19% currency-neutral. No lead times, no sold-out capacity, no price increases driven by scarcity — pricing power comes from network effects and mix, not shortage.

### 2. Backlog / order book
No backlog — **not applicable**; revenue is transaction-driven. Closest forward indicators: travel-related wins signed in Q3 expected to generate >$20bn in incremental network spend over the agreements' lifetimes; 100 new affluent credit/debit programs won and 25+ World Legend programs launched in 2026; 15 Essential Debit programs across 7 markets addressing up to 70m unbanked adults; 3.8bn Mastercard/Maestro cards in issue (+5% YoY); contactless at 81% of in-person switched transactions. Full-year 2026 revenue guidance maintained at the high end of low-double-digit growth (currency-neutral, ex-inorganic).

### 3. Category position
#2 globally behind Visa. Scale: ~$10.6tn GDV (2025), 175bn+ switched transactions; Visa ~$16.7tn total volume / $14.2tn payments volume (FY to Sep 2025). In the US, Visa took 70.4% of combined Visa+Mastercard spend ($7.03tn of $9.99tn in 2025). Mastercard is stronger in Europe, Africa and the Middle East and with fintech issuers (Monzo, Revolut). Together Visa + Mastercard control >70% of global card market share. Within the screen's sub-industry MA is #2 (0.73x Visa's market cap).

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk
Moat: a two-sided network — 3.8bn cards, acceptance at 150m+ merchant locations, issuing/acquiring bank relationships built over decades, plus fraud/authentication infrastructure and 60% of services being network-linked (reinforcing loop). Disintermediation threats are real and named: (a) account-to-account rails — India's UPI, Brazil's Pix, Europe's Wero/EPI (Oct 2026: Bancomat, Bizum, EPI/Wero, SIBS, Vipps linked ~130m users across 13 countries); (b) stablecoins — $33tn in stablecoin transfer value in 2025 (+72%), though real payments were only ~$390bn (McKinsey–Artemis, Feb 2026) and ~90% of stablecoin volume is crypto trading, not payments; (c) AI agents optimizing away card fees (Citrini Research, Feb 2026). Mastercard's response is to own the new rails: Agent Pay for agentic commerce, and the BVNK stablecoin-infrastructure acquisition (up to $1.8bn, the largest crypto deal on record, announced by Oct 2026). **Irreplaceability score: 7/10** — the network is extremely hard to replicate, but the rails themselves can be bypassed at the margin, and that margin is growing.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- Q3 2026 (29–30 Oct 2026): adjusted EPS $5.31 (+21%), beat $5.14 consensus; revenue $9.81bn beat $9.63bn; net income +18%; maintained full-year revenue guidance.
- Value-added services +19% — the fastest-growing, highest-margin engine (cybersecurity, analytics, authentication).
- Agent Pay (agentic commerce) rollout; stablecoin capabilities expanded via BVNK.
- $3.0bn of buybacks in Q3 plus $648m through 26 Oct 2026.
- Affluent spend growing 2x overall credit; travel wins >$20bn lifetime incremental spend.

### 6. Risks
- Cross-border deceleration (15% → 11–12%) from EU customs fees, Middle East conflict weighing on travel, softer intra-European activity; Q4 faces a ~1pp FX headwind and higher rebates/incentives as a share of gross assessments.
- Client incentives rising (Visa's grew 18% last quarter — the same pressure applies across the duopoly) — banks extract more of the economics.
- Regulatory: US interchange scrutiny and the floated 10% credit-card interest-rate cap; A2A rails in India/Brazil/Europe structurally bypassing schemes.
- Stablecoin/AI-agent disintermediation is a slow-burn existential risk, and the stocks have already sold off 18–19% from peaks on it.
- Valuation: fwd PE 27.01 for low-teens revenue growth — no longer cheap for a maturing network.

### 7. Shortage score: 4/10
**Verdict:** A superb toll-booth compounder with a durable network moat and a fast-growing services engine, but there is no shortage here — growth is steady secular volume, and the real story is defending the toll against A2A rails, stablecoins and AI agents.

## V — Visa Inc.

### 1. Shortage thesis
Same as MA: no shortage, secular volume growth. Fiscal Q4 2026 (27 Oct 2026) was Visa's strongest year in 15+ years ex-pandemic recovery: net revenue $12.1bn, +13%; FY26 revenue $45.9bn, +15% (record). Payments volume +9% constant dollars, cross-border volume +14% ex-intra-Europe (accelerating from 12% in fiscal Q3; travel +13%, e-commerce +17%, the latter partly driven by spending on AI platforms), processed transactions 74.2bn in the quarter (+10%), 281.3bn for the year (+9%). Visa Direct >15bn transactions / ~$1.5tn volume in FY26. As with MA, there is no capacity constraint or scarcity pricing — the "surge" is durable transaction growth, and cross-border is re-accelerating.

### 2. Backlog / order book
No backlog — **not applicable**. Forward indicators: commercial payments volume +13% constant dollars (faster than overall volume, consistent with Q3); processing penetration ~80% globally; 16bn+ tokens live; Visa Direct's $1.5tn annual volume; FY26 was a record year with momentum into October (US payments volume +14% and processed transactions +12% in early October per the earnings deck; cross-border still ~13% through 21 Oct). Dividend raised 16% to $0.780/quarter; $24.4bn buyback authorization remaining at 30 Sep 2026; $5.2bn returned in fiscal Q4.

### 3. Category position
#1 globally, clearly. ~$16.7tn total volume / $14.2tn payments volume (FY to Sep 2025), 257.5bn processed transactions; dominant in the US (70.4% of US Visa+Mastercard spend), UK debit, Latin America. Within the screen's sub-industry V is #1, 1.38x Mastercard by market cap. The two together hold >70% of global card share; Amex/Discover are distant.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk
Same moat as MA, larger: the biggest acceptance network on earth, 150m+ merchant locations, trust/brand, fraud and tokenization infrastructure (16bn tokens), and the "network of networks" strategy (Visa Direct, Visa Intelligent Commerce for AI agents, stablecoin settlement pilots including a Lloyds test in the UK; reported talks with Mastercard/Stripe/Coinbase on a joint stablecoin platform, Jun 2026). CEO Ryan McInerney (Q4 FY25 call, Jan 2026): "no product-market fit for stablecoin payments" in developed markets. Threats are identical to MA's: A2A rails, stablecoins (real payments only ~$390bn vs Visa's $14.5tn), AI agents routing around interchange, regulation (interchange, 10% rate-cap proposal). Visa's scale makes it marginally harder to displace than MA. **Irreplaceability score: 8/10.**

### 5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months)
- Fiscal Q4 2026 (27 Oct 2026): non-GAAP EPS $3.48 (+17%), beat $3.43 consensus; revenue $12.14bn beat $12.09bn; GAAP EPS +22%; record FY26 revenue $45.9bn (+15%).
- Cross-border re-acceleration to 14% (from 12%), with e-commerce +17% and travel +13%; World Cup period boosted US volume (+10%, credit +11%).
- Dividend +16% to $0.780/share; $5.2bn returned in Q4.
- Visa Intelligent Commerce and Visa CLI (letting AI agents trigger card payments) — positioning to be the rails for agentic commerce rather than being bypassed by it.
- Other revenue +40% to $1.7bn in Q4 (value-added services strength, mirroring MA).

### 6. Risks
- Same duopoly risks as MA: interchange regulation, the 10% interest-rate-cap proposal, A2A/stablecoin bypass, client incentives rising.
- Cross-border strength is partly cyclical (travel, World Cup) and could normalize; Middle East conflict still weighs on some corridors.
- Consumer-spending sensitivity: a US slowdown hits payments volume directly, and Visa's premium valuation (fwd PE 23.45, cheaper than MA's 27.01 but still full) assumes growth persists.
- The stablecoin/AI-agent threat, while small in real payments today (~0.02% of global payments), is the reason both stocks are 18–19% off peaks — multiple compression risk if the narrative hardens.

### 7. Shortage score: 4/10
**Verdict:** The #1 global payments toll booth, growing at its fastest rate in 15 years with cross-border re-accelerating — a high-quality compounder, but like MA it is a secular-volume story, not a shortage; explosive upside would need a re-rating, not a supply crunch.
