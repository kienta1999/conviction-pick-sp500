## LLY — Eli Lilly

### Claim 1 — Q3 2026: revenue $24.4bn, +39% YoY; Mounjaro $10.72bn (+65%), Zepbound $5.35bn (+49%); volume +53%, realised prices −15%
**Verdict: CONFIRMED**
- Source: Lilly Q3 2026 earnings release, SEC Form 8-K Ex-99.1, 29 Oct 2026 (sec.gov): revenue $24,397m, +39% YoY; "driven by a 53% increase in volume, partially offset by a 15% decrease in realized prices"; Mounjaro $10,723m, +65%; Zepbound $5,352m, +49%.
- Corroborated by WSJ and Morningstar/Dow Jones, 29 Oct 2026 (same figures).
- No correction. All numbers match to the dollar/point as stated.

### Claim 2 — FY2026 guidance raised three times to $91–92bn revenue
**Verdict: CONFIRMED**
- Source: Lilly Q3 2026 earnings release, 29 Oct 2026: revenue guidance raised to "$91.0 to $92.0 billion" from "$85 to $87 billion" (+$5.5bn at the midpoint).
- Source: MarketWatch, 29 Oct 2026: "raised its outlook for the third time this year"; the year began at $80–83bn, was $85–87bn by August, now $91–92bn.
- No correction. Note for completeness: the same release cut reported non-GAAP EPS guidance to $35–36 (from $35.50–36.50) because $4.57/share of acquired IPR&D charges more than offset a $4.07 underlying EPS raise — revenue was raised, EPS was not.

### Claim 3 — Capacity: $18bn+ committed since 2023, incl. Lebanon, Indiana (~$9bn); new GLP-1 plants take 3–4 years to commercial production
**Verdict: CONTRADICTED (in part — the dollar figures are right, the dating is wrong)**
- Lebanon ~$9bn: CONFIRMED. Lilly press release, 24 May 2024: investment in the Lebanon, Indiana site raised from $3.7bn to $9bn (+$5.3bn) for tirzepatide API production; production to begin late 2026, scaling through 2028.
- $18bn+ total: CONFIRMED as a figure, CONTRADICTED as dated. The same 24 May 2024 Lilly release states: "Since 2020, Lilly has committed more than $16 billion to develop new manufacturing sites in the U.S. and Europe" plus $1.2bn updating existing facilities, "together, these manufacturing investments total more than $18 billion." **The $18bn figure is measured since 2020, not since 2023.** The dossier's "since 2023" is wrong.
- Further context: the total has since grown well past $18bn — Lilly's Indiana commitments alone passed $21bn in May 2026, and Nov 2026 reporting puts total US manufacturing commitments since 2020 above $50bn. So "$18bn+ committed" is true but stale/understated as a current figure.
- 3–4 years to commercial production: broadly CONSISTENT, not precisely sourced. Lebanon broke ground April 2023 and begins making medicines late 2026 / opens 2027 — roughly 3.5–4 years from groundbreaking to production. A secondary source (PepCodex, on Lilly's $4bn 2025 expansion) states facilities "require 2–3 years to build" with first production 2028 and full capacity 2030. The 3–4 year claim is a reasonable characterisation of the Lebanon timeline but I found no Lilly statement giving "3–4 years" as a general rule.

### Claim 4 — Retatrutide beat Ozempic head-to-head (trial result reported 2026); BLA planned for Q1 2027
**Verdict: CONFIRMED**
- Source: Lilly topline readout, 28 Oct 2026, reported by BioSpace, Fierce Biotech and BioPharma Dive the same day: TRANSCEND-T2D-2 (Phase 3, 1,298 adults with type 2 diabetes) — retatrutide 9 mg and 12 mg lowered A1C 2.2 percentage points vs 1.8 for Ozempic 2 mg at 80 weeks; weight loss 19.5% (9 mg) and 20.9% (12 mg) vs 9.4% for Ozempic. Met primary and all key secondary endpoints; first head-to-head vs another incretin in Phase 3.
- Filing: CONFIRMED. BioSpace/Fierce Biotech, Oct 2026: "Lilly plans to file for FDA approval in the first quarter of 2027" (previously expected later in 2026).
- Nuances (not contradictions): (a) BioPharma Dive notes the 9 mg A1C advantage vs Ozempic was statistically "nominal" — only the 12 mg dose's A1C win was fully significant; (b) retatrutide had higher adverse-event/discontinuation rates (diarrhoea 32.7%/28.3% vs 26.4%; discontinuations 4.8%/7.9% vs 2.7%); (c) the "BLA" label is the dossier's wording — company/press sources say "file for FDA approval"; BioSpace notes an ongoing legal dispute over whether retatrutide is a biologic (which would determine BLA vs NDA pathway), so calling the Q1 2027 filing a BLA specifically is not verified.

### Claim 5 — Moat: GLP-1 demand exceeds supply since late 2023; penetration below 5% of eligible US patients and below 1% globally
**Verdict: CONTRADICTED (as stated — direction right, numbers not supported)**
- Demand > supply since late 2023: broadly SUPPORTED historically. FDA placed semaglutide and tirzepatide on its drug-shortage list in 2022; shortages persisted through 2023 (American Diabetes Association, Clin Diabetes, 2023: shortages "emerged in late 2022 and have persisted through Q1 2023" from demand outrunning production); Novo confirmed Wegovy starter-dose shortages in Aug 2023. Caveat: the FDA shortage designations have since been resolved for tirzepatide (Dec 2024) — the claim is a historical statement, not a current shortage designation, and should be framed that way.
- Penetration "<5% US / <1% globally": NOT supported by the sources found, and contradicted by Lilly's own CEO. Reuters, Apr 2026 (via Benefits and Pensions Monitor): Lilly CEO David Ricks said "today just 1 in 10 people who are overweight or obese use GLP-1s" (~10%, not <5%), and "today we're treating 21 or 22 million" of ~500 million global candidates — ~4.2–4.4% globally, not below 1%. Separately, the JAMA Summit on Obesity report (Nov 2026, via AJMC) estimates ~1 in 9 US adults (~11%) currently use a GLP-1 RA. WHO (2026) says fewer than 10% of people who could benefit have access — consistent with low penetration, but not with "<1% globally".
- The only source found stating "<5% US" is a market-research vendor page (ResearchIntelo, GLP-1 obesity drug market report) — low-quality, single-source, and inconsistent with the CEO and JAMA figures above. The dossier cites "DataToBrief" for the market-size projection, not for these penetration figures.
- Correction: the moat direction (vast untreated eligible population, demand historically outrunning supply) is real, but the panel should not cite "<5% US / <1% global" — defensible figures are ~10% of overweight/obese Americans on GLP-1s and ~4% of ~500m global candidates treated, per Lilly's CEO (Apr 2026).

## MSI — Motorola Solutions

### Claim 6 — Record backlog $16.6bn at Q3 2026, +14% YoY (~1.27x guided FY2026 revenue of ~$13.05bn), ~95% government customers
**Verdict: CONFIRMED (backlog, growth, ratio); the "~95% government" sub-element is UNVERIFIED**
- Source: Motorola Solutions Q3 2026 earnings release, SEC 8-K Ex-99.1, 4 Nov 2026: "Record ending backlog of $16.6 billion, up 14% or $2.1 billion from the year-ago quarter driven by record Q3 orders in all three technologies"; Products & Systems Integration backlog +$635m to ~$4.2bn (+18%), Software & Services +$1.4bn to ~$12.4bn (+13%).
- FY2026 revenue guidance ~$13.05bn: CONFIRMED (same release / Q3 call, 4 Nov 2026 — raised from $12.975bn). Arithmetic: $16.6bn / $13.05bn = 1.27x — confirmed.
- "~95% government customers": UNVERIFIED from primary sources. The Q3 release and call transcript excerpts retrieved do not state a government share of backlog. The dossier attributes it to Wedgewood Partners' Q3 2026 letter; a secondary source (Tickeron) says only "the vast majority of its backlog [is] tied to government contracts" without a number. I could not independently confirm 95% in the searches run. Treat as an unverified secondary claim, not a company disclosure.

**Verdict: CONFIRMED**

**Verdict: CONFIRMED**

## MSFT — Microsoft

**Verdict: CONFIRMED**
- Source: Microsoft FY27 Q1 earnings call transcript (microsoft.com, 28 Oct 2026): "Commercial remaining performance obligation grew 73% to $677 billion, with a smaller sequential decline relative to historical seasonality due to the large Azure commitment this quarter."
- Quote: CONFIRMED verbatim from the same official transcript. In the Q2 outlook section, Hood said: "In Azure, we expect revenue growth of 49 to 50% in constant currency with continued efficiency gains across our CPU and GPU fleet… Customer demand continues to exceed available supply." The sentence sits in the Azure-capacity context — the claim's attribution to Azure AI capacity is fair.
- Corroboration: Zacks, 29 Oct 2026: "Commercial remaining performance obligation reached $677 billion, up 73% year over year, with a weighted average duration of 2.3 years."

### Claim 10 — Azure grew 48% YoY in the quarter to $33.2bn; capex $45.2bn in the quarter, guided to >$50bn next quarter
**Verdict: CONFIRMED**
- Source: Microsoft FY27 Q1 earnings call / release, 28 Oct 2026: Azure revenue $33.2bn ($33.165bn), +48% YoY — first-ever dollar disclosure for Azure; "Capital expenditures were $45.2 billion, as some third-party capacity deliveries we expected in Q1 were delayed" (includes finance leases; cash PP&E was $39.4bn).
- Next-quarter capex >$50bn: CONFIRMED. Call summaries (Tiger Brokers/iTiger, Motley Fool transcript summary, Oct 2026): "Hood expects second-quarter capital spending to exceed $50 billion, with a full-year total of roughly $170 billion, accelerating further in the second half."
- No correction. Note: the $45.2bn came in below the ~$50.7bn consensus because of the delayed third-party deliveries — it is not a demand signal.

### Claim 11 — Moat: OpenAI is ~45% of the RPO (secondary estimate) and is diversifying compute to AWS — flag whether this figure is disclosed or only a secondary estimate
**Verdict: CONTRADICTED (on the characterisation of the 45% figure); the AWS-diversification leg is CONFIRMED**
- The 45% figure is NOT merely a secondary estimate — it was company-disclosed. Source: Constellation Research, 28 Jan 2026, reporting Microsoft's Q2 FY2026 call: CFO Amy Hood said about 45% of the $625bn commercial RPO at 31 Dec 2025 came from OpenAI (~$281bn). Corroborated by InvestMoat's Q1 FY27 analysis (Nov 2026): "Microsoft said in January that about 45% of the $625 billion was OpenAI, as Constellation Research reported"; TheStreet (Nov 2026): "In January, Hood said about 45% of the company's remaining commercial performance obligation came from OpenAI."
- **Material correction:** the 45% applies to the Dec 2025 RPO of $625bn, NOT to the current $677bn. Microsoft has not repeated the OpenAI split since January — on later calls it gave only the ex-OpenAI growth rate (RPO ex-OpenAI +25% YoY at Jun 2026; all Q4 sequential growth from non-frontier-model customers). Applying 45% to today's $677bn is therefore an extrapolation, and third-party estimates of the current share range ~30–40% (Trend Core note, Oct 2026). The dossier's "secondary estimate, not company-disclosed" gets this backwards: the number was disclosed, but it is stale.
- OpenAI diversifying compute to AWS: CONFIRMED. OpenAI signed a $38bn, 7-year AWS compute agreement (announced 3 Nov 2025, Reuters) and expanded it by $100bn over 8 years on 27 Feb 2026 (Amazon 8-K exhibit; ~2 GW Trainium). The Microsoft–OpenAI partnership was amended 27 Apr 2026, removing Microsoft's right of first refusal on compute and letting OpenAI serve products across clouds, with Microsoft remaining primary cloud partner. OpenAI also has a ~$300bn Oracle/Stargate commitment (Sep 2025). Diversification is real and large — AWS alone is now ~$138bn of OpenAI compute commitments vs $250bn incremental Azure (Oct 2025).
