## 1. SINGLE-PICK BALLOT

- Top pick: LLY
- Runner-up: MSI
- Thesis (3-5 sentences): Lilly is the only name in the set where demand, moat and quality metrics all point the same way: GLP-1 demand has exceeded supply since the Zepbound launch in late 2023, with penetration still below 5% of eligible US patients and below 1% globally against a market projected above $130bn by 2030. Lilly is pulling away from the only rival that matters — Q3 incretins grew +65%/+49% while Novo guided 2026 sales growth of 0% to −6% — on superior efficacy and on manufacturing scale that takes 3–4 years and billions to replicate. The quality is elite by the screen's own numbers: 83.6% gross margin, 51.3% operating margin, 86% ROE, net debt/EBITDA 0.96x, and Q3 revenue +39% with FY2026 guidance raised three times to $91–92bn. The pipeline (retatrutide beating Ozempic head-to-head, oral orforglipron) extends the lead rather than defending it.
- Key shortage/backlog evidence: Q3 2026 volume rose 53% YoY while realized prices fell 15% — revenue still grew 39% because the only governor on growth is how much Lilly can make, not how much it can sell ("revenue trajectory through 2027 is largely a function of capacity, not demand").
- Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk: low — no customer can in-source a drug: patients and payers do not manufacture medicines, and the moat is patents on tirzepatide/retatrutide/orforglipron plus FDA-qualified sterile fill-finish and peptide-API capacity that takes 3–4 years to build. The real threat is competitor supply (Novo's oral Wegovy pill, compounding), not customer in-sourcing or a substitute technology routing around the product.
- 12-18 month return scenario: base +15–25% (capacity ramp toward $45–50bn supportable incretin revenue in 2026 and above $60bn in 2027, plus the $1,470.06 analyst mean target, +9.1% from $1,347.11, as a floor rather than a ceiling); bull +40–60% if retatrutide's Q1 2027 BLA, oral Foundayo scale-up and Medicare coverage expansion compound on top of the capacity ramp.
- Top risk: Concentration — incretins are 66.6% of revenue, so a safety signal, a Novo oral-pill share shift, or accelerating price erosion (realized prices already −15% in Q3) hits the whole company; valuation at ~40x FY26 non-GAAP EPS already prices years of flawless execution.
- Conviction (1-10): 8

### Top 3 — most compelling shortage/backlog or moat data point, and 12–18mo scenarios

**LLY:** The single most compelling fact is that volume grew 53% YoY in Q3 while realized prices fell 15% and revenue still rose 39% — demand is not the constraint, capacity is, and Lilly is adding $18bn+ of capacity since 2023 (Lebanon IN $9bn, RTP NC $4.5bn) that only comes online over 3–4 years, so the shortage persists into 2027. Base +15–25% on the capacity ramp and guidance raises; bull +40–60% if retatrutide's Q1 2027 BLA and the oral drug scale on top.

**MSI:** The single most compelling fact is the record $16.6bn backlog (+14% YoY, +$1B sequentially, ~1.27x guided FY2026 revenue of ~$13.05bn), ~95% of it government customers, on mission-critical P25 radio infrastructure that agencies cannot rip and replace — and Silvus lead times are still 12–14 weeks after doubling capacity, i.e. demand still outruns supply. Base +10–20% as the backlog converts and Silvus grows ~30% in 2027; bull +30–40% if defence-drone demand keeps compounding and memory-cost pressure is passed through via pricing.

**WST:** The single most compelling fact is qualification lock-in: a stopper/seal is a drug-contact component, so switching supplier requires re-validation, regulatory filings and stability studies — no pharma company re-qualifies a closure to save pennies on ~1% of a biologic's cost — and GLP-1-related HVP revenue is growing "well into the 30s%" with 200+ HVP upgrade projects added in 2026 on the EU GMP Annex 1 tailwind. Base +15–25% (analyst mean target $471.00, +20.7% from $390.30) as HVP mix compounds; bull +35–50% if the GLP-1 ramp and upgrade cycle sustain 30%+ HVP growth without a GLP-1 demand wobble.

### Deliberately left out of the top 10

Left out: EOG, COP, CVX, ABBV, VEEV. EOG (irreplaceability 3/10), COP (2/10) and CVX (3/10) sell a fungible commodity — any customer can buy the identical barrel from anyone, the upside is the oil price which none of them controls, and COP/CVX's war premium is already fading (WTI ~$113 on 30 Oct to ~$83 by 19 Nov). ABBV is a share war, not a moat: payers/PBMs can substitute via annual formulary placement, and there is zero supply constraint. VEEV's moat is real but no longer a monopoly — the forced Vault CRM migration is a fresh vendor decision for every customer and the Salesforce+IQVIA alliance is winning some of the biggest names, so the "contest, not monopoly" risk fails this lens's irreplaceability bar.

### Lens note — why MSFT is only 9th despite the biggest backlog

MSFT has the largest shortage evidence in the dossier (RPO $677bn, +73% YoY; CFO: "customer demand continues to exceed our available supply") and the largest analyst upside (+31.3%), but this lens's trap is disintermediation/in-sourcing, and the marginal AI workload that drives the growth is exactly where that trap bites: the dossier scores the AI workload only 5/10 on irreplaceability, OpenAI is ~45% of RPO (secondary estimate) while diversifying to other clouds including a reported ~$100bn AWS agreement, and the biggest customers all build their own silicon (AWS Trainium/Graviton, Google TPU) — Microsoft's own Maia/Cobalt programme proves the direction of travel. A customer cannot replicate Azure's fleet, but it can multi-cloud and self-supply the AI silicon, so the backlog is less locked-in than LLY's, MSI's or WST's.
