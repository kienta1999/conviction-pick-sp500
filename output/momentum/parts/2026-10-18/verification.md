## MU (Micron Technology)

### Claim 1 — ~$150B RPO across 26 take-or-pay SCAs, ~$32B customer financial commitments, covering >35% of expected revenue through 2030 (FQ4 FY2026 call, 30 Sep 2026); deposits received to date
**Verdict: CONFIRMED (with deposit detail below).**
- Micron reported FQ4 FY2026 results after close on 30 Sep 2026 for the quarter ended 3 Sep 2026. Multiple independent reports of the call/prepared remarks agree: strategic customer agreements rose from 16 to 26, all with take-or-pay volume commitments; management estimates they cover more than 35% of revenue through 2030 (some now extend into 2031); remaining performance obligations of ~$150B; customer financial commitments of $32B.
- Qualifier the dossier should keep: the $150B RPO counts **only** SCAs with a determined pricing framework (fixed or floor-and-ceiling — roughly three-quarters of expected SCA revenue), at committed volumes and **minimum** pricing; CFO Mark Murphy called it "inherently conservative."
- Deposits actually received: CFO on the call — "Customer cash deposits received during fiscal Q4 were $12.3 billion. Customer cash deposits on our balance sheet at the end of fiscal Q4 were $12.7 billion" (booked in financing cash flow, not revenue/FCF; deposits are unrestricted and returnable in the latter half of each agreement term if minimum purchases are met). The 8-K-based analysis puts FY2026 deposits received at $12.75B and noncurrent customer contract liabilities at $12.90B at 3 Sep 2026 (vs $568M at 28 May 2026). So of the $32B of commitments ("the vast majority" cash deposits per CEO Mehrotra), only ~$12.7B was actually held as cash deposits at quarter-end — the $32B is a commitment total, NOT cash in hand.
- Sources: Micron FQ4 FY2026 earnings call transcript (Longbridge / TradingKey republication), 30 Sep 2026; Zacks earnings recap, 30 Sep–1 Oct 2026; SignalStack 8-K analysis, ~2 Oct 2026; CNBC via AIStockWire, 30 Sep 2026.

### Claim 2 — HBM sold out CY2026; large majority of CY2027 HBM volume agreed at significantly higher prices; only ~50–66% of key customers' HBM demand can be satisfied
**Verdict: CONFIRMED in substance, with a sourcing nuance on the 50–66% figure.**
- CY2026 sold out: established earlier in FY2026 (2026 HBM price/volume negotiations completed in late 2025) and restated through 2026 coverage; FQ4 coverage states projected HBM capacity is sold out through 2027.
- CY2027: On the 30 Sep 2026 call Mehrotra said "for 2027, a large part of the volume is already sold out for 2027 for HBM, and the prices are much higher than 2026 prices"; prepared remarks / other coverage phrase it as agreements covering "the vast majority of calendar year 2027 HBM bit supply at significantly higher year-over-year prices." Also: >75% of total CY2027 output (all memory) is already committed.
- 50–66%: The figure is real but its provenance is broader/older than HBM-only FQ4 language. Mehrotra has repeatedly said Micron can satisfy only ~50% to two-thirds of key customers' demand (originally framed for medium-term bit demand generally); a JPMorgan note after meeting Micron finance/business-development leadership, reported 1 Oct 2026, restated that Micron "can currently serve only 50% to two-thirds of key customers' medium-term bit demand." It is a Micron-specific supply-coverage statement, not an industry-wide HBM statistic, and not HBM-exclusive in every telling. Do not cite it as a precise FQ4 HBM-only disclosure.
- Sources: MarketBeat transcript of Micron FQ4 FY2026 call, 30 Sep 2026; Zacks, 30 Sep 2026; TechTimes Micron FQ4 recap, 1 Oct 2026 (JPMorgan/Harlan Sur note); Particle News summary, 21–23 Sep 2026.

## NVDA (Nvidia)

### Claim 5 — $500B Blackwell+Rubin is management demand visibility through CY2026 ($1T through 2027), NOT filing backlog/RPO; actual filing RPO ~$3.2B
**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**
- At GTC on 16 Mar 2026, CEO Jensen Huang said: last year Nvidia "saw about $500 billion of very high confidence demand and purchase orders for Blackwell and Rubin through 2026" and "right now… I see through 2027, at least $1 trillion." CFO Colette Kress confirmed the figure covers only Blackwell + Vera Rubin plus related networking. This is a keynote demand-visibility projection ("I see"), not a contracted backlog disclosed in a filing.
- Filing RPO: Analysis of Nvidia's Q2 FY2027 Form 10-Q (quarter ended 26 Jul 2026) puts remaining performance obligations at US$3.2B for contracts longer than one year — comprising ~$3.0B already deferred and $244M not yet billed/recognized. For scale: total deferred revenue was $6.412B and customer advances within current deferred revenue were $2.8B at 26 Jul 2026. None of these filing figures resembles $500B/$1T.
- Sources: TechCrunch, 16 Mar 2026; Data Center Dynamics GTC report, Mar 2026; BTW Media analysis of Nvidia Q2 FY2027 10-Q, ~Aug/Sep 2026.

### Claim 7 — Hyperscaler ASIC status: Google TPU and AWS Trainium shipping in volume; OpenAI-Broadcom chip NOT yet shipping (engineering samples/development); plus Microsoft Maia / Meta MTIA
**Verdict: CONFIRMED (with one timing nuance on OpenAI).**

TPU Ironwood (v7) is the current production generation on GCP.
- AWS Trainium — shipping in volume: AWS has deployed >1M Trainium chips; Project Rainier runs >500K Trainium2 chips scaling toward 1M (Anthropic's Claude already running on >1M Trainium2 per Mar 2026 reporting); Trainium3 UltraServers are generally available; Trainium is described by AWS as a multi-billion-dollar business.

As of the 18 Oct 2026 run date it is a development/engineering-sample program, not a volume-shipping product. Nuance: tape-out is done and initial small deployments are slated for late 2026, so "not yet shipping" is correct today but the gap is months, not years.
- Microsoft Maia — in production, narrower than TPU/Trainium: Maia 200 (announced 26 Jan 2026, TSMC 3nm inference chip) is in production (Iowa/Arizona first) and, per Microsoft's 12 Oct 2026 update, deploying across the US, Europe and Australia serving Copilot/Teams/OpenAI and MAI models. Maia 300 was announced 12 Oct 2026 with production deployments only in 2027. Maia is genuinely deployed, but Microsoft only now (Oct 2026) frames it as becoming its "primary inference system" — scale today is well behind TPU/Trainium.

### Claim 8 — Q2 FY2027 receivables rose ~55% sequentially, DSO ~45 → ~60 days, consistent with customer/vendor financing arrangements
**Verdict: CONFIRMED on the numbers and the stated cause; the "financing" framing is an analyst interpretation, not Nvidia's wording.**
- Accounts receivable rose from ~$40.7B in Q1 FY2027 to $63.1B at 26 Jul 2026 — a ~55% sequential increase against +18% sequential revenue growth (receivables were $38.5B at FY2026 year-end, Jan 2026, giving a coherent sequence $38.5B → $40.7B → $63.1B). DSO rose from 45 days to 60 days sequentially.
- Nvidia's own explanation in the release/10-Q commentary: extended payment terms on large, multi-quarter agreements with certain investment-grade customers (some reporting specifies terms of 90 days to one year). Operating cash flow roughly halved sequentially ($24.1B vs $50.3B in Q1) partly on the $22.3B receivables build.
- DISAGREEMENT FLAG: the superseded verification.md at this path cited AR of $56.7B vs $36.5B (+55.3%). The +55% growth rate agrees, but the levels do not: multiple independent filing analyses (Beancount, Zacks, Motley Fool, CryptoBriefing) put Q2-end AR at $63.1B, and $36.5B for Q1-end is inconsistent with the $38.5B reported at the January 2026 fiscal year-end. This verifier's levels ($40.7B → $63.1B) are the corroborated ones.
- Caveat: whether this amounts to "vendor financing" is inference by analysts (Beancount, Tunguz, Motley Fool all flag it as Nvidia extending credit to a financed customer base); the filing fact is extended payment terms. Also disclosed alongside: $15.6B of customer advances received in H1 FY2027 and $25B of senior notes issued in Q2 — the balance sheet is expanding in both directions.
- Sources: Beancount analysis of Nvidia Q2 FY2027 filing, 26 Aug 2026; Zacks post-earnings analysis, Sep 2026; Motley Fool, 23 Sep 2026; CryptoBriefing, Oct 2026.
