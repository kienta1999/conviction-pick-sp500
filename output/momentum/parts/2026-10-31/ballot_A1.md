# Panelist A (Supply-chain analyst) — Momentum ballot, 2026-10-31

Lens: shortage/backlog evidence and multi-year revenue visibility weighted above all. All figures below are from the consolidated dossier for 2026-10-31; where the dossier has no figure, that is stated as "not found."

## 1. SINGLE-PICK ballot

- Top pick: MU
- Runner-up: NVDA
- Thesis (3-5 sentences): Micron is the purest shortage in the dossier: AI demand for HBM is simultaneously starving conventional DRAM, so the constraint is structural rather than a single quarter's ordering spike. With HBM sold out, most of next year's output already committed, and no large new volume until Micron's Idaho fab in late 2028, pricing power sits with the three-firm oligopoly rather than with buyers. That duration — shortage visibility stretching into 2028 and, on SK hynix's read, beyond — gives MU the longest booked runway in the panel. The explosive case is therefore not a new product cycle but a multi-year period in which Micron fills only a fraction of demand at triple-digit price increases.
- Key shortage/backlog evidence: CEO Sanjay Mehrotra on the fiscal Q4 2026 call (30 Sep 2026) said Micron can fill only about half to two-thirds of core customer orders, with supply "significantly tighter in fiscal 2027 and 2028 than it was in 2026" and no line of sight to supply/demand balance.
- Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk: low — hyperscalers cannot realistically in-source DRAM/HBM: only three merchant firms make it at scale, a leading-edge fab costs tens of billions and takes years, and HBM qualifies into specific accelerator designs over 12–18 months. The real cap, noted in the dossier (irreplaceability 8/10), is rival substitution within the oligopoly — Samsung and SK hynix, not a customer building its own memory.
- 12-18 month return scenario: base — shortage persists through fiscal 2027–2028 as guided, supporting a move toward the dossier's analyst mean target of $1,561.91 (67.2% upside from the $983.23 screen price); bull — if the SK hynix view of demand outrunning supply through 2030 proves closer to the mark and contract prices hold, upside above that mean target, with the risk that the gain is given back when new capacity finally lands.
- Top risk: Classic memory cyclicality — the three suppliers' added capacity eventually tips the market into surplus and the ~86% guided gross margin mean-reverts; MU is also the smallest HBM supplier (~18% share vs SK hynix ~50% and Samsung ~33%), so it benefits least if HBM pricing normalises first.
- Conviction (1-10): 9

### Top 3 detail

**MU:** The single most compelling point is the fill rate itself — filling only about half to two-thirds of core orders, with more than 75% of fiscal 2027 output already locked into customer commitments and HBM fully sold out for 2026, while new large-volume capacity does not arrive until Idaho's first wafers in late 2028. That is a booked, multi-year shortage, not a spot-price anecdote. 12–18mo scenario: base toward the $1,561.91 mean target (67.2% upside from $983.23); bull above it if tightness extends toward the SK hynix 2029–2030 horizon.

**NVDA:** The most compelling visibility figure is the $279B of supply and capacity commitments as of 26 Jul 2026, up from $119B one quarter earlier and mostly landing in FY2027–FY2029, reinforced by $15.6B of customer advances added in H1 FY2027 — Nvidia is pre-buying at a scale that only makes sense against booked demand, even as its own Rubin platform ships with roughly half the planned CPU memory because of the memory shortage. 12–18mo scenario: base toward the $328.95 mean target (54.0% upside from $215.38) on Q3 FY2027 guidance of ~$108B ± 2% and Rubin ramp; bull above it if the reported >$35B of Chinese Blackwell orders proves fillable and licensable, a point the dossier flags as anonymously sourced, not company guidance.

**CSCO:** The most compelling point is the order surge with an audited forward anchor: $9.3B of hyperscaler AI-infrastructure orders in FY2026 (~4.5x FY2025's ~$2bn, including $4.0B in Q4 alone), FY2027 AI revenue guided to ~$7.5bn, and company-wide RPO of $46.73bn (+7.4% YoY) plus $29.78bn deferred revenue — while the dossier explicitly warns the $9.3bn minus ~$4bn AI revenue difference ($5.3bn) is not a disclosed AI backlog and must not be quoted as one. 12–18mo scenario: base toward the $136.16 mean target (25.4% upside from $108.59) if AI revenue converts toward the $7.5bn guide; bull above it if hyperscaler deliveries accelerate, tempered by the margin tax (product gross margin −270bp YoY in Q4 FY2026 on memory costs).
