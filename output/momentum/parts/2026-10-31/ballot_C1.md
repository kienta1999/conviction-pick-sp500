## 1. SINGLE-PICK ballot

- Top pick: ISRG
- Runner-up: EW
- Thesis (3-5 sentences): Intuitive Surgical is the only name in the dossier whose moat is close to uncopyable: a 13,246-system installed base, procedure-specific instruments, clinical evidence and regulatory clearances that force any hospital switching supplier to retrain surgeons, revalidate procedures and abandon a proprietary instrument ecosystem. That lock-in converts into 84% recurring revenue, so growth compounds off the installed base rather than depending on winning each new sale from scratch. The explosive leg here is not shortage pricing but a depressed price (down 17.7% over 12 months on screen, the only decliner among the 14) meeting an upgrade cycle: 546 da Vinci placements in Q3 2026, up 28% year on year, with da Vinci 5 now over 2,000 installed. If procedure growth re-accelerates from the soft US print, the market is paying a compounder's price for what the dossier shows is still a dominant, recurring-revenue franchise.
- Key shortage/backlog evidence: the single most compelling data point is 546 da Vinci systems placed in Q3 2026, up 28% year on year (427 a year earlier), including 315 da Vinci 5 — an adoption/upgrade surge, not a disclosed backlog (no formal backlog figure was found for ISRG in the dossier).
- Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk: low — hospitals cannot realistically in-source a surgical robot, and switching means retraining, revalidation and abandoning proprietary instruments; the dossier rates ISRG 9/10, the highest irreplaceability score in the dossier. The cap on that score is slow substitution at the margin for new placements only (Medtronic Hugo, J&J Ottava nearing approval), not disintermediation of the installed base.
- 12-18 month return scenario: rough base / bull case: Base — recovery toward and modestly above the screen analyst mean target of $482.63 (screen upside 7.5% from $444.18), supported by FY2026 da Vinci procedure guidance narrowed to 14–15% and total procedures up 15% in Q3 2026. Bull — materially above that target (rough judgment, not a dossier forecast: on the order of 25–35%) if da Vinci 5 placements sustain near the Q3 28% pace, SP procedures sustain near their 55% Q3 growth, and US procedure growth re-accelerates from the 10% Q3 print. The dossier contains no published 12–18 month return forecast for ISRG, so the bull range is my rough judgment, not a dossier figure.
- Top risk: Planned pricing actions from H1 2027 (extended-life EndoWrist instruments, volume-based pricing, plus a China policy price cut from November) are expected to cut instrument/accessory revenue per procedure by about 3–5% on a constant-mix basis, on top of the $450m pre-tax charge for the proposed no-fault settlement over third-party instrument remanufacturing/system servicing.

### Top 3 — most compelling shortage/backlog or moat data point, and 12–18 month scenario

**1. ISRG —** The moat data point is the installed base itself: 13,246 systems (12,094 da Vinci + 1,152 Ion), up 13% year on year, with da Vinci 5 now over 2,000 installed and over 25% of US procedures, and 84% of revenue recurring (instruments & accessories $1.80bn, up 18%, plus services $482m in Q3 2026). No hospital can replicate that base, and no rival is close (next player cited at 10.3% share versus Intuitive's over 76%). Scenario (rough judgment, not a dossier forecast): base toward the $482.63 analyst mean target (screen upside 7.5%); bull on the order of 25–35% if the da Vinci 5 upgrade cycle and procedure re-acceleration sustain.

**2. EW —** The moat data point is category dominance plus a temporary exclusive: about 66% of TAVR (Medtronic about 28%), with SAPIEN 3 described in the dossier as the only FDA-labelled TAVR device for asymptomatic severe aortic stenosis, following the CMS final TAVR National Coverage Determination on 10 September 2026 extending Medicare coverage to asymptomatic severe aortic stenosis (under Coverage with Evidence Development) and ending Coverage with Evidence Development requirements for symptomatic patients. That is a payer-led demand unlock on top of an already dominant platform, not a supply shortage (shortage score 4/10). Scenario (rough judgment, not a dossier forecast): base toward the $101.87 analyst mean target (screen upside 7.9% from $94.44), on FY2026 sales-growth guidance raised to 10–11% constant-currency; bull materially above target if asymptomatic volumes convert through the Coverage with Evidence Development pathway faster than expected, despite Medtronic Evolut as a credible duopoly rival.

**3. WST —** The capacity data point is the closest thing in the dossier to demand outrunning supply in a qualified product: HVP Components revenue $467m in Q3 2026, up 20% organically and now 53% of company sales, with GLP-1-related HVP revenue growing well into the 30% range organically, nearly 800 Annex 1-related projects (up 50% year on year in the pre-Q3 preview) and 200+ HVP upgrade projects added during 2026 alone, while new HVP finishing capacity in Europe and the US is expected to take about 18–24 months to make a meaningful contribution. The moat is drug-by-drug FDA qualification: switching a stopper/closure means re-validation, stability testing and regulatory risk for a component trivial in cost versus the drug's value. Scenario (rough judgment, not a dossier forecast): base roughly flat to modestly positive — the screen analyst mean target is $416.40 versus $403.64 (screen upside −0.1%), and the stock is already at its 52-week high on a 41.73 forward PE, so the base case is mostly earnings compounding rather than re-rating; bull on the order of 20–30% only if HVP/GLP-1 growth sustains and the new finishing capacity converts the project pipeline without a GLP-1 demand shock.

### Deliberately left out of the top 10 and why

Left out: ANET, NUE, EOG, FANG. ANET — EOS and high-speed switching leadership are real, but Nvidia Spectrum-X/NVLink and white-box/SONiC switches attack exactly its AI growth engine, and its constraint is shared components, not a proprietary shortage (dossier irreplaceability 6/10). NUE — the largest US steelmaker with a strong backlog, but steel is a commodity and customers can switch suppliers routinely; hard to replicate the company, easy to replace the supplier (5/10). EOG and FANG — both sell fungible barrels; refiners and gas buyers need no lock-in to either producer, and neither has an order book (4/10 and 5/10 respectively) — FANG's core-Midland rock is scarce, but the product itself is not irreplaceable, so neither clears a moat-first top 10.
