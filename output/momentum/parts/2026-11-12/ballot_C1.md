## 1. SINGLE-PICK BALLOT

- Top pick: MU
- Runner-up: CF
- Thesis (3-5 sentences): Micron is the rare name in this field where the shortage and the moat point the same way: memory is the binding constraint on the AI buildout, and only three firms on earth can make DRAM at scale, behind tens of billions in fabs and Nvidia qualification lock-in for HBM. Customers are not just ordering — they have handed Micron $32B in cash prepayments and signed ~$100B in binding multi-year contracts, so the demand is contracted, not hoped for. At $811.30, −33.1% off its 52-week high, with a forward P/E of 3.9x and +92.5% analyst upside, the market is pricing a short cycle; if the shortage lasts into 2027–2028 as management and SK Hynix indicate, earnings power is far above what that multiple implies. This is a cyclical oligopoly, not a permanent tollbooth, but the cycle is being paid for in advance by the customers themselves.
- Key shortage/backlog evidence: Customer financial commitments, mostly cash prepayments on long-term supply contracts, climbed to $32B (Q4 FY26 call, 30 Sep 2026) — HBM sold out through 2026 with a large share of 2027 output already committed, and Micron can satisfy only 50–66% of key customer HBM demand.
- Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk: low — hyperscalers cannot make DRAM themselves; only Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron can, requiring fab scale, process-tech lead (1-gamma node) and per-platform Nvidia qualification, and no substitute technology routes around DRAM/HBM in AI servers (memory is ~29% of a Vera Rubin VR200 system's bill of materials). The deduction is that DRAM remains a commodity at heart: when supply normalises the three makers compete on price, and CXMT (9% share) is the long-run margin threat — but that is a cycle risk, not an in-sourcing risk.
- 12-18 month return scenario: rough base +40–60% as contracted HBM volumes and higher 2027 HBM prices flow through earnings at a still-low multiple; rough bull +90–120%, toward the ~$1,562 screen mean analyst target, if the shortage persists through 2027–2028 and the market re-rates the cycle as durable rather than a one-year spike.
- Top risk: Classic memory cyclicality — prices have already risen ~400% since start-2024 on shipments up only ~4–20%, so panic/double-ordering could flip shortage to glut when new fabs land in 2028–2030; HBM gross margin (83%) also trails ordinary DRAM/NAND (~90%) in Q4 FY26, so mix shift dilutes margin until HBM prices rise.
- Conviction (1-10): 8

### Top 3 in detail

**1. MU —** The single most compelling data point is the $32B of customer cash prepayments on long-term contracts (plus ~$100B in binding multi-year AI memory contracts and 26 strategic agreements covering >35% of revenue through 2030): customers are funding Micron's capacity in advance because they cannot get HBM anywhere else, and a 16Gb DDR5 chip went from $8.70 (Oct 2025) to $48 (Sep 2026). Rough 12–18mo scenario: base +40–60% on contracted volumes and 2027 HBM price increases; bull +90–120% toward the ~$1,562 screen mean target if the shortage holds into 2027–2028.

**2. CF —** The single most compelling data point is the quantified supply shock: Middle East hostilities since late February 2026 removed ~9M metric tons of urea supply, with exports running ~800,000 t/month versus a normal 1.5–1.7M t/month, while CF's own ammonia capacity ran at 97% utilisation YTD and Q3 UAN shipments of nearly 2M tons were the highest quarterly volume of the decade — and management says capacity under construction is insufficient to meet demand growth and permanent shutdowns through 2030. Rough 12–18mo scenario: base +10–20% as tight pricing persists into H1 2027 (consensus is only Hold, ~$123 target, essentially at the $123.24 screen price); bull +40% or more if hostilities persist and the price premium holds through 2027.

**3. CDNS —** The single most compelling data point is the record $8.2B backlog at 30 Sep 2026 (+17% YoY), with $4.2B of remaining performance obligations to be recognised in the next 12 months (+20%), in a Synopsys–Cadence duopoly (~74% combined share) with 80–85% recurring revenue and near-100% retention — no hyperscaler builds its own EDA, and switching mid-design requires re-qualification with the foundry. Rough 12–18mo scenario: base +20–30% (re-rating from −20.9% off its high toward the +25.1% analyst upside) on ~21% growth compounding; bull +40–50% if AI design starts accelerate and the market pays up for the tollbooth.

### Deliberately left out of the top 10

Left out: ANET, PM, NEM, EOG, COP. ANET is the deliberate lens exclusion: despite Q3 revenue +58.5% YoY and FY27 guidance of ~$18.5B, its biggest customers demonstrably build their own switches (Meta-designed switches; Celestica was #1 in Ethernet AI back-end switch sales in early 2026) and its largest competitor Nvidia is now the #1 datacenter Ethernet vendor — a live in-sourcing/disintermediation threat no moat score of 6/10 can excuse. PM was left out for near-zero switching costs and Zyn share loss to Velo (two-thirds of category growth in 2025) plus US Q4 profitability guided to ~zero. NEM, EOG and COP were left out because all three sell a perfectly fungible commodity as price-takers with no product moat — NEM's own production fell 8% YoY, and EOG/COP ride the same war-premium oil tape that the EIA expects to flip to a 4.91 mb/d surplus in 2027.
