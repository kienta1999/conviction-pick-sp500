# Panelist D — Contrarian / risk skeptic — Momentum ballot, 2026-11-01

Lens note: I am hunting for a thesis that is real but still underappreciated, and I am penalising names where the run has already happened, where the backlog is unverified or fungible, and where disintermediation is live. All figures below are from the 2026-11-01 dossier or shortlist unless labelled as my scenario estimate.

## (a) Single-pick ballot

Top pick: APH; Runner-up: CDNS

Thesis (3-5 sentences): Amphenol is the supply-constrained grower the market has not yet repriced, while the headline AI names have either already tripled or are fully owned. Its Q3 2026 orders outran record shipments by a wide margin, with hyperscalers locking capacity years ahead and roughly 50-week fiber lead times, yet the stock is up only 14.3% over 12 months against 51.6% TTM revenue growth. That gap between a verified, dated order surge and a modest price run is exactly the underappreciated setup this lens wants. The AI exposure is also concrete rather than thematic: IT Datacom grew 77% organically, with AI about three-quarters of that segment.

Key shortage/backlog evidence: Q3 2026 orders (bookings) were a record \$12.17 billion, up 99% year over year and 13% sequentially, against sales of \$10.23 billion — a 1.19x book-to-bill, with orders exceeding shipments by roughly \$1.9 billion in the quarter (company release, 2026-10-28).

Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk: medium — interconnect is multi-sourced (TE Connectivity, Molex, Samtec in the dossier), so share can be lost at renewal even though a supplier cannot be fully bypassed; the dossier scores irreplaceability 7, which I read as strong but not locked-in.

12-18 month return scenario: base roughly in line with the analyst mean target of \$105.00 (+32.4% from \$79.30) if the book-to-bill stays above 1x and lead times only slowly normalise; bull roughly +50% or more if hyperscaler capacity locking extends and pricing holds despite dual-sourcing. These scenario figures are my estimates, not dossier figures; the analyst target and upside above are dossier figures.

Top risk: dual-sourcing caps pricing power and lets customers shift share at renewal, turning a capacity shortage into a margin squeeze rather than a repricing.

Conviction (1-10): 7

### Top 3 detail

**1. APH:** The single most compelling point is the Q3 2026 print itself: record \$12.17 billion orders, up 99% year over year, against \$10.23 billion sales — a 1.19x book-to-bill — plus roughly 50-week fiber lead times and a raised guide. That is a dated, company-reported order flow, not an unverified backlog claim of the kind I rejected elsewhere in this dossier. Base/bull scenario (my estimates, anchored to the \$105.00 analyst mean target, +32.4%): base roughly +25–35% over 12–18 months as orders convert; bull roughly +50% or more if lead times stay extended and hyperscaler locking persists.

**2. CDNS:** The single most compelling point is a record \$8.2 billion backlog at 2026-09-30, with \$4.2 billion of RPO expected in the next 12 months, alongside Q3 2026 revenue of \$1.633 billion (+22% year over year) and a raised 21% growth guide — while the stock is down 4.0% over 12 months. In an EDA oligopoly with verification hardware described as the bottleneck, that flat price against a record backlog is the underappreciated gap. Base/bull scenario (my estimates, anchored to the \$411.76 analyst mean target, +25.6%): base roughly +20–30% as the backlog converts; bull roughly +40% or more if AI/HPC verification demand keeps outrunning design capacity.

**3. NVDA:** The single most compelling point is about \$500 billion in Blackwell and Rubin orders/revenue visibility through end of calendar 2026, against a stock down 0.9% over 12 months and an analyst mean target of \$328.95 (+60.7%) — the market has, on price, barely paid for the visibility. I rank it third rather than first because the contrarian discount is real: the hyperscaler-ASIC trap is live, receivables concentration in five customers is disclosed, and the dossier flags HIGH_ACCRUALS and RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN. Base/bull scenario (my estimates, anchored to that +60.7% analyst upside): base roughly +30–50% if Blackwell converts and concentration does not bite; bull roughly +60% or more only if the platform moat holds against in-house ASICs, which is precisely the risk I am not willing to assume away.

Left out: LLY, CF, EOG, VRTX — LLY because its tirzepatide shortage is formally resolved and analyst upside is -1.0% (fully priced); CF and EOG because their products are fungible commodities with no contracted backlog (CF) or no scarcity at all (EOG); VRTX because it is monopoly compounding, not a shortage play, with its explosive case resting on unproven-at-scale optionality.
