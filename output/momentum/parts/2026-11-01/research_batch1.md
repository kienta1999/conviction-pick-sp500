## MU — Micron Technology

### 1. Shortage thesis

Yes — structural, not transitory. High-bandwidth memory (HBM) stacked beside every AI accelerator is the bottleneck of the AI supply chain, and HBM production cannibalises ordinary DRAM: producing a gigabyte of HBM takes roughly three times the wafer capacity of standard DDR5, so the shift to HBM tightens PC, server and enterprise memory at the same time.

Concrete evidence:

- CEO Sanjay Mehrotra, on the fiscal Q4 2026 call (30 September 2026), said memory and storage supply would be significantly tighter in fiscal 2027 and 2028 than in 2026, that HBM is fully sold out for calendar 2026, that more than 75% of 2027 output is already committed, and that most current sales conversations are about 2028, with no line of sight to supply-demand balance.
- The same reporting says Micron can fill only about 50-67% of core customer orders.
- Pricing: DRAM contract prices rose about 170% year over year in Q3 (calendar) per industry trackers cited in press coverage, with Q4 PC DRAM contracts expected 25-30% higher sequentially; Barclays data cited in late-October coverage put DDR4 contract prices up 24% and DDR5 up about 20% over the three months to October.
- TrendForce, cited August 2026, projected HBM bit shipments growing 50-60% year over year in 2027 and still failing to match demand growth.

### 2. Backlog / order book

Latest reported figure, fiscal Q4 2026 earnings, 30 September 2026: approximately \$150 billion in remaining performance obligations (RPO) across 26 strategic customer agreements, up from 16 agreements in June 2026 and from about \$100 billion cumulative at minimum contract prices for 14 of 16 agreements in Q3.

Detail: the \$150 billion counts only agreements with a determined pricing framework (fixed price or floor/ceiling) and is based on committed volumes and minimum pricing, which CFO Mark Murphy called inherently conservative. All agreements are take-or-pay — customers pay whether or not they take the volume. Customers have put up \$32 billion in financial commitments, the vast majority cash deposits, including \$12.3 billion in Q4 alone. Micron estimates the 26 agreements cover more than 35% of revenue through 2030, with several running into 2031; HP and Dell were reported to have extended agreements into 2031, with Alphabet reported in talks to extend to 2035. How far out it is booked: effectively through 2030-2031 on contracted volume, with 2027 already more than 75% committed and 2028 under active negotiation.

### 3. Category position

Not number 1 in HBM. Counterpoint Research, Q2 2026 HBM revenue share: SK Hynix 50%, Samsung 33% (32% in some summaries; 101% total attributed to rounding), Micron 18%, down from 21% in Q1 2026 and a year earlier. In overall DRAM, the three suppliers together control about 95% of the market. Micron is therefore the number 3 HBM supplier, the only one listed directly on a US exchange, and is targeting HBM share near its DRAM share (roughly 20%) by end-2026. HBM capacity: Micron plans to roughly double monthly HBM wafers to about 100,000 by end-2026, versus an estimated 150,000-200,000 each for Samsung and SK Hynix.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk

Moat: membership of a three-supplier global oligopoly for a product that requires leading-edge DRAM process, advanced stacking/packaging and customer qualification on a specific accelerator package (notably Nvidia CoWoS). A stack that does not qualify on the customer's package does not ship. That qualification plus take-or-pay deposits is the moat.

Threat, stated plainly: hyperscalers do not need to in-source memory fabrication themselves — they dual-source among the three. Samsung's HBM share jump (21% to 33% in one quarter) shows share can move fast on qualification, and one 2026 report noted Micron was not selected as a core HBM4 supplier for Nvidia's Rubin in that cycle. Chinese rival CXMT is a longer-term density-workaround threat, which Micron explicitly rejected following in its 21 October 2026 HBM roadmap webinar.

Irreplaceability score: 7 out of 10 — nobody outside the three can replicate it at scale, but within the three Micron is replaceable at the margin.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last about 3 months)

- Fiscal Q4 2026, 30 September 2026: revenue \$54.2 billion (reported \$54.229 billion), up 379% year over year and up 31% sequentially, beating LSEG expectations of about \$51.1 billion; non-GAAP EPS \$33.42 versus about \$31.61 expected; non-GAAP gross margin 87.0%, up 2.1 points sequentially; DRAM revenue \$38.26 billion (about 70.5% of revenue); fiscal 2026 revenue \$133.2 billion, up 256%.
- Guidance for fiscal Q1 2027: revenue about \$61.5 billion plus or minus \$1.5 billion, non-GAAP EPS about \$38.15, gross margin about 86.25% — above consensus at the time.
- HBM4: over \$1 billion cumulative HBM4 revenue shipped by June 2026; HBM revenue grew faster than total revenue in Q4; 12-high HBM4 in volume production, HBM4E expected to ramp in calendar 2027.
- Roadmap webinar, 21 October 2026: a new HBM generation every year through 2030, plus new vHBM and vEIM co-designed categories.
- Capacity: Singapore HBM advanced packaging, initial output early calendar 2027 (about \$7 billion investment); Boise Fab 1 first wafers mid-calendar 2027, Fab 2 late 2028; Clay, New York first fab initial wafers calendar 2030; 20 October 2026 strategic investment in Indium Corporation (Rome, New York) for critical-minerals processing.
- Analyst: Bank of America reaffirmed buy after management meetings in late October 2026, per 27 October coverage.

### 6. Risks

Classic memory cyclicality: every prior upcycle ended when capacity tipped into surplus, and Micron's own capex is enormous (fiscal 2026 capex \$27.37 billion per one summary; tens of billions planned). New fabs only arrive late 2028-2030, so the risk is 2028-2029 oversupply, not 2026 demand. Share loss to Samsung in HBM4, customer concentration in hyperscalers, and valuation already pricing peak-cycle margins (about 86-87% gross margin) — if contract prices roll over sequentially, the multiple compresses before revenue does.

### 7. Shortage score and verdict

Shortage score: 10 out of 10.

Verdict: The purest sold-out AI-memory shortage in the batch, with take-or-pay contracts locking in price and volume, but it is the number 3 HBM supplier in a famously cyclical industry.

## LLY — Eli Lilly

### 1. Shortage thesis

No — not a current shortage, and the dossier must not claim one. The FDA declared the tirzepatide shortage resolved on 2 October 2024 and reaffirmed it on 19 December 2024; semaglutide shortage was resolved 21 February 2025. An October 2026 GLP-1 supply tracker summarises it directly: supply is no longer the constraint.

What remains is a demand surge: US adult obesity prevalence 40.3% (CDC NHANES, August 2021-August 2023), about 12% of US adults taking a GLP-1 (KFF, late 2025), and Lilly's own Q1 2026 disclosure that Mounjaro captured 51% of US incretin-class prescriptions, up from 39% in Q1 2025. Management said on the Q1 2026 call that price reductions have significantly increased volume, especially outside the US where about 75% of Mounjaro sales are out-of-pocket. So the driver is volume-led demand growth on resolved supply, plus new indications and oral expansion — not scarcity pricing.

### 2. Backlog / order book

Not found — and not applicable in the usual sense. Lilly, like other pharma companies, does not disclose an order backlog or bookings figure. The closest audited proxies are prescription share, volume growth and guidance:

- Q3 2026 (reported 29 October 2026): revenue \$24.4 billion, up 39% year over year, driven by a 53% increase in volume partly offset by a 15% decrease in realised prices; Mounjaro \$10.72 billion (up 65%), Zepbound \$5.35 billion (up 49%), combined over \$16 billion, about 65.9% of total revenue.
- Q1 2026: diabetes and obesity portfolio \$15.8 billion, up 71% year over year; Mounjaro \$8.6 billion.
- Full-year 2026 revenue guidance raised 29 October 2026 to \$91-\$92 billion from \$85-\$87 billion (midpoint up \$5.5 billion); performance margin guidance raised to 52.0-52.5% from 49.0-50.5%.

No booked-out horizon can be stated; do not substitute guidance for backlog.

### 3. Category position

Yes — number 1 in the incretin class on the prescription measure that matters. Mounjaro held 51% of US incretin prescriptions in Q1 2026, up from 39% a year earlier and 48% in Q4 2025, ahead of Novo Nordisk's semaglutide franchise (Ozempic/Wegovy). By drug type, semaglutide still accounted for about 49% of the GLP-1 market in 2025 in one market sizing, with tirzepatide projected as the fastest-growing segment — i.e. Lilly leads the growth and the US incretin prescription share, while Novo retains a large installed base. Head-to-head, tirzepatide showed superior efficacy versus semaglutide in SURPASS-2, which is the clinical basis for the share gain.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk

Moat: patented dual GIP/GLP-1 molecule, large-scale biologics/API manufacturing that took years to build, and a pipeline that extends the franchise (oral Foundayo/orforglipron, approved 1 April 2026; triple agonist retatrutide in late-stage development, Phase 3 package completed, regulatory submission planned 2027; eloraTZP combining tirzepatide with an amylin analogue).

In-sourcing threat: patients and payers cannot in-source a patented biologic, but substitution is real and different in kind from chips: Novo's oral Wegovy pill launched 5 January 2026 and passed 5 million US prescriptions by 4 August 2026 (weekly prescriptions above 265,000); Pfizer, Roche and Amgen oral programmes; compounded copies while a shortage lasted (now narrowed by FDA enforcement after shortage resolution); and generic semaglutide entry in India from March 2026, which paradoxically grew Mounjaro prescription volume about 10% there. The durable threat is a better oral or triple-agonist molecule, not a customer building its own factory.

Irreplaceability score: 8 out of 10 — patent plus manufacturing scale, discounted for oral competition and eventual loss of exclusivity.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last about 3 months)

- Q3 2026, 29 October 2026: revenue \$24.40 billion, up 39%, beating consensus \$22.11 billion by 10.4%; non-GAAP EPS \$7.48 versus \$5.38 consensus (up 7% year over year), including \$4.57 of acquired in-process R\&D charges; third revenue-guidance raise of the year, to \$91-\$92 billion.
- Q3 detail: Mounjaro US revenue \$5.0 billion (up 41%), international \$5.7 billion (up 93%, partly offset by lower realised prices after China National Reimbursement Drug List inclusion); Zepbound up 49%; Foundayo \$184 million in Q3 versus \$98 million in Q2; Medicare GLP-1 prescriptions topped 500,000; about \$900 million of Q3 benefits including \$410 million of Jardiance milestones (no further milestones remain).
- Regulatory, Q3 2026: FDA approval of Mounjaro to reduce cardiovascular risk in type 2 diabetes; Onswik once-weekly basal insulin; Inluriyo with Verzenio; expanded Jaypirca indication.
- Manufacturing: 6 May 2026 additional \$4.5 billion across Lebanon, Indiana sites (total Indiana commitments over \$21 billion since 2020), Lebanon API opening 2027 as what Lilly calls the largest API site in US history, planned for tirzepatide, Foundayo and retatrutide; 2 February 2026 \$3.5 billion Lehigh Valley, Pennsylvania injectable plant, operational 2031, for tirzepatide and retatrutide.
- Pipeline: retatrutide readouts described as promising in Q3 coverage; six triple-G readouts expected in 2026 per Q1 coverage.

### 6. Risks

Concentration: tirzepatide was about two-thirds of revenue in Q1 2026 and Mounjaro plus Zepbound were 65.9% of Q3 revenue — a single-franchise company for practical purposes. Realised prices fell 15% in Q3 while volume rose 53%: the growth is volume bought with price. Payer/Medicare pressure, TrumpRx and direct-to-patient discounting, oral competition (notably Novo's Wegovy pill), and patent-cliff timing in the 2030s. Valuation already prices years of incretin dominance; a retatrutide miss or an oral efficacy surprise would hit the multiple, not just the quarter.

### 7. Shortage score and verdict

Shortage score: 4 out of 10 — demand surge without a current shortage.

Verdict: A demand surge, not a current shortage — the tirzepatide shortage is formally resolved — so the explosive case rests on volume growth and pipeline, not scarcity pricing.

## NVDA — Nvidia

### 1. Shortage thesis

Yes — the defining shortage of the AI buildout, at the accelerator level. CEO Jensen Huang has described Blackwell demand as off the charts / insane, and said cloud GPUs are sold out. The constraint has shifted from raw silicon to energised power and delivery: one October 2026 analysis notes near-term volume is probably limited by energised capacity, not orders, which supports pricing but makes delivery timing the main risk.

Concrete evidence:

- Blackwell systems sold out through mid-2026, with backlogs reported at 3.6 million units in earlier 2026 coverage, and Blackwell capacity for the next 12 months essentially fully booked on the FY26 Q3 call.
- CFO Colette Kress, on the earnings call covered by Data Center Dynamics in mid-October 2026: visibility to \$0.5 trillion in Blackwell and Rubin revenue from the start of the year through end of calendar 2026, excluding the newest Anthropic and expanded Humain (Saudi Arabia) deals.
- At GTC Washington, 28 October 2026, Huang put total Blackwell plus Rubin orders at about \$500 billion through 2026, said 6 million Blackwell GPUs had shipped in the past four quarters (versus 4 million Hopper in its entire lifecycle), and guided to 20 million Blackwell plus Rubin GPUs over the 2025-2026 cycle.
- Q2 FY27 (quarter ended 27 July 2026, reported 26 August 2026): revenue \$96.22 billion, up 106% year over year and 18% sequentially; data centre revenue \$89.02 billion; guidance for Q3 FY27 revenue about \$108 billion plus or minus 2%, assuming no data-centre compute revenue from China. Q3 FY27 results are due 18 November 2026 and were not yet reported at this run date.

### 2. Backlog / order book

Latest quantified visibility, October 2026: about \$500 billion in Blackwell and Rubin orders/revenue through end of calendar 2026 (GTC, 28 October 2026; CFO visibility statement, mid-October 2026). That is a platform order book, not a GAAP backlog line — Nvidia does not publish a single backlog total in the way Micron publishes RPO.

Supporting balance-sheet proxies, first half of FY27 (ended 26 July 2026): supply and capacity commitments rose to \$279 billion from \$119 billion one quarter earlier, mostly for memory, expected largely in fiscal 2027-2029; customer advances added \$15.6 billion in the half, up from \$7.5 billion a year earlier, of which \$13.0 billion was recognised as revenue in the period. How far out it is booked: Blackwell plus Rubin visibility runs through end of calendar 2026 on the \$500 billion figure, with supply commitments extending into fiscal 2029; the Rubin platform began production shipments in Q3 FY27.

Note on source conflict: some secondary summaries of an earlier quarter report revenue of \$57 billion (up 62%) and data-centre revenue \$51 billion. Those figures do not match the Q2 FY27 print (\$96.22 billion) used here and appear to describe an earlier quarter; they are not used as the latest figure.

### 3. Category position

Yes — number 1 by a wide margin. AI accelerator market share is put at 80-90% by revenue in most 2025-2026 estimates, 81-90% in one competitive review, with AMD second at about 5-10% (doubled from 5% to 10%; about 7% in Q3 2025 per one source). One projection has Nvidia's share easing from about 87% in 2024 toward 75% by 2026 as custom silicon grows, with a floor of 65-70% even in a competitive scenario. Nvidia also became the leading data-centre Ethernet switching supplier in Q1 2026 per IDC (21.5%, versus Arista 20.7% and Cisco 17.8%) — relevant to the ANET section below.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk

Moat: not the silicon alone but the full stack — CUDA (20-plus years as the industry standard), NVLink, NVL72 rack-scale systems, networking (Spectrum-X, InfiniBand), and priority TSMC CoWoS allocation (about 60% versus about 11% for AMD in one analysis). Switching costs are punitive because models, tooling and deployment frameworks are built on CUDA.

Concrete in-sourcing threat: the customers themselves. Google TPU, Amazon Trainium/Inferentia, Microsoft Maia and Meta MTIA, built with Broadcom/Marvell, are the terminal threat in every competitive review. Hyperscalers account for 40-50% of Nvidia revenue in one estimate, and 55% of data-centre revenue in Q2 FY27 on the newer hyperscaler/ACIE split (hyperscale \$48.7 billion versus ACIE \$40.3 billion, with ACIE up 138.5% year over year and growing faster). Inference — estimated at about 80% of long-term AI compute — is less CUDA-dependent and better suited to custom ASIC total cost of ownership. Mitigant: neoclouds and enterprises (ACIE) do not design their own chips and are now almost as large a channel as hyperscalers, and a hyperscaler that slows Nvidia purchases risks falling behind rivals that do not.

Irreplaceability score: 9 out of 10 — replicable in inference at the margin, not replicable as a full training platform today.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last about 3 months)

- Q2 FY27, 26 August 2026: revenue \$96.22 billion (up 106%), non-GAAP EPS \$2.22 versus about \$2.10 consensus; data-centre revenue \$89.02 billion; Q3 guidance about \$108 billion; management also guided FY28 revenue of about \$691 billion (about 70% growth) on that call per I/O Fund coverage — an unusually long-dated guide.
- GTC Washington, 28 October 2026: \$500 billion Blackwell plus Rubin order book through 2026, Rubin production shipments, TSMC US Blackwell capacity launched.
- Deals named on the call and in October coverage: expanded Humain (Saudi Arabia) deal, Anthropic orders (dollar figure for Nvidia specifically not disclosed in the Anthropic S-1 summaries), Saudi agreement involving 400,000-600,000 additional GPUs over three years per one earnings-call summary.
- Financing footprint (see earnings-quality note): \$30 billion into OpenAI's funding round (originally up to \$100 billion, reworked February 2026), up to \$10 billion Anthropic commitment, nearly \$50 billion invested across frontier labs, equity portfolio reported at about \$99-\$101 billion; August 2026 guarantee of up to \$105 billion for an OpenAI-affiliate Ohio campus (SB Energy / PORTS-Pike), effective as data centres enter service from fiscal 2029.
- Networking: data-centre networking revenue \$8.2 billion, up 162% year over year, in the quarter covered by Data Center Dynamics.

### 6. Risks

Customer concentration (one direct customer 16% of Q2 FY27 revenue; five customers 70% of receivables — see below); hyperscaler custom silicon eating inference share; China revenue assumed at zero in guidance; memory cost inflation (supply commitments up to \$279 billion, mostly memory) pressuring the about 74-75% gross margin guide; customer-financing risk — the order book increasingly rests on customers' ability to finance buildouts (Meta's upsized \$97.5 billion raise, Anthropic's large multi-year commitments and rising borrowing costs in October 2026 coverage); and valuation pricing in the \$500 billion platform book. The \$105 billion Ohio guarantee is contingent, not current debt, but it ties Nvidia to a customer's lease/power obligations.

### 7. Shortage score and verdict

Shortage score: 9 out of 10.

Verdict: The bottleneck asset of the AI buildout, sold out with half-a-trillion dollars of platform visibility, whose main discount is customer concentration and customer-financed demand.

## NVDA — earnings-quality flags (screen inputs)

Screen flags supplied: HIGH_ACCRUALS (accrual ratio 0.2539), RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN (receivables divergence 0.2091), CFO/NI 0.6966.

### HIGH_ACCRUALS — accrual ratio 0.2539

One-line verdict: Mostly a hypergrowth working-capital artefact, not on its own evidence of earnings fabrication — but the ratio is high enough that cash conversion, not EPS, is the series to watch.

Explanation: at 106% revenue growth, the balance sheet must expand faster than cash collection — inventory and supply commitments (\$279 billion, mostly memory) plus receivables build mechanically raise accruals. A balance-sheet accruals series cited in one analysis rose to 79.34% by January 2026 on a different definition, so definitions differ sharply; the screen's 0.2539 should be read as elevated, not as a restatement signal. Offsetting evidence: customer advances added \$15.6 billion in H1 FY27 (cash in before revenue), and free cash flow was \$127.01 billion on a trailing-twelve-month basis to October 2026 per Insider Monkey (net income \$192.88 billion, operating cash flow \$134.36 billion) — a large, profitable, cash-generative business with a timing gap, not a cashless one.

### RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN — receivables divergence 0.2091

One-line verdict: Real and disclosed — Nvidia is deliberately financing its largest customers — which explains the divergence without needing an accounting explanation, but it concentrates collection risk in five names.

Explanation: net accounts receivable rose to \$63.059 billion at 26 July 2026 from \$38.466 billion at the start of the half; days sales outstanding stretched from about 45 days to about 60 days; Nvidia discloses in its 10-Q that investment-grade customers receive extended terms of 90 days to one year on large data-centre purchases; five direct customers were 22%, 14%, 13%, 11% and 10% of the receivables balance (70% combined). That is a business-model choice — selling rack-scale systems on multi-quarter agreements to a handful of buyers — and it is the direct cause of receivables outrunning the prior trend. It is benign only while those buyers keep paying on extended terms; a single large-customer delay would show up here first.

### CFO/NI 0.6966

One-line verdict: Earnings are running ahead of cash in timing terms, as expected at this growth rate — about 70 cents of operating cash per dollar of net income — but the gap is receivables and inventory, not capex (capex only about \$7.35 billion in the trailing-twelve-month summary cited above).

Explanation: the Insider Monkey reconciliation makes the point cleanly — net income \$192.88 billion, operating cash flow \$134.36 billion, free cash flow \$127.01 billion; the about \$59 billion profit-to-operating-cash gap is almost entirely uncollected receivables on 105.90% revenue growth, and it unwinds when growth slows. Customer advances (\$15.6 billion added, \$13.0 billion recognised) cushion the gap but do not close it. Watch operating cash flow turning before earnings as the early warning, exactly as that analysis concludes.

## ANET — Arista Networks

### 1. Shortage thesis

Partly — a component-constrained demand surge, not a shortage of Arista's own product in the way HBM is short. Management's named limit is component availability: on the Q2 2026 call (4 August 2026) executives said the broader industry faces a supply problem they do not expect to end until 2028, and Arista has promised suppliers more — multiyear purchase commitments of about \$9.7 billion, almost triple a year earlier. Arista itself warned of wafer shortages, supply de-commits and lead times beyond 52 weeks limiting its ability to fill AI demand. The demand side is unambiguous (AI Etherlink switches to more than 100 customers), but the shortage is upstream silicon/memory, shared with every networking vendor, not an Arista-only scarcity.

### 2. Backlog / order book

No precise company-disclosed AI backlog figure was found — one preview claims a \$10 billion AI backlog, but Arista has not disclosed a precise figure in the sources reviewed, so that number is treated as unverified and is not adopted here.

Audited proxies, Q2 2026 (reported 4 August 2026):

- Multiyear purchase commitments: \$9.7 billion, up from \$3.6 billion a year earlier (and \$3.6 billion three quarters earlier in another summary) — this is Arista's commitment to suppliers, not customer backlog, and one Seeking Alpha analysis explicitly warns deferred revenue should not be viewed as equivalent to backlog.
- Deferred revenue: \$6.9 billion product-related, up \$600 million quarter over quarter, at all-time highs, reflecting long acceptance cycles for large AI projects; management expects it to end 2026 higher than 2025.
- AI revenue target: at least \$3.6 billion in 2026 (raised from \$3.5 billion in Q1; some previews still cite \$3.5 billion), with Scale-Across about 30% of that target; FY2026 total revenue guidance \$12.6 billion (up about 40% year over year), raised by \$1.1 billion at Q2.
- How far out it is booked: management says it sees customer demand only about two quarters ahead — materially shorter visibility than Micron's multi-year take-or-pay book or Nvidia's \$500 billion platform book.

### 3. Category position

Contested number 1, depending on the cut. Arista overtook Cisco as the largest data-centre Ethernet switch vendor in 2024 and held that title into 2026 per Fierce Network, with about 20-22% of the data-centre switching market per Motley Fool (October 2026) and 18.2% in one Verified Market Research summary (Cisco 21.4% on a broader definition). But IDC, cited in September 2026 coverage, put Nvidia first in data-centre Ethernet switching in Q1 2026 at 21.5%, versus Arista 20.7% and Cisco 17.8%, on a \$15.4 billion quarterly market up 39.8% year over year. Fair statement: Arista is number 1 or co-number 1 in high-speed data-centre switching, number 2 behind Nvidia on the IDC Q1 2026 Ethernet cut, and a small-share challenger (about 5% target, \$1.25 billion campus target for 2026) in campus networking against Cisco.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk

Moat: EOS — one Extensible Operating System and one management stack (CloudVision) across every box, from 1-gig campus to 800-gig and 1.6T AI spines — described as the gold standard for programmable network stability, versus rivals carrying a different OS, chipset and management tool per product line. Openness is the second leg: Arista switches combine with accelerators and NICs from several suppliers, versus Nvidia's tightly integrated stack.

Concrete threat: Nvidia is now the primary opponent, not Cisco — Spectrum-X Ethernet plus BlueField DPUs, cables, optics, software and GPU systems, owning the scale-up networking Arista will not reach until 2027 per Fierce. Arista also buys key switching chips mainly from Broadcom, so supplier dependence caps pricing power and exposes margins to component inflation — the core of a 21 October 2026 Seeking Alpha sell thesis. Hyperscalers can also white-box: Broadcom silicon plus open software is a standing alternative, and Microsoft and Meta (each over 10% of revenue, the disclosure threshold) could shift volume.

Irreplaceability score: 6 out of 10 — EOS loyalty is real, but the silicon is bought in and the largest customer-adjacent competitor sells the whole stack.

### 5. Recent catalysts (last about 3 months)

- Q2 2026, 4 August 2026: revenue \$3.036 billion (first quarter above \$3 billion), up 37.7% year over year and 12.1% sequentially, gross margin 63.4%, EPS \$1.02; FY2026 guidance raised to \$12.6 billion (from \$11.5 billion at Q1) with gross margin 62-64% and operating margin 48-49%; AI target at least \$3.6 billion; campus at least \$1.25 billion.
- AI traction: Etherlink AI switches to more than 100 customers (from a handful in 2024); Scale-Across about 30% of the AI target, with a \$15-\$20 billion Scale-Across market forecast by 2030.
- Q3 2026 earnings due Tuesday 3 November 2026 (per Trefis, 9 October 2026) — not yet reported at this run date; consensus in previews about \$2.3 billion revenue in one preview and \$3,326 million in another — the previews conflict, so no consensus figure is adopted here.
- Note: ANET fell 8.34% on 30 October 2026 to \$168.86 (Finnhub close data) and was down about 18.56% over the prior month in that snapshot, despite the Q2 beat and guide raise — a reminder that the Q3 print on 3 November is the near-term catalyst, not the Q2 print.

### 6. Risks

Customer concentration: Microsoft and Meta each over 10% of revenue, and over 40% from two customers in one SWOT summary; a single hyperscaler pause hits Arista disproportionately. Nvidia Spectrum-X share gains (IDC Q1 2026 number 1 in Ethernet switching) and Cisco's Nvidia-backed infrastructure strategy in campus. Broadcom chip dependence and 52-week-plus lead times. Valuation: about 58 times trailing earnings (Finnhub, 30 October 2026) and about 65.7 times in a Trefis summary versus 21.5 times for the S\&P 500 — a premium that needs the 40% growth guide to hold. Purchase commitments of \$9.7 billion shift execution and working-capital risk onto Arista if demand pauses.

### 7. Shortage score and verdict

Shortage score: 6 out of 10 — component-constrained AI demand, not product scarcity.

Verdict: A strong AI-networking demand surge constrained by components, not a product shortage, with a real but contestable EOS moat against Nvidia and Cisco.
