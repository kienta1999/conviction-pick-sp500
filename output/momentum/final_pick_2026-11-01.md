## One-paragraph thesis

Micron is the purest shortage in the dossier: HBM is sold out under take-or-pay contracts that lock both price and volume, and as one of only three firms making DRAM at scale its output cannot be replicated by a new entrant or in-sourced by any customer. That scarcity is already showing in the screen — revenue growth of +148.3% year over year and the largest analyst upside in the dossier at +76.5% — while the forward multiple of 4.3x leaves room if take-or-pay pricing holds through the cycle. Even after a +290.8% 12-month return, the explosive case is contracted revenue, not spot hope.

## Why it's the category leader

Micron is one of only three DRAM makers on earth. Leadership here is not share dominance — Micron is the number 3 HBM supplier — it is membership in an oligopoly that cannot be expanded quickly: a new fab takes years and tens of billions. All three incumbents are sold out, so pricing power sits with the oligopoly rather than with buyers.

## Why it's irreplaceable

No hyperscaler can realistically build a DRAM/HBM fab — the process-tech lead, capital intensity, and qualification lock-in are prohibitive, and there is no substitute technology that routes around DRAM for AI training memory. The honest discount is within-oligopoly share loss: customers have two large alternatives, so Micron can lose share even while the category stays sold out. That is a share risk, not the doctrine's disintermediation trap — the trap (a customer becoming the supplier) cannot fire here. Irreplaceability score 7/10 (research batch 1) — nobody outside the three can replicate at scale, but within the three Micron is replaceable at the margin.
