# Momentum conviction pick — 2026-10-12

## THE PICK
**MU — Micron Technology, Inc.** Semiconductors (memory: DRAM + NAND + HBM). Market cap ~$1.16T. Current price **$1,029.00** (2026-10-12 screen).

## One-paragraph thesis
Micron owns the single tightest physical chokepoint in the AI buildout — advanced HBM — backed by ~$100B in binding multi-year AI memory contracts (take-or-pay, price floors through 2030), while the market still prices it as a cyclical memory stock at ~7x forward GAAP earnings vs a 29.5x sector median. HBM4 is sold out for all of 2026 with only ~50–66% of key-customer demand fillable into 2027, and only ~3 firms on earth can make advanced HBM — so the explosive leg is a re-rating from "cyclical memory" to "contracted AI infrastructure" as the bound contracts convert to cash through 2027.

## The shortage + backlog evidence
- **~$100B in binding multi-year AI memory contracts** through end of decade (16 multi-year strategic customer agreements; cumulative minimum-price revenue, take-or-pay, contractual price floors through 2030; ~$22B in customer deposits/commitments). CONFIRMED via Micron's Q4 FY2026 press release (investors.micron.com, Sept 30, 2026).
- **HBM4 (12-Hi) sold out for all of 2026**; only 50–66% of key-customer HBM demand fillable into 2027 (~40% shortfall through end-2027, easing gradually in 2028). Multiple independent outlets (newscase.com, momoview.com, ainvest.com, Tom's Hardware, June–Sept 2026).
- FQ4 FY2026 (Sept 30, 2026): record revenue **$54.23B** at **87.0% non-GAAP gross margin**, guided FQ1 FY2027 $61.5B ± $1.5B.
- Contract DRAM prices up ~93–98% in one quarter; Cloud Memory BU data-center SSD revenue approached $10B in FQ4 (10x YoY).

## Why it's the category leader
Not #1 in DRAM (~19–20%, #3 behind Samsung and SK Hynix) — but a co-leader in the part that matters: HBM, targeting 20–25% HBM share by late 2026, volume-shipping HBM4 qualified at Nvidia (alongside SK Hynix) with an >11 Gbps speed lead. The only major US memory maker, with US on-shore packaging.

## Why it's irreplaceable
One of only ~3 firms on earth that can manufacture advanced HBM at scale (SK Hynix, Samsung, Micron — independently confirmed). Multi-year customer qualification cycles (Nvidia qualification), yield leadership, and fab economics that prohibit hyperscaler in-sourcing. No practical substitute for HBM-class bandwidth in AI compute. The honest caveat: roughly half of Micron's volume is still commodity DRAM/NAND, which is cyclically contestable — the irreplaceability (8/10) applies to HBM, not the whole book.
