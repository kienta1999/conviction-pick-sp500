## Per-name thesis

**1. MSI —** The only name in the set with a literal, quantified demand-outruns-supply dynamic on both sides: a record \$16.6bn backlog (+14% YoY, +\$1bn sequentially, ~1.27x guided FY2026 revenue of ~\$13.05bn), ~95% government customers, with record Q3 orders up >20% YoY — over a year of revenue already booked before a dollar of new selling. Silvus (MANET tactical comms) did ~\$300m in Q3 alone, is on pace for >\$900m in 2026 with ~30% growth guided for 2027, and lead times are still 12–14 weeks after doubling capacity. The moat is mission-critical, certified, life-safety infrastructure with multi-decade switching costs (irreplaceability 9/10, the highest in the batch). The catch, and why it is Borda #1 but not the single pick: the analyst mean target implies only +5.4% upside, and the AI-driven memory shortage squeezes its own inputs (~\$150m cost in 2026, potentially ~\$300m in 2027) — MSI is on the buying end of the component shortage too.

**2. LLY —** The purest shortage in the field: GLP-1 demand exceeds supply since late 2023, Q3 2026 revenue \$24.4bn (+39% YoY) with volume +53% while realised prices fell 15%, FY2026 guidance raised three times to \$91–92bn, and \$18bn+ of capacity committed since 2020 (Lebanon ~\$9bn) with new plants taking 3–4 years to commercial production. Verification corrected two dossier figures — the capacity commitment is since 2020, not 2023, and penetration is ~10% of overweight/obese Americans / ~4% globally, not <5% / <1% — but the low-penetration thesis survives at the corrected levels. The moat is patents plus FDA-qualified sterile fill-finish capacity; no customer can in-source a drug. The honest caveat: ~40x FY26 non-GAAP EPS and the smallest analyst upside in the set (+9.1%) — flawless execution is already priced in, which is why the skeptic lens ranked it 10th.

**3. MSFT —** The largest backlog in the set by an order of magnitude: commercial RPO \$677bn (+73% YoY), with CFO Amy Hood stating plainly that "customer demand continues to exceed our available supply" for Azure AI capacity, and Azure growing 48% YoY to \$33.2bn in the quarter. The trap is the doctrine's own: OpenAI is ~45% of the RPO — company-disclosed by Hood on the Q2 FY2026 call for the Dec 2025 RPO (\$625bn), not repeated since — and OpenAI is diversifying compute to AWS (\$38bn Nov 2025 + \$100bn Feb 2026; exclusivity removed Apr 2026). The enterprise platform's irreplaceability is 7/10; the incremental AI-compute workload that drives current growth is only 5/10. Yesterday's pick; today the panel moved to LLY.

**4. CBOE —** Exclusive supply of a product in a structural retail-driven demand surge: the SPX options licence was extended 25 years through 31 Dec 2051 (signed 28 Sep 2026), and 0DTE is a record 65% of SPX volume. Irreplaceability 9/10 for the SPX/VIX complex — no competitor can list SPX options — but only ~4/10 for the multi-listed and equities businesses, and the analyst mean target implies just +0.7% upside: the market has already paid for the licence.

**5. WST —** A real, regulatory-reinforced demand surge for validated high-value containment, with GLP-1 HVP growing well into the 30s% and 200+ upgrade projects in the pipeline; qualification lock-in means no pharma re-validates a closure to save pennies (irreplaceability 8/10). The richest multiple in the batch (37.55x forward P/E) is the discount on the thesis, and the RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN flag is mostly benign working capital behind the HVP surge (CFO/NI still 1.02x) — a flag to clear on the Q4 print, not dismiss outright.

**6. EW —** TAVR ~66% share with the eligible-patient pool expanding structurally (PROGRESS trial in moderate aortic stenosis, NEJM Nov 2026; CMS coverage expansion) while hospital throughput is the constraint — genuine demand-outruns-supply dynamics, though the key catalyst (moderate-AS indication) is unapproved and may take years. Q3 was a beat-and-raise (Nov 4) with a Truist upgrade to \$113.

**7. V —** The #1 network with the fastest growth in 15+ years: fiscal Q4 2026 revenue \$12.1bn (+13%), FY26 \$45.9bn (+15%), cross-border re-accelerated to +14%, dividend +16%. No shortage (4/10) — this is a secular compounder, not a shortage bet — and the disintermediation risks (A2A rails, stablecoins, AI agents routing around interchange) are the same as Mastercard's, with scale as the defence.

**8. VEEV —** The contrarian pick: a forced, deadline-driven migration (every legacy customer must move off Salesforce-based CRM onto Vault CRM by Dec 2029 end-of-support; 12 of the top 20 committed, 180+ live) yet -17.6% off its 52-week high with +22.4% analyst upside. The skeptic lens picked it #1. The moat is GxP-validated workflow lock-in (7/10), but Salesforce+IQVIA is a credible, well-funded #2 already winning some of the largest accounts — this is a contest now, not a monopoly. Next earnings Dec 3 is the nearest catalyst in the set.

**9. VRTX —** A CF monopoly on a fixed patient pool (irreplaceability 10/10 in CF — the only perfect score in the batch), but no demand surge: the near-term leg is the povetacicept PDUFA on 30 Nov 2026, a binary event, not a shortage. The INVENTORY_BUILD flag is most plausibly benign launch stocking (Alyftrek rollout, pre-launch povetacicept), though no management explanation was found.

**10. UNP —** An unbuildable network with record intermodal volumes and pricing power (irreplaceability 7/10); the explosive leg is the Norfolk Southern merger, with an STB decision expected ~mid-2027.
