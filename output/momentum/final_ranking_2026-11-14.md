## Per-name thesis

**1. CF — CF Industries.** The cleanest shortage in the field, verified claim by claim: 97% available ammonia utilisation in 9M 2026, fill-programme order book into Q1 2027, ~9 Mt of urea supply removed by Middle East hostilities (exports ~800,000 t/month vs a normal 1.5–1.7 Mt/month), >35% of European ammonia capacity curtailed, with the #1 producer holding ~39% of US capacity on the lowest-cost North American gas. Nitrogen is biologically irreplaceable and no customer can build its own ammonia plant. The honest caveat: the thesis is one geopolitical event from unwinding — management itself says normalisation takes 4–6 months if hostilities end — and the early-November urea tape is already softening. **EV guardrail flag: CF's EV upside is +12.7%, below the +15% guardrail** — it stays in the ranking, but it is not actionable as a single pick on these numbers.

**2. GE — GE Aerospace.** The strongest moat-plus-shortage combination: a >$170B backlog (RPO $180.4B at Sep 30, 2026), Q3 orders $19.0B (+49% YoY), LEAP-1B sole-sourced on every 737 MAX, and Pratt & Whitney's GTF crisis grounding ~650 A320neo-family aircraft while GE's own spare-parts delinquency rises — so constrained it is buying the casting bottleneck itself (CPP, $11.75B). The shortage is two-sided, and the second side is GE's own failure to deliver. **EV guardrail note: GE's EV upside is +15.4% — it barely clears the +15% guardrail.**

**3. APH — Amphenol.** The panel's single pick (see `final_pick_2026-11-14.md`): record Q3 orders of $12.17B (+99% YoY) at a 1.19 book-to-bill for a fourth straight quarter, 40+ week high-speed interconnect lead times, AI now ~36% of the company — with the stock below its 50-day average and the widest analyst upside gap in the set (+36.7%). The honest caveat: the backlog is cancellable, Amphenol is #2 in connectors behind TE, and Nvidia's 2028 Kyber shift is a real architectural risk — the moat is qualification depth, not sole-source lock-in. **EV upside +28.5% — clears the guardrail comfortably.**

**4. PH — Parker Hannifin.** Record $13.7B backlog (up $1.2B in two quarters), rolling 12-month order rates +13%, Q1 FY27 sales $6.0B (+18%, +11.5% organic — first double-digit organic quarter in 3+ years), guidance raised. The moat is FAA/EASA certification lock-in on flight-critical parts plus a 47% aftermarket mix. Flag: long-term debt nearly doubled to $14.64B to fund the Filtration Group deal, with the $2.55B CIRCOR Aerospace deal still pending — the explosive leg is now partly a leverage-and-integration story.

**5. WST — West Pharmaceutical Services.** A real tollbooth: HVP components +20% organic (53% of sales), GLP-1-related HVP growth well into the 30% range, pricing power (gross margin +310bp), and Dublin expansion plus new HVP finishing capacity that takes 18–24 months to contribute — capacity is the constraint. The moat is regulatory switching costs on injectable containment. Flags: no backlog disclosed, 40.1x forward earnings after a +52.5% 12-month run, and a RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN flag the dossier calls mostly benign (growth-driven) but worth watching into Q4.

**6. LLY — Eli Lilly.** #1 GLP-1 franchise (63% US share vs Novo 36.6%), Q3 revenue $24.4bn (+39%), guidance raised $5bn — and a genuinely new leg: China approved orforglipron on Nov 12, 2026, the first oral GLP-1 weight-loss pill in China. But the shortage is over — the FDA declared the tirzepatide shortage resolved Dec 19, 2024 — realised prices fell 15% in Q3, and the analyst upside is only +0.7%. The explosive case is retatrutide (BLA filing Q1 2027), not scarcity.

**7. ANET — Arista Networks.** Q3 2026 revenue \$3.66bn, +58.5% y/y — the fastest since IPO — with AI-fabric revenue targeted to double to ~\$7.0bn in 2027 and \$8.6B in remaining performance obligations. **Trap flag (visible, not vetoed in ranked mode):** Nvidia just overtook Arista in datacenter Ethernet revenue (21.5% vs 20.7%), Arista buys its switch silicon from Broadcom, and its two biggest customers (Microsoft 26%, Meta 16%) run white-box SONiC/FBOSS. Deferred revenue also fell sequentially for the first time in Q3.

**8. MSFT — Microsoft.** Azure demand genuinely exceeds supply (management, Oct 28 call), Azure +48% to \$33.2B in Q1 FY27, commercial RPO \$677B (+73% y/y). But RPO was flat sequentially, growth is concentrated in two frontier labs (OpenAI = \$10B of quarterly revenue), the Nov 12–13 global M365 outage handed rivals a live reliability talking point days before Ignite (Nov 17–20), and the ~\$170B calendar-2026 capex bill has unproven ROI. At \$523.88 the shortage is the consensus story; the explosive leg needs the ROI to show, not just the shortage to persist.

**9. ISRG — Intuitive Surgical.** The strongest moat in the set (4,500+ patents, 76,000+ trained surgeons, 12,094-system installed base, 84% recurring revenue) and a genuine procedure-demand flywheel (da Vinci procedures +14%, placements +28% in Q3). But 2026 is the first year the monopoly has real, FDA-cleared challengers (Medtronic Hugo, J&J Ottava), US procedure growth decelerated to 10%, and the $450M settlement (confirmed Nov 12, no-fault, third-party servicing class action) buys legal peace at the price of a formal third-party servicing framework. The stock is -22.5% over 12 months despite the Q3 beat — the market has already de-rated it.

**10. MSI — Motorola Solutions.** A superb franchise — 80%+ of the US land-mobile-radio market, record \$16.6B backlog (+14% YoY, +\$1B sequentially), orders +20%, Silvus ~\$300M in Q3 guided +30% in 2027.
