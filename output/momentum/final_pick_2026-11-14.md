## One-paragraph thesis

Amphenol is the fastest compounder in the 11-name field riding a genuine AI-infrastructure shortage the market has not yet chased: Q3 2026 sales grew 65% to a first-ever \$10.23bn, orders hit a record \$12.17bn (+99% YoY) at a 1.19 book-to-bill for a fourth straight quarter, and high-speed interconnect lead times run 40+ weeks — yet the stock closed Nov 13 at \$78.23, below its 50-day average and only +11.0% over 12 months, with the analyst consensus target at \$106.97 (+36.7%), the widest gap in the set. AI is now ~36% of the whole company and IT datacom 48% of sales, so this is a pure AI-infrastructure shortage play at 21.6x forward earnings — the cheapest AI-infrastructure multiple in the set after the commodity plays. The honest caveat: the backlog is cancellable (no deposits or minimums), Amphenol is #2 in connectors behind TE, and Nvidia's 2028 Kyber platform shift is a real architectural risk — which is why this is a bet on the order book reloading, not on a locked-in moat.

- **Record Q3 2026 orders of \$12.17bn (+99% YoY, +13% QoQ)** at a **1.19 book-to-bill — the fourth straight quarter at 1.19 or higher** — with orders exceeding sales by \$1.94bn in the quarter and a **\$5.7bn orders-minus-sales build year-to-date** through Sep 30, 2026 (company press release, Oct 28, 2026). Verified CONFIRMED.
- **High-speed interconnect lead times of 40+ weeks** vs 8–16 weeks for standard connectors — a capacity shortage, not just strong demand (industry lead-time data, 2026). Verified CONFIRMED with a nuance: the 40+ week figure is the category level (800G transceivers); granular backplane/IO connectors run 16–26+ weeks — still ~2x standard.
- **Q3 sales \$10.23bn (+65% YoY), the first \$10bn quarter**, with IT datacom +117% YoY / +77% organically and AI now ~36% of the whole company. Verified CONFIRMED.
- **Q4 guidance \$10.3–10.5bn sales (+60–63% YoY)** — management is guiding the deceleration (mid-single-digit sequential datacom growth) while the order book keeps reloading; the panel's case is that the market is pricing the deceleration as the run-rate.

## Why it's the category leader

Amphenol is **#2 in connectors overall** (TE Connectivity leads with >12% share) — but it is **#1 in fiber-optic connectors (12.5% share vs Corning 10.8%, TE 9.2%)** and the leader in the high-speed interconnect node that matters for AI: 224 Gb/s per lane, where qualification is slow and expensive and a failed link can take down a whole training run. The category-leader claim here is about the *AI interconnect tier*, not connectors in aggregate: Amphenol's \$10.5bn CommScope fiber acquisition also hedges the copper-to-optical shift. The honest framing: Amphenol leads the fastest-growing, hardest-to-qualify slice of a market where it is #2 overall, and hyperscalers dual-source — so the moat is qualification depth, not sole-source lock-in.

## Why it's irreplaceable

High-speed interconnect at 224 Gb/s per lane is a narrow node — only Amphenol, TE and Molex have the process knowledge, products are qualified into rack architectures where a failed link can kill a training run, and **no hyperscaler manufactures precision connectors at scale** or has shown intent to build connector factories. **Disintermediation risk: medium** — the customer controls the architecture even if it cannot build the part: Nvidia's shift from Oberon to Kyber (theoretically 2028) removes the backplane Amphenol supplies, and co-packaged optics / silicon photonics could compress copper content per rack over time. The CommScope fiber acquisition hedges exactly that shift. Two of four panelists picked APH with this risk fully in view; the moat lens (C) ranked it 7th on exactly this ground, and the bear case below carries it.

5. **Lead times** — 40+ weeks is the shortage's fingerprint; normalisation to 8–16 weeks would say capacity caught up.
