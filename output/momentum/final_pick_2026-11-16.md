# Final pick — momentum 2026-11-16: MU (Micron Technology)

**THE PICK:** Micron Technology, Inc. (MU) — Semiconductors — market cap ~$880.6B — price at pick $778.31 (screen cache, 2026-11-16). Next earnings: 2026-12-23 (FQ1 2027).

## One-paragraph thesis

Micron is the purest structural-shortage bet in the S&P 500 right now: its high-bandwidth memory — the memory every AI accelerator needs — is sold out through 2026, the vast majority of calendar-2027 HBM supply is already contracted at significantly higher prices, and more than 75% of all fiscal-2027 output is committed before the year has even begun. Only three firms on earth can make HBM at scale, and no hyperscaler can in-source a memory fab, so the shortage cannot be routed around. The market's Nov 5 selloff priced a 2028 Taiwan fab delay as if it were a demand problem; the contracted 2027 order book says it is not. This is a bet that contracted pricing converts into earnings over the next 12–18 months.

## The shortage + backlog evidence

- **HBM sold out through 2026; CY2027 mostly contracted at "significantly higher" prices** — CEO Sanjay Mehrotra, FQ4 2026 earnings call, 2026-09-30. **Verified (Phase 3.5).**
- **>75% of FY2027 output already committed**, with customer negotiations already centred on 2028 — same call. **Verified.**
- **26 strategic customer agreements, ~$150B remaining performance obligations** — take-or-pay style contracts, ~35% of revenue through 2030. **Verified.**
- **FQ1 2027 guidance: revenue $61.5B ± $1.5B, non-GAAP EPS $38.15 ± $1.00** — Micron IR press release, 2026-09-30, above Street (~$57B / ~$35.40). **Verified.**
- **FQ4 2026: revenue $54.23B (+379% YoY), adj EPS $33.42** vs ~$31.16 expected; FY2026 revenue $133.19B (+256%). **Verified** — the dossier's earlier flag on the FY total was wrong arithmetic and is withdrawn.
- **Industry context:** AI servers/HBM now consume ~70% of total DRAM output; SK Hynix sold out HBM/DRAM through 2029; meaningful new global supply not before 2028. Micron can satisfy only ~50–66% of key customer HBM demand.

## Why it's the category leader

Micron is one of only three HBM makers at scale (with SK Hynix ~50–57% share and Samsung ~28–38%; Micron ~9–22% depending on the estimate). It is the only US-headquartered HBM producer and the only pure-play memory name in the field. Its HBM4 is in high-volume shipment to its lead customer's platform, HBM4E production is scheduled for calendar 2027, and DRAM revenue crossed $100B annually for the first time in company history. Operating margin hit 74.6% TTM (82–87% in FQ4 on some measures) — the pricing power of a seller in a shortage.

## Why it's irreplaceable

HBM requires leading-edge DRAM process technology plus high-volume 3D stacking (through-silicon vias, hybrid bonding) and customer qualification at AI-accelerator scale — five compounded process barriers built on ~30 years of DRAM yield know-how. A hyperscaler can design its own chip far more easily than it can build a memory fab; Micron is spending $200B on domestic fab expansion and $27.4B of FY2026 capex precisely because the capacity cannot be conjured. The disintermediation trap is **low**: the threat is a customer shifting its accelerator roadmap (concentration), not in-sourcing the product.

## Key risks (and what would invalidate the thesis)

- **The trap, honestly stated:** the disintermediation risk is low, but the *cyclicality* risk is the real trap — memory booms end, and when they end the earnings collapse is fast. A 50% peak-to-trough earnings drawdown is what past cycles actually delivered.
- **Lead-customer concentration:** one customer dominates the HBM book; a roadmap shift is a single-point failure.
- **Capex oversupply:** $27.4B of FY2026 capex and six new plants could oversupply the market if AI spending softens.
- **Taiwan fab delay:** Fab 11E construction halted over a labor dispute; a longer delay pushes 2028 supply later (bullish for pricing, bearish for Micron's own 2028 volumes).
- **Already +218% in 12 months:** the easy money is behind it; the remaining return depends on the shortage persisting, not on a rerating from a low base.
