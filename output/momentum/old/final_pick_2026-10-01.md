## Why it's the category leader

Only three firms on earth — Samsung, SK hynix and Micron — manufacture leading-edge DRAM/HBM at scale, and Micron is qualification-locked into Nvidia's AI roadmap (HBM4 shipping for Vera Rubin; custom HBM4E in co-development). Its fiscal Q4 2026 print is the scoreboard: revenue \$54.23B (+379% YoY) at an 87.0% non-GAAP gross margin. In a shortage, the leader with committed volumes captures the rent; Micron's SCA book makes it the landlord, not a bidder.

## Why it's irreplaceable (the trap test — PASS)

The doctrine's trap is disintermediation: a customer in-sourcing the supplier. It cannot fire here on any investable horizon. A leading-edge fab costs ~\$10–20B and takes 2–3+ years plus per-generation Nvidia qualification; hyperscalers hold no memory process IP. Their revealed behaviour settles the question — they are prepaying Micron \$32B in commitments rather than attempting to build memory. All three lenses that picked MU rated the disintermediation risk **low**; the residual risks are share shift *inside* the oligopoly (HBM share 21% → 18% as Samsung resurged) and China's CXMT in conventional DRAM — substitution between irreplaceable suppliers, not bypass. **Trap veto: not triggered.**
