## Per-name findings

### MU — Micron Technology (shortage 10, irreplaceability 7)

Shortage thesis: the purest sold-out AI-memory shortage in the field; HBM sold out with take-or-pay contracts locking price and volume. Backlog: multi-year HBM commitments (see batch 1 for the dated figures); the batch notes Micron is the number 3 HBM supplier. Category: one of only three firms that make DRAM at scale. Irreplaceability: 7 — nobody outside the three can replicate at scale, but within the three Micron is replaceable at the margin. Risks: famously cyclical memory industry; pricing can roll over fast. Verdict: the purest sold-out AI-memory shortage, but the number 3 HBM supplier in a cyclical industry.

### LLY — Eli Lilly (shortage 4, irreplaceability 8)

Shortage thesis: a demand surge, not a current shortage — the tirzepatide shortage is formally resolved, so the case rests on volume growth and pipeline, not scarcity pricing. Backlog: not found and not applicable — pharma does not disclose an order backlog; do not substitute guidance for backlog. Category: incretin duopoly leader with patent plus manufacturing scale. Irreplaceability: 8, discounted for oral competition and eventual loss of exclusivity. Risks: oral competition, pricing pressure, valuation at the analyst mean target (upside -1.0%).

### NVDA — Nvidia (shortage 9, irreplaceability 9)

Shortage thesis: the bottleneck asset of the AI buildout; Blackwell systems sold out, with about \$500 billion in Blackwell and Rubin orders/revenue visibility through end of calendar 2026 (GTC, 28 October 2026; CFO visibility statement, mid-October 2026). Backlog: a platform order book, not a single GAAP backlog total — Nvidia does not publish one. Category: ~80-90% estimated accelerator share, CUDA platform. Irreplaceability: 9 — replicable in inference at the margin, not replicable as a full training platform today; but the trap is live (hyperscaler ASICs: TPU, Trainium, Maia, MTIA). Earnings-quality flags: HIGH_ACCRUALS and RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN judged mostly a hypergrowth working-capital artefact, not evidence of fabrication on its own — but cash conversion, not EPS, is the series to watch; receivables concentration in five customers is disclosed and real. Risks: customer concentration, customer-financed demand, 17 November print. Verdict: sold out with half-a-trillion dollars of platform visibility, discounted by concentration and customer-financed demand.

### ANET — Arista Networks (shortage 6, irreplaceability 6)

Shortage thesis: component-constrained AI demand, not product scarcity; AI-networking demand surge constrained by components. Backlog: no precise company-disclosed AI backlog figure found — a claimed \$10 billion AI backlog is unverified and not adopted; multiyear purchase commitments of \$9.7 billion are Arista's commitments to suppliers, not customer backlog. Category: Ethernet-for-AI leader. Irreplaceability: 6 — EOS loyalty is real, but the silicon is bought in and the largest customer-adjacent competitor sells the whole stack. Risks: Nvidia and Cisco contesting AI fabrics; 3 November print. Verdict: a strong AI-networking demand surge with a real but contestable EOS moat.

### CF — CF Industries (shortage 6, irreplaceability 5)

Shortage thesis: credible cyclical tightness with a genuine North American cost advantage, but the product is fungible. Backlog: not found — fertilizer producers do not report an order backlog; closest forward quantity is full-year 2026 gross ammonia production guidance of approximately 9.5 million tons (Q2 2026 release, 2026-08-05). Category: largest US nitrogen producer. Irreplaceability: 5 — commodity, replaceable by imports when geopolitics ease. Risks: volumes fell in Q2, thesis depends on geopolitics staying tight; 4 November print. Verdict: a cyclical tightness trade, not an explosive structural-shortage bet.

### EOG — EOG Resources (shortage 3, irreplaceability 4)

Shortage thesis: fails the core test — its product is not scarce, its customers have infinite substitutes, and near-term inventories were building. Backlog: not applicable; the analogue is reserves (proved reserves up 16% to 5.5 billion boe at end-2025, replacing 254% of 2025 production), which is undeveloped resource, not contracted orders. Category: one of the largest US independents, lowest-cost Permian operator. Irreplaceability: 4. Risks: oil price beta, inventory builds. Verdict: a best-in-class shale operator and cash-return machine, but not a shortage-driven repricing candidate.

### APH — Amphenol (shortage 9, irreplaceability 7)

Shortage thesis: the standout of its batch — record orders outrunning record shipments, ~50-week fiber lead times, hyperscalers locking capacity years ahead, and a raised guide. Backlog: Q3 2026 orders (bookings) were a record \$12.17 billion, up 99% year over year and 13% sequentially, against sales of \$10.23 billion — a 1.19x book-to-bill (company release, 2026-10-28); orders exceeded shipments by roughly \$1.9 billion in the quarter. Absolute backlog stock not found. Category: interconnect leader; IT Datacom grew 77% organically with AI ~three-quarters of that segment. Irreplaceability: 7 — interconnect is multi-sourced (TE Connectivity, Molex, Samtec), so share can be lost at renewal even though the supplier cannot be fully bypassed. Risks: dual-sourcing caps pricing power. Verdict: the closest thing here to a genuine supply-constrained explosive grower.

### MSFT — Microsoft (shortage 8, irreplaceability 8)

Shortage thesis: the purest "demand exceeds available capacity" case at hyperscale, but the shortage is being bought away with enormous capex. Backlog: commercial remaining performance obligation (RPO) of \$677 billion at 2026-09-30, up 73% year over year (Q1 FY2027 release, 2026-10-28); total RPO \$683 billion, ~30% due within twelve months; leases not yet commenced a further \$364.6 billion. Note: RPO includes OpenAI-related commitments and is not a pure third-party Azure backlog; a standalone Azure-only split was not found. Category: Azure plus the Windows/M365/GitHub enterprise stack. Irreplaceability: 8 — no enterprise can replicate the footprint at comparable cost; the realistic alternative is switching clouds, which is slow and expensive. Risks: explosive upside depends on demand staying ahead of a ~\$170 billion annual build-out. Verdict: a genuine capacity-shortage story whose shortage is being aggressively bought away.

### NUE — Nucor (shortage 7, irreplaceability 4)

Shortage thesis: a genuine, tariff-amplified demand surge with record volumes, a doubled backlog into 2028 and weekly price increases — but a commodity surge, not an irreplaceable bottleneck. Backlog: no dollar figure disclosed; steel-products backlogs up ~25% (Q3 2026 call, October 2026), towers/structures backlog more than doubled since the start of the year and extending into early 2028; data centres ~15% of company-wide backlog. Category: largest US steelmaker; can supply ~95% of the steel inside and outside a data centre. Irreplaceability: 4 — steel is replaceable, tariff protection is policy, not a moat. Risks: steel price cycle, tariff reversal. Verdict: price and volume, not a moat.

### WST — West Pharmaceutical Services (shortage 6, irreplaceability 8)

Shortage thesis: a regulated, validation-locked demand surge — GLP-1 plus Annex 1 upgrades growing high-value products (HVP) at 20% organically — not a sold-out shortage; no lead-time extension, allocation or stock-out quote was found. Backlog: formal dollar backlog not found; proxies only (HVP growth, capacity additions) — do not convert them into a dollar backlog. Category: HVP packaging leader for biologics/GLP-1. Irreplaceability: 8 — regulatory qualification lock-in. Risks: full valuation (41.3x forward), no verifiable backlog. Verdict: durable compounding rather than a scarcity spike.

### CSCO — Cisco (shortage 7, irreplaceability 6)

Shortage thesis: the strongest order-flow surge in its batch, but contested and margin-dilutive at a full price. Backlog: no AI-only backlog disclosed — do not compute one from \$9.3 billion orders minus ~\$4 billion revenue; total RPO at 25 July 2026 was \$46.734 billion (company-wide, not AI). Order flow: \$9.3 billion of fiscal 2026 hyperscaler AI orders, \$4 billion in Q4 alone; fiscal 2027 AI revenue guide ~\$7.5 billion. Category: enterprise networking incumbent; ~8/10 in enterprise campus, ~5/10 in hyperscaler AI fabrics (overall 6). Irreplaceability: 6. Earnings-quality flag: INVENTORY_BUILD (0.6239) — mostly benign pre-build against a known order book (inventory ~\$5.694 billion at 25 July 2026, purchase commitments \$17.17 billion, management expects inventory to rise further "as we work to fulfill this demand"), with a real overhang tail if hyperscaler capex pauses; finished goods rose from \$933 million to \$1,525 million. Risks: 11 November print must confirm AI revenue conversion, not just orders; share-fight vs ANET/NVDA. Verdict: needs the print to confirm conversion.

### CDNS — Cadence Design Systems (shortage 7, irreplaceability 8)

Shortage thesis: AI chip-design demand outrunning verification and design capacity; hardware-assisted verification (Palladium/Protium) delivered "another record quarter, driven by unprecedented demand from AI and HPC customers" (Q3 2026 call, 2026-10-26/27). Backlog: record \$8.2 billion at 2026-09-30 (Q3 2026 release, 2026-10-26), with \$4.2 billion of RPO expected in the next 12 months; recurring revenue growth accelerated to 18% year over year. Q3 2026 revenue \$1.633 billion, +22% year over year; raised 21% growth guide. Category: EDA oligopoly (with Synopsys); verification hardware is the bottleneck. Irreplaceability: 8. Earnings-quality flags: RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN (0.2417) and INVENTORY_BUILD (0.3483) judged largely benign — ratable revenue timing plus a record backlog mechanically lifts billed-not-yet-collected balances; CFO/NI 1.4254 contradicts an earnings-ahead-of-cash story; inventory pre-build tied by management to fulfilling a significant hardware backlog, clearing only if Palladium/Protium shipments convert; watch DSO (66 days) and Q4 inventory drawdown. Risks: EDA cyclicality, Hexagon acquisition comparatives. Verdict: the strongest shortage-adjacent name in its batch.

### VRTX — Vertex Pharmaceuticals (shortage 4, irreplaceability 8)

Shortage thesis: a near-monopoly cash machine, not a shortage play — the explosive case rests on Journavx, Casgevy and renal/endocrine optionality, not on supply failing to meet CF demand. Backlog: not found, not applicable — pharma recurring-prescription model; closest forward figures are CF product revenue \$3,207.9 million in Q2 2026 (+11% year over year) and full-year 2026 guidance of \$13.1-13.2 billion (raised August 2026). Category: cystic-fibrosis near-monopoly (9/10 for the CF franchise alone). Irreplaceability: 8 overall. Risks: Q3 due 2026-11-02; pain/gene-therapy optionality unproven at scale. Verdict: monopoly compounding, not a shortage.

### AME — Ametek (shortage 6, irreplaceability 6)

Shortage thesis: a broad infrastructure-demand compounder with a record order book, but no single irreplaceable product and no documented price-gouging shortage, so steadier than explosive. Backlog: record \$4.11 billion at 2026-06-30 (Q2 2026 call, 2026-08-04), up ~21% since end-2025; Q2 orders a record \$2.3 billion (+28% year over year, organic +25%), book-to-bill 1.12; ~80% expected to ship within 12 months, "filling demand into 2027". Category: diversified certified electrical/aerospace/medical niches. Irreplaceability: 6. Risks: 3 November print binary; part of the order surge is acquisition-flattered (organic +25% is the cleaner figure). Verdict: steadier than explosive.
