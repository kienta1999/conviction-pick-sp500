## Per-name thesis

**1. APH — the shortage is booked, not hoped for.** Q3 2026 orders hit a record 12.17B USD, up 99% year over year, at a 1.19x book-to-bill on 10.23B USD of sales, with AI at about three-quarters of IT Datacom revenue (plus 77% organic) and fiber lead times extending to about 50 weeks as hyperscalers secure capacity years ahead — customers are asking Amphenol to source Corning-spec-equivalent fiber because they cannot get enough directly from Corning. It is number 2 globally by connector sales (Bishop 2024: TE 12.88B USD, Amphenol 11.51B USD) and arguably number 1 in the AI-relevant high-speed/fiber niche. The moat is breadth plus qualification lock-in (6/10): the qualified supplier base at AI scale is tiny, but multi-sourcing by design means the risk is share loss to TE/Molex, not bypass. See the final pick for scenarios, sizing, and exit triggers.

**2. WST — the cleanest structural shortage in the batch, at the fullest price.** A filed-in, qualification-locked component riding GLP-1 volume (HVP revenue well into the 30% range organically in Q3, just under 12% of total revenue) plus a multi-year Annex 1 upgrade wave (nearly 800 projects in hand, up about 50% year over year; 200+ HVP upgrade projects added during 2026; third-party estimate only about 15% converted at end-2025). The Q3 print (2026-10-29) confirmed rather than merely promising it: net sales 874.5M USD, plus 11.9% organic, vs consensus about 827.1M USD; adjusted EPS 2.66 USD vs consensus 2.20 USD; gross margin expanded 310 bps to 39.7%; guidance raised to FY2026 sales 3.41-3.42B USD and adjusted EPS 9.38-9.48 USD. The constraint on explosive upside is valuation, not demand: screen analyst upside minus 0.1%, P/E about 49-53x, largest customer 15.8% of 2025 sales. Irreplaceability 8/10: stoppers written into approved drug filings, switching forces equivalence data.

**3. MU — the deepest shortage, the sharpest cycle risk.** DRAM, NAND and HBM constrained at once: HBM sold out nearly all calendar 2027 supply at significantly higher prices, customer discussions moved to 2028, over 75% of expected 2027 output already committed, 26 long-term agreements representing 32B USD in customer commitments. Fiscal Q4 2026 (2026-09-30): revenue 54.23B USD, plus 379% YoY; Q1 FY27 guide about 61.5B USD. The category is irreplaceable (one of three DRAM makers on earth) but Micron specifically is substitutable within the oligopoly — SK hynix holds about 50% HBM share, Samsung gained 21% to 33% in one quarter (7/10). The colossal growth is mostly conventional DRAM/NAND prices, which can reverse; under 10% of revenue is HBM.

**4. NVDA — demand exceeds supply, and the customers are already routing around it.** The purest demand-supply gap in the dossier by management's own admission (cloud GPUs sold out, FY2028 growth outlook supply-constrained at about 70%), with visibility framed at 500B USD through end-2026 and at least 1T USD through 2027 — neither an audited backlog, and the two figures must not be added together. Category number 1 by an order of magnitude (about 75-86% data-center AI-accelerator revenue share) with the full stack (GPU plus CPU plus NVLink plus networking plus CUDA) and TSMC CoWoS priority allocation (8/10). The live trap: Google TPU, AWS Trainium, Microsoft Maia, Meta MTIA, and Broadcom AI-ASIC revenue of 8.4B USD in a single quarter — dual-sourcing is now the norm, and the shortage is partly financed by Nvidia itself (receivables 63.1B USD, 5 customers equal to 70% of the balance; H1 FY27 net income includes 23.7B USD of equity-security gains).

**5. ISRG — the purest moat in the dossier, with no shortage to ride.** 12,094 da Vinci systems plus 1,152 Ion systems, 84% recurring revenue, Q3 2026 placements up 28% to 546 da Vinci systems, procedures up 14-15% — but supply meets demand, so the strength is irreplaceability (9/10: 25+ years of FDA clearances, surgeon training, EndoWrist instruments, switching costs), not scarcity (5/10). The near-term risk is self-inflicted: a deliberate 3-5% cut to instrument revenue per procedure from extended-life instruments, volume-based pricing from H1 2027, and a China price cut from November, plus a 450M USD settlement and the first real competition in 25 years (Hugo FDA-cleared Dec 2025, Ottava pending). The Q3 beat did not lift the stock — the market prices dominance and punishes deceleration (screen upside only plus 7.5%).

**6. FCX —** Mined-copper scarcity at record prices (realised 6.54 USD/lb, plus 39.7% YoY) with a 17-year mine-build clock, but a recovery story selling into that shortage at reduced volumes (Grasberg at 67% of pre-incident capacity; 2026/2027 sales guides just cut).

**7. SNDK —** Purest NAND proxy with NBM agreements booked through 2030 (at least 93.9B USD minimum expected revenue, about 91B USD remaining performance obligations), but the smallest of the big five (about 11% share) in a commodity market — and its Q1 FY27 beat-and-raise still sold off after hours.

**8. CSCO —** A genuine, dated, quantified demand surge (9.3B USD AI orders, plus 35% product orders) that Cisco is furiously supplying rather than rationing — inventory up about 80% vs revenue up 11.8%, purchase commitments 17.17B USD — with the 12 Nov print as the conversion test.

**9. NUE —** Tariff-walled domestic steel squeeze with record shipments (over 7.2m tons, third consecutive quarterly record) and a plus 25% downstream backlog, but the wall is a policy choice (Section 232 at 50%) that can be legislated away.

**10. AME —** Record 4.11B USD backlog at Q2 (plus 21% since year-end 2025, book-to-bill 1.12) and a multiyear infrastructure-demand story across niches, but a diversified portfolio rather than a single shortage — and the 2026-11-03 print is a binary event, not a confirmed acceleration.

## Risk lens & leverage-safety note

Sector concentration: the top 3 span electronic components, health-care supplies, and semiconductors — but the underlying driver is concentrated: AI datacenter buildout (APH, MU, NVDA, SNDK, CSCO, ANET) plus commodity cycles (FCX, NUE, CF, FANG). A hyperscaler capex pause hits the AI names together; a copper or steel price reversal hits the commodity names together. Highest-risk vs steadier: MU and SNDK are the highest-beta cyclicals (memory prices can reverse violently — prior-cycle history for MU includes a decades-long recovery from its summer 2000 high); WST and ISRG are the steadier compounders (recurring/filing-locked revenue) but carry the fullest valuations. Drawdown reality: memory names have drawn down 50%+ fast in past cycles; APH dropped 7% in a single session on sentiment alone on 2026-10-21. Education, not advice: no specific leverage multiple or position size is recommended here — policy for picks from this system is cash-only.
