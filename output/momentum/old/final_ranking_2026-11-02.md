## Per-name thesis (top 5)

**1. NVDA —** The capacity monopolist of AI compute: CFO visibility to ~\$0.5tn in Blackwell +
Rubin revenue for calendar 2025–2026 (corrected window — see verification; superseded by
\$1tn+ visibility through 2027 given March 2026), \$279bn of supply/capacity commitments at
26 Jul 2026 (10-Q Note 10), cloud GPUs sold out per CEO language. Moat: CUDA + full-stack
platform, irreplaceability 9/10 today decaying toward ~7/10 as inference grows. Caveat, stated
plainly: hyperscaler custom silicon is the live trap, and the receivables/guarantee financing
loop is the contrarian lens's named overhang. Next earnings 2026-11-17.

**2. CDNS —** See final_pick_2026-11-02.md for the full thesis. Record \$8.2bn backlog
(\$4.2bn RPO due in 12 months), FY2026 guide raised to \$6.387–\$6.417bn (~21% growth), third
raise of 2026. The trap inverts here: hyperscaler in-sourcing creates Cadence customers.

**3. APH —** Record Q3 2026 orders \$12.17bn (+99% YoY, +13% sequentially), book-to-bill
1.19:1, Q3 sales a record \$10.2bn (+65% YoY, +37% organically). IT Datacom roughly 4x its size
of two years ago, 48% of Q3 sales, AI about three-quarters of IT Datacom. Moat: breadth across
copper/optics/power, 100+ plants, long qualification cycles — but individual sockets are
contestable and customers hold pricing power. Watch for a double-ordering unwind.

**4. MU —** Sold-out HBM through 2026, only 50–66% of key demand met; revenue growth TTM
+148.3%, forward PE 4.3x, analyst mean target implies +76.5% upside. Yesterday's single pick,
today's Borda #4: the panel cooled on the #3 HBM position (SK hynix ~50% share) and the
cyclical reversal risk, not on the shortage itself.

**5. WST —** Demand exceeding supply for high-value pharma packaging components with an
18–24-month capacity lag; HVP Components revenue \$467m in Q3 2026 (+20% organically, 53% of
sales); #1 in elastomeric closures (~24% vs Datwyler ~18%). Re-validation lock-in is the moat
(8/10); the flag is the 41.3x multiple and GLP-1 concentration.

**6. ANET —** Deferred revenue \$6.9bn, demand outstripping component supply to 2028, EOS
switching costs; but the silicon is Broadcom's and the biggest customer (via Nvidia) is also
the biggest rival. **7. LLY —** Mounjaro 51% of US incretin-class prescriptions, FY2026 guide
raised to \$91–\$92bn; but the shortage is over (FDA delisted Mar 2025), realised prices fell
15% YoY in Q3, and the stock sits at its 52-week high with negative analyst upside.
**8. NUE —** Record Q3 shipments (>7.2m tons), Steel Products backlog +25%, Towers backlog
doubled into early 2028, HRC at a four-year high — tariff-assisted cyclical, not structural.
**9. CSCO —** AI orders \$9.3bn in FY2026 (~4.5x FY2025), #1 overall in switching/routing, but
#~4 in AI back-end fabrics; inventory build (+80% to \$5.69bn) is disclosed and demand-driven
but a real margin risk if hyperscaler orders slow. **10. VRTX —** CF monopoly (>95% of CF drug
revenue, patents cited through 2037); the explosive case is povetacicept (PDUFA Nov 30) and the
~\$8.8bn Crinetics deal, not any shortage — and Q3 reports after close today, Nov 2.

## Risk lens & leverage-safety note

Sector concentration: 6 of the top 10 are Technology, and the top 4 are all AI-capex proxies —
a single hyperscaler capex pause hits the whole top of the list at once. Highest-risk names:
MU (memory cyclicality, 50%+ drawdowns in past cycles) and NVDA (multiple + financing-loop
overhang); steadier compounders: CDNS (contracted backlog), WST (re-validation lock-in),
VRTX (franchise monopoly). Drawdown reality: these are high-beta names and the system's policy
is cash-only; nothing here recommends any leverage multiple or position size.

  rollover), ANET (deferred revenue decline).
