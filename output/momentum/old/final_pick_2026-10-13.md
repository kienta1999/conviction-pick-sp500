# Momentum conviction pick — 2026-10-13

## THE PICK: MU — Micron Technology

- Sector / sub-industry: Information Technology / Semiconductors
- Price at pick: $989.04 (screen, shortlist_2026-10-13.json) — analyst mean target $1,555.13 (+57.2%)
- Market cap: ~$1,162B — forward P/E 4.8 — next earnings 2026-12-23

One-paragraph thesis: Micron is the only name in the 14-name dossier where a hard physical shortage, a hard contract book, and multi-year revenue visibility all coincide. HBM is sold out for calendar 2026 with the vast majority of 2027 already contracted and management guiding 2027/2028 tighter still, and 26 take-or-pay Strategic Customer Agreements convert that scarcity into roughly $150B of remaining performance obligations at minimum pricing, backed by ~$32B of customer financial commitments. The market still prices a normal memory cycle (4.8x forward earnings) while the contract structure is explicitly designed to damp that cycle — if the floors hold, the return comes from both earnings delivery and re-rating.

## The shortage + backlog evidence

- ~$150B RPO under 26 take-or-pay SCAs (up from 16 in June 2026), covering >35% of estimated revenue through 2030, some agreements into 2031; ~$32B in customer commitments, mostly cash deposits. Verified with one nuance: the $150B covers SCAs with defined pricing frameworks (about 3/4 of expected SCA revenue) at minimum pricing — i.e. it is a floor, not an expected value (see What was verified).
- HBM sold out for calendar 2026; >75% of 2027 output already committed; DRAM lead times reported >40 weeks; no meaningful new fab volume until late 2028.
- FQ4 FY26 revenue $54.23B (+379% YoY); FQ1 FY27 guided to $61.5B ± $1.5B, above consensus at the time of the print.
- SNDK-style spot exposure is the contrast inside the same dossier: NAND pricing gains are already decelerating (+70–75% in Q2 2026 toward a forecast +10–20%), while Micron's pricing is increasingly floored by contract.

## Why it is the category leader

Micron is one of only three firms on earth making leading-edge DRAM/HBM at scale (with Samsung and SK hynix) — #3 in DRAM (~23%) and #3 in HBM (~18% revenue share, Counterpoint Q2 2026: SK hynix 50 / Samsung 33 / Micron 18). It is not the share leader; it is the only US-headquartered member of the oligopoly, which is precisely why hyperscalers are prepaying it to secure supply.

## Why it is irreplaceable — and the honest caveat

Hyperscalers cannot in-source DRAM/HBM: they have no memory process technology, and a leading-edge fab costs tens of billions and takes years. The dossier's evidence runs the other way — customers committed ~$32B, largely in deposits, to lock Micron's output; that is the opposite of disintermediation, and panelist A rated the trap risk low on exactly this ground. The honest caveat, which caps the dossier irreplaceability score at 6/10: substitution *inside* the oligopoly is real. Micron's HBM share slipped 21% → 18% in Q2 2026 as Samsung's HBM4 ramped, and CXMT has reached ~10% DRAM share. A customer can switch supplier; it cannot bypass memory or build it. The trap that kills this pick is not in-sourcing — it is the commodity half of the book plus a pricing rollover, which is what the bear case below prices.
