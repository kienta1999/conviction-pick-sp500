## CAT — Caterpillar Inc. (baseline $805.25)

Hyperscale data centers (50 MW+ facilities) are being built faster than utility grid connections can be established — a grid interconnection can take 3–7 years, while data centers need power on day one — so CAT's large reciprocating engines and gas turbine gensets are being ordered as prime (continuous) power, not just backup (techtimes.com, 2026-07-29). Power-generation sales to users grew 72% YoY in Q2 2026; in Q1 2026 power-gen revenue grew 41% YoY to $2.817B (trefis.com, 2026-09-09; techtimes.com, 2026-07-29). "Data center developers keep asking for more units than Caterpillar can ship, and its backlog of generator orders now stretches to 2030" — the binding constraint is capacity, not demand (businessmodelanalyst.com, ~2026-08-16). CAT is scaling large reciprocating-engine output from 2× its 2024 level toward nearly 3× by 2030, with most capex planned 2027–2029, and is restarting a gas engine platform shut down years ago to bring ~1.5 GW back online (techtimes.com, 2026-07-29; trefis.com, 2026-09-09).

**2. Backlog / order book.** Record **$72B** at end of Q2 2026 (reported 2026-08-04), **+92% YoY** and +$9B sequentially, up across all three segments; ~59% expected to deliver within 12 months (Zacks, 2026-08-04 / 2026-08-13). Q1 2026 backlog was $63B, +79% YoY (techtimes.com, 2026-07-29). Some Power & Energy customers are placing orders as far out as **2030** (dollarstockclub.com, 2026-09-15). On the 2026-08-04 earnings call, CEO Joe Creed said he had "not seen demand slowing in the data center market" and customers are planning with CAT on long timelines (dollarstockclub.com, 2026-09-15).

**3. Category position.** "The world's leading manufacturer of construction and mining equipment" (Tickeron, 2026-09-14) and the world's dominant maker of construction, mining, and power generation equipment (dollarstockclub.com, 2026-09-15). In hyperscale power specifically, one of **fewer than five qualified vendors** of hyperscale-grade backup/prime power in North America (edgen.tech, cited 2026-07-17). Key power-gen peers: Cummins, MTU, Mitsubishi Power, Eaton.

**4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk.** Can hyperscalers build their own gensets? Realistically no at scale: the moat is (a) qualification lock-in — hyperscale-grade prime-power certification with only a handful of qualified North American vendors; (b) manufacturing/process scale — CAT is the only player with an installed base, global dealer-service network, and large-engine production capacity that can be tripled by 2030; (c) capital intensity — a competitor building comparable large-recip capacity needs years of plant/tooling investment. Evidence of demand-side lock-in: in April 2026 CAT signed an agreement to supply **up to 2.1 GW of large gas gensets to PROPWR** (ProPetro), primarily for data centers — its sixth agreement of at least 1 GW for prime power (techtimes.com, 2026-07-29). Risk: the power-gen wedge could narrow if Cummins or Mitsubishi Power close the capacity gap (edgen.tech), and ~$2.2B tariff bill flagged as a margin drag (ainvest.com, 2026-09-14). **Irreplaceability score: 8/10** for the power-gen/AI prime-power wedge; the construction/mining core is a 6 (Komatsu/Deere alternatives exist), blended **7/10**.

**5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months).** Q2 2026 (2026-08-04): first-ever quarter over $20B revenue ($20.5B, +24% YoY), adjusted EPS $8.17 (+73%) vs ~$6.20–6.25 consensus, adjusted operating margin ~20.9–21.9%; raised full-year 2026 sales guidance to mid-to-high teens growth (Zacks, 2026-08-04; ainvest.com, 2026-09-14). Analysts: Freedom Broker upgrade to Buy with $980 PT (2026-09-10); President Capital initiated at Buy, $999 PT (2026-09-18); Zacks upgraded to Strong-Buy (2026-08-12); consensus "Moderate Buy," avg target ~$994 (MarketBeat, via dailypolitical.com 2026-09-11). Wamego, Kansas work-tool plant converted in under a year to ship the PGM130 data-center genset unit (trefis.com, 2026-09-09).

**6. Risks.** Valuation priced for perfection: ~33–35× trailing earnings, ~27% below its $1,073.46 52-week high after a ~90%+ 12-month run (ainvest.com, 2026-09-14). The bear case is a cycle-peak read: record margin at a record multiple mean-reverting (ainvest.com, 2026-09-14). State/local data-center regulation (Texas, New York) was cited when the stock fell ~4% to ~$784 in mid-September; Baird's concern centers on the 2027–2029 capacity-expansion execution timeline (techtimes.com, 2026-07-29; dollarstockclub.com, 2026-09-15). Heavy insider selling: CEO Joseph Creed and others sold substantial shares in recent months (MarketBeat, 2026-09-11).

**7. Shortage score: 9/10.** The purest structural-shortage name in the batch: demand visibly exceeds CAT's ability to manufacture, with backlog booked to 2030 and price/backlog — not plant — absorbing the boom.

**Verdict:** The AI power-gap is real, booked ($72B, +92%), and durably CAT's — but you pay ~34× trailing earnings for it after a 90% run, so the explosive upside depends on the margin story holding through the capacity ramp.

## GOOGL — Alphabet Inc. (baseline $342.36)

**1. Shortage thesis.** The shortage is AI compute capacity itself. CEO Sundar Pichai has told investors "we are compute constrained in the near term" (via scout-ci battlecard citing CNBC, 2026-04-29). Demand for GPUs and TPUs is outstripping available capacity; Q1 2026 generative-AI products grew nearly 800%, Gemini Enterprise +40% QoQ, API token usage 10B→16B/min (webpronews.com, ~2026-06-25). Evidence it's a real shortage, not spin: in June 2026 Google agreed to pay SpaceX **~$920M/month (~$30B total)** for access to ~110,000 Nvidia GPUs housed in SpaceX's Colossus data centers — described by Google itself as short-term "bridge capacity" for surging Gemini Enterprise demand, separate from a ~$1.25B/month deal Anthropic separately signed (techtimes.com, 2026-06-30; dev.to, 2026-09-20). Existing cloud accounts are expanding consumption beyond baseline commitments by **>50%** (Trefis, 2026-09-04).

**2. Backlog / order book.** Cloud backlog (remaining performance obligations) hit **~$514B** at end of Q2 2026 (reported 2026-07-22), up **>$50B sequentially** (Q1 2026 was ~$462–460B, roughly doubling QoQ); **>50% expected to convert to revenue within 24 months**, and the backlog exceeds Alphabet's entire trailing-12-month revenue (~$446B) (cryptobriefing.com, 2026-09-14; fool.com, 2026-09-25). CEO Pichai attributed the surge largely to multi-year AI-workload commitments. Q2 Cloud revenue $24.8B, **+82% YoY** (~$99B annual run rate), operating income tripled to $8.8B (35.6% margin) (fool.com, 2026-09-25; cryptobriefing.com, 2026-09-14).

**3. Category position.** Two categories: (a) Search — #1 globally, **~91%** of search-engine referrals (StatCounter, Aug 2026; digitalapplied.com, 2026-09-22) vs Bing ~4.5%, mobile share 94–95% (resourcera.com, 2026). (b) Cloud — #3 globally at **15%** share (record high, up from 13% a year ago), behind AWS (28%) and Azure (20%); but fastest-growing at 82% vs AWS 37% and Azure 43% (Synergy Research via tech-insider.org, channelweb.co.uk, 2026-09). Search is still 55% of TTM revenue ($243B) and the core profit engine (fool.com, 2026-09-13).

**4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk.** The moat is vertical integration: Gemini models + in-house TPUs (Broadcom partnership; Anthropic expanded to up to 1M TPUs in Oct 2025 and secured ~5 GW of next-gen TPU capacity in April 2026, per CNBC via fool.com, 2026-08-31) + global data-center infrastructure + distribution (Search/Chrome/Android) — tradingview.com, 2026-09-18 notes this gives Alphabet "greater control over inference costs and performance than rivals relying more heavily on third-party technology." Can customers in-source? Hyperscaler customers don't manufacture chips at Google's TPU scale; only Apple/Meta-tier builders can, and even they rent Google capacity (e.g., Meta was reported rationed on Gemini capacity, techtimes.com, 2026-06-30). Substitute risk is in search (AI chatbots — though ChatGPT etc. collectively <1% of search referrals, tech-insider.org, 2026-09-22) and cloud multi-sourcing. **Irreplaceability score: 8/10** — the search moat is near-absolute and the TPU stack is hard to replicate; docked points for being #3 in cloud and for the emerging AI-answer-layer threat.

**5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months).** Q2 2026 (2026-07-22): revenue $119.8B (+24%), EPS ~$8–9.11 (incl. Anthropic stake mark-up, ~14% Alphabet-owned), Search $63.3–63.4B (+17%), Cloud +82%; capex guide raised to $195–205B for 2026 (Q2 capex $44.9B, doubled YoY) (wsj-digest via github.com, 2026-07-25; cryptobriefing.com, 2026-09-14). Google agreed to invest up to $40B in Anthropic ($10B upfront, up to $30B more on milestones) (fool.com, 2026-08-31). Analysts: Evercore ISI reiterated Buy, PT $420→**$450** (2026-09-16); Tigress Street-high **$485**, Strong Buy (~2026-09-18); BofA Buy $430; consensus ~$421–428 (finbold.com, 2026-09-17; tradingview.com, 2026-09-18). ~75% of September analyst notes recommend buying (finbold.com, 2026-09-17). Gemini app: 950M MAUs; ~90% of Fortune 100 use Gemini Enterprise (fool.com, 2026-09-25).

**6. Risks.** Capex shock: Q2 produced Alphabet's first negative FCF print (−$5.9B) since IPO after the guide raise; 2027 spend "increase significantly" (wsj-digest, 2026-07-25). The $514B backlog doesn't generate enough cash on its own to fund a ~$200B single-year build (Trefis, 2026-09-04). Concentration risk: a meaningful slice of contracted future revenue rests on Anthropic's continued commercial success (the Broadcom filing says so), and Alphabet is both supplier and investor (fool.com, 2026-08-31). Stock fell ~7% the day after Q2 on capex concerns and sits ~16% below its $408.61 52-week high — though that also means it's the least-extended mega-cap on valuation (~17× trailing P/E per Finnhub).

**7. Shortage score: 8/10.** Compute demand is genuinely outrunning supply — Google is leasing a competitor's GPUs at $920M/month to bridge — with a $514B contracted backlog as the receipt.

**Verdict:** Fastest-growing hyperscaler at the cheapest multiple of the Big Six, with demand so hot it's rationing capacity — the momentum setup works if capex converts to Cloud margin rather than a multi-year FCF hole.

## MSFT — Microsoft Corporation (baseline $497.93)

**1. Shortage thesis.** Same AI-compute shortage as Alphabet, on an even bigger enterprise base: "customer demand for Azure continued to exceed available capacity" (Q4 FY2026 call, 2026-07-29; tradersunion.com, 2026-09-22). Azure growth **accelerated to 43%** in Q4 FY2026 as new data-center/GPU capacity came online — i.e., growth had been supply-gated earlier in the year (tech-insider.org, 2026-09-17). Microsoft expects **~45% constant-currency Azure growth** for the September quarter and guides calendar-2026 capex of ~**$175B** (tradersunion.com, 2026-09-22). Microsoft 365 Copilot crossed **30M paid seats** (from 20M in April 2026 — +10M in one quarter, fastest since launch), yet penetration is only ~6.5% of the ~464M commercial seat base, so demand runway is mostly unmonetized (alphastreet.com, 2026-09-08).

**2. Backlog / order book.** Commercial remaining performance obligation (RPO) hit **$678B** at end of Q4 FY2026 (reported 2026-07-29), **+84% YoY** (up from $392B at FQ1 FY2026, +51% then); weighted-average duration **2.3 years**, ~30% to be recognized within 12 months, and that near-term slice grew 37% YoY — implying >$200B of contracted revenue landing in FY2027 (tech-insider.org, 2026-09-17; tradingnews.com, 2026-09-24; fool.com, 2025-11-05 for the Q1 figure). All sequential RPO growth in Q4 came from customers outside frontier-model builders — demand is broadening beyond the AI labs (tradersunion.com, 2026-09-22). Azure itself crossed **$100B annual revenue** for the first time (41% FY growth) (tickeron.com, 2026-09-14).

**3. Category position.** #2 in global cloud infra at **20%** share, flat YoY, behind AWS (28%) but ahead of Google Cloud (15%) (Synergy via channelweb.co.uk, 2026-09). In enterprise productivity it is #1 with no close rival: Microsoft Cloud did $59.3B in Q4 FY26 (+27%); the ~464M Microsoft 365 commercial seats are the distribution base nobody else has (alphastreet.com, 2026-09-08). GitHub Copilot hit 50M users (60%+ QoQ revenue growth), AI agents involved in 1 of 3 pull requests (neowin.net, 2026-07-30).

**4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk.** The moat is enterprise lock-in: 464M commercial seats, Active Directory/identity, and Azure-as-default for the enterprise — multi-cloud hedging exists but enterprises "burned by regional capacity limits in 2025 and early 2026" are splitting workloads for risk management, not leaving (tech-insider.org, 2026-09-17). Can customers in-source? Frontier-model customers (OpenAI) are Microsoft's investment, not its competitors; hyperscalers' own-chip efforts (Microsoft Maia) disintermediate Nvidia, not Azure. The concrete concentration risk: ~**45% of commercial RPO is tied to OpenAI** (Reuters, via tech-insider.org, 2026-09-17) — a demand-source concentration, not a build-around threat. **Irreplaceability score: 9/10** — no realistic path exists for an enterprise to route around Microsoft's distribution + Azure + identity stack.

**5. Recent catalysts (last ~3 months).** Q4 FY2026 (2026-07-29): revenue $90.0B (+18%), adjusted EPS $4.74 vs $4.24 est. (+23%), GAAP EPS $4.81 (+32%); Azure +43% (acceleration from 40%); FY2026 revenue $331.8B, net income $133.7B; stock jumped ~8.9% after hours, +37% over the following three months; $260B of market value added in a single evening (tickeron.com, 2026-09-14; indmoney.com, 2026-07-30). Stifel upgraded Hold→Buy, PT $530→**$575** (2026-09-23): "clearly turned the corner" (thestreet.com, 2026-09-25). AnaChart: 92.86% Buy among 30 analysts, avg target $565.57; Wells Fargo Street-high $700 (anachart.com, 2026-09-23). Guidance: double-digit FY27 revenue and operating income growth (tradersunion.com, 2026-09-22).

**6. Risks.** Capex: ~$41B in the June quarter (two-thirds short-lived CPUs/GPUs); FY27 capex grows YoY while operating margins shrink <1pt; FCF fell ~6.5% to ~$67B in FY2026 (tradingnews.com, 2026-09-24). OpenAI concentration (~45% of the $678B RPO) — if OpenAI's economics wobble, the marquee backlog number wobbles too. Valuation: ~28× trailing P/E (Finnhub, live) — not extreme vs the last decade, but the stock is range-bound near $497–516 while the AI bill keeps climbing, and ~11% below its $553.72 52-week high. One analyst note flagged that part of the EPS beat came from a one-time Anthropic investment gain ($3.2B) rather than core operations (indmoney.com, 2026-07-30).

**7. Shortage score: 8/10.** Demand exceeds Azure capacity with accelerating growth as new capacity lands — a genuine compute shortage — though less acute than the peak-constraint quarters, and Microsoft has been bringing supply on faster than Alphabet.

**Verdict:** The enterprise AI monetization engine is working — 30M Copilot seats at 6.5% penetration and $678B of contracted future revenue — but OpenAI's 45% share of that backlog and ~$175B of 2026 capex are the two numbers that can break the multiple.
