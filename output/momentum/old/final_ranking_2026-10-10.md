## Per-name thesis

**1. MU** — The single pick (see `final_pick_2026-10-10.md`): $150B of
take-or-pay RPO at conservative floor pricing with $12.7B of customer deposits
already in hand, HBM sold out through 2027, trading at ~5x forward earnings
because the market prices a spot cycler. The disintermediation trap cannot
fire — no customer can in-source HBM — while the verified Netlist ITC fight is
a bounded tail risk.

**2. SNDK** — Ten "new business model" agreements locking a minimum $93.9B of
committed NAND revenue at floor pricing, backed by $16.5B of enforceable
financial guarantees, covering ~2/3 of FY2028 bit output — while NAND prices
ran +70–75% in Q2 2026 and the stock sits 32% below its high. The growth
lens's pick: +372% YoY revenue acceleration at ~5.9x forward earnings. The
honest caveat (growth lens's own): NAND is the most commoditized memory,
YMTC is gaining share, and every up-cycle has ended in oversupply.

**3. KLAC** — Record $12.57B backlog (+60% YoY), a defect-classification data
moat that compounds on every installed tool, and fabs standardized on KLA as
the reference platform so switching vendors would break process comparability.
The moat lens's pick: 9/10 irreplaceable, the lowest disintermediation risk in
the field. The temper: ~53x trailing earnings after +99% in a year prices a
long AI cycle; WFE bookings are exactly what peaks look like. (Phase 3.5 could
not verify the dossier's "12–24 month component lead time" CEO quote — it is
not cited here.)

**4. AMAT** — Customers' fabs "running at full capacity," 10+ new fabs
announced, contracts being negotiated for 2030, record $9.12B quarter at ~44x
earnings after +141% in a year. Never a nominee, consistently 3rd–6th: the
consensus compounder, not the explosive bet.

**5. CAT** — The contrarian lens's pick: datacenter genset demand outpacing
manufacturing capacity (admitted by rival Cummins), $72.1B record backlog with
orders into 2030, tripling large-engine capacity by 2030 — trading ~25% off
its high on datacenter regulatory fears that actually lengthen grid timelines
and strengthen the prime-power case.

**6. LRCX** — Effectively a duopoly/triopoly on etch; re-qualifying a vendor
means re-qualifying the entire process. Low-$150B WFE outlook, Micron's $32B
commitments as read-through. The earnings-quality flags were judged benign in
Phase 2 (back-loaded quarter-end shipments). China at 26% of revenue and
~56x earnings are the real risks.

**7. NVDA** — The definitive shortage stock (10/10): growth ceiling is its
suppliers' output through FY2028, Vera Rubin the fastest ramp ever. Demoted
because ~$5.8T is fully priced — the explosive bet is on share gains and the
inference-ASIC lane (d-Matrix, NVLink Fusion) versus hyperscaler
disintermediation. The contrarian lens passed it outright as priced in.

**8. LLY** — The purest demand surge of the twelve: #1 in a $100B+ incretin
market it cannot fully supply, volumes (+60%) overwhelming price decay (−13%),
and the shortage thesis still expanding into orals (Foundayo ~1/3 of new oral
starts). No backlog in the pharma sense, so it can never top a shortage lens.

**9. ANET** — Demand "materially above available supply" through ~2028, $9.7B
supply commitments. But Nvidia's bundled Spectrum-X now leads DC Ethernet
share and ~67x P/E prices flawless execution; hyperscalers can white-box
in-source. The AI fabric's #1, fighting a bundler.

**10. APH** — Cleanest demand signal in the field (1.23 book-to-bill, orders
+94%, IT datacom 4x in two years) — but a scale-and-qualification leader, not
a monopoly (irreplaceability 6/10), with ~40% AI revenue concentration and
$18.7B of deal debt.
