# Momentum final pick — 2026-11-13

## THE PICK

**MU — Micron Technology, Inc.** — Technology / Semiconductors (memory). Market cap ~\$820bn at the Nov 12 screen price of **\$811.30** (price_at_pick from `shortlist_2026-11-12.json`). ## One-paragraph thesis

Micron is the purest shortage in the 12-name field: HBM and DRAM are sold out into 2027, with ~\$100bn of binding multi-year contracts and \$32bn of customer cash prepayments backing the order book, while the stock still trades ~33% below its June 2026 peak of \$1,213.37 because the market is pricing the *duration* of 80%+ gross margins, not the shortage itself. Three firms on earth make HBM at scale and no hyperscaler can in-source a memory fab, so the bet is on the cycle lasting — and management, SK Hynix and Samsung all guide to conditions tighter in 2027–28 than in 2026.

## The shortage + backlog evidence

- **~\$100bn in binding multi-year AI memory contracts** — 26 strategic customer agreements (take-or-pay, typically five-year terms through 2030) covering >35% of estimated revenue through 2030. First disclosed June 24, 2026 (16 agreements, ~\$100bn at minimum contracted prices); grown to 26 by the FQ4 call. Verified CONFIRMED.
- **\$32bn in customer financial commitments, mostly cash prepayments** — up from \$22bn a quarter earlier, the vast majority sitting on Micron's balance sheet as cash deposits (CFO Mark Murphy, FQ4 2026 call, Sep 30, 2026). Verified CONFIRMED. Customers are funding capacity years before the memory exists.
- **HBM sold out through 2026 and largely committed for 2027** — CEO Sanjay Mehrotra, FQ4 call: "for 2027, a large part of the volume is already sold out for HBM, and the prices are much higher than 2026 prices"; >75% of Micron's total 2027 output already committed; most customer discussions have moved to 2028 supply. Verified CONFIRMED.
- **Prices did the work in a single quarter** — FQ4 2026 (Sep 30, 2026): DRAM prices up high-teens %, NAND up ~30%, on bit shipments up only mid-single digits. Counterpoint: global DRAM revenue +369% y/y in Q3 2026, shipments up only ~4%.
- **The scale itself changed** — FQ4 2026 revenue \$54.23bn (+379% y/y), FY2026 revenue \$133.19bn (+256%), non-GAAP gross margin 87% in Q4. The dossier flagged these as implausible; verification against Micron's own Sep 30, 2026 release CONFIRMED them exactly (fiscal 2026 was a 53-week year, Q4 ran 14 weeks — the sequential +31% slightly overstates the run-rate, ~21.5% per-week).

## Why it's the category leader

Micron is the **only major US-headquartered memory manufacturer** and #3 in DRAM overall (24% revenue share, Q2 2026) — but it is **#3 in HBM, not #1**: Counterpoint Q2 2026 HBM revenue share is SK Hynix 50%, Samsung 33%, Micron 18% (down from 21% in Q1). The category-leader claim here is about the *oligopoly*, not Micron alone: the three firms make >95% of world DRAM, and HBM consumes ~3x the wafer capacity per GB of ordinary DRAM, so AI servers + HBM now consume close to 70% of total DRAM output. Micron's HBM4 is in volume production for Nvidia's Vera Rubin platform, and its 1-gamma DRAM node claims 30% better bit density at >20% lower power. The honest framing: Micron leads in being the US supplier of a sold-out commodity, not in HBM share.

## Why it's irreplaceable

Only **three firms on earth** make HBM at scale. HBM requires advanced DRAM process + TSV stacking + packaging and Nvidia/AMD/Google qualification — a multi-year lock-in. A hyperscaler cannot realistically make memory itself: fabs cost tens of billions and take years (Micron's New York fab delivers its first wafer only in 2030), and the process-tech depth is not purchasable. No substitute technology routes around HBM in the near term. **Disintermediation risk: low** — all four panelists rated it low. The caveat, stated plainly: the moat protects the *oligopoly*, not Micron specifically. Customers dual-/triple-source among the three, and Samsung took HBM share from 15% to 33% in a year while Micron fell 21% to 18%. The trap cannot touch the memory category; it can still cost Micron share inside it.

## Key swing factors

1. **HBM/DRAM contract pricing through 2027** — whether the "significant price increases year over year" already agreed for 2027 hold or roll over.
2. **Backlog conversion vs cancellation** — whether the \$100bn of SCAs and \$32bn of prepayments convert to shipments or get deferred/cancelled if AI buildout slows.
3. **Micron's HBM share** — 18% and falling vs Samsung's 33% and rising; share loss inside a sold-out market still costs revenue.
4. **Datacenter power wall** — whether power availability caps the AI buildout that drives memory demand.
5. **Double-ordering** — whether the 2026 panic buying created phantom demand that unwinds into 2027 oversupply.

## Holding period & exit plan

**Horizon:** 12–18 months, tied to the thesis timeline — the 2027 HBM contract repricing and the 2027–28 tightness guidance. Base target \$1,213 by ~2027-Q2; bull target \$1,560 by ~2027-Q4 if the shortage extends and share holds.

**Exit if the price hits the downside trigger \$560** (the bear target — the scorecard-enforced stop) **or any thesis-break trigger fires:**

- Backlog shrinking: SCAs cancelled/deferred, or the \$32bn of customer deposits drawn down without shipment.
- Pricing rolling over: DRAM/NAND contract prices falling QoQ for two consecutive quarters.
- Margins peaking QoQ: non-GAAP gross margin rolling over from the 87% Q4 level.
- A top customer announcing in-sourcing of memory (not credible near-term, but the trigger stands).
- HBM share falling below ~15% in Counterpoint's tracker while Samsung keeps gaining.

That drawdown profile is brutal with margin; this system is cash-only by policy (POLICY.md §2). This is risk education, not a leverage recommendation.

## Key risks

- **Cyclicality / duration is the whole debate** — the market is re-pricing how long 80%+ gross margins last. Double-ordering could flip severe shortage into oversupply.
- **HBM share loss** — 21% → 18% while Samsung went 15% → 33% in a year; Samsung–Nvidia HBM4 shipments are a live threat.
- **Customer concentration** — HBM demand is Nvidia/AMD/hyperscaler-concentrated; 26 agreements cover >35% of revenue through 2030.
- **Cash/capex** — Barclays flagged cash could get "uncomfortably tight by November 2026" if capex stays on its current path.
- **Valuation already pricing it in, partially** — the stock ran to \$1,213 in June 2026 on this exact thesis; the FQ4 blowout did not reclaim the peak by Nov 12.
