## One-paragraph thesis

Micron is the purest supply-constrained play in the dossier: AI demand is pulling HBM, server DDR5 and enterprise SSDs faster than wafer supply can grow, and the tightness is contracted rather than merely spotted — remaining performance obligations of ~\$150B, \$32B in customer financial commitments mostly in cash deposits, and more than 75% of fiscal 2027 output already committed, with management guiding that 2027 and 2028 will be tighter than 2026 and no new fab relief at scale before late 2027. The Nov 4 selloff (−7.0%, −26% in a month, −34–37% from the \$1,255 52-week high) repriced the name after a sentiment reset — Democratic midterm wins raising datacenter-permit fears plus Kioxia's NAND warning — not after any order cancellation or capex cut. At a 4.1x forward PE against a \$1,561.91 analyst mean target, the market is still pricing a memory cycle rather than a contracted shortage, so the explosive case is duration: every quarter the shortage persists beyond 2028 reprices the contracted book upward.

## The shortage + backlog evidence

- Remaining performance obligations ~\$150B at fiscal Q4 2026 (from ~\$100B the prior quarter), a minimum-pricing figure covering only strategic customer agreements with a determined pricing framework — verified; management says actual revenue from those agreements is expected to exceed it.
- \$32B in customer financial commitments under 26 strategic customer agreements, "the vast majority" cash deposits (\$12.3B of deposits in the fourth quarter alone) — verified (Micron fiscal Q4 2026 call, Sep 30, 2026).
- More than 75% of fiscal 2027 output already committed, across SCA and non-SCA customers — verified, with the precision caveat that the figure covers allocations and non-SCA contracts, not only formal take-or-pay SCAs (SCAs cover only ~35% of revenue through 2030).
- Management: "we expect memory and storage supply-demand conditions to be much tighter in fiscal 2027 and 2028 than they were in 2026" and "we do not have line of sight to when supply and demand will return to balance" (30 Sep 2026 call) — verified. Precision caveat from the verifier: the through-2028 language covers overall DRAM/NAND supply constraint; HBM specifically is sold out through 2026 and largely contracted for 2027, not literally "sold out through 2028".
- FQ4 2026: revenue \$54.23B, non-GAAP EPS \$33.42 (beat \$31.61 consensus), non-GAAP gross margin 87.0% (86.8% GAAP) — verified unanimously across sources, no inter-source inconsistency. FQ1 FY27 guidance: revenue ~\$61.5B, adjusted EPS ~\$38.15, non-GAAP gross margin ~86.25%.
- Datacenters will absorb ~70% of all memory produced worldwide in 2026, up from 20–30% in 2022 (IDC). Apple CEO John Ternus on Nov 2 called the memory-pricing environment "unprecedented" — a buyer confirming the seller's pricing power.
- Dossier shortage score 8/10 — the joint-highest in the field alongside NUE and CF.

## Why it's the category leader

Micron is one of only three firms making leading-edge DRAM/HBM at scale (Samsung 39.4%, SK hynix 24.9%, Micron 23.3% of DRAM revenue, TrendForce 2Q26) — verified with the qualifier "leading-edge … at scale", since CXMT holds ~7.6–10% of overall DRAM revenue in mature segments. It is #3 in DRAM and #3 in HBM (~18%; SK hynix 50%, Samsung 33%), so leadership here means membership of an irreplaceable trio, not sole dominance — the honest version of the claim. It is the only US-headquartered HBM maker. Samsung's HBM share surge (15% → 33% in four quarters) is the share risk, carried in the bear case below.

## Why it's irreplaceable

The moat is fab physics and capital intensity: leading-edge DRAM/HBM requires EUV lithography, TSV stacking and tens of billions in capex, and no source was found of any hyperscaler fabricating DRAM itself — hyperscalers appear in every supplier list only as buyers absorbing price increases on partial fulfilments. That is the product-level answer to this doctrine's trap: a customer cannot in-source the thing itself. The genuine caveat is individual substitutability *within* the trio — Samsung's HBM share jumped from 21% to 33% in one quarter — which is share risk, not disintermediation, and it is why the pick carries a real bear case rather than a clean moat story. Dossier irreplaceability score 6/10 (trio member, individually replaceable).
