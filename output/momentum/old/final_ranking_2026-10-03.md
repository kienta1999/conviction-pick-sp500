## Per-name thesis

**1. MU (Micron Technology).** The single best expression of this doctrine: one of only three DRAM makers on earth, riding a shortage its CEO says will be "much tighter in calendar 2027 and 2028 than in 2026," with demand hard-contracted — 26 take-or-pay Strategic Customer Agreements covering >35% of revenue through 2030, ~\$150B of remaining performance obligations (exceeding entire FY2026 revenue of \$133.2B), and \$12.7B of customer cash deposits on the balance sheet. The custom NV-HBM4E deal with NVIDIA adds a technology differentiator inside the hardest-to-make product. The bet is that 2027 contract repricing keeps beating the "peak earnings" skeptics.

**2. LRCX (Lam Research).** Owns the etch chokepoint of a fab buildout the industry itself admits it cannot keep up with — the CFO's own words at the Sept 2026 Citi conference: "the industry is fundamentally under-supplying demand, and clean room is a constraint." ~45% global dry-etch share with tools qualified as process-of-record into fab recipes no fab will rip out mid-cycle; customers are TSMC, Samsung, Micron — not hyperscalers — so the in-sourcing trap doesn't apply. Deferred revenue +9.5% to \$2.43B, calendar-2026 WFE outlook raised to the low-\$150B range.

**3. KLAC (KLA Corporation).** The closest thing to a monopoly in the field: ~55–62% process-control share, ~85% of reticle inspection, with a \$12.57B backlog (+60% YoY) as hard proof that yield intensity is the scarce input as nodes shrink. Advanced-packaging process-control revenue guided +70% YoY to ~\$1.1B. Physics + algorithms + fab-data moat no customer can in-source. **EV upside +12.9% is below the +15% guardrail — it stays in the ranking, but the actionability warning is explicit.**

**4. CAT (Caterpillar).** The most literal shortage in the field: record \$72.1B backlog (+92% YoY, ~1x TTM revenue) with gas prime-power engine lead times into late 2028–2029 and orders placed as far out as 2030 — booked-before-built demand for the one input (power) an AI datacenter cannot open without. The hyperscale qualified-vendor list is an oligopoly (CAT, Cummins, Generac — all capacity-constrained). Valuation prices in years of it.

**5. SNDK (SanDisk).** Purest NAND lever: 8 NBM customers signed covering ~50% of FY2027 bits and nearly two-thirds of FY2028 bits at a \$93.9B floor-priced minimum. But #5–6 in NAND share (~11%), owns no fabs (buys wafers from the Kioxia JV), and is +3,000% YTD with press already citing priced-for-perfection risk — explosive, but the moat is the weakest of the memory names.

**6. AMAT (Applied Materials).** Broadest WFE toolbox, full fabs, 8-quarter visibility, 2030 contracts being negotiated, +52% DRAM tool revenue. The caveat: record ~\$7B/quarter tool sales may be a cyclical peak, and China curbs hit ~2% of revenue.

**7. APH (Amphenol).** Physical toll-bridge of the AI-cluster buildout: record \$10.7B orders vs \$8.76B sales (book-to-bill 1.23), IT datacom 4x in two years and 43% of sales. Less defensible moat than the equipment names, and 42x trailing PE near ATH prices in much of the story.

**8. FCX (Freeport-McMoRan).** Largest publicly traded copper producer in a ~400 kmt structural deficit — but its own Grasberg mine is the supply shock (65% capacity, full recovery pushed to 2028). Upside requires the ramp to hold.

**9. CSCO (Cisco).** \$9.3B AI orders (4.5x YoY) in a Dell'Oro-certified supply-constrained market — but #3–4 in the AI back-end it rides, white-box/ODM at 30–40% of hyperscale ports, and hyperscaler customers who can self-source. The trap is flagged; it stays in the ranking as the honest caveat, not the pick.

**10. LLY (Eli Lilly).** Demand-surge compounder (48% Q2 revenue growth), not a shortage — FDA resolved the GLP-1 shortage, capacity is catching up, US realized price decaying −9% y/y. Great business, wrong explosive thesis.

## Risk lens & leverage-safety note

Sector concentration: 5 of the top 10 (MU, LRCX, KLAC, SNDK, AMAT) are memory/semiconductor-cyclicals riding the same AI-capex wave — the ranking is a concentrated cycle bet, not diversification. Steadier compounders: CAT (infrastructure), LLY (pharma). Highest-risk vs steadier: SNDK (explosive, weakest moat) and FCX (execution-gated) at the risky end; LRCX/KLAC (equipment monopolies with hard backlogs) the steadier expressions of the same shortage.

This is risk education, not a recommendation: memory is the highest-beta cyclical in the field (past drawdowns 50%+). Any leverage on a memory name must survive that drawdown reality — this is the doctrine's own warning, not a leverage suggestion. Never a specific leverage multiple or position size.
