# Momentum conviction pick — 2026-10-11

## THE PICK

**MU — Micron Technology**, Semiconductors (memory: DRAM, NAND, HBM).
Market cap ~\$1.16T at pick; current price \$1,029.00.

## One-paragraph thesis

Micron has sold out its HBM3E/HBM4 output through the end of calendar 2027 and
converted the shortage into ~\$150B of remaining performance obligations under
take-or-pay strategic customer agreements at conservative floor pricing, backed
by \$12.7B of customer cash already deposited — yet the stock trades at ~5x
forward earnings, ~15% off its high, because the market still prices it as a
peak-cycle commodity memory stock rather than a contracted-revenue oligopoly.
Hyperscalers cannot in-source DRAM/HBM — Micron is one of exactly three firms
on earth that can make HBM at AI scale — so the classic disintermediation trap
cannot fire here. The one open tail risk from yesterday's pick — the Netlist
ITC exclusion-order fight — was independently verified to have SETTLED on
Oct 6, 2026 (\$600M, 5-year license, all disputes ended), so today's build
removes that bear driver and re-weights the scenarios accordingly.

- **~\$150B of RPO** under 26 strategic customer agreements (up from 16 in
  June), all with take-or-pay volume provisions, priced on "committed volumes
  and minimum pricing" that CFO Mark Murphy called "inherently conservative"
  (Sept 30, 2026 Q4 FY2026 call). >35% of estimated revenue through 2030, some
  extending to 2031. CONFIRMED by Phase 3.5.
- **HBM3E/HBM4 sold out through calendar 2027**, order books extending into
  2028; supply "will likely stay tight past 2027" per management. CONFIRMED.
- **\$12.7B of customer deposits received to date**, with \$12.3B arriving in
  Q4 alone; total customer financial commitments ~\$32B. CONFIRMED.
- **New fab wafer output only arrives mid-2027**, so 2026–H1-2027 supply is
  physically fixed while 2027 HBM pricing is being locked "significantly
  higher" than 2026.
- **Netlist ↔ Micron SETTLED Oct 6, 2026** (Reuters/Dow Jones): \$600M over 5
  years (\~\$30M/quarter, Q4 2026–Q3 2031) for a worldwide non-exclusive 5-year
  license; all lawsuits and ITC actions dropped. The import-ban tail risk is
  gone; the predictable license cost is not a thesis risk.

## Why it's the category leader

Micron is the only US maker of DRAM/HBM and is gaining share in both DRAM and
HBM; the HBM market is a triopoly (Micron, Samsung, SK Hynix). It is the
designated second-source scale supplier to the hyperscalers' primary AI-memory
budgets, with 26 strategic agreements across Amazon, Microsoft, Google, NVIDIA,
and Apple locking in supply.

## Why it's irreplaceable

DRAM/HBM fabrication requires process-tech leads, EUV-class capital intensity,
and qualification cycles that take years — a hyperscaler can design a custom
ASIC far more easily than it can build a memory fab, and that asymmetry is
exactly this doctrine's moat. Disintermediation risk: **low-medium** — the
Oct 6 Netlist settlement removed the import-ban risk; the honest remaining
caveats are CXMT taking ~9.5% DRAM share at 82% EBIT margins and conventional
DRAM's commodity cycle, neither of which lets a customer in-source the
product.

## Holding period & exit plan

Recommended hold: through the 2027 HBM repricing cycle, ~12–18 months — tied
to the thesis that the contracted book converts and reprices. Targets paired
with timing: base \$1,500 by ~2027-Q4; bull \$2,000 by ~2028-Q1 if the shortage
extends. **Exit now if:** the stock closes below the \$640 downside trigger, or

**Thesis-break exit triggers:** HBM order cancellations or take-or-pay
renegotiations; conventional DRAM pricing rolling over while HBM pricing
follows; new-fab output arriving far ahead of mid-2027. (Note: the ITC
exclusion-order trigger from the 10-10 run is retired — the Netlist dispute

is risk education, not a leverage recommendation.

## Key risks and what would invalidate the thesis (the trap case)

The classic memory peak: customers double-book, pricing rolls, and the
take-or-pay floor turns out to have volume outs the contracts don't show.
Watch CXMT's DRAM share and conventional DRAM pricing first — those move
before the HBM book does. Disintermediation per se is the smallest risk here:
no hyperscaler can fab HBM.
