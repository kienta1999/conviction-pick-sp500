## Per-name theses — top 5

**1. MU — Micron Technology.** The shortage with a contract attached: 26 take-or-pay SCAs, ~\$150B RPO at floor pricing, \$12.3B of deposits in one quarter, HBM effectively sold out through 2027 and >75% of 2027 output committed. It is a top-3 global oligopolist whose customers prepay rather than in-source — the trap cannot fire — and at 6.6x forward earnings the market is still pricing the old cyclical Micron. Full writeup in `final_pick_2026-10-01.md`.

**2. KEYS — Keysight Technologies.** Every 800G/1.6T ramp must be tested, and Keysight is the test chokepoint: record Q3 FY26 orders of \$2.091B (+56% YoY, verified), a record backlog (amount undisclosed — corrected at verification), wireline *revenue* passing wireless for the first time, and component supply — not demand — capping conversion. Self-certification by customers is not credible, which is what an 8/10 irreplaceability score means in practice. The skeptic's discount (the stock fell on the beat) is the entry, not the warning.

**3. CDNS — Cadence Design Systems.** Half of the EDA duopoly through which every leading-edge AI chip must pass: record \$8.1B backlog (\$4.2B due within 12 months, verified), foundry-certified flows on TSMC N2P/A14, ~80% recurring revenue. The contrarian lens's \#1: flat for 12 months because the market fears AI agents bypass EDA, while the dossier shows agents *multiply* engine usage — a mispriced disintermediation discount on a 9/10 moat. The honest caveat is valuation (36.7x forward) and its scenario EV below (flagged).

**4. LLY — Eli Lilly.** The healthcare shortage: incretin demand exceeds what Lilly can manufacture, so revenue is capacity-gated behind a >\$55B plant buildout; ~61% US share against a \#2 guiding sales down, an 84%-gross-margin product, a patent estate into the late 2030s, and the successor (retatrutide, 28.3% Phase-3 weight loss) already in hand. The risks are price, not volume: −13% realised pricing and a ~\$1T market cap that assumes flawless execution.

**5. CAT — Caterpillar.** The shortage moved to power: a record \$72B backlog (+92% YoY, +\$9B in one quarter), large engines sold out through 2027 and into 2028, some Power & Energy orders dated to 2030, and power-generation sales to users +72% in Q2 — while CAT triples large-recip capacity by 2030 and still meets only ~60% of 2026 demand. Two lenses made it their runner-up; the dealer/service annuity (\$30B services target by 2030) cushions the equipment cycle underneath.

## Risk lens & leverage-safety note

This is a concentrated AI-capex book wearing ten tickers: seven of the ten are information technology, and MU/SNDK/LRCX/AMAT/APH/KEYS/ANET-adjacent demand all trace to the same hyperscaler buildout — a capex digestion quarter hits them together, and memory (MU, SNDK) can draw down 50%+ peak-to-trough. The steadier compounders are CDNS (~80% recurring revenue) and LLY (capacity-gated drug demand); the highest-beta seats are SNDK (already +~1,493% in 12 months on decelerating NAND price growth) and FCX (unhedged copper beta). Education, not advice: on a list like this, leverage doesn't change the ranking, it changes who survives the bear case — size so the bear rows above can be held, never so they must be sold.
