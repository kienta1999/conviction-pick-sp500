## One-paragraph thesis

Micron is the purest supply-constrained play in the dossier: AI demand is pulling HBM, server DDR5 and enterprise SSDs faster than wafer supply can grow, every HBM wafer displaces roughly 3x the conventional DRAM wafer capacity, and the tightness is contracted rather than merely spotted — more than 75% of fiscal 2027 output already committed, with management guiding that 2027 and 2028 will be tighter than 2026 and no new fab relief at scale before late 2027. At a 4.1x forward PE against an \$1,561.91 analyst mean target, the market is still pricing a memory cycle rather than a contracted shortage, so the explosive case is duration: every quarter the shortage persists beyond 2028 reprices the contracted book upward.

## The shortage + backlog evidence

- More than 75% of fiscal 2027 shipments committed (Micron Q4 FY2026 earnings call, 30 Sep 2026, CEO Sanjay Mehrotra) — verified; precision caveat from the verifier: the figure covers customer allocations and non-SCA contracts, not only formal take-or-pay Strategic Customer Agreements (SCAs cover only ~35% of revenue through 2030).
- Price-and-volume agreements completed for the entire calendar 2026 HBM supply, including HBM4 (FQ1 2026 call, Dec 2025) — verified.
- Management: "we expect memory and storage supply-demand conditions to be much tighter in fiscal 2027 and 2028 than they were in 2026" and "we do not have line of sight to when supply and demand will return to balance" (30 Sep 2026 call) — verified.
- FQ4 2026: 87% non-GAAP gross margin, a peak-cycle print the bear case takes seriously; FY2027 capex guided above \$50bn (~double FY2026).
- Dossier shortage score 9/10 — the highest in the field alongside NVDA.

## Why it's the category leader

Micron is one of only three firms making leading-edge DRAM/HBM at scale (Samsung 39.4%, SK hynix 24.9%, Micron 23.3% of DRAM revenue, TrendForce 2Q26) — verified with the qualifier "leading-edge … at scale", since CXMT holds ~7.6–10% of overall DRAM revenue in mature segments. It is #3 in DRAM and #3 in HBM (~18%), so leadership here means membership of an irreplaceable trio, not sole dominance — the honest version of the claim.

## Why it's irreplaceable

The moat is fab physics and capital intensity: leading-edge DRAM/HBM requires EUV lithography, TSV stacking and tens of billions in capex, and no source was found of any hyperscaler fabricating DRAM itself — hyperscalers appear in every supplier list only as buyers absorbing price increases on partial fulfilments. That is the product-level answer to this doctrine's trap: a customer cannot in-source the thing itself. The genuine caveat is individual substitutability *within* the trio — Samsung's HBM share jumped from 21% to 33% in one quarter — which is share risk, not disintermediation, and it is why the pick carries a real bear case rather than a clean moat story. Dossier irreplaceability score 7/10 (trio member, individually replaceable).
