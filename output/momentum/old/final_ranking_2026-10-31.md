## Per-name thesis

**1. NVDA — the demand engine of the AI buildout, supply-constrained, but the
in-sourcing target.** Fiscal Q2 2027 commentary put supply and capacity
commitments at \$279B (10-Q, quarter ended 26 July 2026), and management called
its forecast supply-constrained with the whole supply chain running flat out.
The moat is CUDA plus an ~80–90% estimated accelerator share, but the trap is
live: hyperscalers ship their own ASICs (TPU, Trainium, Maia, MTIA), and
receivables concentration (5 customers ~70% of receivables) is the watch item
into the 17 November print. FLAG: disintermediation risk is the doctrine's trap
and it touches NVDA more than any other top-3 name.

**2. APH — booked demand, not forecast demand.** Q3 2026 orders were a record
\$12.17B (+99% year over year) at a 1.19x book-to-bill, IT Datacom grew 77%
organically with AI ~three-quarters of that segment, and the quarter was a
beat-and-raise (28 October 2026). The caveat is honest: interconnect is
multi-sourced (TE Connectivity, Molex, Samtec) and customers own the
architecture, so APH can lose share at a renewal even though it cannot be fully
bypassed. FLAG: dual-sourcing caps pricing power; the order book, not a
monopoly, is the thesis.

**3. MU — the purest shortage in the dossier.** HBM is sold out and more than
75% of expected 2027 output is already committed, with no large new volume
until the Idaho fab (first wafers mid-2027 for ID1, meaningful volume later;
ID2 late 2028). Only three firms on earth make DRAM at scale, so no customer
can in-source this — the trap cannot touch MU the way it touches NVDA. FLAG:
the sharpest cyclical reversal risk in the top 3; the stock is up 321% in 12
months at peak margins, and conventional DRAM/NAND pricing can roll over fast.

**4. CSCO — the incumbent's AI second act.** \$9.3B of hyperscaler AI orders in
one year and \$46.7B of remaining performance obligations, with the enterprise
installed base as ballast. The screen's INVENTORY_BUILD flag reads as largely
benign pre-build against that order book, but it is a margin and
working-capital watch item. FLAG: share-fight in datacenter networking against
ANET and NVDA's Spectrum-X, not a monopoly shortage.

**5. AME — the quiet backlog compounder.** A record \$4.11B backlog at a 1.12
book-to-bill, driven by a named multiyear build-out across electrical,
aerospace, and medical niches. No single shortage moat, but certified niches
and diversification blunt any one customer's in-sourcing. FLAG: 3 November
print binary; part of the order surge is acquisition-flattered (organic orders
+25% is the cleaner figure).

**6. FCX — mined copper scarcity.** Realised copper \$6.54/lb (+40% year over
year) in Q3 2026, 2027 sales guided to 3.7B lb (+20%+), and a 17-year mine-build
clock nobody can shortcut. FLAG: Grasberg at 67% capacity and just-trimmed
2026–27 guidance.

**7. WST — the GLP-1 bottleneck.** High-value packaging capacity is the
constraint on biologics and GLP-1 delivery, with a multi-year Annex 1 wave
behind it. FLAG: at its 52-week high on a 41.73x forward multiple — little room
for a stumble.

**8. ISRG — moat without shortage.** da Vinci's installed base and procedure
growth make it the panel's quality pick (lens C's #1), but the explosive case
rests on procedure re-acceleration, not scarcity pricing. FLAG: down 17% over
12 months; valuation still rich at 36.04x forward.

**9. ANET — Ethernet-for-AI leader under attack.** #1 in high-speed switching
with a \$9.7B purchase-commitment book, but component-bottlenecked through
2028 while NVDA (Spectrum-X/NVLink) and white-box SONiC attack its AI engine.
FLAG: weakest moat among the AI networking names.

**10. EW — TAVR leader, payer-led story.** Category leadership plus an FDA
evidence moat, but the explosive case needs asymptomatic volumes to convert
through the CED pathway faster than expected against a credible Medtronic
rival. FLAG: single-product concentration in TAVR.

## Risk lens and leverage-safety note

Sector concentration: 5 of the top 10 are Information Technology and 3 are
Health Care — the ranking is an AI-capex plus medtech list, not a diversified
portfolio. Highest-risk: MU and NVDA (cyclical, high-beta; memory can draw down
50%+ fast). Steadier: ISRG, EW, AME (installed-base compounders). Picks from
this system are cash-only per POLICY.md; nothing here is a leverage
recommendation, and any use of margin against a cyclical like MU would
amplify a drawdown that the bear targets above already size at 20–27%.

## Thesis-break / trap triggers to watch

Backlog shrinking (APH book-to-bill below 1.0, AME backlog declining), pricing
rolling over (DRAM/NAND spot prices, copper below cost support), margins
peaking quarter over quarter (MU gross margin rollover), a customer announcing
in-sourcing (a hyperscaler shifting volume to its own ASIC at NVDA's expense,
or qualifying a second interconnect source at APH's expense), and NVDA's
receivables concentration worsening into the 17 November print.
