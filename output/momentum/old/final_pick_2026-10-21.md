# Momentum pick — 2026-10-21

## THE PICK: MU — Micron Technology

Sector / sub-industry: Information Technology / Semiconductors. Market cap about \$1,031B (screen). Price at pick: \$911.63 (2026-10-20 close, shortlist_2026-10-21.json).

One-paragraph thesis: Micron is the purest contracted-shortage trade in the field — HBM and DRAM supply is committed under 26 take-or-pay strategic customer agreements, customers have prepaid billions in cash deposits to secure bits they cannot make themselves, and the stock still trades at 4.4x forward earnings because the market prices a memory bust that the contracted book pushes out past 2027. It won the panel's weighted single-pick vote jointly with NVDA (4 points each) and wins the adjudication on the trap filter: hyperscalers prepay Micron rather than in-source memory, while NVDA's ASIC trap is live and shipping.

## The shortage + backlog evidence

- Management stated on the FQ4 FY2026 call (30 Sep 2026) that remaining performance obligations across 26 take-or-pay SCAs are about \$150B, counting only agreements with a defined pricing framework at minimum pricing; Phase 3.5 notes the audited 10-K figure is about \$134B — both are cited here, the filing number is the conservative one.
- Customer financial commitments are about \$32B; the 10-K (filed 9 Oct 2026) confirms \$12.75B of cash deposits actually received in fiscal 2026, plus about \$7B of letters of credit.
- The vast majority of calendar-2027 HBM bit supply is already under agreement at significantly higher prices (FQ4 call language — not a literal 100% sell-out), and over 75% of fiscal 2027 output is committed.
- FQ4 FY2026 revenue was \$54.23B; FQ1 FY2027 is guided to \$61.5B plus or minus \$1.5B at about 86.25% non-GAAP gross margin (all Phase 3.5 confirmed from the primary release).
- The often-quoted "Micron can meet only 50-66% of key customers' demand" figure dates to the FQ1 FY2026 call on 17 Dec 2025 and covered several key customers' overall medium-term demand, not HBM alone — stated with that date and scope here.

## Why it's the category leader

Micron is one of only three firms on earth making HBM at scale (SK hynix about 50% share, Samsung about 33%, Micron about 18% — Counterpoint, Q2 2026, Phase 3.5 confirmed). It is not the share leader; it is the only US-based member of a three-firm oligopoly whose output is the binding constraint on AI accelerator supply, which is why customers sign take-or-pay contracts and prepay deposits instead of negotiating spot.

## Why it's irreplaceable

A hyperscaler can design a chip; it cannot build a leading-edge memory fab on any investable timeline — process technology, capital intensity (tens of billions per fab) and a three-firm supplier base are the moat (dossier irreplaceability 7/10). The honest caveat: irreplaceability is at the industry level, not the firm level — Micron's residual trap is intra-oligopoly share loss to SK hynix or Samsung, plus the classic memory cycle, not customer in-sourcing. Disintermediation risk: low-to-medium.
