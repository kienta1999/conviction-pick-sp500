# Momentum conviction pick — 2026-11-17

## One-paragraph thesis

Micron is selling every high-bandwidth memory chip it can make, years into the future, at prices that are rising double digits per quarter — and the market prices it at 3.7x forward earnings. FQ4 FY26 (reported Sep 30, 2026) was a record: revenue $54.23B (+379% YoY), non-GAAP EPS $33.42, 87.0% gross margin. HBM is sold out through 2027, more than 75% of FY27 output is committed under 26 customer agreements running through 2030, and the FQ1 FY27 guide is $61.5B of revenue and $38.15 of adjusted EPS. Only three firms on earth can make HBM, and a hyperscaler cannot in-source a memory fab — that asymmetry is exactly what this doctrine hunts.

## The shortage + backlog evidence

- **HBM sold out through 2027:** the "vast majority" of CY2027 HBM supply is sold (Micron's own release); more than 75% of FY27 output is committed.
- **26 customer agreements through 2030** — $32B of commitments, ~$150B of remaining performance obligations. The backlog is contracted, not hoped for.
- **DRAM contract prices +90–95% QoQ in Q1 and +58–63% in Q2** (TrendForce); SK Hynix expects demand to outstrip supply through 2030 — balance is not expected before 2030.
- **FQ1 FY27 guide $61.5B revenue / $38.15 adjusted EPS** — the guide itself is bigger than most companies' years.
- Shortage score 10/10 (research); irreplaceability 9/10 (research).

## Why it's the category leader

Micron is one of only three firms that can make HBM (with SK Hynix and Samsung), and it is the #3 HBM supplier at ~18% share vs SK Hynix ~50% — the laggard with the most room to gain as the shortage persists. It is the category leader in the screen's sense (margin leader, category #1 in its sub-industry by the funnel) and the only memory name in the field with a sold-out order book running to 2030. The FQ4 print was a record on every line, and the guide accelerates from it.

## Why it's irreplaceable

A hyperscaler can in-source a chip design far more easily than a memory fab: HBM requires process-tech leads, extreme capital intensity, and qualification lock-in that takes years to replicate. Only ~3 firms on earth can make it, and the customer base is every AI accelerator maker at once — no single customer can credibly threaten to build its own. The honest caveat is the cycle, not in-sourcing: memory is the most cyclical slice of semis, and the "E" in a 3.7x P/E can shrink faster than the multiple expands. The disintermediation risk is low; the cyclicality risk is the real one.
