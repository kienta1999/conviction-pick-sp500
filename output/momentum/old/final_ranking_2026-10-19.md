## Per-name theses (top 5)

**MU (1).** The shortage is contracted, not spot: HBM is sold out for CY2026 with the vast majority of CY2027 already agreed at significantly higher prices, Micron can satisfy only about 50-66% of key customers' medium-term demand (a figure Phase 3.5 dates to March 2026 FQ2 remarks, still the operative management framing), and a 150B RPO across 26 take-or-pay strategic customer agreements floors revenue — over 35% of revenue through 2030, counting only SCA revenue with a defined pricing framework, at minimum pricing. Phase 3.5 nuance: of the 32B of customer financial commitments, about 12.7B was held as deposits at FQ4-end (10-K: 12.75B received, plus 7B letters of credit). Micron is one of only three firms making HBM at scale. FQ4 FY26 revenue was 54.23B with FQ1 FY27 guided to 61.5B plus or minus 1.5B at ~86.25% non-GAAP gross margin. It is both the Borda #1 and the panel's single-pick winner today (see If forced to ONE).

**GOOGL (2).** Google Cloud backlog of 514B in Q2 2026, just over 50% expected to convert within 24 months, on Cloud revenue +82% to 24.8B, with Search still growing +17% — the AI-displacement thesis contradicted by the print again. It is the moat lens's single pick and the supply-chain lens's runner-up: the full-stack winner still priced as a Search casualty at 22.3x forward. Mandatory haircut: Q2 headline EPS of 9.11 was materially flattered by ~99B of unrealised equity-securities gains (about 6.26 of the 9.11, verified in Phase 3.5) — judge GOOGL on operating income (+30%) and backlog conversion, not reported EPS. Capex guided up to 195-205B for 2026 is the real downside variable.

**NVDA (3).** Nvidia holds about 85-92% of data-centre GPU revenue (dossier range) with a CUDA/full-stack moat no rival can copy in one step — a rival must replicate the whole stack, not just a chip (supply/capacity commitments of ~279B because HBM and CoWoS, not demand, are the bottleneck). Q2 FY27 revenue was 96.2B and Q3 is formally guided to 108.0B plus or minus 2% (GlobeNewswire release, verified in Phase 3.5). The honest framing of the big numbers: its 500B Blackwell+Rubin figure (1T through 2027) is management demand visibility, NOT a filing RPO — the filing RPO is ~3.2B on a narrow perimeter because hardware ships in weeks. The trap is live: Google TPU and AWS Trainium ship in volume, Meta MTIA ships, Microsoft's Maia 200 is in production, and the OpenAI-Broadcom chip is taped out only (samples; deployment targeted end-2026 — Phase 3.5 correction to any "shipping" wording). Both earnings-quality flags are live — non-cash equity gains in accruals, and receivables +55% in Q2 FY27 to 63.1B (DSO ~45 to ~60) on disclosed extended payment terms for large multi-quarter agreements.

**SNDK (4).** The purest NAND price leverage and the skeptic lens's single pick: 93.9B of minimum revenue under floored New Business Model deals covering over 50% of FY2027 bits and about two-thirds of FY2028 bits, the entire CY2026 supply booked, NAND on allocation beyond CY2027, TrendForce still projecting Q4 2026 NAND contract prices +15-20% quarter-on-quarter, and an 84.6% FQ4 gross margin NAND has never sustained. Ranked #2 by the growth lens and excluded entirely by the moat lens — the polarising name of this panel, as yesterday. It is the #5 NAND supplier at about 11.4% share, so this is price, not share gain, and its output runs through the Kioxia JV it does not control. The 2026-10-29 print (consensus ~46.5 EPS on ~10.7B revenue in the dossier) is the near proof point after a -17.86% week into the last close.

**FCX (5).** New to the shortlist today and straight into the top 5 on all four ballots — the run's main change. Copper is a textbook structural shortage: the IEA expects supply to fall ~30% short of 2035 requirements, ore grades are down ~40% since 1991, and new mines take ~17-20 years, while FCX realised over 6.50/lb expected for Q3 2026 against ~1.90/lb guided unit cash costs. It is a top-3 global producer with 112.3B lb of reserves and a leach initiative (~40B lb in stockpiles at under 1/lb cost, running 200M lb/yr toward a 300M lb/yr target and an 800M ambition) that grows volume without a new mine. There is no order book for a commodity miner — guided 2026 sales of 3.1B lb (cut from 3.4B in April on Grasberg) are the analogue. The INVENTORY_BUILD flag is verified benign in the dossier: PTFI now books refined rather than concentrate sales, so production runs ahead of sales while the restarted Eastern Java smelter fills its pipeline, with cash conversion strong (CFO/net income 2.00). Risks are sovereign and cyclical, not disintermediation: Grasberg recovery (about 67% of normalised rates in Q3, full recovery guided by end-2027), the February 2026 IUPK-extension MOU under which Indonesia takes a further 12% of PTFI for free in 2041, and record-price leverage (EBITDA roughly 13B at 5/lb copper vs ~20B at 7/lb on management frames).
