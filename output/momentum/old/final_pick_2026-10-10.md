# Momentum conviction pick — 2026-10-10

## THE PICK

**MU — Micron Technology**, Semiconductors (memory: DRAM, NAND, HBM).
Market cap ~$1.16T at pick; current price $1,029.00.

## One-paragraph thesis

Micron has sold out its HBM3E/HBM4 output through the end of calendar 2027 and
converted the shortage into ~$150B of remaining performance obligations under
take-or-pay strategic customer agreements at conservative floor pricing, backed
by $12.7B of customer cash already deposited — yet the stock trades at ~5x
forward earnings, 15% off its high, because the market still prices it as a
peak-cycle commodity memory stock rather than a contracted-revenue oligopoly.
Hyperscalers cannot in-source DRAM/HBM — Micron is one of exactly three firms
on earth that can make HBM at AI scale — so the classic disintermediation trap
cannot fire here; the live tail risk is the Netlist ITC exclusion-order fight,
which is real but priced as a known unknown, not a thesis break.

## The shortage + backlog evidence (verified)

- **~$150B of RPO** under 26 strategic customer agreements (up from 16 in
  June), all with take-or-pay volume provisions, priced on "committed volumes
  and minimum pricing" that CFO Mark Murphy called "inherently conservative"
  (Sept 30, 2026 Q4 FY2026 call). >35% of estimated revenue through 2030, some
  extending to 2031.
- **HBM3E/HBM4 sold out through calendar 2027**, order books extending into
  2028; supply "will likely stay tight past 2027" per management.
- **$12.7B of customer deposits received to date**, with $12.3B arriving in Q4
  alone; total customer financial commitments ~$32B. Customers are pre-paying
  a vendor to hold supply.
- **New fab wafer output only arrives mid-2027**, so 2026–H1-2027 supply is
  physically fixed while 2027 HBM pricing is being locked "significantly
  higher" than 2026.

## Why it's the category leader

Micron is the only US maker of DRAM/HBM and is gaining share in both DRAM and
HBM; the HBM market is a triopoly (Micron, Samsung, SK Hynix). It is the
designated second-source scale supplier to the hyperscalers' primary AI-memory
budgets, with 26 strategic agreements across Amazon, Microsoft, Google, NVIDIA,
and Apple locking in supply.

## Why it's irreplaceable

DRAM/HBM fabrication requires process-tech leads, EUV-class capital intensity,
and qualification cycles that take years — a hyperscaler can design a custom
ASIC far more easily than it can build a memory fab, and that asymmetry is
exactly this doctrine's moat. Disintermediation risk: **medium, not low** — the
honest caveats are CXMT taking ~9.5% DRAM share at 82% EBIT margins and the
Netlist ITC exclusion-order fight (CONFIRMED real by Phase 3.5 verification:
new ITC action Sept 29, 2026 vs. Micron's HBM3E/HBM4/HBM4E; separate
Investigation 337-TA-1523 on DDR5 RDIMMs instituted Sept 23, 2026). Neither
lets a customer in-source the product; both are priced as known risks.
