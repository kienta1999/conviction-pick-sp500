# Momentum final pick — 2026-10-29

## THE PICK

**MU — Micron Technology** (Technology / Semiconductors). Screen price \$983.23, market cap ~\$1.11tn, composite 0.953 (#2 of 50). Next earnings 2026-12-23. No earnings-quality flags.

## One-paragraph thesis

Micron is the only name in this set where an acute, documented shortage is already converted into multi-year contracted revenue rather than merely priced in spot markets. Its HBM supply is sold out, conventional DRAM and NAND are supply-constrained, and pricing power is locked in through long-term agreements rather than left to the next quarter's spot print. Unlike the purer commodity plays, Micron sits in a three-firm global oligopoly for vertically integrated memory, so hyperscalers cannot simply build their way around it. That combination — shortage today, contracted visibility through 2030, and an irreplaceable manufacturing position — gives it the most explosive yet defensible upside over 12–18 months.

## The shortage + backlog evidence

- Remaining performance obligations of ~\$150B across 26 signed multi-year strategic customer agreements, including \$32B in customer financial commitments (mostly cash deposits), covering ~35% of revenue through 2030, with >75% of fiscal 2027 output committed (Micron fiscal Q4 FY26 call, 30 Sep 2026 — verified, with the caveat that RPO is a minimum-pricing floor for priced agreements only, not total contracted value).
- Entire calendar-2026 HBM supply fully allocated; most calendar-2027 HBM supply already under agreement at significantly higher prices (same call — verified).
- Fiscal Q4 FY26: DRAM prices rose high-teens % q/q, NAND ~30% q/q; management expects to meet only ~50–67% of midterm demand from some key customers; JP Morgan cited as estimating a 30%+ supply-demand gap in 2026.
- Shortage score 10/10, the joint-highest in the dossier.

## Why it's the category leader

Top-3 in every memory category, not #1 overall — and that is enough, because the category is a three-firm oligopoly. DRAM revenue share: Samsung 39.4%, SK Hynix 24.9%, Micron 23.3%. NAND Q2 2026: Samsung 28%, SK Hynix 19%, Micron 15%. HBM: SK Hynix ~50%, Samsung ~33%, Micron ~18% (Q2 2026, Counterpoint — verified with the caveat that Samsung's share is ~28% on HBM4-only forecasts). Micron is also the only US-based memory maker of the three, with CHIPS Act support.

## Why it's irreplaceable

Only three vertically integrated memory makers exist at scale — Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron (verified). The moat is process-tech lead (1-gamma DRAM, HBM3E/HBM4 roadmap), fab capital intensity (\$50B+ FY27 capex guided; >\$250B US investment through 2035 announced Jul 2026), and multi-year qualification lock-in with GPU platforms. Hyperscalers cannot build DRAM/HBM fabs themselves. Irreplaceability 8/10 — points off for being #3 in HBM, not #1. The realistic threat is supplier substitution (SK Hynix is the HBM leader and Nvidia's priority supplier) and, longer-term, Chinese entrants (CXMT server DRAM by end-2026; YMTC NAND) — not in-sourcing. Disintermediation risk: low.

- **Base — \$1,500 (+52.6%) by 2027-Q4:** the current backlog converts at today's pricing. Fiscal 2027 delivers the guided sequential quarterly revenue growth, HBM repricing holds, and the price moves toward — but not all the way to — the analyst mean target.
- **Bull — \$1,850 (+88.2%) by 2028-Q2:** the shortage extends and price/mix keeps expanding. Calendar-2027 HBM agreements hold at significantly higher prices and the market concedes the backlog de-risks the cycle, re-rating Micron above the mean target. This needs both continued shortage AND pricing hold simultaneously — that compounding is the risk in the bull case.

**Probabilities — bear 20% / base 55% / bull 25%.** Base gets the weight because the backlog is contracted and deposit-backed, not forecast — conversion is the default path unless something breaks. Bear gets a full fifth, not a token weight, because these are cyclicals late in a shortage cycle and CXMT's October 2026 announcement already triggered a two-day selloff.
