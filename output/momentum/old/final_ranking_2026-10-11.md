## Per-name thesis

**1. MU** — The single pick (see `final_pick_2026-10-11.md`): \$150B of
take-or-pay RPO at conservative floor pricing with \$12.7B of customer deposits
already in hand, HBM sold out through 2027, trading at ~5x forward earnings
because the market prices a spot cycler. The disintermediation trap cannot
fire — no customer can in-source HBM — and the verified Netlist ITC fight that
haunted yesterday's writeup SETTLED Oct 6 (\$600M, 5-year license), removing
the bear driver's import-ban leg.

**2. AMAT** — Customers' fabs "running at full capacity," 10+ new fabs
announced, contracts being negotiated for 2030, record \$9.12B quarter. The
moat lens's pick: the broadest WFE toolbox with \#1 positions across
deposition/epitaxy/CMP/implant (exact share percentages UNVERIFIED — third-party
estimates conflict; leadership directionally confirmed), a \$5B EPIC
co-innovation lock-in, and 9/10 irreplaceability. The temper: +141% in a year
at ~44x trailing earnings prices a long cycle; China export controls are the
sector's tail risk. (No public backlog figure exists — the dossier's "not
found" is correct, so this thesis is built on contract visibility, not a
headline backlog.)

**3. SNDK** — Ten "new business model" agreements locking a minimum \$93.9B of
committed NAND revenue at floor pricing, backed by \$16.5B of enforceable
financial guarantees (\~\$2.5B already received in cash per Oct 9 coverage),
covering >50% of FY2027 and ~2/3 of FY2028 NAND bit output — while the stock
sits well off its high. The growth lenses' \#2: fastest revenue acceleration
in the field at ~5.9x forward earnings. The honest caveat: NAND is the most
commoditized memory, YMTC is gaining share, and every up-cycle has ended in
oversupply. 32% off highs is the cycle discount, not a moat discount.

**4. CAT** — The contrarian lens's pick: datacenter genset demand outpacing
manufacturing capacity (admitted by rival Cummins), \$72.1B record backlog with
orders into 2030, tripling large-engine capacity by 2030 — trading ~25% off
its high on datacenter regulatory fears that actually lengthen grid timelines
and strengthen the prime-power case.

**5. LLY** — The purest demand surge of the twelve: \#1 in a \$100B+ incretin
market it cannot fully supply, volumes (+60%) overwhelming price decay (−13%),
and the shortage thesis still expanding into orals. No backlog in the pharma
sense, so it can never top a shortage lens; the manufacturing moat (7/10) is
real but the oral franchise is genuinely contested.

**6. KLAC** — Record \$12.57B backlog (+60% YoY), a defect-classification data
moat that compounds on every installed tool, and fabs standardized on KLA as
the reference platform so switching vendors would break process comparability.
9/10 irreplaceable — the lowest disintermediation risk in the field. The
temper: ~29x forward earnings after a huge run prices a long AI cycle; WFE
bookings are exactly what peaks look like.

**7. LRCX** — Effectively a duopoly/triopoly on etch; re-qualifying a vendor
means re-qualifying the entire process. Low-\$150B WFE outlook, Micron's \$32B
commitments as read-through. China at 26% of revenue and a demanding multiple
are the real risks.

**8. NVDA** — The definitive shortage stock (10/10): growth ceiling is its
suppliers' output through FY2028, Vera Rubin the fastest ramp ever. Demoted
because ~\$5.8T is fully priced — the explosive bet is on share gains and the
inference-ASIC lane versus hyperscaler disintermediation. The contrarian lens
passed it outright as priced in.

**9. ANET** — Demand "materially above available supply" through ~2028, \$9.7B
supply commitments. But Nvidia's bundled Spectrum-X now leads DC Ethernet
share and ~67x P/E prices flawless execution; hyperscalers can white-box
in-source. The AI fabric's \#1, fighting a bundler.

**10. APH** — Cleanest demand signal in the field (1.23 book-to-bill, orders
+94%, IT datacom 4x in two years) — but a scale-and-qualification leader, not
a monopoly (irreplaceability 6/10), with ~40% AI revenue concentration and
deal-funded debt.
