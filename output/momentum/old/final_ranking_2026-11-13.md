## Per-name thesis

**1. MU — Micron Technology.** The purest shortage in the field, verified claim by claim: ~\$100bn of binding multi-year AI memory contracts (26 strategic customer agreements, take-or-pay through 2030), \$32bn of customer financial commitments mostly in cash prepayments, HBM sold out through 2026 and largely committed for 2027 at "significant price increases year over year," and FQ4 2026 revenue of \$54.23bn (+379% y/y) that verification confirmed against Micron's own release. The stock sits 33% below its June \$1,213 peak at 3.9x forward PE because the market prices the duration of 80%+ margins. The honest caveat: Micron is #3 in HBM (18%, behind SK Hynix 50% and Samsung 33%) — the moat protects the oligopoly, not Micron. Full writeup in `final_pick_2026-11-13.md`.

**2. CDNS — Cadence Design Systems.** The strict-doctrine name: co-#1 in the EDA duopoly with Synopsys (~70% combined share, near-100% retention), a record \$8.2B backlog that *grew in a light renewal year* (\$4.2B converting within 12 months), Q3 revenue +22% with all product groups double-digit. Irreplaceability 9/10 — no hyperscaler has replicated a full-flow, foundry-qualified EDA stack, and every AI accelerator must be designed on one. The earnings-quality flags (RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN, INVENTORY_BUILD) were explained as mostly benign: 9M operating cash flow \$1,414M vs net income \$993M (1.43x), deferred revenue +30%, inventory is emulation hardware pre-built for the backlog. The risk is the multiple: ~39x FY26 EPS needs 20%+ growth to persist, and the July Kimi K3 "AI designs chips" scare showed how fast the narrative can turn.

**3. CF — CF Industries.** The cleanest physical shortage: 97% available ammonia utilisation in 9M 2026, order book into Q1 2027, ~9 Mt of urea supply removed by Middle East hostilities since late February, >35% of European ammonia capacity curtailed, with the #1 producer holding ~39% of US capacity on the lowest-cost North American gas. Nitrogen is biologically irreplaceable and no customer can build its own ammonia plant. The bet is on the disruption lasting into 2027 — management itself says normalisation takes 4–6 months if hostilities end, so a peace deal unwinds the thesis fast. **EV guardrail flag: CF's EV upside is +12.8%, below the +15% guardrail** — it stays in the ranking, but it is not actionable as a single pick on these numbers.

**4. PH — Parker Hannifin.** Record \$13.7B backlog (up \$1.2B in two quarters), rolling 12-month order rates +13%, Q1 FY27 sales \$6.0B (+18%, +11.5% organic — first double-digit organic quarter in 3+ years), guidance raised. The moat is FAA/EASA certification lock-in on flight-critical parts plus a 47% aftermarket mix. Flag: long-term debt nearly doubled to \$14.64B to fund the Filtration Group deal, with the \$2.55B CIRCOR Aerospace deal still pending — the explosive leg is now partly a leverage-and-integration story.

**5. ANET — Arista Networks.** Q3 2026 revenue \$3.66bn, +58.5% y/y — the fastest since IPO — with AI-fabric revenue targeted to double to ~\$7.0bn in 2027 and \$8.6B in remaining performance obligations. **Trap flag (visible, not vetoed in ranked mode):** Nvidia just overtook Arista in datacenter Ethernet revenue (21.5% vs 20.7%), Arista buys its switch silicon from Broadcom, and its two biggest customers (Microsoft 26%, Meta 16%) run white-box SONiC/FBOSS. Deferred revenue also fell sequentially for the first time in Q3.

**6. MSFT — Microsoft.** Azure demand genuinely exceeds supply (management, Oct 28 call), Azure +48% to \$33.2B in Q1 FY27, commercial RPO \$677B (+73% y/y). But RPO was flat sequentially, growth is concentrated in two frontier labs (OpenAI = \$10B of quarterly revenue), and the ~\$170B calendar-2026 capex bill has unproven ROI. At \$521 the shortage is the consensus story; the explosive leg needs the ROI to show, not just the shortage to persist.

**7. ISRG — Intuitive Surgical.** The strongest moat in the set (4,500+ patents, 76,000+ trained surgeons, 12,094-system installed base, 84% recurring revenue) and a genuine procedure-demand flywheel (da Vinci procedures +14%, placements +28% in Q3). But 2026 is the first year the monopoly has real, FDA-cleared challengers (Medtronic Hugo, J&J Ottava), US procedure growth decelerated to 10%, and management is cutting instruments pricing 3–5% into 2027. The stock is -22% over 12 months despite the Q3 beat — the market has already de-rated it.

**8. LLY — Eli Lilly.** #1 GLP-1 franchise (63% US share vs Novo 36.6%), Q3 revenue \$24.4bn (+39%), guidance raised \$5bn. But the shortage is over — the FDA declared the tirzepatide shortage resolved Dec 19, 2024 — realised prices fell 15% in Q3, and the analyst upside is only +1.1% at the 52-week high. The explosive case is retatrutide (BLA filing Q1 2027), not scarcity.

**9. NUE — Nucor.** Tariff-amplified domestic steel tightness (HRC \$1,255/ton, +52% y/y), overall backlogs +25% y/y, Towers & Structures backlog more than doubled into early 2028, data centers ~15% of backlog, Q3 revenue +32%. **Flag:** the squeeze is policy-made and reversible — a tariff easing or the 2027 new-capacity wave (including Nucor's own West Virginia mill) is the clock on it.

**10. NEM — Newmont.** A real structural gold-supply squeeze (mine output +~1%/yr for a decade, central banks absorbing ~25% of output, no major discovery since 2022) with the industry's largest reserve base (118.2 Moz) and a record Q3 (adj. EPS \$2.20). But NEM is a price-taking miner in a production trough (5.3 Moz FY26 guidance) — the shortage lifts the gold price, not NEM's volumes. Irreplaceability 4/10: the metal is irreplaceable, the company is not.
