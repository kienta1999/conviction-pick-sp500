## Per-name thesis (top 5)

**KLAC (#1).** The shortage math is the backlog: \$12.57B, +60% YoY, converting into a supply-constrained 2027 (management: 2H 2026 revenue ~20% above 1H "as additional supply becomes available"). The moat is the industry's inspection choke point (~73.8% metrology share, third-party press): no fab can in-source it, no hyperscaler disintermediation threat, and 25 years of defect data behind every tool. The stock sits 33.5% below its high on cyclical fear while the business accelerates — the exact "real but underappreciated" setup this doctrine hunts. Watch item: the RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN flag read as growth-timing, not trap.

**MU (#2).** The purest physical shortage: HBM "largely sold out" through 2027 (verified, Mehrotra 9/30 call), with the FQ4 print raising the contracted book to ~\$150B take-or-pay RPO and \$32B customer commitments (dossier's \$100B/\$22B were June figures — corrected by verification). Only ~3 firms on earth can make HBM; hyperscalers prepay rather than in-source. The discount: 5.3x forward P/E is a cycle-top artifact (80%+ op margins), the stock faded its own beat-and-raise, and the thesis is now consensus — which is why it loses the tie to KLAC.

**CAT (#3).** The most literal physical shortage in the index: \$72B backlog +92% YoY (+\$9B in a single quarter, verified Q2 2026 call), 18–24-month genset lead times, customers ordering power-gen out to 2030, and a 2-GW West Virginia deal (Nscale Monarch, Microsoft/NVIDIA, verified) delivering 2027–H1 2028. Moat: certification/qualification lock-in on emergency power (99.995% availability specs) plus a service network no newcomer replicates. The caveat: the stock nearly doubled before the −24% pullback on regulatory overhang, and Cummins/mtu are credible substitutes — hence #3, not #1.

**LRCX (#4).** WFE outlook raised twice to the low-\$150Bs, #1 in etch (~40% vs TEL ~25%), +52% YoY revenue guide for the September quarter. The acute-shortage read is the same cycle KLAC rides; the discount to KLAC is valuation (58x trailing P/E — the cycle is priced) and the HIGH_ACCRUALS/RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN flags, both read as growth-timing but real late-cycle watch items, plus 26% China exposure under export controls.

**FCX (#5).** The clearest structural *commodity* deficit: copper at an all-time high (\$14,745/ton, Sep 2026), Shanghai inventories at 2023 lows, 15–20-year mine lead times (no new supply this decade), irreplaceable Tier-1 geology (Grasberg), and a 3.1B→4.1B lb ramp into 2028. The INVENTORY_BUILD flag read benign (smelter-pipeline timing + Grasberg ramp working capital). The discount to the top 4: the deficit is fully priced (analyst consensus implies only ~1% upside — the most crowded target in the screen), and Grasberg execution is the swing factor.
