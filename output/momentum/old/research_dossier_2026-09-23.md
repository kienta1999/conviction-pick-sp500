## Batch 1

## SNDK — Sandisk

1. **Shortage thesis:** Structural NAND shortage, and SanDisk is the only pure-play NAND beneficiary among the listed storage names. The shortage is supply-driven, not demand-driven: Samsung and SK Hynix are systematically reducing NAND wafer output to fund HBM expansion (SK Hynix cut NAND wafer production 10% in 2026, from 1.9M to 1.7M equivalent wafers); global MLC NAND capacity is forecast to drop >40% in 2026; major expansions delayed until 2028+. NAND contract prices rose 70–75% QoQ in Q2 2026 (outpacing DRAM for the first time this cycle); SanDisk says it is on track to double the price of its high-capacity 3D NAND for enterprise SSDs in Q1 2026, with hyperscalers paying top dollar (ainvest, ~2026-09-05).
2. **Backlog / order book:** No formal backlog figure disclosed (merchant NAND/SSD model); forward visibility comes from contract pricing — Nomura sees NAND shortage persisting to 2028; AI-related products drove virtually all sequential IT datacom growth in peers.
3. **Category position:** One of the "Big Four" NAND producers (with Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron), fabbed through the Kioxia JV (Yokkaichi/Kitakami). Pure-play NAND/SSD monoline post the WDC separation (Feb 2025) — revenue/margin is a direct function of the NAND wafer price.
4. **Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk:** Medium. NAND is a commodity product — hyperscalers multi-source and there is no process-moat equivalent to DRAM/EUV; the shortage thesis is supply discipline, not lock-in. The moat is scale (one of four firms on earth that can make 3D NAND at scale) and the Kioxia JV capacity. **Irreplaceability: 5/10.**
5. **Recent catalysts:** Q2 2026 (reported ~Aug 2026): revenue $3.0B (+31% QoQ), EPS $6.20 vs $3.62 Street; Datacenter segment +64% QoQ to $440M; Q3 guide EPS $12–$14, revenue $4.40–$4.80B (mexc, ~2026-03). Melius launched at Buy/$1,350, Cantor $1,400, MS $1,100; BNP Overweight/$650 (all within last ~3 months).
6. **Risks:** The stock is the S&P 500's #1 performer YTD (~+600%) and has slumped 35% from its late-June peak (BigGo, 2026-08-29) — expectations are extreme. NAND is notoriously cyclical; TrendForce forecasts show contract-price growth decelerating from 70–75% QoQ (spring) to 10–15% QoQ (current quarter). Any capex over-investment by the Big Four crashes pricing in 2027–28. JV dependency on Kioxia.
7. **Shortage score: 9/10.** Verdict: the purest shortage play in the field, but the momentum may be closer to a peak than the price admits.

## MU — Micron Technology

1. **Shortage thesis:** Simultaneous HBM + DRAM + NAND shortage. HBM production uses ~3x the silicon wafers of standard DRAM, so every wafer into AI memory is three wafers not going into conventional DDR5 — DDR5 spot prices up ~162% QoQ; standard DDR5 prices up 3.5–4x on many SKUs from late 2025 to Q1 2026. CEO Mehrotra: "tight industry conditions" across DRAM and NAND "persist through and beyond" 2026 (Sept 2026 earnings). Goldman projects 5.9% DRAM undersupply by 2027; BofA GPU supply-demand gap not closing until 2029.
2. **Backlog / order book:** HBM3E and HBM4 supply for 2026 fully sold out (Needham, Aug 2026); HBM supply booked into 2027 with multi-year supply agreements; can meet only ~50–66% of core-customer HBM demand into 2027 (TrendForce). NAND structurally repaired post-SanDisk separation. Idaho new capacity starts 2027; plans 20% shipment increase next year.
3. **Category position:** #3 DRAM maker (behind Samsung, SK Hynix) but qualified at Nvidia for HBM4 alongside SK Hynix with industry-leading >11 Gbps; one of four NAND producers; HBM TAM projected $35B (2025) → $100B (2028).
4. **Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk:** Medium-high. Three firms on earth make leading-edge DRAM/HBM at scale (Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron); qualification lock-in with Nvidia (Vera Rubin alignment). Hyperscalers cannot in-source leading DRAM. Commodity tail remains cyclical. **Irreplaceability: 7/10.**
5. **Recent catalysts [CORRECTED 2026-09-23 by verifier]:** The figures "$13.64B revenue (+21% QoQ), ~57% gross margin, guided non-GAAP EPS +440% YoY" are the **FQ1-26 print reported Dec 2025**, misattributed here as "Sept 2026 earnings" — the latest quarter (FQ4-26, reported Sept 2026) is substantially larger (FQ2-26 revenue was $23.86B; FQ3-26 guided $33.5B). The dossier understated MU's actual latest financials; the shortage thesis is unchanged and, if anything, stronger. Other catalysts: shuttered consumer Crucial business to prioritize HBM; $7B Singapore HBM assembly facility (2026); closed above $1,000/share first time Sept 21, 2026 (+247% past year). Targets: UBS $245, Citi $240, Needham $300 (avg $290.92) — note these targets are far below the current ~$1,016 price, suggesting stale or post-split-unadjusted figures; treat with caution.
6. **Risks:** Yesterday's momentum pick — must re-earn it. Memory is deeply cyclical; ~+$1,000 price embeds a perfect cycle. Mizuho trimmed to $1,300 citing multiple compression on "de-specing" concerns for future GPU/ASICs. Consumer DRAM demand destruction at these prices. FY27+ figures have wide error bars.
7. **Shortage score: 10/10.** Verdict: the most complete shortage thesis in the field — sold-out 2026 HBM book plus a DRAM shortage it is itself worsening — but cyclicality means the exit discipline matters as much as the entry.

## NVDA — NVIDIA

1. **Shortage thesis:** Structural GPU/compute shortage. B200 lead times 36–52 weeks; H100 1-yr contract index rose ~40% (Oct 2025–Mar 2026) even as Blackwell ramped; B200 resale value 158% of launch (58% premium); Blackwell cloud rental hit $4.08/hr in April 2026 (+48% in two months, WSJ). Bottlenecks: TSMC CoWoS fully subscribed through at least mid-2027; HBM sold out 2026. BofA: GPU supply-demand gap not closing until at least 2029. Vera Rubin demand will absorb any near-term relief.
2. **Backlog / order book:** Fiscal Q1 2027 filing (period ended Apr 26, 2026): $119B in manufacturing/supply/capacity commitments, $95B due within FY2027 (sec.gov). Data Center revenue record $75.2B in the quarter (+92% YoY); ~1.5–2M B200 units shipping in CY2026 at $30–50k ASP. Multi-billion-dollar forward contracts lock up most Blackwell allocation with hyperscalers.
3. **Category position:** Unambiguous #1 — ~90%+ of AI accelerator market; CUDA ecosystem moat; Vera Rubin platform (unveiled CES 2026) extends the lead. Google TPU 8t/8i is the only credible challenger and it is specialized, not general-purpose.
4. **Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk:** High moat, medium-term watch item. CUDA + full-stack networking + software is the moat; hyperscaler custom ASICs (Google TPU, Meta MTIA, Amazon Trainium) are the real in-sourcing threat but remain narrow. **Irreplaceability: 9/10.**
5. **Recent catalysts:** GB300 Blackwell Ultra in mass production (~$89B quarterly DC revenue attributed); Q3 FY2026 revenue $57B (+62% YoY, DC $51.2B +66%); Huang: Blackwell Ultra demand "off the charts." Forward PE ~32x FY2027 on the cited estimates (PEG ~0.71).
6. **Risks:** The AI-bubble question — the entire thesis is the cycle; a hyperscaler capex pause breaks everything at once. Valuation debate is fully priced-in discourse. Export-control/geopolitical risk on China. Custom-ASIC creep at the largest customers.
7. **Shortage score: 9/10.** Verdict: the shortage is real and structural, but NVDA is also the most consensus long in the market — the momentum is undeniable and so is the crowdedness.
