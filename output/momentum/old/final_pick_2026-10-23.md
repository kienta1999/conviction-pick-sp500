# Momentum single pick — 2026-10-23

**THE PICK:** MU — Micron Technology, Inc., Technology / Semiconductors, market cap about 1029.6B dollars.

**One-paragraph thesis.** Micron is the doctrine's original shortage archetype returned to the screen at full strength: it can fulfil only between half and two-thirds of customer demand, its 2026 HBM supply is sold out with the vast majority of 2027 HBM bit supply already under agreement, and that scarcity has been converted into contracted revenue — roughly 150B dollars of remaining performance obligations cited on the Q4 FY26 call under 26 Strategic Customer Agreements at minimum prices through 2030/31, backed by about 32B dollars of customer commitments, mostly cash deposits. Hyperscalers prepaying a memory supplier is behaviour reserved for an input they cannot replace, and at 909.72 the stock trades on a 4.4x forward P/E with +71.5% analyst upside after revenue growth of 256.3% — the shortage is contracted, multi-year, and still not priced at the analyst mean.

**The shortage + backlog evidence.** Management: Micron can meet only half to two-thirds of demand; HBM consumes 3-4x the wafer area of conventional DRAM, data centres take about 70% of 2026 memory output, and supply cannot keep up. Contracted visibility (verified with a definitional correction, see What was verified): the Q4 FY26 call (30 Sep 2026) cited roughly 150B dollars of RPO under 26 SCAs at minimum prices running through 2030, some into 2031, with 75% of capacity booked through end-2027 and about 35% booked through 2030, backed by about 32B dollars of customer financial commitments, the vast majority cash deposits; the 10-K (filed 9 Oct 2026) reports accounting RPO of about 134B dollars on a different scope — the two are not interchangeable, and both are cited here rather than blended. Growth is current, not promised: Q4 FY26 revenue 54.23B dollars (vs 11.32B a year earlier), Q1 FY27 guided to 61.5B dollars (plus or minus 1.5B) with sequential growth expected through FY27. Earnings quality supports the book: CFO/NI 1.02, accrual ratio about -0.01, and the screen's RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN flag reads benign in the research — the divergence is growth arithmetic, not channel-stuffing.

**Why it's the category leader.** Micron is one of only three firms on earth that make HBM at scale, and the only US-headquartered one — #3 in HBM at about 16-18% share behind SK Hynix, with #3 positions in DRAM and NAND as well. It is not the share leader, and this writeup does not pretend otherwise: its leadership claim is structural (membership of a three-firm oligopoly in the AI build-out's scarcest input, with qualification and yield learning measured in years), not a #1 share claim.

**Why it's irreplaceable — and the honest disintermediation risk.** No hyperscaler builds memory fabs: HBM fabrication requires process technology, TSV/yield learning curves and capital intensity that customers have demonstrably chosen to prepay (32B dollars) rather than replicate — that prepayment is the moat's receipt. The trap is therefore graded medium, not low, for a different reason: customers cannot bypass the category, but they can shift share within it — Samsung's HBM4 yields (reportedly about 80%) and SK Hynix's lead threaten Micron's slice, and accelerator designs that use less memory would shrink the pie's growth. Irreplaceability 7/10, shortage 10/10 on the dossier rubric. This is share risk inside an irreplaceable category, which is exactly the asymmetry the doctrine hunts — but it is not zero, and it is the pick's main weakness alongside cyclicality.
