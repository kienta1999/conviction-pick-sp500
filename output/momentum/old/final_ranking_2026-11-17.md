## Per-name thesis

**1. MU — the pick.** HBM sold out through 2027, >75% of FY27 output committed under 26 agreements to 2030, DRAM contract prices +90–95% QoQ, at 3.7x forward earnings. The shortage is contracted, not cyclical, and only three firms on earth can make HBM. See `final_pick_2026-11-17.md`.

**2. GE — the strongest moat, fully priced.** $170.2B contracted services backlog, >95% of Q4 spares already in backlog, sole-source LEAP on the 737 MAX and exclusive GE9X on the 777X behind a certification wall no customer can copy (irreplaceability 9/10). But at 35.6x forward with decelerating growth (services +17% in Q3 vs 32% in H1), its EV upside (+13.8%) falls below the +15% guardrail — the moat is real and the price knows it.

**3. APH — the only genuine shortage-plus-backlog name after MU.** Record $12.17B orders (+99% YoY), 1.19 book-to-bill, IT datacom +77% organic, AI ~36% of sales. The −16% pullback from the Oct 6 high on a soft Q4 datacom guide overstates the risk — but the Q4 cadence guide and extended neocloud backlog are the watch items.

**4. ANET — fastest growth, widest disintermediation seam.** Q3 revenue +58.5% YoY (fastest since IPO), FY27 guide +38–40%, $11.4B purchase commitments — but every lens flagged the trap: merchant-silicon dependence, white-box/ODM already taking 30–40% of hyperscale ports, Nvidia Spectrum-X bundling, 42% customer concentration, and the first deferred-revenue decline since 2024. The growth is real; the moat is the question.

**5. CF — real nitrogen tightness at 10.7x forward.** ~9M tons of urea supply lost since Feb, >35% of European ammonia capacity curtailed, 97% utilization — but a commodity producer with no backlog, a broken AN segment (Yazoo City), and only +3.8% analyst upside left.

**6. ISRG — strongest demand surge, strongest moat, 35.6x.** Procedures +15%, net placements +40%, 70% share, 84% recurring revenue — a demand story, not a supply shortage, with competition finally FDA-cleared. GAAP earnings −23.6% and the 12-month price −22.5% despite the surge is why it sits at #6, not #3.

**7. LLY — dominant franchise, fully priced.** 63% of the US incretin market, volume +53% in Q3 — but capacity is being solved with $18B+ of spend, prices are falling 15%, and it trades at its 52-week high with only +1.4% analyst upside. The case is category duration, not shortage.

**8. CVX — real refining tightness, no crude shortage.** Record 1.09M bpd throughput at ~98% utilization, $42/bbl margins, and a unique Venezuela wedge — but the stock has run +49% in a year and crude faces an IEA Q4 surplus.

**9. COP — best operator, worst shortage thesis.** Q3 EPS $3.27 was price (+32% realized), not volume (production −1.7%), into an IEA Q4 oil surplus; Goldman sees Brent $78 by Dec 2027. OPEC+ meets Nov 29.

**10. NEM — the momentum belongs to the metal, not the company.** Record Q3 built on $4,317/oz realized gold that no longer exists — spot ~$3,878 and falling, Q4 AISC guided up to ~$1,900/oz.
