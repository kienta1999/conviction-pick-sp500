# Momentum conviction pick — 2026-11-07

## THE PICK

**WAB — Wabtec Corp.**, Industrials / Construction Machinery & Heavy Transport. Current price \$278.48 (screen `price` field, 2026-11-05 close basis — the last bar in the price cache; the 2026-11-06 session is not reflected). Market cap ~\$46.7bn. Analyst mean target \$332.08 (+19.2%).

## One-paragraph thesis

Wabtec is the only name in the field where the shortage is measured in years of booked production, not in a spot price or a quarterly demand surge: a \$31.44bn multi-year backlog — about 2.5x guided FY2026 revenue — with 24–36 months of coverage on long-lead locomotive programmes, inside a North American duopoly for new high-horsepower freight locomotives where Wabtec is the dominant supplier and railroads standardise fleets around one builder. Q3 showed the backlog converting profitably (adjusted EPS +22%, adjusted operating margin +1.9pts, FY2026 EPS guidance raised to \$10.85–\$11.05), and at 22.1x forward earnings it is priced like an ordinary industrial, not like a bottleneck supplier with expanding margins. The explosive leg is backlog conversion plus a re-rate as the market prices 2.5x-sales of contracted revenue.

## The shortage + backlog evidence

- Total multi-year backlog **\$31.44bn at 30 Sep 2026, +22.9% YoY** (12-month backlog \$8.99bn, +8.8% YoY) — Wabtec Q3 2026 earnings release, 21 Oct 2026. **Verified CONFIRMED** (Phase 3.5).
- CEO Rafael Santana, Q3 2026 call, 21 Oct 2026: order coverage is "the strongest it has ever been", with orders **24–36 months out** for long-lead-time new locomotives. **Verified CONFIRMED** (Phase 3.5).
- Q3 orders: \$1bn international locomotive/service awards (Africa, Brazil) and >\$600m North American locomotive/modernisation orders from two Class I customers, plus \$159m transit brake orders.
- Freight multi-year backlog \$25.77bn (+23.2%), Transit \$5.67bn (+21.4%). North American carload traffic +3.5% in Q3, with a larger active locomotive fleet than a year ago and more parts demand as parked locomotives return to service.

## Why it's the category leader

Wabtec and Caterpillar's Progress Rail form a North American duopoly for new high-horsepower freight locomotives, with Wabtec the dominant supplier (the dossier's precise ~70%/30% split could **not** be verified as a current fact — see "What was verified" — and is not stated here as fact). Globally it is a top-4 locomotive player alongside CRRC, Alstom and Siemens, but North America — the profit pool — is a two-player market it dominates. Q3 2026: sales \$3.12bn (+8.1%), adjusted EPS \$2.83 (+22%, beat \$2.72 consensus), adjusted operating margin 22.9% (+1.9pts; Freight +190bps, Transit +260bps), operating cash flow \$455m (vs \$367m).

## Why it's irreplaceable

The moat is the installed base plus proprietary platforms (the Evolution Series prime mover, Trip Optimizer, digital intelligence), certification/safety approvals, and a service/modernisation annuity on a fleet only Wabtec can fully support. The customers are the Class I railroads themselves — none builds locomotives, and entry requires capital, engineering depth and EPA certification that make a new entrant prohibitive. Railroads standardise fleets around one builder, making switching extremely costly. The battery/hydrogen transition is a long-dated shift Wabtec itself leads rather than a bypass. **Disintermediation risk: low** (both supply-chain and contrarian panelists). The realistic threat is demand-side: railroads deferring fleet renewal in a freight downturn — a timing risk, not an in-sourcing risk.
