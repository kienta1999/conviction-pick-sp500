# Final pick — momentum mode, 2026-09-29

## THE PICK

**MU — Micron Technology**, Semiconductors, market cap ~$1,190B. Price at pick: $1,053.98 (2026-09-26 close).

## One-paragraph thesis

Micron owns the tightest fundamental picture in the S&P 500: its entire 2026 HBM output is sold out under multi-year contracts booked into 2027, it can supply only 50-66% of key customers' medium-term needs, and 16 take-or-pay strategic agreements lock in ~$100B of minimum revenue plus ~$22B of customer financial commitments (~$18B cash deposits) through 2030. Customers are prepaying for supply that does not exist — the doctrine's purest shortage signature — yet the stock trades at 6.5x forward earnings with +44% analyst upside, because the market still prices Micron as a commodity memory cyclical rather than a contracted-backlog compounder. Q4 FY26 earnings on Sep 30 (consensus ~$51B revenue, ~$31.50 adj EPS) is the near-term re-rating catalyst.

## The shortage + backlog evidence

- **2026 HBM output sold out**, booked into 2027; supply covers only ~50-66% of key customers' medium-term requirements (company commentary, Sep 2026; corroborated by SK Hynix "essentially sold out" and Samsung's entire 2026 HBM sold in advance).
- **$100B in cumulative take-or-pay minimum revenue floors** across 14 strategic agreements, **~$22B customer financial commitments (~$18B cash deposits)**, through calendar 2030 (Q3 FY26 10-Q, Sep 2026 reporting). Both verified CONFIRMED by the independent verifier.
- **~20% HBM3E price hikes planned for 2026**; conventional DRAM contract prices +93-98% QoQ earlier in 2026 — the tightest since ~2017.
- HBM is ~52-58% of a B200's BOM (vs ~20% on A100) and uses ~2x the wafer resources per bit — the shortage's epicenter is structural to the AI buildout, not a demand blip.

## Why it's the category leader

DRAM #3 (~24-25% revenue share), HBM #3 (~21%) behind SK Hynix (58%) and Samsung (21%) — not #1 overall, but the shortage does not require #1: all three HBM makers are sold out, and Micron's HBM4 is shipping >$1B revenue with a 12-high ramp tracking 2x faster than HBM3E. Micron retook solo #3 in NAND in Q2 2026 on enterprise-SSD mix. The leverage is the purest among the three: no conglomerate cushion, full exposure to the memory price curve.

## Why it's irreplaceable

No hyperscaler can build a leading-edge DRAM fab: a single fab costs $10-20B+, and HBM's TSV stacking yields require years of process learning. Only ~3 firms on earth make HBM at scale, and the $22B in take-or-pay deposits are proof customers cannot route around Micron — they prepay to secure supply. **Disintermediation risk: medium.** The real threat is not customer in-sourcing but Chinese competition: CXMT went from ~4% to ~10% of global DRAM revenue in one year and is mass-producing LPDDR6 (Sep 2026), though a generational technical gap in HBM remains and export controls still bind its tooling. This is competition, not disintermediation — and it is concentrated in commodity DRAM, not HBM.
