## Per-name thesis

**1. KLAC** — the panel's top name on both conviction (two #1 votes, conviction 9 from C) and consensus (ranked by all four, best average placement 1.5). The shortage: backlog "just short of $13B" ($12.57B, +60% YoY, CONFIRMED) and still growing, with KLA's own supply — not demand — the binding constraint (lead times *lengthening* on supplier constraints). The category leader: ~73.8% metrology/inspection share. The moat: algorithmic (~25 years of defect-classification data), foundry customers only — hyperscalers cannot in-source it; irreplaceability 10/10, the dossier's highest. The 37.6% drawdown is the entry. Base +49% / bull +86% on the adjudicated scenarios; also the single pick (see final_pick_2026-09-28.md).

**2. NVDA** — the shortage IS the product: $279B of supply commitments (Aug 2026 10-Q, corrected upward from the stale $95.2B figure — the correction strengthens the case), $1T in Blackwell+Rubin purchase orders expected through 2027 (CONFIRMED), Vera Rubin NVL72 racks shipping (Sept 23, 2026, CONFIRMED). The honest debate the panel litigated: J.P. Morgan forecasts custom AI chips at 54% of accelerator units by 2027, and TPU v7/Trainium 3 are in volume production (both CONFIRMED) — but the hyperscalers building ASICs remain Nvidia's largest customers (AWS alone deploying ~2M more Nvidia GPUs 2027–28), i.e. coexistence, not substitution. Won the A and B lenses on backlog depth and compounding speed; lost the single-pick vote on the trap question. At 14.4x forward with 45.6% analyst upside, the cheapest multiple in the AI complex.

**3. LRCX** — the cleanest "demand outruns supply" statement in the group, straight from the CFO: "the industry is fundamentally under-supplying demand, and clean room is a constraint" (Citi TMT, Sept 11–12, 2026 — CONFIRMED verbatim; the WFE raise to low-$150B happened at this conference, not at earnings, per the verifier's date correction). #1 etch (~45% share, CONFIRMED), process-of-record qualification moat, record $6.72B quarter with a $8.1B guide (+20% Q/Q). Consensus without conviction: ranked by all four, zero #1 votes. Caveat: its EV upside (+11.1% on my math) sits under the +15% guardrail — it stays in the ranking, but the reader sees it. China is 26% of revenue.

**4. AMAT** — the volume monster of the AI buildout: record $9.12B quarter, first-ever $10B quarter guided, eight-quarter demand forecasts from the largest fabs running out to 2030, CEO Dickerson sizing 100k wafer starts of greenfield DRAM at ~$10B of process equipment. The contrarian's runner-up: ~33% off its high on a record quarter — "priced to disappoint" is either the opportunity or the warning. Deduction: AMAT is *adding* capacity (doubling output by 2028), so the shortage is downstream (DRAM supply gap), and the market is punishing the capacity-build margin friction.

**5. The deduction: the market insists on watching the $195–205B capex bill convert to cash flow first — Q2 FCF went negative, and the stock sits ~20% off its record high. It is the in-sourcer, so disintermediation risk is customer-side, not supplier-side.

The deduction: 30x+ forward earnings and beta 1.6 price it as a compounder, and the fresh overhangs are real — data-center regulatory scrutiny (TX, NY) and Oracle's Sept 25 force-majeure on its Stargate campus, the template for what a build-out pause does to power-equipment names.

## Risk lens & leverage-safety note (across the list)
- **Sector concentration:** 6 of 10 are semis/memory/WFE (KLAC, NVDA, LRCX, AMAT, MU, SNDK) — the ranking is a levered bet on the AI-capex cycle continuing, not a diversified portfolio. A memory-capex rollover hits #1, #3, #4, #6, #10 simultaneously.
- **Highest-risk vs steadiest:** MU (binary print in 2 days), SNDK (+649% YTD commodity rocket), and CAT (beta 1.6 at 30x+) are the high-beta tails; MSFT and GOOGL are the steadier compounders with the least explosiveness.
- **Leverage education, not advice:** memory names (MU, SNDK) can draw down 50%+ in a cycle turn; KLAC itself drew 37.6% this year. Margin leverage on this list's cyclicals is brutal — no leverage multiple or position size is suggested.

## Thesis-break / trap triggers to watch
- Backlog shrinking QoQ (KLAC, LRCX, AMAT, GOOGL, MSFT, CAT) — the shortage leg breaking.
- Memory pricing rolling over / margins peaking QoQ (MU, SNDK) — the cycle turning.
- A hyperscaler announcing custom-silicon wins that displace merchant GPUs at scale (NVDA) or an Ethernet architecture shift to GPU bundles accelerating (ANET, just-missed) — the trap firing.
- Customer in-sourcing announcements, second-source qualifications (KLAC/LRCX/AMAT), or data-center capex pauses (CAT/APH) — demand, not supply, becoming the constraint.
