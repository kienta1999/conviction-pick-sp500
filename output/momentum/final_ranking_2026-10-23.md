## Per-name theses (top 5)

**MU (1).** The shortage the panel converged on: two lenses (supply-chain, growth) made it their single pick at conviction 9, it was ranked by all four, and its average placement (2.25) is the field's best. Micron says it can fulfil only between half and two-thirds of demand; its 2026 HBM supply is sold out and the vast majority of 2027 HBM bit supply is under agreement (the verifier's wording correction — see What was verified). The contracted book is the differentiator: on the Q4 FY26 call Micron cited roughly 150B dollars of remaining performance obligations under 26 Strategic Customer Agreements at minimum prices through 2030/31, backed by about 32B dollars of customer commitments, mostly cash — while the 10-K's accounting RPO is about 134B dollars on a different scope, and the two figures are not interchangeable. Earnings quality supports the story: CFO/NI 1.02 and the RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN flag reads benign (growth arithmetic, not channel-stuffing). The honest caveats are cyclicality (27.37B dollars of FY26 capex, about 25B guided for H1 FY27 alone, arriving in 2027-28 regardless of demand) and #3 HBM share (about 16-18%) behind SK Hynix, with Samsung resurgent on HBM4 yields.

**NVDA (2).** The dominance name and the moat lens's single pick (conviction 8): roughly 80-85% of AI accelerator revenue, about 10x the #2 merchant rival, the CUDA full stack, and a preliminary FY28 revenue-growth expectation near 70% that management described as supply-constrained. The 500B dollar Blackwell + Rubin figure is revenue visibility through end-2026 disclosed in Nov 2025 — roughly 30% already shipped, including networking — not a booked backlog, and it has not been refreshed; the fresher hard numbers are 15.6B dollars of H1 customer advances and very large own supply commitments. It carries the field's most active trap: hyperscalers are 40-50% of revenue and their custom ASICs already hold an estimated 12-18% share growing faster than GPUs, aimed first at inference. Earnings quality is the second discount: CFO/NI 0.70, accrual ratio +0.254, DSO stretched from 45 to 60 days on deliberate extended terms — explained, low default risk, but earnings now run ahead of cash.

**LLY (3).** The capacity name: demand has exceeded supply globally since the Zepbound launch, revenue through 2027 is largely a function of manufacturing capacity, Lilly holds 60.9% of US incretin prescriptions at 1.85x the #2, Mounjaro + Zepbound did 27.7B dollars in H1 2026 (+88%), more than 50B dollars has been committed to plants since 2020, and FY26 guidance was raised to 85-87B dollars — all verified. Nobody can in-source peptide manufacturing, so the trap is low. The problem is purely price: at its 52-week high, 27.5x forward, with only +2.6% analyst upside and realised prices down about 13% in Q2, the ranking scenario EV is below the +15% guardrail (see Return scenarios) — a great business whose momentum entry is priced for continued beats. Earnings land 2026-10-29, inside 10 days.

**FCX (4).** The skeptic's single pick (conviction 7) and the field's contrarian case: it screens last of the kept 14 (composite 0.385, TTM revenue growth 0.2%) only because its own Grasberg outage cut sales, while that outage helps sustain a roughly 330kt (J.P. Morgan) to 407kt (UBS) refined copper deficit for 2026, with mine lead times of 15-18 years making replacement supply a decade-plus project. The recovery is dated — Grasberg at about 65% in H2 2026, about 80% by mid-2027, near-full by end-2027, H2 sales guided more than 20% above H1, and a leach ramp targeted from 300M toward 800M lb/yr (execution lagging at about 30% of target recovery). The INVENTORY_BUILD flag was verified benign by the research (product inventory nearly flat; the build is smelter feed and produced-but-unsold metal, CFO/NI 2.00). Tier-1 geology scores irreplaceability 8/10; the risk is Grasberg execution and Indonesian resource nationalism, not bypass. Earnings 2026-10-27, inside 10 days.

All 2026 capacity is pre-sold, bits stay on allocation beyond calendar 2027, and the New Business Model book carries a 93.9B dollar minimum at floor prices (RPO 59.8B dollars in the 10-K, 91.1B including post-quarter deals), covering more than 50% of FY27 bits and about 67% of FY28 bits. ## Risk lens and leverage-safety note

Concentration is the headline risk in this ranking: the top 2 and #5 are one trade — AI memory/compute — and MU, NVDA and SNDK together mean a single capex-pause headline hits three of the top five simultaneously; CF/FANG/COP add commodity beta that correlates with the same global impulse in a shock. Highest-risk names are the post-vertical-run cyclicals: memory can draw down 50% or more fast when pricing rolls (MU +350% and SNDK +880% in 12 months are the warning, not the reassurance), which is brutal with any leverage. Steadier compounders in the list are CME, LLY and AME — durable moats, lower beta to a single commodity print. This system is cash-only per POLICY.md: no margin, no options as leverage substitutes. Treat any leverage discussion here as risk education, not a recommendation.
