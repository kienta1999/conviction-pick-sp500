Management: DRAM and NAND to remain supply-constrained in both 2027 and 2028, with no line of sight to balance (fiscal Q4 call, 2026-09-30).

- Category position: NOT number 1 overall, closing on number 2. ## CF — CF Industries (nitrogen fertilizer)

- Shortage thesis: cyclical-tight rather than structural-multi-year. Q2 2026 (reported 2026-08-05): average selling prices higher across all segments YoY due to a tight global nitrogen supply-demand balance, further tightened by the Iran conflict (company 8-K). Management expects supplies constrained through end-2026 and into 2027 (reduced Middle Eastern exports, Russian production risks, European gas economics). Quantified disruption: Iran conflict removed about 4-4.5M metric tons of traded urea and about 1M metric tons of ammonia (company estimate). Price evidence: US urea distributor asking prices up about 9.1% week-over-week to 796.25 USD/short ton (week ending 2026-10-02); Q2 granular urea average selling price 593 USD/ton vs 460 USD/ton a year earlier. Counterweight: new nitrogen capacity takes at least three to four years to build, but Chinese exports have resumed and supply normalisation reprices the thesis quickly.
- Backlog: formal backlog figure not found — fertilizer is not sold on a booked backlog basis. Forward indicators: 2026 ammonia production guidance about 9.5M tons (including Yazoo City outage impact); upgraded mid-cycle outlook about 2.9B USD EBITDA and about 1.7B USD free cash flow; Blue Point One 1.4M tons/year low-carbon ammonia targeted 2029. Q3 2026 consensus EPS 3.12 USD (plus 42.5% YoY) ahead of the 2026-11-04 print — a pre-print expectation, not a result.
- Category position: number 1 in its home market, not globally. Largest US ammonia producer; 39% of US domestic ammonia capacity vs Nutrien 16% (number 2), Koch 10%, LSB 5% (farmdoc daily, May 2026, 2023 data). Globally, Chinese state-linked producers are larger in aggregate.
- Irreplaceability 3/10: nitrogen is fungible — urea is urea, buyers switch on price; no patent, certification or software lock-in. Moat is cost and logistics: low North American gas costs, scale (nine complexes, about 10.5M tons gross ammonia capacity), distribution, 3-4 year build time. Customers cannot make their own ammonia but can buy from Nutrien, Koch, Yara or imports.
- Recent catalysts: Q2 2026 net earnings 727M USD (plus 88% YoY, 4.73 USD diluted EPS) vs consensus varying by source (about 5.44 USD Kalkine vs 5.65 USD Barchart — company actual 4.73 USD undisputed); revenue 2.22B USD (plus 18% YoY); adjusted EBITDA 1.19B USD; sales volumes down 15% YoY, more than offset on price; H1 included about 170M USD litigation settlement gain. 2026-08-26 broke ground on Blue Point One (3.7B USD JV: CF 40%, JERA 35%, Mitsui 25%; about 98% CO2 capture). 2026-10-07 dividend 0.60 USD declared; Q2 buyback 2.0M shares for 230M USD. Sep 2026 headline risk: shares fell after a reported US potash deal with Belarus — sentiment, not a nitrogen fundamental.
- Risks: cyclicality is the thesis and the risk; gas feedstock cost; Yazoo City outage (production not expected to resume until H1 2027); Blue Point One and carbon capture absorb about 1.3B USD of 2026 capital; only plus 9.1% analyst upside — the tight market is already substantially priced in.
- Shortage 6/10. Verdict: genuine, price-verified nitrogen squeeze into 2027 with CF as the lowest-cost, largest US beneficiary — but a commodity cycle with a visible exit, not a structural shortage.
