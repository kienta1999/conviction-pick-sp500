## ANET — Arista Networks

**Screen:** rev TTM +35.1%, op margin 43.8%, ret 12m +49.1%, analyst upside +40.0%, fwd PE 29.5.

1. **Shortage thesis.** AI datacenter buildout: demand for high-speed Ethernet switching is outrunning supply of capacity, not of Arista's product per se — but Arista is supply-constrained in the sense that its growth is gated by shipment capacity and component availability. CFO Chantelle Breithaupt reported inventory of $3.2B (up from $2.5B in Q2) and purchase commitments of $11.4B (up from $9.7B) "to support component availability" (Q3 2026 call, Nov 3, 2026). CEO Jayshree Ullal guided >10 GW of AI deployments by end-2027 across 10+ accelerator types. The datacenter Ethernet switch market surged 61% YoY in 1Q26 to $10.0B (IDC). No explicit "sold out" quote from Arista; the shortage is in AI compute/networking capacity broadly, and Arista's constraint is component supply (Broadcom silicon, memory) plus shipment capacity — management expects 3–4 customers above 10% of revenue "subject to shipment capacity."
2. **Backlog / order book.** Arista does not report a formal backlog figure. Proxies: purchase commitments $11.4B (+17.5% QoQ, Q3 2026); deferred revenue expected to end 2026 higher than prior year (management declined to forecast the balance). 2027 revenue outlook ~$18.5B (+38–40%), AI fabrics revenue to double to ~$7B, cognitive campus +60% to ~$2B. Visibility is only ~2 quarters per management — a key caveat.
3. **Category position.** NOT #1 in datacenter Ethernet switching anymore. IDC 1Q26: NVIDIA #1 with 21.5% datacenter share ($2.1B, +192.7% YoY); Arista #2 at 20.7% datacenter share ($2.2B total Ethernet, +37.3% YoY); Cisco 29.3% of total Ethernet market (18.2% datacenter in 2Q26). 2Q26 IDC: NVIDIA 20.4%, Arista 18.7%, Cisco 18.2% — a 1.7pp gap. Arista remains the leading *independent/merchant* Ethernet vendor and #1 in enterprise/campus, but NVIDIA has overtaken it in the AI datacenter segment.
4. **Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk.** Moat: EOS (Extensible Operating System) software — single-image OS, programmability, 10–50x lower CVE counts vs alternatives per management; deep qualification with hyperscalers; >100 Etherlink AI customers (vs 4–5 at cycle start). Threats are concrete and live: (a) NVIDIA Spectrum-X bundles switches+DPUs+optics+cables into the GPU sale — networking becomes a cost center of the AI factory, not a standalone purchase; (b) Arista does NOT design its own switch ASICs — every switch runs Broadcom silicon (Tomahawk/Jericho), so Broadcom holds pricing power and gross margin fell 65.6% → 63.4% YoY (Q2 2026); (c) hyperscalers (Meta, Microsoft = ~26% and ~16% of 2025 revenue) could shift share to NVIDIA bundles or white-box/OCP designs; (d) optical circuit switching is a longer-term architectural bypass. **Irreplaceability score: 5/10** — EOS and qualification are real, but the silicon is someone else's and the biggest customer (NVIDIA) is now the biggest competitor.
5. **Recent catalysts (last ~3 months).** Q3 2026 (Nov 3): revenue $3.66B, +58.5% YoY, beat $3.33B consensus; non-GAAP EPS $1.20 vs $1.08 est.; non-GAAP op margin 49.1%. 2026 revenue guidance raised for the 4th time this year to $13.3B (+48%). 2027 outlook $18.5B introduced, well above Street. Shares +9.6% premarket on the print. 7060XE7 1.6T switch unveiled (Q2). Evercore (Nov 2026) called Arista "the clearest beneficiary" of NVIDIA's CEO departure — a fresh, if speculative, tailwind.
6. **Risks.** Customer concentration (Microsoft ~26%, Meta ~16% of 2025 revenue); NVIDIA Spectrum-X share gains in AI fabrics; Broadcom silicon dependence and margin pressure; only ~2 quarters of demand visibility — H2 2027 depends on customer conversations; scale-up (rack-scale) products still in trials 2027, production 2028, with unsettled fulfillment models; valuation already pricing flawless execution (fwd PE 29.5 on screen, ~59x trailing at Q2 per press).
7. **Shortage score: 7/10. Verdict:** The AI networking wave is real and Arista is a prime beneficiary, but the "shortage" is in AI capacity generally, not an Arista-specific bottleneck — and NVIDIA now leads the datacenter segment Arista used to own.

## LLY — Eli Lilly

**Screen:** rev TTM +37.0%, op margin 51.3%, ret 12m +31.6%, upside +7.7%, fwd PE 26.8.

1. **Shortage thesis.** GLP-1 demand surge: US incretin use for obesity grew 82% in Q3 2026; Lilly holds ~6 in 10 US obesity incretin prescriptions and >7 in 10 injectable prescriptions; ~58% share outside the US (IQVIA gross sales). The acute FDA shortage is OVER — FDA declared the tirzepatide/semaglutide shortage ended Dec 2024, compounding bans took effect Mar–Apr 2025, and WeightWatchers (updated Nov 5, 2026) notes "the FDA reports that there is not currently a shortage of Zepbound." The thesis has shifted from shortage to *penetration*: management says it reaches only ~1 in 10 eligible US patients. Demand driver now is access expansion (Medicare GLP-1 bridge program since Jul 1, 2026: ~20M seniors newly covered, >500k prescriptions filled) plus the oral pill (Foundayo) opening the injection-reluctant market. Supply is no longer the binding constraint — Lilly stopped touting dose counts and now talks about patients reached (Trefis, Nov 19, 2026).
2. **Backlog / order book.** Not applicable — pharma has no order backlog; the analogue is demand/prescription volume. Q3 2026: Mounjaro $10.7B (+65%), Zepbound $5.35B (+49%); combined >$16B, ~66% of total revenue $24.4B (+39%). Volume +53% YoY while realized US price fell 10% (US price −7% per another source; −15% blended). No backlog figure exists — **not found** (and not meaningful for this business model).
3. **Category position.** Clear #1 in GLP-1/incretins. Q3 2026 incretin sales ~$16.3B vs Novo Nordisk ~$9.4B (DKK 60.9B) — Lilly leads ~1.7x. Lilly revenue +39% vs Novo adjusted sales +4% CER. Market cap ~$1.14T vs Novo ~$172B (Oct 29, 2026). Anti-obesity market forecast $8.96B (2026) → $64.96B (2035), 24.7% CAGR (Precedence Research, Nov 16, 2026).
4. **Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk.** Moat: patents on tirzepatide (dual GIP/GLP-1, superior efficacy — SURMOUNT-5 head-to-head win), manufacturing scale ($50B US manufacturing commitment, 10 sites since 2020), and pipeline depth (retatrutide triple agonist, EloraTZP). Threats: (a) Novo's Wegovy pill (DKK 5.4B in Q3, 8M+ prescriptions since launch, Jan 2026) attacks the oral segment where Lilly's Foundayo ($184M in Q3, from $98M in Q2) is still small; (b) Pfizer (Metsera, up to $10B), Roche/Zealand ($5.3B), Amgen (MariTide Ph3) all entering; (c) price erosion is real — US realized price already falling; (d) patents eventually expire and compounding/generics will come. Customers (patients/payers) cannot in-source a drug, but payers can and do squeeze price, and employers may resist coverage. **Irreplaceability score: 7/10** — best-in-class molecule + manufacturing moat, but the moat is patent- and efficacy-based, not structural, and competition is well-funded.
5. **Recent catalysts (last ~3 months).** Q3 2026 (Oct 29): revenue $24.4B (+39%), adj EPS $7.48, big beat; 2026 guidance raised to $91–92B from $85–87B. Retatrutide Phase 3 beat semaglutide head-to-head (weight loss 20–21% vs 9.4%, A1C −2.2% vs −1.8%; up to 22.6% weight loss in TRIUMPH-1, published NEJM Sep 2026); FDA filing planned Q1 2027. EloraTZP Ph2: 23.3% weight loss at 48 weeks; Ph3 starts Q4 2026. Broke ground Sep 21, 2026 on $6.5B Houston API plant (Foundayo; 600+ jobs) and a $5B Richmond, VA plant — part of $50B US push. Stock +45.3% in six months to Nov 16, 2026. Foundayo approved Apr 1, 2026; under review in 40+ countries, global rollout targeted 2027. Hims & Hers direct-to-patient access deal.
6. **Risks.** Concentration: two drugs = ~66% of revenue. Price erosion (US price −10% in Q3; ~$2B of 2026 revenue is one-time items that won't recur in 2027). Medicare/Medicaid policy and employer coverage resistance. Novo's pill momentum and CagriSema FDA decision expected Q4 2026. Massive capex: capex = 61.9% of operating cash flow over the 12 months to Sep 30, 2026 (Trefis, Nov 12, 2026). Valuation: ~44.7x earnings vs 21.5x S&P 500 (Trefis). Growth already decelerating (revenue growth eased each quarter of 2026: 55.5% Q1 → 38.6% Q3).
7. **Shortage score: 5/10. Verdict:** The shortage that made the story is over and supply is now a solved problem; what remains is a dominant #1 with a superior molecule and a deep pipeline, but priced for continued dominance amid falling realized prices.

## APH — Amphenol

**Screen:** rev TTM +48.2%, op margin 28.7%, ret 12m +19.6%, upside +38.2%, fwd PE 21.4.

1. **Shortage thesis.** AI datacenter interconnect is a genuine physical bottleneck: at 224 Gb/s per lane, signals degrade on PCB traces, forcing traffic onto precisely engineered cable assemblies (OverPass, up to 12.8 Tb/s) — a "narrow node" where only a short list of firms (Amphenol, TE Connectivity, Molex) have decades of process knowledge and qualification. High-speed assembly lead times exceed 12 weeks (Markets NXT, Nov 2026), with procurement advised to "lock multi-year volume agreements with Amphenol or TE Connectivity for 2027 delivery." Demand: IT Datacom (48% of sales) grew 100% in dollars / 77% organically in Q3; AI is ~3/4 of IT Datacom (~36% of the whole company). Book-to-bill has run >1.2 for three straight quarters (1.31 Q4 2025, 1.24 Q1 2026, 1.23 Q2 2026, 1.19 Q3 2026) — orders consistently exceed shipments, the cleanest shortage signal in this batch.
2. **Backlog / order book.** Record orders $12.17B in Q3 2026, +99% YoY, +13% QoQ; book-to-bill 1.19:1, positive in nearly every end market. Backlog grew $1.94B in the quarter and $5.7B year-to-date (Aardvark Labs recap, Oct 28, 2026). Orders of $12.17B are 17% above the midpoint of Q4 sales guidance ($10.3–10.5B) — more than a full quarter of shipments already booked. IT Datacom backlog is stretching further out than usual (BofA question on Q3 call); management said longer-dated orders are normal and most demand comes from highly capitalized customers, not neoclouds.
3. **Category position.** #1 interconnect manufacturer globally: >15% share of industrial connectors (TE 13%, Molex 7.5%); >12.5% of fiber optic connectors; 36.2% of military connectors (TE 14.4%); 31.2% of North America circular connectors; 13.0% of cable assemblies (TE 12.5%) — all per Global Market Insights 2025 data. In the AI-relevant high-speed datacom niche it is the widest lane, though TE and Molex are credible #2/#3.
4. **Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk.** Moat: signal-integrity process knowledge at 224G/448G, rack-level qualification lock-in (a failed link can kill an entire training run — "nobody chases the cheapest cable"), vertically integrated global manufacturing, and breadth across copper + fiber + power (CommScope acquisition, $10.5B, closed 2025, adds fiber/structured cabling, $3.6B sales). Hyperscalers cannot realistically in-source this: it is precision manufacturing + qualification, not software; the risk is dual-sourcing (TE/Molex) and technology migration (copper → fiber/CPO at 1.6T), which Amphenol hedges via CommScope and its own optical portfolio (XPO OverPass, 448G co-packaged copper demos). **Irreplaceability score: 8/10** — the highest in this batch; a genuine narrow node with only 2–3 qualified global suppliers.
5. **Recent catalysts (last ~3 months).** Q3 2026 (Oct 28): first-ever $10B quarter — sales $10.23B (+65%, +37% organic), adj EPS $0.80 (+74%), beating Street by 7.8%/9.6% and guide ceiling by $830M. Q4 guide $10.3–10.5B sales, $0.81–0.83 EPS; FY2026 guide $36.9–37.1B sales, $2.82–2.84 EPS (+60% sales, +70% EPS). CommScope outlook raised again to $5.1B sales. 2-for-1 split effective Sep 3, 2026. Committed ~$1.6B to four acquisitions (Empire Electronics, Advanced PCB, Dexgreen, Humanetics), a 20% dividend increase and a new $4B buyback. Stock hit 52-week high $89.93 on Oct 6, 2026.
6. **Risks.** AI concentration: ~36% of revenue now AI-linked; a datacenter capex pause would hit hard. Q4 IT Datacom guided to only mid-single-digit sequential growth after +30% in Q3 — the torrid pace is moderating. Communications networks fell 28% organically (broadband softness). Neocloud funding risk if AI buildout financing tightens (management downplays it). Copper-to-fiber/CPO migration could strand copper expertise over time. The stock is off its highs (−5.7% Nov 10–16) on "what's next for 2027 profit" doubts despite the order book.
7. **Shortage score: 9/10. Verdict:** The strongest shortage thesis in the batch — orders exceed shipments for a fourth straight quarter, lead times >12 weeks, and only three firms on earth can supply qualified 224G interconnect at scale.

## MSFT — Microsoft

**Screen:** rev TTM +14.8%, op margin 47.1%, ret 12m +3.8%, upside +29.2%, fwd PE 20.4.

1. **Shortage thesis.** AI compute shortage, stated plainly by management: CFO Amy Hood on the Oct 28, 2026 call — "Customer demand continues to exceed our available supply." Third-party GPU capacity deliveries slipped from Q1 into H2 FY27, leaving capex $45.2B (~$5B below plan) even as Azure grew 48%. Demand is not the constraint; delivered capacity is. Capex guided >$50B in Q2 FY27 and ~$170B for calendar 2026, accelerating in H2 on CPUs (incl. higher memory/storage costs), GPUs and delayed third-party capacity. Azure Foundry revenue more than tripled YoY; 120,000+ Foundry customers; M365 Copilot daily users +~50% QoQ and >5x YoY. The shortage is real but it is a shortage Microsoft *buys its way out of* at enormous cost — it caps near-term revenue rather than creating pricing power.
2. **Backlog / order book.** Commercial remaining performance obligation (RPO) $677B, +73% YoY — but FLAT sequentially ($678B in Jun 2026): bookings fell 12% YoY and Microsoft converted backlog into revenue faster than it refilled it. RPO has grown only ~8.3% since Dec 2025 (the quarter OpenAI's giant Azure commitment landed), while datacenter leases signed but not yet started more than doubled to $364.6B. The backlog is enormous but no longer growing — the key bearish nuance. OpenAI revenue was $10.0B in the quarter (~11% of total); Microsoft owns a 27% stake in OpenAI with priority access through 2032.
3. **Category position.** #2 cloud, closing on #1: Azure ~19–20% of global cloud infrastructure (Synergy, Q2–Q3 2026) vs AWS ~26–28% and Google Cloud ~15–16%; Azure is ~72% of AWS's size, up from 58% two years ago (CNBC via Die Signal). Azure $33.2B/quarter (+48%) vs AWS $46.0B (+39%) vs Google Cloud $30.7B (+103%) in Q3 2026. In enterprise AI (Foundry, Copilot, OpenAI exclusivity) Microsoft is arguably #1 in commercialization.
4. **Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk.** Moat: enterprise distribution (M365/Windows/Active Directory installed base), the OpenAI partnership (exclusive Azure APIs, priority access through 2032, 27% stake), 80+ regions / 500+ datacenters (most of any provider), and custom silicon (Maia 100/200 accelerators, Cobalt 100/200 Arm CPUs) reducing NVIDIA dependence. Disintermediation risk runs the other way: Microsoft's biggest customers are the AI labs, and OpenAI now sources from five clouds and expanded an AWS agreement (~$100B over 8 years, unconfirmed) — the exclusivity is eroding. Customers can multi-cloud; Google Cloud grew 103% last quarter, fastest of the three. No single customer can in-source Azure, but the AI labs can and do diversify away. **Irreplaceability score: 6/10** — deeply entrenched in the enterprise, but cloud is a three-horse race and the flagship tenant is diversifying.
5. **Recent catalysts (last ~3 months).** Q1 FY27 (Oct 28): revenue $92.3B (+19%), adj EPS $4.89 vs $4.72 est. — 8th consecutive double beat. Azure +48% to $33.2B in its first-ever dollar disclosure, beating 44–45% guide; Q2 Azure guided +49–50% cc. Added 24 datacenters across five continents in the quarter; GB300 dock-to-live time −20% QoQ. Maia 200 in production workloads incl. Copilot; Cobalt 200 claims 50%+ better performance than Cobalt 100. Buybacks $4.38B + dividends $6.76B in the quarter.
6. **Risks.** Capex explosion: ~$170B calendar-2026 capex; FCF fell 27% YoY to $18.6B in Q1 despite revenue +19% — the AI buildout is consuming cash. RPO flat sequentially and bookings −12%: the backlog engine has stalled even as spending accelerates; datacenter lease commitments ($364.6B not yet started) now exceed the growth in customer commitments — a potential overbuild signal. OpenAI concentration (~11% of revenue) plus OpenAI diversifying to AWS/others. Windows OEM/devices revenue −22% (PC market weak, memory prices soaring). Google Cloud's 103% growth is taking share. Stock +3.8% over 12m — the market already doubts the return on this capex.
7. **Shortage score: 7/10. Verdict:** Genuine, management-confirmed demand-exceeds-supply in AI compute — but the shortage constrains Microsoft as much as it rewards it, and the flat backlog plus a $170B capex bill is the price of staying in the race.

## CBOE — Cboe Global Markets

**Screen:** rev TTM +13.5%, op margin 36.4%, ret 12m +39.9%, upside +1.6%, fwd PE 22.8.

1. **Shortage thesis.** No product shortage — this is a *monopoly franchise*, not a scarce physical good. The "shortage" analogue is exclusive supply of the world's benchmark index options: Cboe is the SOLE US venue for standardized SPX options. Demand driver: structural surge in 0DTE (zero-days-to-expiration) trading and retail options participation. SPX 0DTE ADV hit a record 3.2M contracts in Q3 (+34% YoY), 65% of all SPX activity; retail SPX 0DTE volume +46% YoY (58% of 0DTE volume, up from 53%); XSP volume more than doubled YoY (helped by June 2026 repeal of the Pattern Day Trader rule); Global Trading Hours volume +130% YoY, Asian session now >30% of GTH. SPX options hit a record 970.6M contracts in 2025, ADV 3.9M (+25% YoY). Demand is surging; supply is contractually exclusive to Cboe.
2. **Backlog / order book.** Not applicable — exchange revenue is transactional, no backlog. The analogue is volume momentum: index options ADV 6.1M contracts (+24% YoY) in Q3 2026; total options ADV 21.3M (+14%); options net revenue $469.5M (+23%); index options revenue per contract $0.952 (+3%); total options RPC $0.307 (+9%). **Backlog figure: not found / not applicable.**
3. **Category position.** #1 and effectively 100% of US SPX options by exclusive license; also the home of VIX options/futures. In overall US options, Cboe is one of several exchanges (multi-listed options ADV +10% YoY), but its proprietary index complex is a legal monopoly. Derivatives net revenue $402M (+22% YoY) is the growth engine; Data Vantage (market data) $179.1M record (+13%).
4. **Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk.** Moat: the exclusive S&P Dow Jones Indices license, extended Sep 29, 2026 for 25 years through **2051** (8-K Sep 28, 2026; partnership since 1983), covering SPX options plus VIX cross-licenses; SEC-regulated exchange/clearing status; network effects in liquidity (SPX is the global standard for US equity exposure). Disintermediation risk is minimal but not zero: traders can use SPY/QQQ ETF options (multi-listed, competitive) instead of SPX; prediction markets and off-exchange venues (Cboe's answer: SEC-approved Cboe Predicts KPI event contracts, launching Nov 2026); the royalty resets higher from Jan 1, 2027 (Cboe expects volume growth + fee optimization to offset; S&P Global expects the extension to add a couple of points to its index revenue growth in 2027). Nobody can "build their own SPX options" — the index IP is licensed exclusively. **Irreplaceability score: 10/10** — a 25-year contractual monopoly on the benchmark US equity derivatives contract.
5. **Recent catalysts (last ~3 months).** Q3 2026 (Oct 30): net revenue $712M (+18%), adj EPS $3.63 (+36%), beat $3.43 est. by 5.8%; GAAP EPS $4.08 record (helped by gain on sale of Cboe Australia). 2026 organic net revenue guidance raised to high-teens (from mid-to-high teens); expense guidance lowered to $830–845M. SPX license extended to 2051 (shares +5.8% on the Sep 29 announcement). SEC approval for Cboe Predicts event contracts; tokenized S&P 500 options exploration announced with S&P DJI. VIX call volume hit its highest daily level in 2+ years in early October 2026. 52-week high $378.88 on Nov 16, 2026.
6. **Risks.** This is a volume/market-activity business, not a shortage business: a prolonged low-volatility, low-volume regime would stall growth (VIX options ADV only +3% YoY in Q3 despite SPX strength). 0DTE concentration: if regulators curb 0DTE or retail enthusiasm cools, the growth engine slows. Higher S&P royalty from 2027 (impact expected "de minimis" per management, but the exact economics come in February). Futures net revenue −5% YoY. Valuation: upside only +1.6% per screen — the market has largely priced the license extension and the 0DTE boom; ret 12m already +39.9%. No explosive-return shortage dynamic — growth is high-teens, not doubling.
7. **Shortage score: 3/10. Verdict:** A superb, now-25-year monopoly franchise with surging 0DTE volumes — but there is no supply shortage to explode on; it is a steady compounder miscast as a shortage bet.

## 2. EW — Edwards Lifesciences (structural heart: TAVR, TMTT)

### 1. Shortage thesis
**No — this is demand expansion, not supply shortage.** Nothing in EW's supply chain is sold out or capacity-constrained; valves are manufactured to meet procedure volumes, and growth is gated by patient eligibility, physician adoption and reimbursement, not by Edwards' ability to produce. The demand driver is real but clinical/regulatory: TAVR moving into asymptomatic severe aortic stenosis (FDA approval of SAPIEN 3 platform for asymptomatic patients May 1, 2025, on the 901-patient EARLY TAVR trial — treating asymptomatic patients nearly halved death/stroke/CV-hospitalisation vs watchful waiting) plus a CMS final national coverage determination (Sep 11, 2026) extending Medicare coverage to asymptomatic patients and removing coverage-with-evidence-development/volume-threshold requirements for symptomatic patients. Management says the asymptomatic + CMS effects will be "gradual" over multiple years, not a surge. TMTT is the faster demand story (+45.6%), driven by adoption, not scarcity.

### 2. Backlog / order book
**No backlog figure disclosed — not found.** Medtech device makers do not report order backlogs; sales are procedure-driven. Closest forward indicators: TMTT sales $207m in Q3 (up from $173m Q1, $195.9m Q2), FY26 TMTT guidance $780–790m, and a stated $2bn TMTT revenue ambition for 2030. TAVR FY26 guidance $4.8–5.0bn implied. No "booked out to" horizon exists.

### 3. Category position
**#1 in TAVR.** GlobalData market models: Edwards held **65.7% of the US TAVR market in 2025**, vs Medtronic 25.8% and Abbott 5.4%; globally ~56% vs Medtronic ~29% (Global Growth Insights). TAVR is ~72% of EW revenue ($1.24bn of $1.72bn in Q3). Edwards is the *only* company with FDA + CE approval for asymptomatic severe AS — a genuine first-mover edge. In TMTT it is also the pioneer (PASCAL, EVOQUE, SAPIEN M3 — the latter the world's first transcatheter mitral valve replacement system).

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk — score 7/10
Moat: **PMA/FDA approval + clinical-evidence + operator-training lock-in.** A TAVR valve needs years of randomised trial data (EARLY TAVR, PARTNER trials), a trained heart-team/centre network, and reimbursement coding — a hospital cannot "build its own valve," and a new entrant needs a decade of trials. That is a strong moat, but it is *not* a sole-source moat: Medtronic's Evolut is a credible #2 (self-expanding, strong in small annuli ~40% of patients, 5-year low-risk durability data, expanded Redo-TAVR approval Aug 28, 2025 for valve-in-valve regardless of original manufacturer), and Abbott's Navitor is gaining. Management itself guided that future TAVR growth will come from procedure volumes, "rather than competitive share gains," and Q3 US share was only "sequentially stable" — share gains have plateaued. Substitution risk is surgical AVR for younger/low-risk patients and, longer-term, a moderate-AS indication (PROGRESS trial) that is still unapproved with timing deferred to the Dec 4 investor conference. **Irreplaceability: 7/10** — nobody can bypass Edwards' evidence/regulatory position quickly, but a well-funded #2 already exists and takes a quarter of the market.

### 6. Risks
- TAVR growth is decelerating into mid-to-high-single digits long-term (management target); share gains have plateaued and European growth cooled as Boston Scientific's exit annualised.

## 3. V — Visa (payments network)

### 1. Shortage thesis
**No — there is no shortage; Visa sells access to a network, not a scarce physical product.** Demand is healthy but cyclical-consumer-driven, not supply-constrained: Q4 FY26 (Oct 27, 2026) payments volume +9% constant dollars, cross-border ex-intra-Europe +14%, processed transactions 74.2bn (+10%). The genuine growth engine is value-added services (VAS): +31% in Q4 to $4.0bn; $14bn in FY26, +30% — fraud/risk, tokenisation, issuing, acceptance, advisory. Management called FY26 "a banner year" (TD Cowen) with record net revenue $45.9bn (+15%), "strongest growth in over 15 years" ex-post-pandemic/acquisitions. The forward-looking demand drivers are agentic commerce (Visa Intelligent Commerce, OpenAI partnership) and stablecoin settlement ($25bn annualised run rate, 10x YoY, 225+ stablecoin-linked card programs) — but Visa itself discloses **no revenue figure** for stablecoin settlement; it is positioning, not a revenue surge.

### 2. Backlog / order book
**Not applicable — no backlog disclosed (not found, and not a meaningful concept for a network).** The closest forward-looking metric is the client-renewal cycle: **~25% of total payments volume is scheduled for renewal in 2027, up from 20% in FY26** (Q4 FY26 call) — a risk as much as a pipeline, since renewals come with client incentives, which grew 21% to $5.12bn in FY26, faster than revenue. FY27 revenue guidance steps down to low-double-digit growth (from FY26's +15%).

### 3. Category position
**#1 card network, decisively.** US: ~71% of combined Visa+Mastercard volume in Q2 2026 (70.96%, up from 70.28% a year earlier); worldwide (ex-China): Visa $15,337bn vs Mastercard $9,293bn TTM to Jun 30, 2026 — 62.27% share, 1.65x Mastercard on volume, 1.27x on net revenue (Axis Intelligence, Oct 27, 2026). US debit is the stronghold (2.79x Mastercard). ~5.2bn branded cards; ~80% processing penetration globally; 281.3bn transactions processed in FY26 (+9%).

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk — score 7/10
Moat: **two-sided network effects at global scale + trust/acceptance ubiquity** — 5.2bn cards, acceptance effectively everywhere, 175bn+ transactions/yr ecosystem, plus fraud/tokenisation infrastructure that issuers and merchants cannot replicate cheaply. No single customer can in-source it; even the largest merchants need Visa acceptance. **But the bypass threat is the most credible it has ever been, which caps the score.** Concrete threats: (a) **stablecoins/A2A** — Walmart and Amazon are exploring their own stablecoins (WSJ/PYMNTS, 2026); shifting 1pp of Walmart's volume to its own stablecoin would save ~$783m/yr in fees; AI shopping agents "could simply learn to route purchases through stablecoin rails that cost fractions of a penny" (Feb 2026 analysis); (b) **Europe's Wero/EPI** — 50m+ registered users (Feb 2026), e-commerce live in Germany since late 2025, POS coming 2026, backed by 16+ major European banks with explicit payment-sovereignty political support; (c) **UK Payments Delivery Company** — 19 banks/firms raising ~£50m to build a domestic challenger; (d) **regulation** — DOJ debit-monopoly suit (filed Sep 2024, trial sought delayed to 2028, still active); Credit Card Competition Act reintroduced Jan 2026 (Durbin/Marshall) forcing dual-network routing; Nov 2025 merchant settlement cutting swipe fees ~0.1pp for five years; a proposed 10% credit-card rate cap. Visa's response is to *become* the stablecoin/agentic rails (stablecoin settlement pilots, Visa Intelligent Commerce, tokenised deposits, validator seats on Tempo/Canton, Circle Arc invitation) — a rational hedge that preserves a toll whichever standard wins, but concedes the old toll is under attack. **Irreplaceability: 7/10** — irreplaceable today, assailable over a 5–10 year horizon in a way it was not five years ago.

- **Q4/FY26 beat, Oct 27, 2026:** Q4 revenue $12.1bn (+13%), adj. EPS $3.48 (+17%, vs $3.43 consensus); FY26 revenue $45.9bn (+15%). Dividend raised 16% to $0.78/qtr; $24.4bn buyback authorisation remaining; $5.2bn returned in Q4.

### 6. Risks
- The 2027 renewal cycle (25% of volume) + incentives growing faster than revenue = margin pressure in FY27; management already guides growth down to low-double-digits.
- Regulatory overhang is multi-front (DOJ suit, CCCA, swipe-fee settlement, rate-cap proposal) and bipartisan — a single adverse ruling/legislation reprices the toll.

**Shortage score: 2/10** — no scarcity of any kind; demand is a function of consumer spending, and supply is infinitely scalable software.

## UNP — Union Pacific (western freight railroad)

### 1. Shortage thesis

"re-entered a driver shortage for the first time in approximately 3.5 years." C.H. Robinson,
Nov 5: "Union Pacific has identified several capacity-constrained markets and is actively
managing container reservations, giving priority to committed contract customers. Shippers
seeking committed capacity out of California; Texas; Salt Lake City, Utah; and Chicago
should expect limited availability" — with higher rates and tighter capacity; "new
long-term agreements are unlikely to begin before early 2027" (Sep update). Intermodal spot
rates +4.3% YoY net of fuel, with "larger rate increases" expected in Q1 2027 contract renewals.
Railway Age (Nov 2026): rail-bound containers at LA/Long Beach waited 7.19 days in
September, up from 6.34 in July.

### 2. Backlog / order book
No order book disclosed (railroads don't carry backlog). Proxies: intermodal volumes +11%
to 956,000 carloads in Q3; domestic intermodal "fifth consecutive record quarter in volume
and revenue" (EVP Kenny Rocker, Q3 call); intermodal accounted for ~75% of Q3 volume

booked-out book — but no dated backlog figure exists. Not found: backlog $.

### 3. Category position
Largest publicly traded US railroad and one half of the western duopoly (with BNSF; the

Intermodal revenue +27% YoY to ~$1.52bn in Q3 — the fastest growth among North American

closes, the combined company would be the largest railroad in US history: 50,000+ route
miles, 43 states, ~100 ports.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk — score 7/10
Moat: the physical network itself — nobody can build a second transcontinental western
railroad; capital intensity and land/rights-of-way are prohibitive, and only two western
Class Is exist. Shippers cannot in-source rail; the substitute is trucking, which is
currently *more* expensive and capacity-constrained — the shortage itself proves the moat.
Score 7, not higher, because (a) trucks cap rail pricing power on most lanes (intermodal
only wins where it undercuts trucking), (b) ~75% of the volume surge is intermodal, the
most truck-substitutable segment, and (c) the merger that would entrench the moat is
unapproved and opposed.

**Verdict:** A real, quotable capacity crunch with pricing power — but it is a fuel/trucking

## VRSN — VeriSign (.com/.net registry)

### 1. Shortage thesis
No shortage at all — this is a regulated toll road, and the "surge" is a demand
re-acceleration, not scarcity. Management cites a "synergistic convergence" of demand:
new business starts, registrar marketing programs, and an AI tailwind — "AI makes sites
faster to build, and creators want their content found by AI" (CEO Jim Bidzos, Q3 call,
Oct 22, 2026). New registrations hit a record 13.1m in Q3 (vs 12.7m Q2, 10.6m year-ago);
the domain base grew 6.1% YoY after years of ~1–3% growth. There is no capacity
constraint — VeriSign can register unlimited names; the constraint is demand, and demand
is a flow, not a stock. The pricing lever is contractual, not market-driven (see §5).

### 2. Backlog / order book
No backlog. The annuity analogue: .com + .net domain base 182.5m names at Sep 30, 2026
(169.8m .com + 12.6m .net), +6.1% YoY, +3.4m QoQ; net additions 3.4m in Q3 (vs 3.05m Q2,
2.54m Q1). Renewal rate is the retention metric: expected Q3 renewal 74.3% vs 75.4% a year
ago — down, because first-time renewals are a larger share of the base; the record 2026
cohort faces its first renewal test in 2027. Deferred-revenue style visibility is high
(annual renewals), but no multi-year booked figure is disclosed. Not found: backlog $.

### 3. Category position
Literal monopolist: sole operator of the .com registry — "No other entity can provide .com
registry services, and no registrar or registrant can obtain .com domains from any source
other than Verisign" (Kessler v. VeriSign complaint, filed Sep 4, 2026, C.D. Cal.).
.com is ~41% of all domain registrations worldwide (~166m names at Q2 2026). #2 is not a
competitor but a different TLD: .net (also VeriSign), then country-code and new gTLDs run
by others. Also operates 2 of the 13 global internet root servers.

### 4. Irreplaceability / disintermediation risk — score 10/10
The highest score in this batch and the easiest to justify: the moat is a government
contract, not technology. The .com Registry Agreement with ICANN and the Cooperative
Agreement with the US Department of Commerce (NTIA) run through Nov 30, 2030; no customer
— not Google, not a hyperscaler — can route around VeriSign for a .com name, and no
substitute TLD carries .com's default status. Hyperscalers cannot in-source the registry;
the only threats are regulatory (price control, re-bid) or a slow shift in how people
establish online presence (apps, social profiles, AI agents answering without a domain).
The Sep 2026 antitrust suit alleging monopoly pricing (claiming ~$3.10 cost vs $10.26
wholesale price, ~232% markup, 67%+ operating margins) is the concrete form of that
regulatory risk, but it is a private suit, not a government action.

  (initial guide was 1.5–3.5%); revenue $1.755–1.760bn; operating income $1.188–1.192bn.
- Contractual price increases: .com wholesale $10.26 → **$10.97 effective Nov 1, 2026**
  (+6.9%); .net $10.91 → $12.00 effective Mar 1, 2027 (+10.0%, announced Aug 27, 2026).

  of the rest in 2028.
- .web launch: entered the root zone July 2026 after a 10-year dispute (VeriSign paid
  $135m at auction in 2016); sunrise period for trademark holders starts Dec 10, 2026,
  limited registration mid-Jan 2027, general availability Apr 6, 2027 — a new registry
  revenue stream in 2027.
- Dividend: board declared $0.81/share quarterly dividend (record Nov 17, payable
  Nov 24, 2026; ~$3.24 annualised, ~1.0% yield, 34.8% payout); company "intends to

### 6. Risks
- The renewal-rate slide (74.3% vs 75.4%) and the untested 2026 cohort: if the record

### 7. Shortage score: 2/10
**Verdict:** The best moat in the batch and the worst explosive-return setup — a 10/10
irreplaceable monopoly growing 6% at 29x earnings; own it for quality, not for a breakout.
