## Per-name theses (top 5)

**NVDA (1).** Nvidia holds about 80-85% of data-centre AI accelerator revenue (third-party estimates, Phase 3.5 — down from about 92% in 2023) with a CUDA/full-stack moat a rival must replicate whole, not in pieces. Q2 FY2027 revenue was \$96.2B and Q3 is formally guided to \$108.0B plus or minus 2% (both confirmed). The honest framing: the \$1T Blackwell+Rubin figure is GTC management visibility, NOT backlog — the filing RPO is \$3.2B because hardware ships in weeks. The trap is live and verified: TPU, Trainium, Maia and MTIA are all shipping or in production, and both earnings-quality flags stand in the 10-Q — receivables of \$63.1B with DSO 45 to 60 on extended terms, and \$7,771M of Q2 equity-securities gains, primarily unrealised.

**MU (2).** The shortage is contracted, not spot: 26 take-or-pay SCAs, about \$32B of customer commitments and \$12.75B of cash deposits actually received (10-K), with the vast majority of CY2027 HBM bit supply under agreement at higher prices. Phase 3.5's two corrections are carried here: the filing RPO is about \$134B against the \$150B call figure, and the 50-66% fulfilment figure dates to Dec 2025 and covered overall demand. FQ4 FY2026 revenue was \$54.23B with FQ1 guided to \$61.5B plus or minus \$1.5B. Micron is #3 in HBM (18% vs SK hynix 50%) — the bet is oligopoly pricing on contracted volume, not share leadership. It is the panel's single pick (see If forced to ONE).

**APH (3).** The least glamorous seat in the AI rack and the panel's biggest riser: Q2 2026 orders were a record \$10.7B, up 94% reported (+63% organic), for a 1.23 book-to-bill, with sales of \$8.8B up 55% — all confirmed from Amphenol's own release in Phase 3.5. The shortage colour (fiber lead times about 50 weeks, per-TPU connector content roughly doubling toward TPU9i) rests on a single channel source (Edgewater, Sep 2026) and is labelled as such. Amphenol is #2 in connectors behind TE Connectivity; the trap is second-source licensing at the customer's request (it is expected to license backplane IP to FIT/TE at Nvidia's request) — margin leakage, not disintermediation.

**CSCO (4).** The skeptic lens's single pick and the ranking's value case: \$9.3B of FY26 AI orders (4.5x) against only about \$4B recognised, a \$46.7B RPO, and a 19.1x multiple where ANET charges 35x for adjacent exposure. The INVENTORY_BUILD flag (+80% YoY in the dossier) is the watch item — benign only while orders outrun shipments. Trap is medium: Cisco sells Silicon One into the same hyperscaler self-build it competes with, and it is #3 in data-centre switching behind Nvidia and Arista.

**LLY (5).** The GLP-1 leader at 60.9% of US incretin prescriptions, with tirzepatide at \$27.7B in H1 (+88%) and fewer than 1 in 10 eligible patients treated — but the shortage chapter has closed (US supply available; FDA shortage listings resolved into mid-2026 per the dossier), so the return now rides volume growth against guided price declines and the Foundayo oral launch, at only +5.1% analyst upside. A priced compounder ranked on moat (8/10) more than shortage.

VRTX — the field's highest irreplaceability (9/10) with its lowest shortage (3/10); a binary PDUFA, not a demand-pull story.
