## Per-name thesis

**1. MU — Micron ($1,080.53).** The steepest real compounder: Q3 FY26 revenue +345.8% YoY, 5th straight quarterly record, 8/8 EPS beats (avg surprise +20.3%). "We're sold out for 2026" with only 50–66% of medium-term bit demand covered — and $22B of customer cash already collected as prepayments (verified). 6.8x forward is a peak-earnings discount multiple on numbers still accelerating. Near-term binary: FQ4 prints Sept 30 (4 days). In-sourcing risk LOW — one of only 3 HBM makers on earth; no hyperscaler builds DRAM. The 30/45/25 probabilities reflect memory's base rate of a bust, but the prepayments are the mechanism that has muted the flywheel this cycle. Trap caveat: boom-bust; >$25B capex becomes oversupply if AI demand pauses.

**2. MSFT — Microsoft ($497.93).** The most mispriced shortage: $678B commercial RPO (+84% YoY, +$53B in ONE quarter), ~30% converting within 12 months — contracted demand growing ~2x faster than revenue — while Nadella confirms demand exceeds physical capacity ("I don't have warm shells to plug into"; note: quote is from the Nov 2025 BG2Pod podcast, corroborated by earnings-call substance). Yet it is the only megacap researched with a negative 12-month return (−1.4%) and trades at 21.0x forward — the cheapest megacap multiple — because the market fights over FY2027 capex instead of the backlog it funds. Disintermediation runs the other way: Microsoft IS the in-sourcer (Maia, Anthropic custom silicon). Caveat: the verifier found the FY2027 capex case is thinner than the dossier claimed — official guidance is ~$175B (CFO Hood, Jul 29 call), not $255–260B; the larger figure is secondary commentary only. EV upside +12.4% sits below the +15% guardrail — it stays ranked, but this is not an actionable single pick on this lens's own terms.

**3. KLAC — KLA ($187.11).** The nearest thing to a monopoly in the AI supply chain: ~75–80% of patterned-wafer inspection, ~80%+ of reticle inspection, and the moat that nobody can copy — ~25 years of accumulated cross-customer defect-classification data (newcomers can copy optics but not the algorithmic substrate; 24% recurring services revenue). Backlog just short of $13B in the Aug 5, 2026 10-K and management expects it to keep growing (Sept 2026 Citi TMT). The AI memory shortage directly expands KLAC's content per wafer: HBM4 16-Hi needs ~3x the inspection intensity of standard DRAM; advanced-packaging process-control revenue ~$1.1B CY2026 (+70% YoY, share 2%→7–8%). The market sold it ~38% off its July $307 peak while the monopoly didn't move — fear priced into a moat. No disintermediation case exists: fabs literally cannot run without inspection; no hyperscaler competes with KLAC. Irreplaceability 10/10, the highest in the field. The panel's conviction pick (also the strict trap-veto outcome — see below).

**4. CAT — Caterpillar ($805.25).** The non-AI-magic-shortage entrant with the hardest backlog in industrials: $72B (+92% YoY), orders stretching to 2030, genset sales +72% — six ≥1 GW prime-power deals (ProPower 2.1 GW). Power delivery is the AI buildout's binding constraint and CAT sells the machines that make power. #1 globally in construction machinery with dealer lock-in. Caveats: 24.9x forward for a cyclical at ~21% analyst upside; datacenter regulation (permitting, emissions on prime power) is the non-cycle risk.

**5. SNDK — SanDisk ($1,777.80).** The purest NAND super-cycle: ≥$93.9B in expected revenue + $16.5B financial guarantees under >4-year framework agreements covering >50% of FY2027 and ~two-thirds of FY2028 bits; bits "allocated beyond calendar 2027"; Citi estimates NAND +186% YoY in 2026. FQ4 FY26 printed $8.97B revenue (+371% YoY) at 84.6% GM — but FQ1 FY27 guided $10.3–10.8B vs $11.16B consensus, and TrendForce sees price-hike deceleration (70–75% → 10–15% QoQ). TRAP FLAG: +1,680% in 12 months, NAND is a commodity at wafer level (moat = pricing power, not lock-in), YMTC just overtook SanDisk to #3 (14%) and CXMT is entering NAND — the 2027 supply wave is real.

**6. It is the WFE supercycle's derived-demand play, not the shortage good itself — which is why the supply-chain lens ranked it 13th. Flank: 73% of revenue from Taiwan/China/Korea (export-curb and geography risk).
