## Per-name thesis

**1. MU — Micron Technology.** HBM is sold out through 2026 and the vast majority of calendar-2027 supply is already contracted at significantly higher prices, with >75% of FY2027 output committed and 26 strategic customer agreements worth ~$150B in remaining performance obligations. Only three firms on earth make HBM at scale, and no hyperscaler can in-source a memory fab. FQ1 2027 guide ($61.5B revenue, $38.15 adj EPS) sits above Street. The risk is the cycle, not the demand: memory has drawn down 50%+ peak-to-trough before, and one lead customer concentrates the HBM book. *Trap flag: none on disintermediation — low risk; the trap here is cyclicality.*

**2. APH — Amphenol.** Record Q3 orders of $12.17B (+99% YoY), book-to-bill 1.19:1, backlog +$5.7B year to date; IT datacom (+117% YoY, ~75% AI) is now 48% of sales. Architecture-agnostic — it wins whichever accelerator wins — and the stock is only +11% over 12 months at 21.6x forward earnings with +36.7% analyst upside, the least-priced AI infrastructure name in the field. *Trap flag: medium — connectors are a component; hyperscalers can dual-source, though they cannot in-source thousands of qualified SKUs.*

**3. GE — GE Aerospace.** The largest hard backlog in the field: commercial services backlog above $170B, Q3 orders +49% to ~$19B, LEAP deliveries +26% year to date and the installed base set to more than double by 2030. Engines are certified, safety-critical, and sole-sourced per airframe — a moat no customer can route around. Growth is steadier than explosive (low-double-digit services growth guided for 2027), and its EV upside (+12.6%) sits below the +15% guardrail — flagged, not excluded. *Trap flag: none — no customer builds its own jet engine.*

**4. WST — West Pharmaceutical.** The cleanest "sell shovels into the shortage" name after MU/APH: HVP components +20% organically, GLP-1-related HVP growing well into the 30s% and ~12% of revenue, 200+ new upgrade projects this year. Components are specified into drug filings, so switching means regulatory re-validation — a genuine qualification lock-in. The multiple is rich (40.1x forward) and West Vantage is in a trough. *Trap flag: low.*

**5. ANET — Arista Networks.** The fastest compounder in the field: Q3 revenue +58.5% YoY (fastest since IPO), FY2026 raised a fourth time to ~$13.3B (+48%), first 2027 look ~$18.5B with AI fabrics doubling to ~$7B. The moat is software (EOS), not fabs, and scale-up Ethernet revenue is immaterial until 2028 — Nvidia keeps the rack-scale interconnect. *Trap flag: medium — hyperscaler in-sourcing and InfiniBand are live threats.*

**6. NEM — Newmont.** A gold-price lever with the best costs among majors (AISC $1,454/oz vs ~$1,930 industry average in Q2 2026), record Q3 adj earnings ($2.20/share), and a gold floor near $4,000/oz. But production fell 8% YoY and costs are rising industry-wide; the thesis is commodity beta, not a backlog. *Trap flag: none on disintermediation — the trap is gold mean-reversion.*

**7. LLY — Eli Lilly.** The dominant GLP-1 franchise (Mounjaro $10.7B +65%, Zepbound $5.35B +49% in Q3; ~66% of revenue), but the shortage phase is ending and price is the new battleground (realized price −15% on volume +53%). Analyst upside is only +1.4% — the thesis is fully priced. *Trap flag: medium — oral competitors, compounding, and price erosion.*

**8. MSI — Motorola Solutions.** The most defensible backlog/moat combination after GE: record $16.6B backlog (+14% YoY), P25 networks governments cannot rip out, Silvus on pace for >$900M. But growth is ~12% — steady, not explosive — and analyst upside is only +3.0%. *Trap flag: none.*

**9. CF — CF Industries.** A genuine, geopolitics-driven nitrogen shortage: ~9M tons of urea supply lost since Feb 2026, >35% of European ammonia capacity curtailed, order book into Q1 2027. But it is a commodity cycle — Middle East normalization could restore supply in 4–6 months. *Trap flag: none on disintermediation — the trap is the cycle turning.*

**10. DVN — Devon Energy.** Sharpening into a Permian pure-play: Eagle Ford sale ($4.2B) funding buybacks accelerating past $1B in Q4, Delaware Basin cost leadership (sub-$800/ft D&C). Still an oil-price lever at heart. *Trap flag: none on disintermediation — the trap is oil price beta.*
