# Final pick — momentum — 2026-10-30

## THE PICK

**APH — Amphenol Corporation, Information Technology / Electronic Components, market cap 202.4B USD.**

## One-paragraph thesis

Amphenol is the rare momentum name whose explosive case is already booked rather than hoped for: Q3 2026 orders hit a record 12.17B USD, up 99% year over year, at a 1.19x book-to-bill on 10.23B USD of sales, with AI now about three-quarters of IT Datacom revenue and fiber lead times extending to about 50 weeks as hyperscalers secure capacity years ahead. The shortage here is documented in orders, not asserted in narrative — customers are asking Amphenol to source Corning-spec-equivalent fiber because they cannot get enough directly from Corning, which is rationing behaviour, not just strong demand. At 82.08 USD the stock sits 7.4% below its 52-week high and below the 105.00 USD analyst mean target, so the order-book conversion story still has room to surprise even after a strong run.

## The shortage + backlog evidence

- Q3 2026 orders (bookings): 12.17B USD, plus 99% YoY and plus 13% sequentially, a record; book-to-bill 1.19:1, positive in all but one end market (company 8-K Ex. 99.1, 2026-10-28; verified Phase 3.5).
- IT Datacom organic sales plus 77% YoY in Q3 2026; AI about three-quarters of IT Datacom revenue, up from about half in 2025 (verified Phase 3.5).
- Channel evidence (Edgewater Research via Passive Components, Sep 2026): connector supply tightening incrementally, lead times extending, CCS/Amphenol fiber lead times extending to about 50 weeks; customers coming to Amphenol asking it to source Corning-spec-equivalent fiber because they cannot get enough supply directly from Corning (verified Phase 3.5 as a documented channel report).
- Content-per-system rising, not just volume: TPU9i cabled backplane expected to drive connector TAM to over 750 USD/TPU vs 300-350 USD for TPU7/8 (Edgewater).
- Guidance raised with the print: Q4 2026 sales 10.3-10.5B USD and adjusted EPS 0.81-0.83 USD; FY2026 sales 36.9-37.1B USD, adjusted EPS 2.82-2.84 USD; CommScope FY sales outlook raised to 5.1B USD from 4.6B USD.
- Exact backlog duration in months is not disclosed — the company discloses quarterly bookings flow and book-to-bill, not a backlog stock or horizon. The 1.19 book-to-bill plus about 50-week fiber lead times imply visibility well beyond one quarter, but no longer-dated horizon is asserted here.

## Why it's the category leader

Number 2 globally by connector sales and closing on number 1 — arguably number 1 in the AI-relevant high-speed/fiber niche. Bishop and Associates (2024 world sales): TE Connectivity number 1 at 12.88B USD, Amphenol number 2 at 11.51B USD (plus 18.7% YoY vs TE plus 1.1%), Molex number 3 at 4.87B USD — Amphenol is about 2.4x number 3 (verified Phase 3.5). Scale after M and A: 2026 sales guidance 36.9-37.1B USD. Note the Bishop ranking predates CommScope consolidation in Amphenol's 2025 sales, so the gap to TE has likely narrowed further since.

## Why it's irreplaceable

The moat is breadth plus qualification lock-in, not a single patent wall — and that distinction matters for the trap test. Amphenol sells tens of thousands of SKUs across 7 end markets; high-speed/fiber/power interconnects are designed into customer systems and qualified over long cycles (signal integrity, density, thermal, harsh-environment specs), and hyperscalers qualify to performance spec with multi-sourcing — which sounds replaceable, but in practice the qualified supplier base for 448G/backplane/fiber at AI scale is tiny (Amphenol, TE, Molex, Corning for fiber, a few others), and lead times plus long-term agreements lock capacity years ahead. The honest disintermediation caveat: hyperscalers do not generally manufacture connectors themselves, but they dual-source aggressively and can shift share between Amphenol/TE/Molex/Foxconn/Luxshare on price, and Google's TPU backplane work shows the customer owns the architecture even where Amphenol is lead designer. Irreplaceability score in the dossier: 6/10 — critical and hard to qualify away quickly, but multi-sourced by design. That is share-loss risk, not bypass risk: no credible threat was found of a customer building its own connectors at scale, which is why the trap filter does not veto this pick even though it caps the moat score.
