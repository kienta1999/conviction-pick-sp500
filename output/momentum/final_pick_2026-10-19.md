## Thesis

Micron is the only name in the field where extreme shortage, a hard contracted backlog, and fab-level irreplaceability coincide. HBM is sold out for CY2026 with the vast majority of CY2027 already agreed at significantly higher prices, Micron can satisfy only 50-66% of key customers' HBM demand, and a 150B take-or-pay RPO floors revenue through the window in which a normal memory cycle would have rolled over. Micron won the weighted single-pick vote (4 points — top pick of both the shortage and growth lenses) against a genuinely split panel: the moat lens picked GOOGL, the skeptic picked SNDK, and the skeptic ranked Micron only 4th on peak-cycle grounds. What carries the adjudication is that Micron's evidence is the only top-tier evidence that is contracted rather than narrated, and the trap cannot touch it the way it touches NVDA or ANET: its customers prepay it rather than build its product. Today it is also Borda #1 and the cross-run consensus #1 — all three orderings agree, which neither of the previous two runs achieved.

## The shortage + backlog evidence

- 150B remaining performance obligations across 26 take-or-pay strategic customer agreements, disclosed on the 30 Sep 2026 FQ4 call, with 32B in customer financial commitments (Phase 3.5 clarification: about 12.7B of deposits actually received to date — 10-K filed 2026-10-09 shows 12.75B received, plus 7B of letters of credit — phased as capacity delivers); the agreements cover over 35% of revenue through 2030, at minimum pricing where a pricing framework is defined. Verified in Phase 3.5.
- 2026 HBM sold out and the vast majority of 2027 HBM agreed at significant price increases; management could satisfy only about 50-66% of key customers' HBM demand (figure dated by Phase 3.5 to March 2026 FQ2 remarks, still the operative framing).
- FQ4 FY26 revenue 54.23B, FQ1 FY27 guided to 61.5B plus or minus 1.5B (non-GAAP gross margin ~86.25%, non-GAAP EPS 38.15 plus or minus 1.00); TTM revenue growth +256.3% on the screen; TrendForce still projects Q4 2026 DRAM contract prices +10-15% quarter-on-quarter (+15-20% including HBM).

## Why it is the category leader

Micron is #3 in the DRAM/HBM oligopoly with SK hynix and Samsung — the only three firms that perform HBM stacking at scale — and it is the only US-based HBM maker. Category leadership here is oligopoly membership plus purity of exposure, not the #1 share slot; the honest caveat is intra-oligopoly share competition from those two rivals (Samsung's HBM share recovered from 21% in Q1 to 33% in Q2 2026 in the dossier) and China's CXMT rising in conventional DRAM.

## Why it is irreplaceable

Only about 3 firms on earth make HBM at scale. Fabs cost tens of billions, qualification and yield know-how are multi-year, and no hyperscaler operates a memory fab — instead customers committed 32B to secure Micron's output, with about 12.7B of deposits already received. Irreplaceability score 8/10 in the dossier (docked because SK hynix and Samsung are genuine substitutes for each other, and Nvidia's common TSMC base-die standard for custom HBM, reported 2026-10-18, standardises one layer across all three suppliers). Phase 3.5 note: the prepay behaviour is CONFIRMED; "rather than in-source" as a stated customer motive is an inference from that structure, not a sourced quote. The real risk is not disintermediation; it is share loss within the oligopoly and the cycle itself, both carried in the bear case.
