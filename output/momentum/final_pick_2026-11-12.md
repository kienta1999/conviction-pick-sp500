# Momentum single pick — 2026-11-12

## THE PICK

**MU — Micron Technology, Inc.** — Information Technology / Semiconductors. Market cap ~\$820.4bn. Screen price \$811.30 (2026-11-11 close), analyst mean target \$1,561.91 (+92.5%), forward PE 3.9x. Next earnings 2026-12-23 (**estimated date, not company-confirmed** — see verification).

## One-paragraph thesis

Micron is the only name in this field where the shortage is both physical and already contracted in cash: HBM is sold out through 2026 with most of 2027 committed, customers have handed Micron \$32bn in prepayments on a contract book that reached ~\$150bn of remaining performance obligations at 30 Sep 2026, and DRAM prices have risen ~400% since the start of 2024 while shipments grew only ~4–20% — this is a price spike on rationed supply, not a volume boom. Supply cannot respond before 2028 at the earliest (Idaho fab ~mid-2027, New York first wafer 2030), so the constraint binds through the whole 12–18 month window. At \$811.30, 33.1% below its 52-week high, with a 3.9x forward PE and +92.5% analyst upside, the market is pricing a short cycle; if the shortage lasts into 2027–2028 as management and SK Hynix indicate, earnings power is far above what that multiple implies.

## The shortage + backlog evidence

- **HBM sold out through 2026, most of 2027 committed** (Q4 FY26 call, 30 Sep 2026); Micron can satisfy only 50–66% of key customer HBM demand — demand is rationed, not chased.
- **\$32bn of customer cash prepayments** on long-term supply contracts, with ~\$150bn RPO at 30 Sep 2026 (the ~\$100bn figure in the dossier was the stale June disclosure — corrected by verification) and 26 strategic agreements covering >35% of revenue through 2030.
- **DRAM prices +400% since 2024** (J.P. Morgan, via dossier): a 16Gb DDR5 chip went from \$8.70 (Oct 2025) to \$48 (Sep 2026).
- **Q4 FY26 revenue \$54.23bn, +379% YoY; FY26 revenue \$133.19bn, +256% YoY** (30 Sep 2026). Nuance: the Q4 comparison is a 14-week quarter vs a 13-week year-ago quarter, so the growth rate is flattered by the extra week. FQ1 FY27 guided to ~\$61.5bn revenue with adj. EPS ~\$38.15, both above consensus, with sequential growth guided every quarter of FY27.
- **Screen:** rev growth +135.6% TTM, op margin 80.7%, ROE 88.3%, net debt/EBITDA -0.35x, +220.7% in 12m, 4 EPS beats in 4 quarters (+20.1% avg surprise).

## Why it's the category leader

Micron is one of the three firms that together hold ~90% of DRAM revenue (Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron), and in HBM — the AI memory that matters — it is one of only ~3 qualified suppliers. It is the #1-ranked name in today's 50-name screen (composite 0.972, the highest in the field by a wide margin) and the only new entrant to the screen at #1. No other name in the field combines triple-digit revenue growth, repeated guidance raises, and a 3.9x forward PE.

## Why it's irreplaceable

Hyperscalers cannot make DRAM themselves: it requires tens of billions in fabs, a process-tech lead (1-gamma node), and per-platform Nvidia qualification for HBM, and there is no substitute for DRAM/HBM in AI servers (memory is ~29% of a Vera Rubin VR200 system's bill of materials). The moat is a highly concentrated oligopoly, **not** a literal triopoly — verification corrected the dossier's wording: China's CXMT is a fourth producer at scale with ~9.5% DRAM revenue share (TrendForce Q2 2026) and is the long-run margin threat. That is a cycle risk, not an in-sourcing risk: no customer is building its own DRAM fab. Panelist disintermediation ratings: A low, B medium, C low. The trap does **not** veto this pick.

## Key swing factors

1. **Calendar-2027 HBM contract repricing** — the falsifiable test of duration: if 2027 prices hold or rise, the cycle is contracted, not hoped for.
2. **DRAM spot prices and the double-ordering signal** — shipments up ~4% vs prices up 180–400% is the classic panic-buying footprint; a spot-price rollover precedes a glut.
3. **Fab supply timing** — Idaho ~mid-2027, New York first wafer 2030; any acceleration pulls the glut forward.
4. **CXMT share growth** — the fourth producer at 9.5% is the long-run margin threat; >15% would change the oligopoly math.
5. **Hyperscaler capex durability** — the demand side is concentrated in a handful of buyers funding the prepayments.

## Key risks

- **Memory cyclicality (the dominant risk):** prices +400% on shipments +~4% is the classic top-of-cycle footprint; double-ordering could flip shortage to glut when fabs land in 2028–2030.
- **Customer concentration:** a handful of hyperscalers fund the prepayments; a capex pause hits both price and volume.
- **CXMT:** the fourth DRAM producer at 9.5% share is the long-run margin threat if its capacity growth accelerates.
- **Valuation already pricing it in (the contrarian lens's objection):** +220.7% in 12 months is the most crowded shortage in the market; Panelist D demoted MU to #7 on exactly this ground.
- **HBM gross margin (83%) trails ordinary DRAM/NAND (~90%)** in Q4 FY26, so mix shift dilutes margin until HBM prices rise.
