## Top-3 theses

**1. MSFT — Microsoft (\$484.52).** The cleanest shortage signal in the field, and the single pick — see `final_pick_2026-11-22.md`. Commercial RPO \$677B (+73% YoY) with ~\$203B to be recognised in 12 months, Azure +48% against demand that "continues to exceed available supply" (Q1 FY27 call, 28 Oct 2026), and ~\$170B of calendar-2026 capex to close the gap. The moat is the enterprise stack plus an 80+ region footprint plus the exclusive OpenAI partnership — but the trap is live and runs from the customer side: OpenAI (11% of revenue) is building its own compute (Stargate) and multi-clouding. Shares are flat over 12 months while the backlog grew 73%, so the shortage is not being chased at a high.

**2. MSI — Motorola Solutions (\$520.21).** The strongest verified order momentum in the field: record backlog \$16.6B (+14% YoY, ~1.3 years of sales), Q3 orders up over 20% (25% per the CFO, total orders, with record orders in all three technologies), record Q3 sales \$3.38B (+12%) and non-GAAP EPS \$4.69 (+16%, beat), guidance raised again. The moat is the best in the field (irreplaceability 9/10): switching a P25 system means replacing every handset, vehicle unit and base station at once on 15–20-year cycles, and governments cannot in-source radio infrastructure. Caveats: the ">80% of the US public-safety LMR market" claim is a Morningstar analyst estimate of uncertain vintage — recent estimates say 60%+ for North American LMR — and the "shortage" is a procurement-driven order surge, not supply scarcity, so upside is durable compounding rather than an explosive squeeze. Leverage from the \$4.4B Silvus deal (long-term debt \$9.36B) is the balance-sheet watch item. Its EV upside (+12.5%) sits below the +15% guardrail — flagged in the scenarios below.

**3. WST — West Pharmaceutical (\$390.30).** The GLP-1 tollbooth: HVP components \$466.7m in Q3 2026, +20.3% organically and 53% of sales, GLP-1 category organic growth well into the 30s, HVP for GLP-1 just under 12% of total revenue, and 200+ EU Annex 1 upgrade projects added year to date. The #1 position in injectable containment is protected by qualification/regulatory lock-in — components are qualified into a drug programme years before launch. The constraint is the 18–24-month capacity lag, with new finishing capacity planned for late 2028. Caveats: at 37.6x forward earnings the story is already paid for (+51.8% in 12 months), and its EV upside (+13.7%) sits below the +15% guardrail — flagged in the scenarios below. Flag note: the screen raised RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN for WST; the research verdict is benign-to-watch (working-capital timing on genuine HVP volume, 9M free cash flow \$278m, −5% on higher receivables/inventory).

**4. LLY — Eli Lilly (\$1347.11).** The fastest compounder in the field: Q3 2026 revenue \$24.4B (+39% YoY) on +53% volume, Mounjaro + Zepbound over \$16B in the quarter, guidance raised \$5.5B to \$91–92B, and only ~1 in 10 eligible US patients on an incretin. The ceiling is price: realised prices fell 15% in Q3 and most 2027 access gains come from lower-priced Medicare/Medicaid channels; the shortage is maturing into a price war, and Novo's semaglutide is an interchangeable-in-practice substitute. Retatrutide (BLA planned Q1 2027, up to 25.0% weight loss) is the pipeline kicker.

**5. The ceiling is time: core TAVR growth is high-single-digits and moderate-AS revenue depends on guideline/coverage decisions years away.
