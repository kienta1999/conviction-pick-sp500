# Momentum final pick — 2026-10-15

## THE PICK: MU — Micron Technology

Sector/sub-industry: Information Technology / Semiconductors. Current price 953.19; market cap ~1078B.

## Thesis

Micron is the only name in the field where extreme shortage, a hard contracted backlog, and fab-level irreplaceability coincide. HBM is sold out for CY2026 with the vast majority of CY2027 already agreed at significantly higher prices, management sees 2027 and 2028 tighter than 2026 with no meaningful new cleanroom output until after late 2028, and a ~150B take-or-pay RPO floors revenue through the window in which a normal memory cycle would have rolled over. The panel split on doctrine — the moat lenses preferred KLAC — but Micron is Borda #1, took two of four top picks, and the trap cannot touch it the way it touches NVDA or ANET: its customers prepay it rather than build its product.

## The shortage + backlog evidence

- ~150B RPO across 26 take-or-pay Strategic Customer Agreements, disclosed 30 Sep 2026, exceeding FY26 revenue of 133.2B; 32B in customer financial commitments, mostly cash deposits. Verified nuance: the 150B is counted at minimum pricing on the contracts with a defined pricing framework (~3/4 of SCA revenue); all 26 are take-or-pay on volume.
- Over 75% of calendar-2027 output already committed; 2026 HBM sold out (per the Dec 2025 disclosure) and the vast majority of 2027 HBM agreed at significant price increases; management could satisfy only ~50-66% of key customers' HBM demand.
- FQ4 FY26 revenue 54.23B (+379% YoY, +31% QoQ), FQ1 FY27 guided to 61.5B ± 1.5B at ~86% gross margin; TTM revenue growth +256.3% on the screen.

## Why it is the category leader

Micron is #3 in DRAM (~22-24%) and HBM (~18% share in Q2 2026, down from 21% in Q1 — Samsung is gaining, which is the honest intra-oligopoly caveat) in a 3-firm oligopoly with SK hynix and Samsung that together control ~90% or more of supply, and it is the only US-based HBM maker. Category leadership here is oligopoly membership plus purity of exposure, not the #1 share slot.

## Why it is irreplaceable

Only ~3 firms on earth make HBM/leading-edge DRAM at scale. Fabs cost tens of billions (Micron FY27 capex is guided above 50B), qualification and yield know-how are multi-year, and no hyperscaler has realistically attempted to in-source memory — instead they committed 32B to secure Micron's output. Irreplaceability score 8/10. The real risk is not disintermediation; it is share loss within the oligopoly and the cycle itself, both carried in the bear case.
