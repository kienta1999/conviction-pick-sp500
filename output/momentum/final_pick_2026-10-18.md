## Thesis

Micron is the only name in the field where extreme shortage, a hard contracted backlog, and fab-level irreplaceability coincide. HBM is sold out for CY2026 with the large majority of CY2027 already agreed at significantly higher prices, Micron can satisfy only 50-66% of key customers' HBM demand, and a $150B take-or-pay RPO floors revenue through the window in which a normal memory cycle would have rolled over. Micron won the weighted single-pick vote (4 points — top pick of both the shortage and growth lenses) against a genuinely split panel: the moat lens picked NVDA, the skeptic picked GOOGL, and the skeptic ranked Micron only 9th on cycle history. It is also the cross-run consensus #2.

## The shortage + backlog evidence

- $150B remaining performance obligations across 26 take-or-pay strategic customer agreements, disclosed on the 30 Sep 2026 FQ4 call, with $32B in customer financial commitments (Phase 3.5 clarification: about $12.7B of deposits actually received to date, phased as capacity delivers); the agreements cover over 35% of revenue through 2030, at minimum pricing where a pricing framework is defined. - FQ4 FY26 revenue $54.23B, FQ1 FY27 guided to $61.5B ± $1.5B (non-GAAP gross margin ~86.25%, non-GAAP EPS $38.15 ± $1.00); TTM revenue growth +256.3% on the screen.

## Why it is the category leader

Micron is #3 in the DRAM/HBM oligopoly with SK hynix and Samsung — the only three firms that perform HBM stacking at scale — and it is the only US-based HBM maker. Category leadership here is oligopoly membership plus purity of exposure, not the #1 share slot; the honest caveat is intra-oligopoly share competition from those two rivals and China's CXMT rising in conventional DRAM.

## Why it is irreplaceable

Only about 3 firms on earth make HBM at scale. Fabs cost tens of billions, qualification and yield know-how are multi-year, and no hyperscaler operates a memory fab — instead customers committed $32B to secure Micron's output, with about $12.7B of deposits already received. Irreplaceability score 7/10 in the dossier (docked because SK hynix and Samsung are genuine substitutes for each other). Phase 3.5 note: the prepay behaviour is CONFIRMED; "rather than in-source" as a stated customer motive is an inference from that structure, not a sourced quote. The real risk is not disintermediation; it is share loss within the oligopoly and the cycle itself, both carried in the bear case.
