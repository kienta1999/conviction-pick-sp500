## THE PICK

**MU — Micron Technology**, Information Technology / Semiconductors, market cap ~$1.20T.

**One-paragraph thesis:** Memory is in the worst shortage in 15 years and Micron is one of three firms on earth that can make HBM — the only one headquartered in the US. Customers have prepaid $32B in cash deposits (up $10B in a single quarter), HBM is sold out through 2027, and management just guided 2027–2028 tighter than 2026 for the first time. The stock trades at ~5.1x forward earnings against a 44% analyst upside because the market still prices it as a commodity cyclical, while the contracted order book says the cycle has years of visibility. This is a structural shortage, not a cyclical blip.

## The shortage + backlog evidence

- ~$32B in strategic customer agreement commitments, the vast majority as balance-sheet cash deposits — up from ~$22B one quarter earlier (FQ4 FY2026 call, Sep 30, 2026; verified).
- HBM sold out through fiscal 2027; management guided supply tighter through 2027–2028 — the first-ever 2028 tightness callout (verified).
- DRAM share ~25%, ~1pt behind SK Hynix; NAND bits on allocation into 2027+.

## Why it's the category leader

One of three HBM producers globally (Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron), the only US-based maker. ~$150B of contracted revenue/RPO context from 26 take-or-pay agreements per the FQ4 call.

## Why it's irreplaceable

A hyperscaler can in-source a chip design far more easily than a memory fab: HBM requires process-tech leads, extreme capital intensity, and qualification lock-in that only ~3 firms on earth possess. Irreplaceability 9/10. No in-source path exists.
