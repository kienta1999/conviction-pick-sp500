## One-paragraph thesis

Lilly is the only name in the field where demand has exceeded supply since late 2023 and the constraint is literally how much it can manufacture, not how much it can sell: Q3 2026 revenue was \$24.4bn, +39% YoY, with volume +53% YoY while realised prices fell 15%, and FY2026 guidance has been raised three times to \$91–92bn. Mounjaro (\$10.72bn, +65% YoY) and Zepbound (\$5.35bn, +49%) were two-thirds of the quarter, and the market is projected to exceed \$130bn by 2030 against a treated population that is still small — verification corrected the dossier's penetration numbers (Lilly CEO David Ricks, Reuters Apr 2026: ~1 in 10 overweight/obese Americans use GLP-1s, ~21–22m of ~500m global candidates treated, ~4%) — so the low-penetration thesis survives even at the corrected, higher levels. At \$1,347.11, -6.7% off its 52-week high and +20.8% above its 200-day SMA, with TTM revenue growth of 37.2% and forward PE 26.48, the momentum and the shortage point the same way.

## The shortage + backlog evidence

The single most compelling data point is that volume rose 53% YoY in Q3 2026 while realised prices fell 15% — demand is so far ahead of supply that Lilly sells everything it can make and still cannot meet it. The capacity bridge is concrete: \$18bn+ committed since 2020 (verification corrected the dossier's "since 2023" — Lilly PR, 24 May 2024; total US commitments since 2020 now exceed \$50bn), including the Lebanon, Indiana site at ~\$9bn, with Lebanon API production starting late 2026 and scaling through 2028, supporting \$45–50bn of incretin revenue in 2026 and above \$60bn in 2027. New GLP-1 plants take 3–4 years from groundbreaking to commercial production, so the supply governor — and the pricing power that comes with scarcity — does not resolve quickly. Pharma does not report an order backlog; the proxy is the capacity ramp itself, and the dossier's summary stands: "Lilly's revenue trajectory through 2027 is largely a function of capacity, not demand."

## Why it's the category leader

Lilly is the co-leader of the GLP-1 market with Novo Nordisk, and the only one whose incretin franchise is still supply-constrained rather than demand-constrained: Mounjaro and Zepbound are the volume engine, retatrutide beat Ozempic head-to-head, and the oral orforglipron (Foundayo) extends the franchise beyond injectables. Q3 2026 was the third guidance raise of the year. The competitive threat is real — Novo's oral Wegovy pill launched in the US on 5 Jan 2026 with 265k+ weekly prescriptions by Jul 2026, and compounding pharmacies persist — but Lilly's capacity build is the larger of the two, and its pipeline (retatrutide filing planned Q1 2027) is the deeper one.

## Why it's irreplaceable

The moat is patents on tirzepatide/retatrutide/orforglipron plus sterile fill-finish and synthetic-peptide API capacity that takes 3–4 years and billions to build, FDA-qualified site by site. No customer can in-source this: patients and payers do not manufacture drugs. The threat is competitor supply (Novo, compounding), not customer in-sourcing — dossier irreplaceability 8/10. The honest limit: it is a two-player race, not a monopoly, and patent cliffs arrive in the 2030s.
