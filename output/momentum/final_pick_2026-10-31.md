## One-paragraph thesis

Micron is the purest shortage in the dossier: AI demand for HBM is
simultaneously starving conventional DRAM, so the constraint is structural
rather than one quarter's ordering spike. HBM is sold out, more than 75% of
expected 2027 output is already committed, and no large new volume arrives
until the Idaho fab scales — so pricing power sits with a three-firm oligopoly
rather than with buyers. The explosive case is not a new product cycle but a
multi-year period in which Micron fills only a fraction of demand at
triple-digit price increases, and no customer can in-source its way out of
that constraint.

## The shortage + backlog evidence

- HBM sold out for calendar 2026 and nearly all of calendar 2027, at
  significantly higher prices; more than 75% of expected 2027 output already
  committed (fiscal Q4 2026 call, 30 September 2026).
- Fiscal Q4 2026 revenue rose 379% year over year with a beat-and-raise Q1
  FY27 guide, and management said DRAM and NAND would remain supply-constrained
  in both 2027 and 2028 with no line of sight to balance.
- 26 long-term agreements representing \$32B in customer financial
  commitments, up from \$22B.
- Verified correction (see "What was verified"): the "fill only about half
  to two-thirds of core customer orders" quote is genuine but dates from the
  17 December 2025 fiscal Q1 2026 call (repeated March 2026), not the
  September 2026 call — the Q4 call's verified statement is that supply will
  be much tighter in fiscal 2027 and 2028 than in 2026. The duration claim
  stands on the Q4 statement; the older quote is not used as Q4 evidence.

## Why it's the category leader

Micron is one of only three DRAM makers on earth (with SK hynix and Samsung),
and the #3 in HBM (~18% share vs SK hynix ~50%) inside a market where all
three are sold out. Leadership here is not share dominance — it is membership
in an oligopoly that cannot be expanded quickly: a new fab takes years and
tens of billions, and Micron spent \$27.37B in FY26 capex to stay in the race.

## Why it's irreplaceable

No hyperscaler can realistically build a DRAM/HBM fab — the process-tech lead,
capital intensity, and qualification lock-in are prohibitive, and there is no
substitute technology that routes around DRAM for AI training memory. The
honest discount is within-oligopoly share loss: SK hynix is Nvidia's primary
HBM partner and Samsung gained HBM share rapidly in one quarter, so Micron can
lose share even while the category stays sold out. That is a share risk, not
the doctrine's disintermediation trap — the trap (a customer becoming the
supplier) cannot fire here. Irreplaceability score 8/10, shortage score 10/10
(research batches 1).

## Key swing factors

1. Duration of the shortage into 2028 (Idaho fab timing, competitor ramps).
2. Conventional DRAM/NAND pricing — most revenue is not HBM, so a pricing
   rollover hits before any HBM slowdown shows.
3. HBM share vs SK hynix and Samsung.
4. Hyperscaler capex continuity (a capex pause unwinds the order book first).
5. Gross-margin trajectory — peaking quarter over quarter is the earliest
   quantitative tell.

## Holding period and exit plan

Recommended hold horizon 12–18 months, tied to the shortage timeline: base
target \$1300 by ~2027-Q4; bull target \$1550 by ~2027-Q4 if the shortage
extends and price/mix keeps expanding; exit if the price hits the downside
trigger \$720 or any thesis-break trigger fires. Thesis-break exit triggers

pricing rolling over, gross margins peaking quarter over quarter, a major
customer shifting HBM volume decisively to SK hynix or Samsung, or a

cyclical that can draw down 50%+ fast — brutal with margin — and this system's
policy is cash-only; nothing here recommends any leverage multiple or
position size.

## Key risks and what would invalidate the thesis

Cyclical price reversal (the dominant risk — most revenue is conventional
memory, not HBM); HBM share loss inside the oligopoly; a class-action overhang
alleging coordinated production shifts toward HBM; customer concentration in
a handful of hyperscalers; and valuation — the stock is up 321% in 12 months,
so a good quarter that merely meets expectations can still sell off. The
thesis breaks if pricing rolls over while volume is still constrained — that
combination means demand, not supply, gave way.
