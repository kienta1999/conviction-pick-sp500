## One-paragraph thesis

Micron is the memory-cycler that stopped cycling: it converted a structural HBM shortage into ~$150B of take-or-pay remaining performance obligations backed by $32B in customer cash commitments, so its earnings now resemble a contracted offtake utility while the market still prices it at ~5x forward earnings like a spot commodity player. Demand for HBM runs 50–67% above supply, the product is sold out through 2026, and no new cleanroom output arrives until 2027–2028 — giving Micron multi-year pricing power that converts directly into margin expansion. This is the panel's model name for the doctrine: a profitable category leader riding a shortage the world cannot supply fast enough, with a product its customers cannot build themselves.

## The shortage + backlog evidence (doctrine)

- **~$150B in remaining performance obligations, all take-or-pay**, backed by **~$32B in customer financial commitments (mostly cash deposits)** across 26 agreements — company-described as "more than sold out" (CONFIRMED, Sept 30, 2026 FQ4 report).
- **HBM demand gap of 50–67%**; HBM sold out through 2026; shortage consensus extends into 2028+ (CONFIRMED).
- **Capacity wall:** no new cleanroom output until Idaho ID1 initial output mid-2027, with the larger new-wafer capacity landing late 2028 (CONFIRMED with nuance — the panel's "no new cleanroom until late 2028" was the headline; Idaho starts mid-2027 but does not relieve the shortage).
- FQ4 FY2026 (reported Sept 30, 2026): record **$54.23B revenue** and **$33.42 non-GAAP EPS**, with FQ1'27 guided at **$61.5B ± $1.5B revenue / $38.15 ± $1.00 EPS** — the acceleration is still accelerating (CONFIRMED).

## Why it's the category leader

Micron is one of exactly three firms on earth that can manufacture AI-grade HBM (Micron, SK Hynix, Samsung — CONFIRMED), and it is the largest US memory maker with the broadest HBM qualification footprint across the major AI accelerator platforms. Its 86% gross margin and ~81% operating margin in the latest quarter are oligopoly pricing-power prints, not cycle noise.

## Why it's irreplaceable

A hyperscaler can in-source a chip *design* far more easily than a memory *fab*: building an HBM-capable fab is tens of billions in capex, 3–5 years of yield learning, and process know-how that cannot be bought off the shelf. Instead of competing, the hyperscalers are **signing take-or-pay agreements and posting cash deposits** — the customers themselves are certifying that they cannot replicate the product. Irreplaceability: **8/10**; disintermediation risk: **low**.
