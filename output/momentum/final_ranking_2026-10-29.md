## Per-name thesis (top 5)

**1. MU (\$983.23).** The shortage is contracted: ~\$150B RPO under 26 take-or-pay SCAs (minimum-pricing floor for priced agreements, per verification), ~\$32B customer commitments, >75% of fiscal 2027 output committed, from one of only three HBM makers, with only 50–66% of key demand satisfiable. Also today's single pick. Caveat: HBM #3 share (~18%) and the memory cycle — not in-sourcing risk.

**2. NVDA (\$215.38).** Demonstrated shortage — Q2 FY27 revenue \$96.2B +106% verified, Q3 guided ~\$108B — on CUDA/NVLink lock-in for frontier training. The verified framing matters: the \$500B Blackwell+Rubin figure is cumulative revenue visibility first given on the 26 Aug 2026 call and restated at GTC 28 Oct 2026, NOT a forward-only backlog and NOT formal guidance; the ~\$307bn "cleaned" figure is a secondary estimate whose published breakdowns do not reconcile to \$500bn minus shipped product. Receivables and custom-silicon erosion are the live caveats, and the growth lens ranked it #3 while no lens gave it a #1 vote.

**3. APH (\$78.21).** The shortage is at the fiber layer, not every connector: record Q3 2026 orders \$12.2B at 1.19x book-to-bill, Q3 sales \$10.23B +65.1% (+37% organic), FY26 guidance raised to \$36.9–\$37.1B (all verified from the 28 Oct 2026 release), with CCS fiber lead times ~50 weeks (fiber only, from the Edgewater survey summary). Caveat: hyperscalers qualify to spec and second-source — irreplaceability only 6/10.

**4. SNDK (\$1,404.77).** The purest NAND price lever in the set (shortage 10/10, ~85% gross margin on the price spike) — but a price-cycle bet, not a moat bet: no disclosed backlog, substitutable at renewal, and today's fiscal Q1 FY27 print is a binary event at peak expectations. The moat lens ranked it #10; the skeptic #3.

**5. FCX (\$66.60).** A documented 2026 refined-copper deficit with record prices and a 2027–28 volume snap-back as Grasberg recovers; the INVENTORY_BUILD flag was explained benign in research. Caveat: earnings quality is price-dependent and Grasberg execution, not demand, is the swing factor.

**6. WST (\$403.64).** A qualified-demand-surge compounder: ~800 Annex 1 / GLP-1 high-value-product projects and a qualified-supplier lock-in — but the explosive leg depends on today's still-unreported Q3 print confirming high-teens HVP growth.

**7. ANET (\$187.00).** A genuine AI-infrastructure compounder with a raised \$12.6B outlook — but a demand-share story with a live disintermediation threat (white-box/SONiC, Nvidia Spectrum-X bundling) and a full multiple; explosive upside needs continued share gains, not a shortage repricing.

**8. ISRG (\$444.18).** The set's most irreplaceable franchise (9/10: installed-base lock-in, razor/blade, certification moat) with no shortage driver (4/10) — moat without surge, which is why the moat lens ranked it #3 and the shortage-weighted lenses ranked it last.

**9. CSCO (\$111.38).** AI orders surging (\$9.3B, 4.5x) with the largest enterprise installed base — but a datacenter laggard behind ANET and Nvidia, with an INVENTORY_BUILD flag explained in research.

**10. AME (\$246.22).** A high-quality, record-backlog compounder (\$4.11B backlog) riding a multi-year power/defence/AI-infrastructure order wave — but a diversified order book, not a structural shortage, so the case rests on continued beats rather than scarcity repricing.

## Risk lens & leverage-safety note

Sector concentration: the top 4 are all technology hardware/semiconductors or adjacent interconnect — a single AI-capex pause hits MU, NVDA, APH and SNDK together, and FCX adds commodity cyclicality on top. Steadiest names: ISRG and WST (qualified medical moats, low shortage beta). Highest-risk: SNDK (binary print today, no backlog) and ANET (live disintermediation). Drawdown reality: memory names can fall 50%+ fast in a cycle turn, which is brutal with margin — education, not a leverage recommendation; no specific leverage multiple or position size is suggested here.

## Thesis-break / trap triggers to watch

Backlog shrinking (MU RPO, APH orders/book-to-bill, AME backlog); HBM or NAND pricing rolling over; margins peaking quarter-on-quarter; a customer announcing in-sourcing (most dangerous for ANET via white-box, for APH via second-sourcing); CXMT/YMTC reaching HBM scale; NVDA receivables deteriorating further or a hyperscaler shifting frontier training off CUDA.
