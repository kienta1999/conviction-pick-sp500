## One-paragraph thesis

Applied Materials just reported the strongest quarter in this field — record
Q4 FY2026 revenue of $10.41B (+53% YoY) and non-GAAP EPS of $4.16 — then
guided Q1 FY2027 to ~$11.1B of revenue and $4.35 of EPS, with the CEO
calling 2027 "another record year" and wafer-fab-equipment spending up more
than 30%. The stock is still ~49% below its 52-week high after a Nov 5
sector capitulation that had nothing to do with the company, and the market's
reaction to the print was flat — the re-rate has not happened yet. At 15.1x
forward earnings with a net-cash balance sheet, this is the cheapest
equipment name in the field on numbers that are still accelerating.

## Why the dip is temporary, not permanent

The drawdown is a sentiment event, not a business event. On Nov 5, 2026 —
two sessions after the US midterm elections — AMAT fell 13.6% in one session
as the whole chip-equipment complex sold off on data-center policy risk,
Qualcomm's weak guidance and crowded AI positioning (SimianX, Nov 9). By the
Nov 6 close it was ~54–55% below its June 30 record close. Three business
days later (Nov 12) the company reported record results and raised its
outlook: Q4 revenue $10.41B (+53%), EPS $4.16 vs $4.06 expected, full-year
revenue $34.45B (+21%), and a Q1 FY2027 guide above consensus. The business
improved while the price halved — the classic temporary-dislocation
signature. The Nov 5 cause (policy fear + positioning) is already fading:
no data-center regulation has materialized, and hyperscaler capex (e.g.
CoreWeave's raised range) keeps being confirmed.

## Why it's the category leader / why the moat is intact

Applied is one of the world's largest suppliers of the machines that make
chips (AP, Nov 13) — the largest wafer-fab-equipment vendor, with leadership
in etch and deposition. Its moat is process technology, installed base and
customer integration at the leading edge: leading-edge logic and DRAM fabs
are operating at full utilization (CFO Brice Hill, Q4 call, Nov 12), and
customers are asking for tools sooner, not later. **AI cannot disrupt this
franchise — AI demand is its demand.** Every AI accelerator needs more
advanced wafers, which need more of Applied's tools. China is a managed
risk, not a moat breach: China was 25% of Q4 revenue (down from 29% a year
ago) and management expects it below 25% in FY2027 as AI markets grow
faster — the growth engine is deliberately shifting away from the
export-control exposure.
