## One-paragraph thesis

KLA is the quasi-monopoly inspector of the semiconductor industry — it sells the process-control tools every leading fab must buy — and its business is accelerating while its price has halved. Q1 FY27 (reported Oct 28, 2026) was a record: revenue $4.14B (+29% YoY, +13% QoQ), record operating margin 46.1%, non-GAAP EPS $1.22 vs $1.18 consensus. The stock is −49.5% off its 52-week high and −16.1% below its 200-day SMA not because demand broke, but because the whole equipment group de-rated: a −13.3% day on Nov 5 after the midterms, then another leg on Nov 16 when SemiAnalysis cut its AI accelerator capex forecast. The dip is multiple compression against accelerating revenue — the classic setup this doctrine buys.

## Why the dip is temporary, not permanent

The drawdown is a sector-wide equipment de-rating, not a KLA-specific failure. KLAC set its split-adjusted record close Jun 30, 2026 ($301.71; 10-for-1 split in June 2026), fell ~30% from early August to early November on a sector pullback, then −13.3% on Nov 5 alone (Lam −16.4%, AMAT −13.6% the same day), leaving it ~−54% below the record by Nov 6. The Nov 14–15 weekend SemiAnalysis capex cut drove another leg: −5.35% to $143.96 on Nov 16 (verified Finnhub close), a third straight down day. Meanwhile fundamentals *accelerated*: the Q1 FY27 print was a record on every line. The only company-specific negative was gross margin: 62.7% with December guided to ~62.5%, as persistently high DRAM/memory component costs delay the 63–64% gross-margin model to mid-2027 into 2028; Wells Fargo and UBS cut price targets on it. That margin drag is real but bounded and management-flagged, not a demand loss. The open question is whether the SemiAnalysis slope-cut to 2027–29 accelerator capex trims the WFE >$200B 2027 outlook KLA guided to — a slope cut, not a contraction: capex still grows every year through 2030 under the revised model.

## Why it's the category leader / why the moat is intact

KLA holds ~50–55% of process control and ~80%+ of reticle inspection (third-party estimates; KLA does not publish share — verified Phase 3.5). No fab can ship leading-edge wafers without inspection, and every shrink makes inspection *more* necessary, not less. AI cannot disrupt it: AI is a customer of KLA's tools, not a substitute for them — more AI chips means more wafers to inspect. Irreplaceability 9/10 (research). The balance sheet is fortress-grade: net debt/EBITDA 0.19x, $4.74B cash, FCF $1.34B in Q1 FY27 alone. History is friendly: since 2010, all eight KLAC >20% 30-day falls with a full year behind them ended that year higher, median +67% (Trefis) — though the two most recent (Jul 28 and Nov 4, 2026) are too new to judge, and the July dip was still −22% three months later.

- **Nvidia Q3 FY27 print tonight (Nov 17, after close)** — the group-wide re-rating trigger; a beat-and-raise re-rates the whole equipment complex.
- **WFE 2027 >$200B outlook** — KLA's own call; if the 2027 WFE market grows as guided, KLA's revenue acceleration continues into 2027.
