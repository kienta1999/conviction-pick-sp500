## One-paragraph thesis

KLAC is the purest quality compounder in today's field, bought after a de-rating that had nothing to do with its business: the stock is −46.6% off its 52-week high while revenue growth accelerated for a third straight quarter into a record Q1 FY2027 (revenue \$4.14bn, +29% YoY; non-GAAP operating margin 46.1%, the first time ever above 46%; record FCF \$1.34bn), with ~12-month lead times because the company is behind on supply, not short on demand. The panel agreed more strongly than on any recent pick: three of four lenses picked KLAC outright (convictions 8, 9 and 7), the fourth ranked it #4, and it took 37 of 40 possible Borda points with three #1 votes. At 22.7x forward earnings for a >56%-share quasi-monopoly whose moat gets stronger as chips get harder to make, this is a great business bought on sale — the dip is multiple compression on a margin-cost quibble, not a broken franchise.

## Why the dip is temporary, not permanent

The single most compelling evidence is the shape of the fall: KLAC rallied the day after its 28 Oct earnings, then fell −13.3% on 5 Nov with no company news, in the broad AI rout — and its −54.1% peak-to-trough drawdown from the 30 Jun record close came while TTM net income rose to \$5.26bn from \$4.24bn a year earlier. The bear case is a margin-growth-rate quibble (memory input costs capping Dec gross margin at 62.5% vs the 63–64% Investor Day target), not demand: the Dec-quarter guide is \$4.7bn ± \$200m revenue (a ~+25% H2-over-H1 acceleration), purchase commitments are \$8.87bn (3.5x a year earlier), and management says it "remains behind in supply with lead times averaging around 12 months." A business that cannot ship fast enough is not a business in decline.

## The dip depth

KLAC is −46.6% off its 52-week high (\$307.37 split-adjusted, 30 Jun 2026) and −11.4% below its 200-day SMA. The cause, in sequence: a ~30% slide from 8 Aug to 6 Nov (vs −4.7% for the S&P 500) on margin-guide worries and analyst target cuts after an otherwise strong print, then the 5 Nov AI rout (−13.3% that day, the 4th-worst day since 2010) with no company news. ## Why it's the category leader / why the moat is intact

KLA is the global leader in semiconductor process control (inspection + metrology) with >56% worldwide market share — "the most dominant player in any semiconductor equipment category," a quasi-monopoly comparable to ASML in lithography (verified: 56.8% per CSET/TechInsights 2025; 56.5% per Gartner 2024, cited by KLA). Gross margins consistently above 60% are the proof. AI is the demand driver, not the disruptor: as nodes shrink (GAA, sub-3nm) and HBM stacks grow, inspection/metrology intensity per wafer rises — AI makes KLA more necessary, not less. Advanced packaging process-control revenue is on track for ~\$1.1bn in CY2026. No credible substitute exists: yield management is physics-bound (you cannot ship uninspected wafers), and KLA's installed base plus defect-library data creates switching costs. Irreplaceability scored 10/10 — the highest in the field. The soft spot is cyclicality, not substitution: WFE is cyclical, and if the AI buildout pauses, KLA's order book defers.

## The rebound catalyst & timing

Two triggers, both fundamental: the Dec-quarter print (late Jan / early Feb 2027 — the date is estimated at 4 Feb 2027 per FMP/TipRanks/Zacks, unconfirmed by the company) against the \$4.7bn revenue guide the company set itself — hitting it, plus any gross-margin progress toward 63–64%, re-rates the margin story; and the CY2027 WFE outlook above \$200bn (management's own outlook, raised alongside Lam's), with leading-edge foundry logic (60% of Dec process-control mix) and DRAM (84% of memory mix) as the demand drivers. Near-term sentiment support: \$8.81bn of buyback authorisation remaining, with record capital returns of \$1.24bn in Q1 alone (\$936m buybacks — the second-highest ever — plus \$305m dividends). NVDA's 17 Nov print is a sentiment catalyst either way for the whole semicap complex.
