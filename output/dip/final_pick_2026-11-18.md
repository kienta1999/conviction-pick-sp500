## One-paragraph thesis

KLA is the quasi-monopoly inspector of the semiconductor industry — it sells the process-control tools every leading fab must buy — and its business is accelerating while its price has halved. Q1 FY27 (reported Oct 28, 2026) was a record: revenue $4.14B (+29% YoY, +13% QoQ), non-GAAP EPS $1.22 vs $1.18 consensus, record operating cash flow $1.53B and record FCF $1.34B. The stock is −52.0% off its 52-week high and −20.3% below its 200-day SMA not because demand broke, but because the whole equipment group de-rated: a −13.3% day on Nov 5 after the midterms, then another leg when SemiAnalysis cut its AI accelerator capex forecast over the Nov 14–15 weekend. Nvidia's Nov 17 print — revenue $115.8B (+103% YoY), Q4 guide $130B, FY28 growth outlook raised to ~75% — directly rebuts the demand-fear leg, and the market's after-hours reaction (+2–4%) has not yet repriced the group. The dip is multiple compression against accelerating revenue — the classic setup this doctrine buys. This is the second straight day KLAC is the dip pick (yesterday at $152.32); the price fell a further ~5% while nothing in the thesis changed.

## Why the dip is temporary, not permanent

The drawdown is a sector-wide equipment de-rating, not a KLA-specific failure. KLAC set its split-adjusted record close Jun 30, 2026 ($301.71; 10-for-1 split in June 2026), fell ~30% from early August to early November on a sector pullback, then −13.3% on Nov 5 alone (Lam −16.4%, AMAT −13.6% the same day), leaving it ~−54% below the record by Nov 6. The Nov 14–15 weekend SemiAnalysis capex cut drove another leg. Meanwhile fundamentals *accelerated*: the Q1 FY27 print was a record on every line, revenue growth has accelerated in each of the last three quarters, and the December-quarter guide (~$4.7B) sits above consensus. The only company-specific negative was gross margin: 62.7% with December guided to ~62.5%, as persistently high DRAM/memory component costs delay the 63–64% gross-margin model to mid-2027 into 2028; Wells Fargo and UBS cut price targets on it. That margin drag is real but bounded and management-flagged, not a demand loss. Verification (Phase 3.5, 12/12 confirmed) re-checked the record quarter, the guide, the backlog, the moat share and the valuation figures against independent sources — nothing contradicted.

## Why it's the category leader / why the moat is intact

KLA holds ~50–55% of process control and ~80%+ of reticle inspection (third-party estimates; KLA does not publish share — confirmed Phase 3.5). No fab can ship leading-edge wafers without inspection, and every shrink makes inspection *more* necessary, not less. AI cannot disrupt it: AI is a customer of KLA's tools, not a substitute for them — more AI chips means more wafers to inspect, and advanced packaging is driving process-control revenue toward $1.1B in calendar 2026 (+70%+ YoY). Irreplaceability 10/10 (research — the highest in the field). The balance sheet is fortress-grade: net debt/EBITDA 0.19x, $4.74B cash, FCF $1.34B in Q1 FY27 alone, $8.81B of buyback authorisation remaining. The backlog (~$12.5–12.6B at Jun 30, 2026, up ~60% YoY — the last hard disclosed figure, with management saying it "continues to grow" on the Q1 call) gives the longest visibility in the field.
