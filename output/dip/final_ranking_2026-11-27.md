## Per-name thesis

**1. FIX — Comfort Systems USA.** The dip is temporary: a record Q3 (revenue \$3.77B +54%, EPS \$13.91 +69%, backlog \$17.81B +90%) sold off 17% in two days on sector rotation plus the CFO-retirement headline, while 65–75% of the backlog converts within 12 months and the company holds ~\$2.1B net cash. The rebound catalyst is backlog conversion through 2027, confirmed at the Q4 print (18 Feb 2027). The moat — scarce craft labour, modular fabrication ~40% faster, scale across ~45 operating companies — is physical and AI-proof; AI data centres are the customer, not a substitute. The honest value-trap caveat is concentration: 59% of revenue from tech customers, so a hyperscaler capex pause is the single-point failure.

**2. APH — Amphenol.** The dip is temporary: Q3 was a record on every line (revenue \$10.23B +65%, orders \$12.17B +99%, book-to-bill 1.19) with guidance raised — the stock actually rose on earnings day and fell three sessions later on a broad AI sell-off (verification corrected the "analyst target cuts" cause; Redburn's move was a split restatement). The rebound catalyst is Q4 results (~27 Jan 2027) against raised guidance, with IT Datacom guided +100%+ for FY2026 and a new \$4B buyback from 1 Jan 2027. The moat — custom-engineering qualification lock-in in high-speed interconnect — holds, and AI is the demand driver (IT Datacom +117% YoY). The honest value-trap caveat is AI concentration: ~75% of IT Datacom (48% of sales) is AI, and the \$10.5B CommScope deal loaded \$12.6B of net debt.

**3. CAT — Caterpillar.** The dip is temporary: Q3 beat on EPS (sales \$20.68B +17.3%, adj EPS \$7.20 +45.5%, record \$78.1B backlog) but the stock fell ~12.7% in two days because management held guidance instead of raising it — an expectations reset, not a broken business (revenue slightly missed FactSet consensus and ~\$0.21 of EPS was a tariff-refund gain). The rebound catalyst is the Q4 print (estimated 4 Feb 2027) with the first 2027 outlook issued at that release — not a separate January event, as verification corrected. The moat — century-deep dealer network, installed-base services, engine/turbine depth — makes AI a customer for CAT power generation. The honest value-trap caveat is cyclicality: a genuine construction/mining downcycle, and buybacks plus dividends (\$10.9B TTM) exceeded FCF.

**4. META — Meta Platforms.** The dip is a capex-ROI derating, not a demand collapse: Q3 revenue +26% to \$64.60B, yet the stock fell ~7% as capex guidance was narrowed up to \$135–145B and Q3 FCF turned negative (-\$2.12B, first since the 2012 IPO). The rebound needs a capex-peak signal at Q4 earnings (late Jan 2027), not another revenue beat. The moat is the strongest in the field (9/10 irreplaceability — 3.64B daily active people, network effects, AI improving targeting). The value-trap caveat: if \$135B+/year never earns a return, Meta becomes a capital-intensive utility with FCF permanently impaired and repeated equity raises diluting holders.

**5. KEYS — Keysight Technologies.** The dip is temporary: a record Q4 FY2026 (revenue +41%, orders +53%, book-to-bill 1.17) sold off 7% because demand outran shipment capacity in ~12–24 AI SKUs — a supply bottleneck, not a demand break. The rebound catalyst is backlog conversion as capacity expands, with Q1 FY2027 guidance 13–15% above consensus on EPS; the Q1 print (~22 Feb 2027) is the falsifier. The moat (8/10) is certification-grade test/measurement — AI accelerators are harder to test, not easier. The value-trap caveat: if the supply constraint is a cap rather than a bottleneck, the ~\$18.74 forward EPS ramp never arrives and the 15.5x forward P/E is an illusion.

**6. CSCO — Cisco.** Record Q1 FY2027 (revenue \$18.5B +24%, EPS \$1.40 +40%, guidance raised) sold off 6%+ on gross-margin contraction (65.6%, -250 bps on AI-hardware mix + memory costs) and order growth slowing 35%→21%. The Feb 2027 margin print (guided 65–66%) is the make-or-break test. Moat is #1 overall Ethernet switching (28.7%) and #1 campus (53.5%), but #3 in AI data-centre fabric behind Nvidia and Arista. Value-trap caveat: if the margin reset is structural rather than mix, EPS growth compresses to low-teens. INVENTORY_BUILD flag benign (buffer-build ahead of AI shipments; OCF +36%).

**7. APP — AppLovin.** Q3 already proved the dip was a model-timing scare (revenue +55%, EBITDA margin 84.3%, FCF \$1.42B), and the rebound is underway — the remaining upside is a re-rating on e-commerce proof. **VALUE-TRAP FLAG:** the moat is performance, not lock-in (6/10 irreplaceability); growth is model-release dependent (one delayed AXON upgrade cost ~20% in a day), e-commerce is unproven, and the SEC-probe closure claim rests on TechTimes/MatterFact rather than a re-read 10-Q. Ranked #7 on only 2 of 4 ballots despite the highest average placement among the top 7 — the panel is split on whether the rebound is already priced.

**8. HWM — Howmet Aerospace.** Best operating momentum in the batch (Q3 revenue +29%, adj EPS +54%, guidance raised, \$5B buyback) — but at 31.8x forward earnings there is no margin of safety, and the competitive overhang has a multi-year fuse: GE and SpaceX are explicitly trying to replicate its casting process in-house. **VALUE-TRAP FLAG (D lens):** if the scarcity premium erodes structurally, a 32x multiple compresses toward a 20x industrial multiple even as earnings grow.

**9. GS — Goldman Sachs.** Best deal franchise at 10.8x forward P/E with a Q3 beat (EPS \$17.01 vs \$12.86, ROE 20.7% 9M) — but the multiple is at its own historical median, not below it, so the margin of safety is the earnings level, which is cyclical. The rebound needs deal flow to survive the AI-financing scare (undisclosed retained SpaceX GPU paper); Q4 results (~19 Jan 2027) are the test. LOW_CASH_CONVERSION flag is benign / not applicable (dealer-bank mechanics).

**10. VST — Vistra.** Record Q3 (EBITDA \$2.378B +50.4%), 2027 guidance \$7.7–8.5B EBITDA initiated, ~98% of 2027 output hedged — sold off on the Texas governor's race (data-centre moratorium platform; inauguration 19 Jan 2027). The dip is political, not operational, but the 2028 hedge gap (~78% hedged) is the real valuation risk, and the mean target (\$197.18) is stale. The rebound is a Fed/politics call as much as a company call.

## Risk lens & leverage-safety note

Sector concentration: the top 3 are all AI-infrastructure-adjacent (FIX data-centre construction, APH AI interconnect, CAT AI power) — a hyperscaler capex pause is a correlated risk across the top of the list, not three independent bets. The steadier compounders in the list are GS (10.8x, best franchise) and CSCO (15.6x, #1 campus switching); the highest-risk names are APP (beta 2.19, model-dependent growth) and ADBE (just missed; AI-disruption binary on 9 Dec).
