## Per-name thesis

**1. LRCX — Lam Research.** The dip is a supply story, not a demand story: a record fiscal Q1 2027 (revenue \$8.22bn, +54% YoY; non-GAAP operating margin 40.2%, first time above 40%) sold off because the December guide (\$8.60bn ± \$400m) merely met consensus after component and clean-room constraints capped it, with shipments deferred to the March quarter rather than lost. The moat is the etch leadership (verified #1 in etch, #2 in deposition) that AI makes more valuable, not less — more layers, more etch steps per wafer. The catalyst is December delivery at the late-January print plus a confirmed above-\$200bn calendar-2027 WFE view. The value-trap case is the AI-financing chain; no lens found it credible enough to veto.

**2. KLAC — KLA Corporation.** The best franchise in the field — a process-control monopoly (verified >50% share, >85% in optical inspection; Semiconductor Process Control 90.0% of FY2025 revenue) printing a record 46.1% non-GAAP operating margin and guiding the December quarter above consensus — yet down 47.6% on sector de-rating. The dip is temporary (ten straight quarters of YoY revenue growth, no downgrades after the print); the honest caveat is valuation: at ~22–27x forward it is still premium to its own history, so the rebound pays for quality, not for a valuation bargain. The KLAC "~18x historical average" P/E anchor could not be verified and is dropped from this writeup. Catalyst: delivery against the \$4.7bn December guide at the late-January/early-February print.

**3. NFLX — Netflix.** The highest-quality dip in the field on valuation: a dominant, cash-generative franchise (verified >325m paid subscribers, ~2.5x Disney+ standalone) repriced as a mature asset after six straight post-earnings drops. The Q3 print beat on EPS (\$0.87 vs \$0.82) but missed on Q4 guidance and cut the 2026 ad forecast to ~\$2.7bn from ~\$3bn. The dip is mostly temporary — execution and a guidance reset, with a maturing-growth undertow: the ad tier is the designated second engine, and whether it is delayed or broken is decided at the Q4 print (estimated Jan 19, 2027) and the March 2027 ad-tier expansion to 15 new countries. Trading at ~16.8–18.7x forward vs a 37.8x five-year average. FLAG: its EV upside (+14.9%) sits just below the +15% guardrail — see Return scenarios.

**4. VRT — Vertiv.** The strongest fundamentals in the field — a 61% sales beat-and-raise with orders doubling — trading at a ~46% discount to its May high because the market fears its customers' financing, not its products. Power and cooling cannot be routed around by AI; the NVIDIA reference-design lock-in is the moat (8/10). The trap door is financing: if project finance stays expensive, the 12–18-month backlog converts slower, and at ~25x forward / ~38x trailing a single growth disappointment re-rates it violently (beta 2.17–2.22). The INVENTORY_BUILD earnings-quality flag is mostly benign (build ahead of an order-doubling backlog) but worth monitoring into Q4.

**5. FIX — Comfort Systems USA.** A record backlog (\$17.81bn, +90% YoY), \$2.1bn net cash, and a forward multiple cut by a third, sold off on sentiment and a principled refusal to chase weak AI-lab credit on loose terms. The rebound needs margin stabilisation (gross margin faded to 24.8%) and AI-capex sentiment, not just a cheap multiple. Concentration is the watch item: technology customers are 59–60.8% of revenue.

**6. HWM — Howmet Aerospace.** Record growth (+29% revenue, +25% organic — the strongest quarterly growth rate on record), a 9/10 moat (certification-gated turbine-blade casting, only three or four foundries worldwide at the required precision), and a \$5bn buyback top-up — already bouncing off its trough. But at ~33x forward earnings the rebound is a multiple-stabilisation story, not a value re-rating, and the GE/CPP and SpaceX in-sourcing overhang will cap it until in-sourcing fears are disproved by volumes.

**7. AMAT — Applied Materials.** Two record quarters, a 47% drawdown on contagion alone, and the second-cheapest equipment multiple after Lam — but the entire thesis is decided in one week by the Nov 12 print, so this is a pre-earnings rebound bet, not a settled bargain. The panel ranked it down for that binary, not for its business.

**8. SCHW — Charles Schwab.** Record flows (Q3 net new assets \$190.4bn, +38% YoY) and a real franchise at ~11–12x forward earnings, with the whole thesis hinging on NIM — a genuine dip, not a broken company, but the AI cash-sweep threat is the one risk that could make it structural. The RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN flag is benign (margin loans scaling with activity).

**9. META — Meta Platforms.** The cheapest mega-cap AI bet on forward earnings (17.3x vs a ~21x five-year average), with the strongest ads engine (+26% YoY) — but the rebound is a referendum on management's capex discipline at the Q4 call, and the dilution is already real (up to \$95bn equity programme; FCF −\$2.12bn in Q3; S&P outlook negative). The panel kept it in the top 10 for the ads engine, ranked down for the funding model.

**10. TPR — Tapestry.** The best value setup in the field — a #1 brand at ~13x forward earnings with a beat streak — but the score is provisional until today's 8:00 AM ET print confirms Coach's momentum and tariff handling. Coach is 86% of revenue and all operating profit, so concentration is the trap; the panel ranked it last among the top 10 precisely because the print is a binary either way.

## Risk lens & leverage-safety note

Sector concentration: the top 7 is dominated by one correlated bet — semiconductor capital equipment (LRCX, KLAC, AMAT) plus AI-infrastructure industrials (VRT, FIX, HWM) — all of which re-rate together on AI-capex sentiment and rates. The deepest drawdowns (SNDK −54%, KLAC −47.6%, AMAT −47.1%, NFLX −46.4%) are also the names most likely to keep falling if the AI-financing fear proves right; the steadier compounders in the list are SCHW (11.4x forward, record flows) and CPRT (unranked, widest moat). A beaten-down name can keep falling — "catching a knife" with margin is brutal; size for the possibility the dip deepens before it turns. This is risk education, not a leverage recommendation and not a specific position size.
