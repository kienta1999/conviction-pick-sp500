## One-paragraph thesis

FIX is the one name in the dip field whose business is accelerating into the selloff rather than breaking: Q3 2026 revenue rose 53.7% YoY to \$3.77bn with EPS \$13.91 beating consensus, the backlog nearly doubled to a record \$17.81bn (+90% YoY), FY2027 EPS estimates were revised *up* to \$65.30 from \$58.85 while the stock halved, and cash runs ahead of earnings (Q3 operating cash flow \$1.01bn vs \$489m net income). The work is physical — mechanical, electrical and HVAC installation for data centers and mission-critical buildings — and no model can pour concrete, bend pipe, or commission a cooling system, which makes FIX the most AI-irreplaceable name in the batch (dossier score 9/10). At ~15.5x forward earnings versus a 5-year median of ~23.6x, the market is pricing a demand break that has not happened.

## Why the dip is temporary, not permanent

The single most compelling evidence is that cash runs *ahead* of earnings: Q3 operating cash flow was \$1.01bn vs \$489m net income, and 9M operating cash flow was \$2.54bn vs \$1.30bn net income — the exact opposite of the value-trap signature, where earnings run ahead of cash. Receivables grew +53.7% YoY, exactly in line with revenue +53.7%, and contract liabilities of \$3.87bn (customers paying in advance) exceed the receivables build. The selloff was triggered by no forward guidance, a CFO transition, a modest gross-margin slip (26.3% → 24.8%), and AI-capex sentiment — none of which is a lost customer, a cancelled project (management confirmed on the Oct 23 call that no FIX project has been delayed by any data-center moratorium), or a substitute product. Verification corrected one dossier label: the "\$3.8bn of new bookings" is the sequential backlog *build*; implied new bookings were ~\$7.5bn (book-to-bill ~2.0x) — the demand story is stronger than the dossier stated.

## The dip depth

FIX is -49% off its 52-week high and -35% below its 200-day SMA. The stock doubled in under five months (\$1,032 to \$2,073.99, Jan–May 2026) and then gave it all back on a multiple compression, not a fundamentals break — Q3 was the eighth straight EPS beat.

## Why it's the category leader / why the moat is intact

FIX is the largest mechanical/HVAC installer in the US (211 locations, 22,700 employees) with a reputation and certification lock-in on mission-critical builds. Technology customers are now 59–60.8% of revenue (vs 42% a year earlier), so FIX is deepening its entrenchment with the AI buildout rather than being displaced by it. The binding constraint is labour, not demand — demand exceeds FIX's ability to staff it, the opposite of a share-loss signal. AI cannot substitute skilled-trades installation at scale; rising rack densities make physical cooling more, not less, essential. The moat's limit is that FIX is a contractor, not a technology owner — no patents or network effects, pricing power bounded by competitive bidding, and margins are cyclical with construction.

## The rebound catalyst & timing

H1 2027 margin normalisation as early-stage jobs mature — management's own expectation — with the Q4 2026 print as the first test that gross margin stabilises near 25% and bookings keep coming. The Q4 date is not confirmed by the company; vendor estimates span Feb 17–25, 2027 (the screen carries Feb 18, 2027, unconfirmed). JPMorgan (Overweight, \$1,700, Nov 10) and Morgan Stanley (Overweight, \$1,410, Nov 17) both initiated coverage into the selloff rather than downgrading. A stabilising tape could re-rate it sooner given the below-median multiple.

## Key swing factors

1. **H1 2027 margin normalisation** — does gross margin recover from 24.8% toward the Q1 level of 26.3% as early-stage jobs mature?
2. **Hyperscaler capex in 2027–28** — does data-center spending hold, or does AI-buildout financing tighten and slow new bookings?
3. **Backlog conversion pace** — the \$17.81bn now converts over 18–24 months rather than 9–12; delays would stretch the thesis without breaking it.
4. **Labour availability and inflation** — the binding constraint on staffing the backlog; wage pressure squeezes contractor margins fast.
5. **Data-center moratoria** — work shifting to friendlier jurisdictions is manageable, but a widening freeze would slow bookings.

- **Labour inflation** or an inability to staff the backlog would delay conversion and squeeze margins further.
- **CFO transition** leaves a visibility gap until the Q4 print.
- **Concentration:** 59–61% of revenue rides one end-market (technology/data centers).
