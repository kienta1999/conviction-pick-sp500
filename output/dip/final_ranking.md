## Per-name thesis

### 1. BR — Broadridge Financial Solutions
The cleanest temporary-dip evidence in the field: Broadridge **raised** FY26 guidance at Q3 FY26
(recurring-revenue growth to "at or above 7%" cc, adjusted EPS growth to 10–12%, on Q3 recurring
revenue of $1,288M +6% cc and adjusted EPS $2.72 +11%) — and the stock fell ~42% from its $271.91
high anyway. The drawdown is a bet on two narratives: AI eroding proxy processing, and tokenized
equities routing around governance. The moat is **regulatory, not technological** — SEC rules require
proxy distribution to beneficial owners at NYSE-regulated fees, which no model can replicate — and BR
has gone commercially live on tokenization rather than being bypassed (Alpaca governance 2026-07-20,
Ondo custodial tokenized securities including IVV ~2026-07-06, $7.5T processed on DLR in June 2026).
The honest negative: closed-sales guidance was **cut from $290–330M to $240–290M** with YTD closed
sales −16%. **Caveat: earnings tomorrow pre-open**, carrying the first FY27 guide.

### 2. VST — Vistra Corp.
The purest "AI is the customer, not the disruptor" asset in the field. Its irreplaceability is
*measured* rather than asserted: PJM's capacity auction drew only **525 MW of new supply at the $325
/MW-day price cap**, and verification surfaced a stronger fact the dossier missed — **PJM fell 6,831
MW short of its reliability requirement, the first time in its history**. This run also removed
VST's largest apparent risk: the screen's −$0.16B FCF was a data artifact (true TTM operating cash
flow $4,670M − capex $2,867M = **+$1,803M**), confirmed to the dollar. Guidance excludes 5,500 MW of
acquired capacity and ~3,800 MW of signed hyperscaler PPAs. **Two caveats:** the entry has degraded
(up 9.2% since 2026-07-30 with analyst targets unchanged, closing the SMA200 gap to −4.4%), and
verification found leverage **worse than claimed — gross debt $21.0B, net $20.3B, ~3.0× trailing**,
not 2.5–2.8×. Reports 2026-08-07.

### 3. ISRG — Intuitive Surgical
A clean Q2 beat (revenue **$2,892.3M +19%**, non-GAAP EPS **$2.80 +28%**) that the market punished
**−14.15%** purely on guidance, because FY26 procedure growth of 13.5–15.5% sits below the 18%
delivered in 2025. Management's causes are largely exogenous — ACA subsidy expiration changing when
patients seek care, deferrable benign procedures, GLP-1s shrinking bariatrics, China pricing. Then
**J&J's OTTAVA won FDA De Novo authorization on 2026-07-21**, the first table-integrated soft-tissue
robot, following Medtronic's Hugo — the first real competition in two decades. The moat is still
formidable: **86% of US robotic accessories** (the sticky annuity) versus 58% of systems, revenue per
procedure *rising* to ~$1,830, an **11,710-system installed base**, and a dV5 refresh only ~15%
complete. **Caveats:** at 31.1× forward it is the priciest of the top 3, and the dossier's
"85% recurring revenue" is **unverified — filings support 76.3%**.

### 4. META — Meta Platforms
The de-rating is a capital-allocation scare, not a demand scare: Q2 revenue **$60.8B +28%** with ad
impressions **+14%** *and* average price per ad **+12%** — pricing accelerating is the opposite of a
demand problem. What broke is cash and EPS quality: FY26 capex guided to **$130–145B**, free cash
flow collapsed **91% to $784M**, operating margin fell to 31% from 43%, and adjusted EPS missed by
~14% on $2.4B of legal charges and $1.2B of severance. The catalyst is the **2027 capex number on
Oct 28** — which management explicitly refused to bound. 🚩 The flag: "16.9× forward" rests on a
depreciation-life extension worth ~$2.9B a year that Michael Burry argues overstates earnings by up
to 20.8% by 2028 — on a normalized schedule this is closer to 20–21×.

### 5. DECK — Deckers Brands
The strongest *mechanical* catalyst in the field, and the only drawdown uncorrelated with everything
else on this list. Q1 FY2027 (2026-07-23): net sales **+5.7% to $1.02B**, HOKA **+7.7%**, UGG
**+4.9%**, **DTC +13% with comps +6.8%** — direct consumer pull, which is not what a fading brand
produces — EPS **$0.94 vs $0.88**, and **FY27 EPS guidance raised**. The stock fell 3–6% anyway. The
buyback authorization is **~$5B against a $14B market cap (36%)**, and management bought FY26 stock
at an average **$102.43 — above today's $99.50**. Net cash, 11.9× forward, EV/EBITDA 9.86 (~27% below
median). ⚠️ Its risk is **fashion, not AI**: On grew +30.0% versus HOKA's +23.6%, preference surveys
put Hoka third at 9%, and "other brands" fell −18.1%.

## What the panel revealed

**Where they agreed.** Every lens ranked **BR, VST, ISRG, META, DECK, VRSK, NEM and HOOD** — eight
names on 4/4 ballots. And the panel was unanimous in the negative: **ADBE and GNRC were ranked by
nobody**, which is a stronger signal than any single top pick. All four lenses independently
concluded that Adobe's cheapness is the market correctly pricing a terminal-value risk rather than
an error of inattention.

**Where they split.** **APP is the fault line.** The growth lens (B) made it its top pick — deepest
dip, accelerating growth, halved multiple. The catalyst lens (A) ranked it #2. And the moat lens (C)
and the skeptic (D) **refused to rank it at all**, on irreplaceability (an algorithm, not a mandate)
and on the SEC probe (an uncapped tail attacking the margin's mechanism). That produced the run's
defining statistic: APP has the **best average placement in the field (1.50)** and only **2/4
appearances** — maximum conviction, minimum consensus. It sits 6th on Borda purely because two lenses
zeroed it.

**Conviction vs consensus.** Four names took a #1 vote (BR, VST, APP, NEM) but only BR converted
conviction into consensus. **NEM is the mirror image of APP**: the skeptic's top pick, ranked by
everyone, but placed 7th on average — a name every lens respects and none loves. **ISRG and META are
pure consensus plays**: 4/4 appearances, zero #1 votes, and top-4 Borda finishes achieved entirely by
never being anyone's throwaway.

**The correlation the ranking hides.** BR, VRSK, ROL and ADBE are four expressions of one bet — that
the market is wrong about AI commoditizing the toll-booth. Ranks 1 and 7 (plus two just-missed names)
are therefore **not independent**. A reader who sizes BR and VRSK together is doubling one thesis, not
diversifying. **NEM, DECK, ISRG and VST are the genuinely uncorrelated drawdowns here.**

## Risk lens & leverage-safety note

**Sector concentration.** The top 10 spans Industrials (BR), Utilities (VST), Health Care (ISRG),
Communication Services (META, NFLX), Consumer Discretionary (DECK), IT (APP), Financials (HOOD) and
Materials (NEM) — superficially diversified. **The real concentration is thematic, not sectoral:**
BR and VRSK are the same AI-toll-booth bet; META, VST and APP are three different bets on the AI
capex cycle continuing. A portfolio built from ranks 1, 2, 4, 6 and 7 is far less diversified than
its GICS labels suggest.

**Highest-risk names:** **APP** (deepest drawdown, uncapped legal tail, binary print Aug 5),
**HOOD** (34× EV/EBITDA with no asset floor and binary state-level litigation), **NFLX** (a
four-quarter growth step-down and withdrawn disclosure). **Steadiest compounders:** **DECK** (net
cash, buyback = 36% of cap, guided-up EPS), **NEM** (net cash, ~10% FCF yield, hard-asset floor),
**ISRG** ($8.63B cash and no debt).

**Deepest drawdowns / most likely to keep falling:** **APP (−44.6%)**, **NFLX (−42.0%)**,
**ROL (−41.4%, and still 29× — the one most likely to keep de-rating)**, **HOOD (−40.7%)**,
**BR (−39.8%)**. Note **ROL sits −30.0% below its own 200-day SMA**, the widest gap in the field —
a momentum fact, not a valuation argument.

**Leverage-safety note (education, never a recommendation).** Every name here has already fallen
20–45%, and several fell *while their earnings rose* — which is the demonstration that a beaten-down
quality name can keep falling for reasons unrelated to its results. The modeled bear cases in the
top 3 run **−20% to −28% from today with no operational deterioration required**, purely from further
multiple compression. Four names report within four days, where a single overnight gap can jump
straight past any stop. Catching a falling knife with borrowed money is how a correct thesis still
ends in a forced exit at the bottom. **POLICY.md §2 is unambiguous: picks from this system are
cash-only — no margin, no options as leverage substitutes, no borrowing against the position.** This
paragraph is risk education about volatility, not a suggestion about any specific ratio or size.
