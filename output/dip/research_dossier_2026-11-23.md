## BKNG — Booking Holdings Inc.

### 1. Why it's down — temporary vs permanent
BKNG is down ~32% YTD (MarketBeat: $214.21 split-adjusted on 1 Jan 2026 → $144.87 on 20 Nov 2026), −33.6% off its 52-week high of $220.81 (16 Dec 2025, split-adjusted), and made a 52-week low of $144.10 on 20 Nov 2026. Three stacked causes:
(a) **The September 2026 AI-agent selloff.** Meta launched its Muse personal AI agent on 8 Sep 2026, able to plan and book travel; Google began hotel booking inside AI Mode on 27 Aug 2026. Investors read agents as disintermediation: Booking fell ~21% in September alone, Expedia and Airbnb ~18% each, ~$54bn wiped off the sector in about two weeks (Platform Aeronaut, 24 Sep 2026, via Tribii). The fear, not yet the P&L, drove the first leg.
(b) **Q3 2026 earnings, 3 Nov 2026 — a genuine miss plus a one-off charge.** Adjusted EPS $4.66 (+17% YoY) beat the $4.49 FactSet consensus and gross bookings rose 10% to $54.5bn (vs $52.63bn expected), room nights +7% to 345m. But GAAP revenue fell 1% to $8.9bn vs ~$9.54bn expected, because of a **$900m revenue reduction and $100m accrued loss tied to an FTC matter** (Priceline hotel-advertising practices, per Reuters, Oct 2026). Excluding it, adjusted revenue was $9.8bn, +9%. Management then guided a weak Q4: room-night growth 1–3% (vs 7% in Q3), gross bookings +2–4%, adjusted revenue +0–2%, adjusted EBITDA roughly flat YoY; Evercore ISI noted the implied Q4 revenue midpoint of $6.41bn was 5.6% below Street (Investor's Business Daily, 3 Nov 2026). Causes cited: softer international travel, Middle East disruptions, elevated airfares, localized unrest in some European cities.
(c) **Technicals/sentiment:** the 50-day crossed below the 200-day on 23 Oct 2026 (death cross), MACD turned negative 18 Nov 2026; shares fell ~2% after hours on earnings and kept sliding into the 20 Nov low.
**Verdict: mostly temporary (cyclical travel softness + a one-off regulatory charge + AI sentiment), with a structural question mark.** The FTC item is one-off; the Q4 slowdown is cyclical/geopolitical; the AI-agent threat is real but unproven in the numbers (see §2). No moat has been lost yet — volumes still grew 7–10%.

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — **7/10**
Moat: **inventory scale + reviews + direct channel + brand**. ~4.4m properties listed at end-2025 including ~500k hotels/motels/resorts (2025 10-K, via Conduit); brands Booking.com, Priceline, Agoda, KAYAK, OpenTable; >50% of room nights (mid-fifties) now come through the direct channel, insulating it from paid-search disruption. The key dated fact against the disruption thesis: on the Q2 2026 call (4 Aug 2026) CFO Ewout Steenbergen said traffic from large language models, paid and unpaid, was **"significantly below 1% of our room nights."** Agents today still book *through* OTA inventory: Google AI Mode's launch partners include Booking.com, Expedia, Hotels.com, Priceline and Trip.com (Skift, 27 Aug 2026); Meta's Muse shops consumer sites such as Expedia/Hotels.com for hotels and in Skift's October 2026 spot tests failed at the room-and-rate step on Hilton/Marriott/Hyatt sites. BTIG's Jake Fuller (via Skift, 2 Oct 2026) argues agents are more likely to become a customer-acquisition channel for OTAs than a replacement, because OTA inventory/pricing data is faster to use than crawling hotel sites. Booking's own 10-K warns agents "may further evolve into full-service booking platforms, increasing competitive pressure and disintermediating" OTAs — the risk is disclosed, not hidden. The real exposure: the indirect (paid-search) half of bookings is where agents can intercept demand, and if agents start charging for placement, Booking's $8.19bn 2025 marketing spend (30% of revenue) partly migrates to agent owners. Score 7: inventory and direct traffic are durable; the discovery layer is contestable.

### 3. Rebound catalyst
- **Q4 2026 earnings, 24 Feb 2027** — proof the Q4 slowdown was a trough (guided room nights 1–3%, EBITDA flat) rather than a new run-rate; any stabilization in international/Middle East travel reads through immediately.
- **FTC matter closure** — the $900m is booked; final resolution removes an overhang (no timetable found — "not found").
- **Buybacks at depressed prices**: $2.1bn repurchased in Q3, $9.5bn in the first nine months of 2026, $10.5bn total capital returned in 9M; $12.4bn authorization remaining at 30 Sep 2026; diluted share count fell 7.5% YoY (Q3 2026 release/10-Q). At ~$145, each buyback dollar retires far more of the company than at $220.
- **AI-channel positioning**: Booking is a launch partner inside Google AI Mode and Google's Direct Offers pilot; if agents become a paid channel, Booking's scale lets it buy placement — the bear case requires agents to go direct to hotels at scale, which has not happened.
- **Dividend**: $0.42/share quarterly (declared, payable 31 Dec 2026) — small (~1.2% yield) but a floor signal.
Timing: no near-term catalyst before the Feb 2027 print; the rebound is a 1–2 quarter story, not a December story.

### 4. Balance-sheet survival
Strong, with one cosmetic quirk. At 30 Sep 2026 (10-Q, filed 3 Nov 2026): cash and equivalents $15.81bn, total debt $20.06bn ($18.07bn long-term + $1.99bn short-term), **net debt ~$4.25bn, 0.56x EBITDA** (screen); stockholders' **deficit of −$10.4bn** because of years of buybacks — price-to-book is meaningless here (screen shows −10.4x). 9M 2026 operating cash flow $8.07bn, TTM FCF ~$9.24bn (frontierpicks/10-Q filings); Q3 FCF was $1.07bn, −22% YoY, depressed by a seasonal $3.6bn unwind of deferred merchant bookings. On 13 Nov 2026 Booking closed €3.55bn of new euro notes (coupons 3.95–5.00%) to fund buybacks and debt repayment. No distress risk; leverage is modest for a business generating ~$9bn of annual FCF.

### 5. Margin of safety / valuation
Screen forward P/E **11.84x**, trailing ~15.6x (MarketBeat, 20 Nov 2026). A 5-year average forward P/E for BKNG was **not found** in the sources checked — do not assert a historical discount beyond the screen's drawdown. For context: at the Nov 2026 close the shares traded at ~4.4x sales, ~13.3x EBIT and a ~7.8% FCF yield (frontierpicks, 20 Nov 2026). Analyst mean target: $215.06 (+48.4%, screen) vs MarketBeat consensus $221.90 (+53.2%), Moderate Buy. Peer comparison: Expedia at 9.44x forward is cheaper, but Booking is the larger, higher-margin, more direct-traffic business (operating margin 32.8% vs Expedia 18.4%; gross margin 87.2% vs 90.5%). **Genuinely cheap for a business still growing bookings double-digit — if Q4 proves to be a trough and not a step-down.**

### 6. Category position
Still the **#1 global OTA** by gross bookings ($186.1bn in 2025, ~14.5% take rate on $26.9bn revenue) and by property supply; the dip is not a share-loss story — room nights +7%, bookings +10% in Q3, ahead of the market's volume expectations. The threat is to the *take rate and discovery economics*, not to volume leadership today.

### 7. Value-trap risk (bear case)
The falling-knife scenario: AI agents learn to book direct with hotels/airlines at scale (UCP/direct-connect standards mature, agents stop needing OTA inventory), Booking's take rate compresses, and its $8bn marketing machine becomes a tax paid to agent owners rather than a moat. Layer on a real travel recession (Middle East escalation, US/Europe demand contraction) and Q4's 1–3% room-night growth becomes the new normal — then 11.8x forward earnings is not cheap because forward earnings fall. The FTC matter could also prove to be a template for further regulatory action on OTA advertising/pricing. Note the stock has already halved in split-adjusted terms from its Dec 2025 high while volumes grew — the market is pricing the structural case, and it will not re-rate on one decent quarter.

### 8. Rebound score: **7/10**
**Verdict: The #1 OTA with a fortress balance sheet and a $12.4bn buyback war chest, sold off on an AI fear that has produced <1% of room nights so far — the best risk/reward of the three, but the re-rating waits for the Feb 2027 print.**

## EXPE — Expedia Group Inc.

### 1. Why it's down — temporary vs permanent
EXPE is −30.5% off its 52-week high of $342 (26 Aug 2026) and −18.7% in the last month alone (Finnhub, 20 Nov 2026). The fall has two legs:
(a) **The September 2026 AI-agent selloff** shared with BKNG (~−18% in September; Meta Muse launched 8 Sep, Google AI Mode hotel booking 27 Aug 2026).
(b) **The Q3 2026 earnings reaction, 4–5 Nov 2026 — a beat-and-raise that still sold off.** Q3 (reported 4 Nov 2026) beat everywhere: revenue $4.94bn, +12% YoY vs $4.73bn expected; adjusted EPS $9.39 (+24% YoY) vs $8.89; adjusted EBITDA $1.69bn (34.2% margin) vs $1.55bn expected; gross bookings +11% to $34.0bn; room nights +7% to 115.7m; B2B bookings +19%, B2C +7%. Management *raised* full-year guidance (bookings $131.4–131.9bn, +10%; revenue $16.37–16.43bn, +11–12%; EBITDA margin expansion 180–200bp). But Q4 guidance disappointed: gross bookings growth only 3.3–5.2%, revenue $3.69–3.75bn (+4–5.7%), adjusted EBITDA $830–880m (midpoint $855m vs $917m consensus). Shares fell >8% after hours, ~13% on 5 Nov (to ~$272 from $315.88), then drifted to $234.96 by 20 Nov — −25.5% since the print (TipRanks). Management's stated causes for the Q4 deceleration: an earlier fall-sale date (pulled forward into Q3), lapping prior-year B2B partner promotions, US election-cycle disruption, and geopolitical travel interruptions (L2 Capital note, 5 Nov 2026; Expedia Q3 call, 4 Nov 2026).
**Verdict: temporary — timing/phasing plus sentiment.** The Q4 slowdown is explicitly attributed to pull-forward and tough compares, while full-year guidance went *up*. No structural break in the quarter's numbers; the market punished the deceleration anyway.

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — **6/10**

Consumer brands Expedia/Hotels.com/Vrbo are #2 to Booking.com in most markets, and Vrbo faces Airbnb in vacation rentals. The AI-agent exposure is the same as BKNG's, with one difference in Expedia's favour: Expedia moved fastest to *be inside* the agents — it announced hotel search/booking inside Meta's Muse at launch (Sep 2026, Expedia as merchant) and is a Google AI Mode launch partner. Its 10-K warns failure to navigate the "agentic revolution" could have a material adverse effect (via Conduit). Score 6: B2B supply/distribution is genuinely hard to disintermediate; the B2C brand is more replaceable than Booking.com's, and Vrbo is not the category leader.

### 3. Rebound catalyst

Quarterly dividend $0.48/share.
- **Agentic-channel economics clarity**: management flagged "uncertainty around emerging agentic-channel economics" on the Q3 call (TipRanks summary) — any disclosure that agent bookings are incremental/commissionable rather than cannibalistic would remove the sector overhang.
Timing: Feb 2027 print is the catalyst; B2B momentum is already visible quarterly.

### 4. Balance-sheet survival

### 8. Rebound score: **7/10**
**Verdict: The cheapest of the three on history, net-cash, B2B-led growth, and a Q4 bar set low — a beat-and-raise that sold off 25% is usually phasing, not impairment; the Feb 2027 print decides.**

### EXPE — RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN flag
