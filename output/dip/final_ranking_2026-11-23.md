## Per-name thesis

**1. ANET —** The dip is a pure multiple compression on a business accelerating into the selloff: Q3 revenue +58.5% YoY (highest since IPO), beating its own \$3.3bn guide by ~11%, with zero debt, \$14.36bn cash and securities, and a 2027 framework that doubles AI fabrics to ~\$7.0bn on ~\$18.5bn revenue. The moat is EOS switching costs plus hyperscaler co-engineering and open-Ethernet leadership — but it is contested: Nvidia's Spectrum-X overtook Arista in data-centre Ethernet switching in Q2 2026 (20.4% vs 18.7%, IDC), and Microsoft + Meta are ~42% of revenue. Catalyst: the Q4 print ~11 Feb 2027 (estimated, corrected from the screen's 9 Feb) landing at/above the \$3.9–4.0bn guide. The honest caveat: this is the shallowest dip in the field (-0.6% vs the 200-day SMA), so it is a re-rating bet, not deep value.

**2. CAT —** The stock halved from \$1,073.46 (30 Jun 2026) to \$510.45 while the business got better: Q3 sales \$20.68bn (+17.3% YoY), adj EPS \$7.20 (+45.5% YoY), record backlog \$78.1bn (+96% YoY), and consensus 2027 EPS estimates actually rose to \$32.18–32.25 from \$30.15 at the June peak. The moat (scale manufacturing, the Cat dealer network, installed-base switching costs) is intact at 9/10 in the dossier, and AI creates demand for its power engines rather than substituting for them — power is only ~15% of 2025 sales. Catalyst: Q4 results in late Jan/early Feb 2027 (date unconfirmed) testing whether power orders hold and dealer destocking ends. The skeptic's caveat stands: an average ~19x multiple on peak-cycle earnings, so the rebound needs 2027 delivery.

**3. FIX —** Yesterday's pick, still the clearest visibility in the field: a record \$17.81bn backlog (+90% YoY, eighth consecutive sequential increase) with 65–75% recognised in the next 12 months, Q3 revenue \$3.77bn (+53.7% YoY) and EPS \$13.91, net cash, and a forward multiple below its own 5-year median. The work — physical HVAC/mechanical installation for data centers — is the most AI-irreplaceable in the batch (dossier score 9/10). Catalyst: H1 2027 margin normalisation, first tested at the Q4 print (expected mid-to-late Feb 2027, date unconfirmed). The risk is concentration: technology customers are 59% of revenue, so a hyperscaler capex pause cuts the growth engine.

**4. APH —** The strongest fundamentals in the batch at its cheapest multiple in years: record Q3 sales \$10.23bn (+65% YoY, +37% organic), record orders \$12.17bn (+99% YoY, book-to-bill 1.19:1), guidance raised, net leverage ~1.1x EBITDA, and a \$4bn buyback starting Jan 2027. The moat is design-in switching costs and breadth in high-speed interconnect — the physical bottleneck of AI data centers. The dip is the shallowest of the top names (-7.6% vs the 200-day), and IT datacom is 48% of sales, so it is the most AI-concentrated name in the top 10.

**5. GOOGL —** The cheapest megacap dip on its own history: forward P/E 17.3x vs a ~21–26x 5-year average, with Q3 revenue +25% to \$127.6bn and Google Cloud +103% to \$30.7bn on a \$561bn backlog. The dip is a capex/FCF shock — Q3 capex \$51.36bn pushed FCF to -\$9.07bn, the second straight negative quarter — plus antitrust overhang. The rebound needs FCF to stop bleeding, a 2027 story, not a 2026 one.

**6. BKNG —** The #1 OTA with a fortress balance sheet and a \$12.4bn buyback authorisation, sold off on an AI-agent fear that has produced <1% of room nights so far (LLM traffic "significantly below 1%" per the Q2 call). Q3 was a genuine miss plus a one-off \$900m FTC charge, and Q4 guidance is weak (room nights +1–3%) — the re-rating waits for the Feb 2027 print.
