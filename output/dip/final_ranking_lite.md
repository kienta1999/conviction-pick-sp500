## Why each one, in a line

1. **ISRG** — a sector-wide surgical-volume scare (HCA's warning) plus the arrival of Medtronic's Hugo took
   38% off a franchise whose own numbers never wobbled: Q2 da Vinci procedures +15%, Ion +36%, 246 DV5
   placements vs 180 a year ago, FY guidance held at 13.5-15.5%. Re-rates on the Oct 20 print reaffirming
   procedure growth against an installed base of 11,395 systems Hugo has barely dented.
2. **IDXX** — clinic *visits* are falling and the stock has been sold as if diagnostics were falling with
   them; the company beat and raised, with CAG recurring revenue +10% organic — an 1,100bp premium over
   visit declines. Utilization per visit is the moat; the new Catalyst proBNP cardiac test extends it.
3. **META** — ads +27% YoY with price per ad +12%; what broke was free cash flow (−91% YoY to $784M) as
   capex guides to $130-145B. That is a reinvestment choice, not demand loss — but it only re-rates when
   the 2027 capex line stops rising, which is an Oct 28 question, not a certainty.
4. **TPR** — the cheapest genuinely-working business in the field: FY26 sales +14%, EPS +38%, Coach handbag
   units +20% with AUR up double digits, FY27 guided to $8.4-8.5B and +50bp margin. It fell on tariffs and
   consumer nerves after a strong run, not on its own numbers, and trades at 13.8× forward.
5. **SYK** — same volume scare, far less damage: Q2 beat, 9% organic, Mako ortho compounding. The problem
   is price — only 22% off its high at 18× forward, so the dislocation is mild and the margin of safety thin.
6. **NFLX** — down 38% on Meta entering video advertising and a general de-rating of streaming; ad revenue
   is on track to double to ~$3B on 7% of its addressable market, and it bought back a record $4.7B in a
   quarter. At 20× forward vs a 30-40× history, the multiple has done the correcting.
7. **BR** — sold off ~43% over 52 weeks on an AI-disruption and tokenized-equities narrative aimed at a
   business that is a *regulatory mandate*: the system of record for proxy voting and post-trade settlement.
   AI does not vote proxies. No dated catalyst is the only thing holding the score down.
8. **GNRC** — the most clearly transitory cause on the board (outages averaged 74k/day vs 228k/day a year
   ago — a mild hurricane season) plus a $1.6B data-center backlog and ~$700M of committed 2027 volume.
   Held out of the buy bucket by the moat gate: in large-megawatt gensets it is the challenger to Caterpillar
   and Cummins, not the incumbent.
9. **XYL** — raised FY26 EPS guidance to $5.55-5.70 on AI-driven industrial water demand while the stock
   sits 30% off its high; the soft patch is municipal treatment, and the catalyst is the Nov 3 print showing
   the industrial/data-center mix carrying the segment.
10. **RL** — tariff pressure met with pricing actions and low-single-digit revenue guidance. Fine business,
    but only 15% off its high — the shallowest drawdown in the field, so there is little dislocation to buy.

## Deviations from the weighted score

- **NFLX above SYK** (both 7.15): NFLX is 38% off its high at 20× forward against a 30-40× history; SYK is
  22% off at 18× with no comparable de-rating. Same score, more dislocation.
- **DECK above ADBE** (both 5.95): DECK's problem is a tariff-driven guide cut on brands still growing;
  ADBE's is an unresolved question about whether generative AI substitutes for its product. Also, ADBE's
  analyst upside is +4% — the sell side has already marked its targets down to the price, so the 9.7× multiple
  is not a margin of safety, it is agreement.
- **GNRC** would rank 8th on score and lands in `watch` only because of the `moat ≥ 6` gate. Flagging it
  explicitly: highest analyst upside in the field (+52%) and the most obviously temporary cause. If you
  disagree that large-MW gensets are a challenger position, it belongs in `buy`.

## Caveat

Nine of these fourteen are long-duration compounders whose multiples move inversely to the 10-year. A 25bp
hike on Sep 16 makes this entire list cheaper before it gets better. Only GNRC (weather + data-center backlog)
and TPR (holiday sell-through) have a leg that is not primarily a rates story.
