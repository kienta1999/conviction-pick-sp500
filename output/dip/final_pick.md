### Why the dip is temporary, not permanent

The Q2 2026 tape (company PR, 2026-07-16 — verified) is the cleanest "price, not business" proof in
the field: revenue $2.89B (+19%, vs $2.82B consensus), adjusted EPS $2.80 (vs $2.51 consensus), and a
*raised* 68–69% gross-margin guide — and the stock fell ~10% the next session because full-year
procedure guidance was held at 13.5–15.5% instead of raised. The three overhangs are all bounded: the
tariff hit is ~1pp of margin (company-quantified), the April 2026 Class II da Vinci recall is a
field correction not a stop-ship, and US surgery softness shows in the guide, not in the quarter.
Procedures — the compounding engine — are still growing mid-teens. A business growing 15–19% with
rising margins does not get permanently impaired by a held guide.

### The dip depth

−33.7% off the 52-week high and −13.6% below the 200-day SMA, on a name that was trading at
~50–55x forward earnings. The de-rating to 32.5x is the entire drawdown: earnings power
*accelerated* while the multiple collapsed. That is the dip-buying setup the doctrine exists for.

### Why it's the category leader / why the moat is intact

da Vinci is the category: 11,700+ installed systems (verified), surgeon-training lock-in (hospitals
train residents on da Vinci; switching means retraining staff), regulatory clearance moat, and
instruments/accessories that generate ~75% recurring revenue. **Why AI or a substitute cannot disrupt
it:** AI is a complement to robotic surgery (vision, planning), not a substitute — a model cannot
hold a scalpel, and FDA-cleared surgical robotics has no credible new entrant. Research
irreplaceability score: 9/10 (batch 3's highest). The one moat watch: the H1-2027 Extended Use
Program could cannibalize some recurring instrument revenue — a pricing decision by the moat-owner,
not a breach of the moat.
