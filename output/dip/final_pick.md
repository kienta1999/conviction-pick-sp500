## The thesis, in a paragraph

Broadridge is the SEC-mandated utility that delivers proxy materials and investor communications to
beneficial owners on behalf of essentially every US broker and bank. It is the only name in this
field that **raised guidance in the middle of its own 40% collapse** — at Q3 FY26 it lifted FY26
recurring-revenue growth to "at or above 7%" constant currency and adjusted EPS growth to 10–12%, on
recurring revenues of $1,288M (+6% cc) and adjusted EPS of $2.72 (+11%). The stock fell anyway,
because the market decided two things about the next decade: that AI will erode its technology
franchises, and that tokenized equities will route around its governance business. Both are
narratives, not numbers — and the evidence runs the other way, because Broadridge is *building the
tokenization rails rather than being bypassed by them*. What you are buying is a regulated
near-monopoly compounding EPS at 10–12%, marked down to roughly half the multiple it carried for
five years, on a fear its own product announcements are actively falsifying.

**The two bear narratives are contradicted by primary evidence.** On AI: the moat is not

*Verification correction:* the dossier's claim that "the SEC has affirmed" tokenized securities carry

individual statement (July 2025)**, which does say "tokenized securities are still securities" and
that "the same legal requirements apply to on- and off-chain versions," but it is **not a Commission
action and does not address proxy mechanics**. The direction of travel is real; the legal certainty
is thinner than the dossier claimed, and this writeup uses the weaker version.

**The one genuinely negative fact, stated honestly.** Closed-sales guidance was **cut** — from

## Why it's the category leader / why the moat is intact — and why AI cannot disrupt it

Broadridge is the dominant proxy and investor-communications processor in the United States — the
plumbing beneath shareholder voting. **The moat is a legal mandate, which is the one moat a language
model cannot route around.** Brokers cannot in-source proxy distribution without assuming the
compliance liability they currently outsource, and any broker-dealer that turned regulated proxy
distribution into a profit center would invite exactly the regulatory review that makes the current
arrangement stable. Nothing in the research showed share loss: the closed-sales shortfall is deal
timing, with origination and pipeline reported up double digits.

The honest disruption risk is not AI at all — it is **market structure**: if tokenized-equity
infrastructure eventually moves governance on-chain in a way that disintermediates *broker-dealers
themselves* (Broadridge's clients), then BR's customer base erodes even though BR is not directly
displaced. That is a real, long-dated risk, and it is the risk BR is hedging by shipping tokenization
products today. Research irreplaceability score: **9/10**, tied for the highest in the field.

## Key risks / what invalidates the thesis (the trap case)

The value-trap case for BR is that **long-dated fears cannot be disproven quickly**, so a compressed
multiple can persist for years even while the business performs. Specifically: (a) closed sales
deteriorate and the $40–45M delta compounds into FY27–28; (b) ICS revenue growth decelerates below
5%, at which point 15.8× is not cheap for a low-single-digit grower; (c) the market-structure risk —
tokenization disintermediating *broker-dealers*, BR's own customers, rather than BR directly; (d) the
legal certainty around tokenized-securities governance is thinner than the dossier claimed (one
Commissioner's statement, not Commission action), so the "this is already settled" comfort is weaker.
