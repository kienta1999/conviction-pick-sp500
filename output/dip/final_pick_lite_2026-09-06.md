**THE PICK:** ISRG — Intuitive Surgical, Health Care Equipment, ~$131B market cap, $366.70.
Score 8.10 (cause 8 / moat 9 / trigger 7 / survival 10 / margin-of-safety 7), #1 of 14 researched.

## Thesis

Intuitive is 38% off its 52-week high and 21% below its 200-day average — the deepest drawdown of any
health-care name the screen returned — on two fears that are about the *sector* and the *future*, not about
its own quarter. The sector fear: HCA flagged soft surgical volumes and every device maker sold off together
(ISRG −6%, Stryker −5.4%, GE Healthcare −7%), with ACA-subsidy noise reviving the procedure-demand debate.
The future fear: Medtronic's Hugo cleared FDA for general surgery and completed its first US cases in
February. Meanwhile the numbers did not cooperate with either story — Q2 da Vinci procedures grew ~15%, Ion
grew ~36%, the company placed 246 da Vinci 5 systems against 180 a year earlier, and management held FY2026
procedure growth guidance at 13.5-15.5%. You are buying a razor-and-blade franchise with 11,395 installed
systems at 30× forward earnings, against a multiple that has spent most of the last decade in the 40s-50s.

## Why the dip is temporary, not permanent

The drawdown is a **multiple** event, not an earnings event. Procedures — the unit that actually drives
recurring instrument revenue — are still compounding in the mid-teens, and the guidance range was maintained
rather than trimmed. The volume scare originated at a hospital operator's commentary, which affects the
timing of capital placements far more than it affects a per-procedure consumable stream. The competitive
scare is real but slow: Hugo's clearance is one clearance against an installed base of 11,395 systems, each
one attached to trained surgeons, validated instrument sets and hospital credentialing — the substitution
cost is measured in years of retraining, not in a purchase decision.

## The dip depth

−38% from the 52-week high, −21% versus the 200-day SMA, −19% over twelve months. Both are the deepest in the
screened health-care set, and unlike Stryker (−22% off high, 18× forward) the multiple has actually compressed.

## Why the moat holds, and why AI cannot route around it

The product is a physical system inside a sterile field, sold with per-procedure instruments and wrapped in
FDA clearances, surgeon training and hospital credentialing. AI improves what happens *on* the platform —
which is Intuitive's own roadmap, not a substitution threat — but it does not remove the instrument from the
patient, and no model reaches through the abdominal wall. The credible threat is a rival *platform*, and the
switching cost there is exactly what the installed base measures. Irreplaceability: high.

## The rebound trigger

**Oct 20, 2026 — Q3 earnings.** The specific thing to watch is US da Vinci procedure growth, which
decelerated to 12% in Q2 from 14% in Q1. A reaffirmation near the 13.5-15.5% midpoint kills the
volume-scare thesis outright; a trim toward the bottom of the range validates it and this pick is wrong.
Secondary: DV5 placement mix (over half of new placements in Q1, 246 units in Q2) and any read on Hugo
displacing rather than expanding.

## Top risk — the value-trap case

The bear case is that the volume slowdown is not a hospital-capex wobble but the front edge of a
procedure-demand plateau, arriving at the same moment a credible second platform reaches the market — a
maturing growth story re-rating from a growth multiple to a medtech multiple. At 30× forward, ISRG is the
most expensive name in this ranking on an absolute basis; if mid-teens procedure growth becomes high-single-
digit, the multiple has a long way further to fall. Sep 16 FOMC is the near-term aggravator: a hike makes
every long-duration compounder on this list cheaper before it gets better.

## Runner-up

+14%, EPS +38%, Coach handbag units +20%). It ranks 4th only because a fashion brand's moat is a weaker
thing to underwrite than an installed base. If the rate scare is your main worry, TPR is the pick with the
