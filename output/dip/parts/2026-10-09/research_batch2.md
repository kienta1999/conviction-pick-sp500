## LVS — Las Vegas Sands ($36.10; 52w $36.08–$70.45; −49% off high, at 52w low; YTD −44.5%)

**1. Why it's down — TRANSITORY (hold variance + promo cycle), verdict: temporary.**
Two legs. (a) Jan 28–29, 2026: Q4 2025 report — Macau adjusted property EBITDA margin collapsed 390 bps to 28.9% on "intense promotional reinvestment" defending share vs local competitors; combined with CEO Robert Goldstein's announced transition to senior advisor and a $500M buyback during rising debt, the stock fell 14.8% intraday to $52.18 — worst single day since pandemic headlines (ainvest, Jan 2026; financialcontent, Jan 30, 2026). (b) Jul 22, 2026: Q2 2026 miss — revenue $3.15B vs $3.38B consensus, adj EPS $0.59 vs $0.79 (Reuters, Jul 22, 2026) — driven by an abnormally low 1.35% VIP rolling hold in Macau (normal ~3%) and June Macau weakness as bettors shifted to the FIFA World Cup; crypto weakness + tighter capital-flow controls also cited. Underneath: rolling volumes +73% YoY, non-rolling drop +15%, slot/ETG handle +30% (Q2 earnings call, Jul 22, 2026) — demand is fine, luck and reinvestment are not. Management's standing target: $700M/quarter Macau EBITDA. Next catalyst: Q3 earnings Oct 21, 2026.

**2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 9/10.** Moat = regulatory licenses (one of 6 Macau concessionaires, concessions renewed to 2032 in 2022), a Singapore duopoly (MBS vs Genting), and capital intensity ($8B MBS expansion). AI/substitutes cannot touch the franchise; the threat is Beijing policy and Macau promo competition, not technology.

**3. Rebound catalyst.** Hold normalization (Q2's 1.35% was ~1.5–2 pts below theoretical; reversion alone recovers ~$0.20+ EPS/quarter); Q3 report Oct 21, 2026 as the tripwire; MBS Singapore printing ($689M EBITDA in Q2); plus an aggressive buyback (14.3% of shares retired since Q4 2023, $5.24B at avg $47.95; $740M in Q1 2026 alone — PR, Q1 2026).

**4. Balance sheet.** Heavily levered but covenant-clean: total debt $15.11B at Jun 30, 2026 (Q2 PR) vs $3.38B unrestricted cash + $1.26B May 2026 seller-note repayment; net debt/Adj Property EBITDA ~2.2x; $4.26B revolver + $4.68B delayed-draw term loan available (Q2 2026 PR). No distress risk, but the $8B MBS capex cycle (~$2.8B spent at Mar 31, 2026) means leverage stays elevated through the build.

**5. Valuation.** Trailing P/E ~13.6 (Finnhub, TTM EPS $2.35); FY2026 EPS est $3.09 → fwd P/E ~11.7x at $36.10, PEG ~0.9 (MarketBeat, Sep/Oct 2026). Consensus target $59.84 (Hold/Moderate Buy) = **+66% upside**. Historically traded 15–25x pre-COVID — genuinely cheap because of the dip. Dividend $1.20/yr, ~3.2% yield, 47% payout.

**6. Category position.** Still the #1 integrated-resort operator by revenue in both core markets; Macau share defense is costing margin (promo reinvestment) — share being defended, not clearly lost, but the 390 bps margin hit is the market's complaint.

**7. Value-trap risk (honest bear).** Macau mass-market margins may be structurally lower (promotional arms race); CEO transition (Patrick Dumont now CEO; Goldstein exited late 2025); $262.5M insider selling over 90 days (Jan 2026) and Dumont's own 60,165-share sale (Mar 2026); $8B capex at 2.2x net leverage leaves no room for a China demand shock or concession politics. If Macau hold stays depressed and promos stick, 11.7x forward isn't cheap — it's a levered cyclical at peak-ish earnings.

**Rebound score: 6/10.** Volumes are roaring but margin and leverage make this a hold-normalization bet, not a compounder — buy the Q3 hold reversion, not the story.

## BKNG — Booking Holdings ($159.97; 52w $150.14–$225; −29% off high; YTD −25.3%)

**1. Why it's down — TRANSITORY (AI-fear + legal + macro), verdict: temporary.**
September 2026 was the damage: −18.5% in 30 days ($199.09 Aug 31 → $162.34 Sep 29), weakest month since June 2022 (tickeron, ~Sep 30, 2026; Motley Fool, Sep 23, 2026). Three drivers: (a) EU General Court upheld the block on the ~€1.6B eTraveli acquisition (Sept 2026) — lost strategic optionality for Connected Trip/flights in Europe; (b) Meta's Muse personal AI agent became the top-downloaded app and Expedia partnered with it (late Sep 2026) — market repriced the whole OTA middleman model; EXPE, ABNB fell too; (c) oil >$100/bbl on renewed Middle East tensions (Sept 2026) reviving airfare/demand fears. None of it showed up in operations: Q2 2026 (Aug 3/4, 2026) beat — EPS $2.54 vs $2.43, revenue $7.35B vs $7.19B. Q3 2026 guidance was soft (4–6% revenue growth), adding fuel. Next catalyst: Q3 earnings Oct 27, 2026.

**2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 6/10.** Moat = two-sided network (largest OTA by gross bookings; inventory depth + Genius loyalty + brand). The AI-agent threat is the one genuine disruption vector — an agent that books directly bypasses acquisition economics. Counter: Booking launched "Lola," its own AI assistant for experiences (Sep 29, 2026), and OTAs survived the identical "Google travel will kill them" scare. Score reflects a real but unproven threat, not a cracked moat.

**3. Rebound catalyst.** Oct 27 Q3 print (Zacks consensus EPS $4.48, revenue ~$9.56B, +6% YoY); travel-demand durability through the oil scare; the market re-rating from "Muse kills OTAs" to "OTAs plug into agents"; ongoing aggressive buybacks; 25-for-1 split (April 2026) widened the retail base.

**4. Balance sheet.** Asset-light fortress: ~35% operating margin (tickeron), net margin 25.5% (MarketBeat, Oct 2026), TTM revenue ~$28.2B; dividend yield ~1.06% with 18.6% payout; negative book equity from buybacks (ROE −103% — an artifact, not distress). Zero survival risk.

**5. Valuation.** TTM P/E 16.3 (Finnhub); Zacks F1 P/E 15.1 (Aug 2026), ~12.9x FY2027 est ($12.38) at $159.97. Historical forward multiple ~18–22x → trading at a clear discount to its own history. Consensus target $236.24–236.98 (Moderate Buy; MarketBeat, Sep/Oct 2026) = **+48% upside**. PEG ~0.98. Cheap because of the dip, not a growth story breaking.

**6. Category position.** World's largest OTA by gross bookings; still taking economics, not losing them — Q2 beat on both lines. The dip is sentiment (AI/legal/oil), not share loss.

**7. Value-trap risk (honest bear).** If AI agents genuinely disintermediate search-and-book, the take-rate model compresses permanently and 13x forward is a melting ice cube; Q3 guide of 4–6% growth shows the business is already decelerating; Google's travel stack never went away. The eTraveli block also removes the flight-booking growth lever in Europe.

**Rebound score: 7/10.** Best risk/reward of the four: #1 franchise, fortress balance sheet, real operational beats, 48% to consensus target — but size the AI-disintermediation tail risk honestly.

## ADBE — Adobe (~$235; 52w $190.12–$363.70; ~−35% off high; ~−60% from 2024 peak; YTD ~−29%)

**1. Why it's down — narrative + leadership, business intact. Verdict: temporary, with a real AI question mark.**
The drawdown is a two-year derating (−25% in 2024, −21% in 2025, ~−29% YTD per tradingkey/ainvest, Sep 2026) driven by the "AI loser" narrative: Sora, Midjourney, Canva, Figma AI tools, and Microsoft Copilot (30M paid seats) eroding the "scarcity of professional expertise" the model was built on. Fiscal Q3 2026 (~Sep 10–11, 2026): record revenue $6.76B (+13% YoY), EPS $6.13, both beats — but Q4 revenue guidance came in light, net new ARR fell ~38% YoY, and cRPO growth slowed to 9% (tickeron/BMO, Sep 2026). Leadership churn compounded it: Narayen → Anil Chakravarthy as CEO effective Dec 1, 2026 (announced Sep 3, 2026); the market's preferred candidate David Wadhwani announced his departure the same day; CFO Dan Durn left for Marvell (June 2026); Narayen sold 125k shares Sep 16–17 at $246–254. AI-first ARR passed $650M (+150% YoY) — but that's ~2.4% of $27.5B consolidated ARR. The feared collapse is not in the numbers: the core creative/marketing-pro segment grew 13% in Q3.

**2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 6/10.** Moat = file-format lock-in (PSD/AI), entrenched creative-professional workflows, switching costs, 97%-subscription revenue, 89% gross margins. The concrete threat: generative AI commoditizes creation itself, pressuring seats and pricing power over time; monetization so far is $650M of AI-first ARR against a $27.5B base. Durable but genuinely contestable — the lowest irreplaceability of the four.

**3. Rebound catalyst.** Q4 FY2026 print (early Dec 2026) — Chakravarthy's first as CEO, setting the FY2027 ARR guide; a guide holding ending ARR growth ≥10% plus AI-first ARR on a path past $1B would re-rate it (analysis.org bull case $300–330); $25B buyback authorization (Apr 2026) retiring stock at ~9x earnings (9.5M shares in Q3; diluted count −7% YoY — Fool, Sep 2026).

**4. Balance sheet.** Cash-machine: record $2.52B quarterly operating cash flow in Q3 (stocksdownunder, Sep 2026); Q1 FY26 OCF was a record $2.96B; ~40% FCF margins, ~40% ROIC (ainvest, Jul 2026). No leverage concern; survival is not the question.

**5. Valuation.** ~9–10x forward (FY2026 guided adj EPS $24.45–24.50 → ~9.6x at $235; ~9x FY2027 est — Fool, Sep 2026) vs S&P 500 >20x and Adobe's own historical 25–35x. Mean target ~$270 (Barchart, Sep 2026; 38 analysts: 23 Hold/9 Buy/6 Sell) = **+15%**; range $195–$385; RBC $315 Outperform vs Stifel $200 Hold. Genuinely cheap *if* ARR growth doesn't slip under 9% — that is the entire bet.

**6. Category position.** Still the #1 creative suite and document platform; no evidence of share loss in the numbers (13% growth in the core pro segment). The dip is multiple compression on AI fear, not customer flight.

**7. Value-trap risk (honest bear).** The freemium paradox: 100M+ free users with slowing paid conversion; if AI-first ARR stalls at ~2% of ARR while net new ARR keeps declining, the 9x multiple is a value trap — software gets cohort-derated and the stock revisits the $190 52w low (~8x). New CEO is an insider enterprise-sales operator, not an AI product visionary (Jefferies). Leadership churn (CEO, CFO, Wadhwani) during an AI platform shift is the wrong kind of uncertainty.

**Rebound score: 5/10.** Cheapest multiple of the four but the smallest upside to consensus (+15%) and the only one where the disruption thesis is aimed squarely at the product — the rebound needs FY2027 ARR proof, not just a cheap P/E.

## CPRT — Copart ($27.48; 52w $26.74–$45.89; −40% off high, near 52w low; YTD −29.8%, 1Y −37.6%)

**1. Why it's down — TEMPORARY (insurance-cycle trough + share loss + cost squeeze), verdict: temporary.**
Stacked headwinds: (a) 2025: Progressive (PGR) became the largest auto insurer and, after CPRT refused better terms, shifted most salvage volume to IAA — unit volumes fell for consecutive quarters; (b) insurance affordability crisis — consumers dropping coverage/renewals shrank the total-loss pool industry-wide; (c) fiscal Q4 2026 (ended Jul 31, reported Sep 10, 2026): revenue +2.4% to $1.15B but EPS $0.35 vs ~$0.38–0.39 est, net income −17.4%, gross profit −5.5% — op expense per car rose 12.7%, negative operating leverage; FY2026 revenue $4.67B (+0.4%), net income $1.48B (−4.4%); (d) same-day announcement of the $1.9B all-cash acquisition of ACV Auctions ($10.50/share, ~23x ACV's guided FY2026 adj EBITDA — ainvest, Sep 2026) — tender extended to Oct 7, 2026 pending HSR review; HSBC cut to Hold, target $46→$36 (Sep 17, 2026). Findvalue23 (Aug 2026): the PGR/insurance-pricing episode is "cyclical" — insurers over-hiked ~20% in 2023, volumes will normalize as pricing cools; PGR's inforce growth has already slowed.

**2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 8/10.** Moat = the yard network (200+ facilities; zoning/land bank nearly unreplicable), two-sided insurer↔global-buyer liquidity, and total-loss data. AI can't touch it; the real long-term threat is autonomous driving gradually reducing accident frequency (Alluvium Q2 2026 letter) — a decades-scale headwind, partially offset by the rising total-loss *rate* (repair costs/tech content keep climbing).

**3. Rebound catalyst.** ACV deal close by YE 2026 (EPS-neutral year one, accretive from FY2028 — management, Sep 2026) opening the 20M-vehicle dealer-to-dealer channel; insurance pricing cycle turn restoring total-loss volumes; op-ex-per-car normalization; Barrington upgraded to Outperform, $40 target (Sep 11, 2026) calling the deal a long-term growth catalyst.

**4. Balance sheet.** Bulletproof: $4.49B cash & equivalents at Jul 31, 2026, **zero long-term debt** (Zacks/Stockopedia balance sheets); ACV funded from cash on hand; $1.63B buybacks in FY2026; the 2003 buyback program completed Sep 29, 2026. ~15% of market cap in cash. No distress or dilution risk whatsoever.

**5. Valuation.** TTM P/E 17.3 (Finnhub, EPS $1.55); forward P/E mid-teens (simplywall.st, Oct 2026) — the lowest multiple in a very long time vs a historical 30–50x. Targets: Barrington $40, Baird $38 (cut from $42, Outperform), HSBC $36 (Hold) → **+31–46%** to that range. Genuinely cheap because of the dip — a quality compounder priced like a no-growther.

**6. Category position.** Still the #1 salvage auctioneer in a duopoly with IAA; the PGR volume shift was a pricing-terms dispute (CPRT refused to cut fees), not a service failure — share was yielded, not lost on merit. ACV adds dealer-to-dealer breadth.

**7. Value-trap risk (honest bear).** Op expense per car +12.7% is the scariest line — if the cost base has structurally reset, the operating-leverage story is broken, not paused; $1.9B for ACV at 23x EBITDA is a full price for a GAAP-loss business and invites integration risk; IAA keeps the PGR volume; autonomous vehicles are a slow secular bleed on the total-loss pool.

**Rebound score: 6/10.** Fortress balance sheet, trough multiple, and a cyclical (not secular) volume hole — but the cost-squeeze has to prove itself reversible before this is more than a dead-cat multiple.
