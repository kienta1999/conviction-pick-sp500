## Cross-cutting claim

### 11. The Nov 2026 AI-financing repricing (SOX down ~12% on 5 Nov 2026) as the common driver of the semiconductor sell-off rather than company misses
**Verdict: CONFIRMED in substance, with a multi-cause qualification**
- The move itself: confirmed. Gate.com: "On Thursday, November 5, 2026, the semiconductor index fell 12.25% to 9,011.14, approximately 38.4% below its June peak" — its second-worst day since 1994 (worst: 16 Mar 2020, −15.9%). Finnhub SOXX data: SOXX closed $131.90 on 5 Nov 2026 after opening $143.08. MaxBuffett research: "the SOX fell 12.25% on November 5 — its second-worst day since 1994 — and 10.73% on November 20."
- Not company misses for these three names: KLAC (28 Oct), LRCX (21 Oct) and AMAT (12 Nov) all beat revenue and EPS consensus and guided up (claims 1, 5, 8, 9 above); LRCX fell ~7–10% after its own beat, KLAC fell ~8% on its print day per the Fool transcript — i.e. strong prints sold off with the sector.
- Driver — AI-financing repricing is a central strand, but not the only one cited: MaxBuffett: "November 5 set the tone: US midterm results raised fears that data-centre construction would slow, and research firm SemiAnalysis cut its AI capital-spending forecasts (about −28% for 2028, −40% for 2030)"; "the triggers were political, regulatory and positional. None of them was a cancelled order, a lost customer or a demand downgrade from inside the industry." Substack analysis (6 Nov 2026): the fall "reflected … markets began to reprice the sequence of commitments that turn planned AI capacity into actual power consumption" — midterm signals, secondary-market sales of data-centre project loans, and the SoftBank/Stargate turbine-order claim (Elon Musk, 6 Nov: "SoftBank is defaulting on their turbine orders" — itself unverified, an anonymous-loan-auction claim, not a confirmed default).
- Gate.com's cause table lists "AI spending and regulatory concerns" for 5 Nov, and also cites "disappointing semiconductor earnings" (Qualcomm margin warning, weak Arm report) as contributors — so the sell-off was not purely a financing repricing; weak prints elsewhere in the sector played a role. The claim is confirmed as the common driver for KLAC/LRCX/AMAT specifically, but "AI-financing repricing" alone overstates the singularity of the cause: midterm/regulatory fears and SemiAnalysis capex cuts were at least as prominently cited, and the SoftBank-default element is unconfirmed.
