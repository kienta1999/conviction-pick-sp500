## NFLX — Netflix — $70.53, -43.2% off 52-week high, -14.9% vs SMA200, screen fwd P/E 18.6, screen target $91.39 (+29.6%), next earnings 2026-10-20

### 1. Why it's down — TEMPORARY valuation impairment, with a real structural engagement warning

**One-line verdict: Benign business-model / one-off timing effect — ad-receivables building with a doubling ad business plus WBD-fee tax timing, not earnings running ahead of cash; re-check only if the divergence widens again without matching ad-revenue growth.**

## HSY — Hershey — $170.83, -26.6% off 52-week high, -9.0% vs SMA200, screen fwd P/E 17.4, screen target $198.00 (+15.9%), next earnings 2026-10-29

### 1. Why it's down — TRANSITORY cost shock, with share damage that has to be won back

The cause is a single input that went vertical and then broke, and the pricing response to it. West African disease and weather (Côte d'Ivoire and Ghana supply roughly 60% of world cocoa) produced a ~500kt global deficit in 2023/24 and a record futures price in Dec 2024. Hershey, like its peers, passed the cost through: Q2 2026 organic sales rose ~3.6% only because roughly +12 points of pricing more than offset roughly -8 points of volume (total company volume ~-8%, North America Confectionery volume ~-10%, International ~-8%; Zacks analysis of Q2, Oct 2026). That arithmetic bought margin and sold share: yesterday's verified Jefferies / Trefis figures (15 Oct 2026 update) had core chocolate down 135bp of dollar share and 145bp of volume share in Q2, at a mid-teens price premium to Mars. Analyst targets have been walked down through the autumn — Wells Fargo $190 to $170 on 5 Oct 2026, JPMorgan to $183 (reported 15 Oct 2026) — and the stock is -14.7% over 52 weeks against the S&P 500's +19.2% (Barchart, 13 Oct 2026).

**Verdict: Temporary input-cost impairment — the brands, shelf space and seasonal franchises are not permanently impaired — but volume and share surrendered at peak prices do not automatically return when prices ease, so the recovery is an execution task, not just a commodity tailwind.**

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 8/10

Reese's, Hershey's, Kisses, Kit Kat (US), Jolly Rancher, plus Dot's Pretzels and SkinnyPop in salty snacks: 132 years of brand, manufacturing scale, Halloween and seasonal ritual ownership, and US chocolate category leadership. AI cannot substitute a physical, branded, impulse food product, and no agent routes around a checkout-lane Reese's. The genuine long threats are not AI at all — they are GLP-1 appetite effects, health and sugar trends, and the pricing headroom Hershey has already spent. Brand irreplaceability is high; pricing power, for now, is exhausted rather than lost. **Irreplaceability: 8/10.**

### 3. Rebound catalyst — cocoa path re-verified, hedge lag still pushes the payoff into 2027

Cocoa, fresh check today: ICE New York futures hit a record $12,906/tonne in Dec 2024 (Trading Economics, via Oct 2026 coverage), fell to a cycle low near $2,846 in Apr 2026 in one series, traded at $5,613 on 8 Oct 2026, and closed at **$5,686/tonne on 16 Oct 2026, down 2.49% on the day** (Kalkine, 16 Oct 2026) — roughly -56% from the peak, still about double the pre-crisis ~$2,500 level. **CHANGE SINCE YESTERDAY — demand-side confirmation arrived with the Q3 grindings (16-17 Oct 2026):** European Q3 grindings rose 3.2% YoY to 348,215 tonnes, the first annual increase since Q2 2024; Asian grindings jumped 29.2% to a record 233,767 tonnes; ICE inventories sit at a 2.25-year high of 3,578,471 bags. Processing is recovering because beans are cheaper — the supply/demand rebalance (2024/25 production +8.5%, a small surplus after the deficit year, per ICCO figures verified yesterday) is now visible in demand data, not just price. The offsetting risk is unchanged: a strong El Niño threat to West African crops is widely cited in October coverage, with some surveys putting Côte d'Ivoire's 2026/27 main crop down 10-20% in a bad-weather case.

Why none of this rescues Q3 2026: Hershey hedges cocoa 3-24 months forward and has told investors it remains hedged above current spot — this October's Halloween candy is still priced and costed off beans bought near the Dec 2024 peak (Business Model Analyst, Oct 2026, from Hershey filings and statements). Management's own framing is that 2026 cocoa cost is only slightly up on 2025 thanks to hedging, which is protection on the way up and a lag on the way down: the P&L benefit lands mainly in 2027. The near catalyst is therefore the print, not the commodity: **29 Oct 2026 Q3 earnings**, against full-year guidance raised at Q2 (30 Jul 2026) to net sales +4.5-5.0%, organic +3-3.5%, adjusted EPS $8.36-8.52 (+32.5-35%), with adjusted gross margin already back to 41.6% in Q2 (+350bp YoY) and management expecting Q3 earnings growth to be the strongest of 2026 as pricing net of commodity costs turns most favourable. Deutsche Bank's October note, quoted 17 Oct 2026, frames the test precisely: September consumption and share trends were softer than expected, so Halloween / October performance now carries the credibility of the expected FY27 volume and share improvement.

### 4. Balance-sheet survival

### 5. Margin of safety / valuation — thinnest in the batch, and trimmed again today

Screen: forward P/E 17.4 (17.3 in yesterday's brief), trailing 23.3, target $198.00 for +15.9% — **CHANGE SINCE YESTERDAY: the screen target was trimmed from $201.62 (+18.0%) to $198.00 (+15.9%), and the freshest named cut is new: Barclays lowered its target from $200 to $178 on 16 Oct 2026, keeping Equal Weight (GuruFocus / EuropeSays summary, 16 Oct 2026).** That makes four cuts in the current window at or below the consensus (Wells Fargo $170 on 5 Oct, JPMorgan $183, Barclays $178), against a broader consensus still clustered near $199-204 (Barchart, 13 Oct 2026: average $198.90, 6 Strong Buy / 15 Hold / 1 Strong Sell of 22; MarketBeat snapshots $200.67-204.78 in mid-October). ### 8. Rebound score: 6/10

**Verdict: A verified cocoa collapse (-56% from the Dec 2024 peak, with Q3 grindings now confirming demand recovery) working through a 3-24 month hedge book — the earnings payoff is a 2027 event, share has already been ceded to Mars, and today's trimmed $198 target (+15.9%) with a fresh Barclays cut to $178 leaves the thinnest cushion in the batch.**

**Verdict: Temporary. A de-rating of the growth rate off an exceptional, target-beating year — management noted on the Q4 call that FY26 hit its three-year Investor Day revenue, margin and EPS commitments two years early — with no cut to the earnings base itself.**

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 7/10

Coach owns the accessible-luxury handbag tier at roughly $300-700: close enough to luxury for status, far enough below LVMH / Kering's $3,000+ price architecture that their multi-year price hikes pushed aspirational buyers down into Coach's arms. Pricing power is in the numbers, re-verified today: FY26 gross margin expanded +120bp to 76.6% (Q4: 78.1%, +180bp) despite a ~130bp tariff headwind, driven by mid-teens handbag average-unit-retail increases at full price with reduced promotion and roughly flat unit volume in Q4. Add direct-to-consumer data, global store and digital reach, and the (Re)Loved circular programme. AI is a tool here — demand prediction, and a September 2026 Google AI-shopping integration that puts Coach / Kate Spade checkout inside Search / Gemini — not a substitute: no agent routes around a physical status object a customer wants to be seen carrying. The true disruption risk is fashion itself. Brand heat is rented by the season, not owned. **Irreplaceability: 7/10.**

### 3. Rebound catalyst

The dated test is **Q1 FY27 earnings on 5 Nov 2026** (date confirmed in SEC-filing summaries, Sep 2026), against Q1 EPS guidance of ~$1.55 versus ~$1.48 consensus at the time of the FY26 print. What the print has to show is narrow and checkable: Coach sustaining the guided high-single-digit growth (see §6), and Kate Spade merely stabilising against a guided high-single-digit FY27 decline. The FY27 algorithm around it, re-verified today from the 13 Aug 2026 release and call: revenue $8.4-8.5bn, operating margin nearly 24% (~+50bp), EPS $7.80-7.90 (+~11%), FCF ~$1.7bn guided in yesterday's verification, and $1.7bn of capital return including ~$1.35bn of buybacks plus a dividend raised 16% to $1.85/year annualised. Coach's longer ambition — a $10bn revenue brand, after adding ~9m of Tapestry's 11m new FY26 customers — is the upside frame if Q1 shows the FY27 guide was sandbagged off a two-years-early base. Any Kate Spade inflection is free optionality, because the guide already assumes it shrinks.

### 4. Balance-sheet survival

Strong and lightly levered. At end-FY26 (27 Jun 2026, per yesterday's verification from the FY26 reporting): cash plus short-term investments $1.15bn, total borrowings $2.38bn, net debt ~$1.2bn, gross leverage ~1.1x adjusted EBITDA; screen net debt/EBITDA is 1.39x. FY26 operating cash flow was $1.98bn (up from $1.22bn) and adjusted FCF ~$1.86bn, a ~23% margin with ROIC of 19.7%. Tapestry returned $1.7bn in FY26 (~$1.35bn of buybacks at a ~$118 average plus ~$326m of dividends) while keeping leverage flat, and inventory fell to $826m from $861m — disciplined, not stuffed. The headline ROE of 197.1% in the screen is a buyback-shrunk equity artefact and should not be read as economics; the cash figures above are the economics. No survival or dilution risk. Note for balance: screen leverage (1.39x) sits a little above the company-framed ~1.1x gross figure — different EBITDA / debt definitions, same conclusion.

### 5. Margin of safety / valuation

Screen: forward P/E 13.5, trailing 16.5, target $165.25 for +37.5% — **CHANGE SINCE YESTERDAY: the screen target was refreshed from $166.50 (+38.5%) to $165.25 (+37.5%), a trivial trim, but a named fresh cut underneath it is not trivial: Raymond James lowered its target from $165 to $140 on 16 Oct 2026, keeping an Outperform rating (Markets Insider via Ad-hoc News, 16 Oct 2026).** Raymond James' reasoning matters more than its number: it cited Coach momentum, higher AUR, international growth and lower promotions, and called mid-single-digit FY27 revenue growth and margin expansion achievable — a multiple cut, not a fundamentals cut. Against that, MarketBeat's consensus in late September 2026 stood at $175.13 / $173.56 in different snapshots. On the FY27 guide itself, $120.18 is ~15.3x guided EPS of $7.85 (the screen's 13.5x uses a later year), versus ~19.6x before the August reset and Ralph Lauren at ~23.5x trailing in yesterday's peer check. That is a real de-rating discount for a brand still growing high-single-digits at a 23.4% operating margin — wider than HSY's cushion, narrower than BKNG's, and now with visible dispersion ($140-$175) in what the Street thinks the multiple should be.

### 6. Category position — #1 in accessible luxury, and increasingly a single-brand company

Coach, re-verified today from the 13 Aug 2026 reporting: FY26 revenue $6.915bn, +24% reported / +23% constant currency; Q4 +15% reported / +14% constant currency, with North America +10%, Greater China +30% and Europe +25%; ~9m of the group's 11m new customers, with Gen Z leading acquisition, and the Tabby franchise as hero product. That is share gain, with pricing power, in its category. The concentration is the flip side and it is growing: Coach is now ~86% of group revenue, while Kate Spade fell -10% reported / -11% constant currency in FY26 to $1.075bn (Q4: -7% to $235.1m, with a $28.9m adjusted operating loss in the quarter) and is guided to a further high-single-digit decline and a continued operating loss in FY27. Tapestry is #1 where it matters and has essentially one engine.

### 7. Value-trap risk — the honest bear case

Single-brand fashion risk is the whole bear case, and history rhymes: Coach's heat can fade as abruptly as it arrived — Capri's Michael Kors dilution is the standing example of an accessible-luxury brand that kept growing a customer file while its pricing power rotted — and Gen Z, ~35% of new customers, is the most fickle cohort in retail. If Coach growth halves again in FY28, FY27's "slowdown" becomes normalisation to low-single digits, a low-teens multiple is deserved rather than depressed, tariffs at the guided mid-20% assumption on US receipts bite harder than the roughly-neutral FY27 framing, a soft US consumer hits the home market that still anchors sales, and Kate Spade consumes investment indefinitely as a shrinking, loss-making second brand. Raymond James' $140 target (16 Oct 2026) is what that world looks like priced by a bull.

### 8. Rebound score: 7.5/10

**Verdict: A 16% single-day multiple reset on a ~$20m revenue-guide miss, backed by ~1.1-1.4x leverage, $1.86bn of FCF and mid-teens AUR pricing power at Coach — second only to BKNG in this batch, with single-brand fashion duration, and a fresh $140 bull-case target, as the honest caveats.**
