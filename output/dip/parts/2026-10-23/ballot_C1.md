# Panelist C — Moat & AI-Irreplaceability — DIP Ballot — 2026-10-23

## SINGLE-PICK BALLOT:
- **Top pick:** CPRT
- **Runner-up:** HWM
- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** Copart is the purest moat in this screen: a two-sided salvage duopoly (Copart/IAA ~80% of US salvage, Copart ~40-45%) built on 250+ mostly owned, permitted yards near metros, a buyer base across ~185 countries, and integrated title/tow/storage that makes insurer switching operationally painful — scored 9/10 for irreplaceability in the dossier. That moat is demonstrably intact in the dip: revenue per unit +5.7% and global ASP +5.5% in FY2026 while volumes fell, which a share-loser without pricing power cannot do. The balance sheet is the strongest in the batch — net cash, net debt/EBITDA -2.36, cash $4.49bn vs ~$0.09bn debt on the screen — and it funded $1,632.5M of FY2026 buybacks plus the all-cash ACV deal internally. At $29.09, -35.3% off the high and 16.1x forward versus a decade average near 29x trailing, I am not paying for the recovery, only for the stall continuing.
- **Why the dip is temporary (not permanent):** The hole is volume and mix, not franchise break: Q4 FY2026 US insurance units -7.5% (-8% for the year) was driven primarily by industry claims frequency (-3.4% YoY), and management stated that excluding one lost customer, domestic insurance assignments would have risen 2.3%. That lost account — widely identified as Progressive, partly reallocated to IAA — is real but bounded: independent decomposition puts the true shift at ~3 points, not the ~15 points first assumed. Underneath, total-loss frequency sits at record levels (~23%+) with severity +8.8%, which is the secular supply line for the model. The RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN flag re-checks as BENIGN on the FY2026 10-K: receivables consumed only $80.6M of cash all year against $1,480.4M net income, with CFO $1,604.5M above net income.
- **Moat / AI-irreplaceability:** Low disruption risk — irreplaceability 9/10. Software cannot replicate physical yards, tow networks and catastrophe surge capacity, and no model total-losses a car into an auction. AI is a tailwind inside the moat, not a substitute for it: vehicle software complexity (management's example of ~100M lines of code in a new Tesla) is precisely why cars get totalled into Copart's funnel, and Copart holds decades of auction data as an internal pricing lever. No substitute technology in the dossier commoditizes permitted salvage land or a 185-country buyer network.
- **Rebound catalyst & timing:** Q1 FY2027 print on 2026-11-19 — the first quarter to verify ex-lost-account assignment growth, cost-per-unit discipline, and early contribution from ACV Auctions, whose all-cash $10.50/share acquisition closed 2026-10-16 and adds dealer trade-in/wholesale volume (dealer units already +5.8% in Q4) as a non-insurance growth leg. Continued buybacks at de-rated prices and record total-loss frequency converting back into unit supply are the supporting legs through 2027.
- **12-18 month return scenario:** Base +25-35% (toward the screen mean target $39.00, +34.1%, on mid-single-digit FY2027 EPS growth and a modest multiple recovery); bull +50%+ if insurance assignments ex-lost-account keep growing, ACV contributes without diluting returns badly, and the multiple re-rates partway toward its historical premium.
- **Top risk (value-trap case):** Claims frequency keeps falling structurally on ADAS/safer cars, the Progressive reallocation becomes the first of several insurer dual-sourcing moves now that IAA has a 50-state footprint, cost-per-unit stays elevated, and lower-margin ACV permanently dilutes the tollbooth economics — leaving FY2026 as a plateau where EPS compounds at 3-5% and 16x forward is simply the correct new multiple. Management's non-quantitative disclosure means trap confirmation would arrive late.
- **Conviction (1-10):** 8
