### Claim 8 — FY2026 operating cash flow $1,604.5M > net income $1,480.4M; receivables build only ~$80.6M (RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN flag = denominator artefact)
**CONFIRMED — all three numbers exact in the primary filing; the interpretation is supported by them.**
- Copart consolidated statements of cash flows, year ended **2026-07-31** (FY2026), in the 8-K earnings release filed **2026-09-10** (Ex-99.1) and the FY2026 10-K: **Net income $1,480,397 thousand; Net cash provided by operating activities $1,604,492 thousand; Accounts receivable change −$80,629 thousand** (i.e. an $80.6M receivables build/use of cash). (8-K via QuoteMedia, https://app.quotemedia.com/data/downloadFiling?webmasterId=90423&ref=320342005&type=PDF&symbol=CPRT&cdn=09aabce08e073c6d1e25db9043811be1&companyName=Copart+Inc.&formType=8-K&formDescription=Current+report+pursuant+to+Section+13+or+15%28d%29&dateFiled=2026-09-10 ; 10-K cash-flow statement via NNTech PDF, identical figures.)
- Arithmetic: CFO/NI = 1,604.5 / 1,480.4 = **1.08×** — cash earnings *exceeded* reported earnings in FY2026, the opposite of earnings running ahead of cash. The $80.6M receivables build is 5.4% of net income and 1.7% of FY26 revenue ($4,666M), after a much larger $145.4M build in FY2024. Larger FY26 cash drags were elsewhere (income taxes receivable −$49.3M, prepaid/other −$47.6M, deferred taxes +$47.4M the other way, D&A +$238.5M). So a receivables-growth screen flag on CPRT for FY26 is indeed consistent with a ratio/denominator artefact rather than deteriorating cash conversion — that characterisation is the verifier's agreement with the dossier's reasoning, grounded in the filing numbers above, not a fact stated by the company.
