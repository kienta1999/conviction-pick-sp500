The drawdown is a one-plant Inari backorder (a low-single-digit-%-of-revenue niche) plus CEO-transition and disclosure-timing noise. At ~18.5x forward earnings versus its customary mid-20s premium, the market is charging compounder price for the problem and ignoring the franchise. The Oct 29 Q3 print is a near-term clearing event, with even trimmed analyst targets clustering $298–360 (+7% to +30%).
- **Why the dip is temporary (not permanent):** The single most compelling evidence is the disconnect between economics and price: +44% EPS growth, 23.6% ROE, and FCF exceeding net income while the stock fell ~30% — the damage came from one supply-chain incident and shifting guidance, not from demand, pricing power, or the installed base. Joint-replacement demand is deferred by demographics, not destroyed.
- **Moat / AI-irreplaceability:** LOW risk. #1 in orthopedics globally with the Mako robotic platform (installed base + surgeon training = switching costs), scale and regulatory-cleared product moats; AI is an ally (robotics/data), not a threat — nothing routes around a physical knee implant.
- **Rebound catalyst & timing:** Q3 earnings Oct 29, 2026 — confirmation the Inari backorder is clearing and ortho procedure volumes are re-accelerating resets the overhang; RBC carries a $420 Buy target. Orderly internal CEO handoff Jan 1, 2027 removes the second noise source.
- **12-18 month return scenario:** Base ~+30% to consensus ~$364–371 as estimates stabilize and the multiple re-rates toward the mid-20s; bull ~+45% ($400+) if Inari recovers by Q4 and ortho volumes beat, closing toward RBC's $420.
- **Top risk (value-trap case):** The Q2→Q3 "fixed by Q3" → "runs into Q4" communication reversal has genuinely damaged management credibility; two plaintiff-firm investigations are live, and ortho procedure recovery has repeatedly disappointed. If the backorder proves to be chronic execution rot at Inari rather than a plant fix, the premium derating is deserved.
