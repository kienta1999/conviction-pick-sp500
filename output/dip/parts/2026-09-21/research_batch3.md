## Earnings-quality flag briefs

### LVS — RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN
**Verdict: benign business-model artifact, with one yellow flag to monitor.**
The 10-Q (Q2 2026, filed 7/24/2026) shows accounts receivable *declined* — net AR $622M at 6/30/2026 vs $742M at 12/31/2025, and gross casino receivables (markers/credit extended to rolling/VIP players) fell $828M → $767M; mall receivables fell $80M → $39M. Casino markers are structurally part of the business model (credit is how premium rolling play works in Macau/Singapore), so the level of AR is not itself an earnings-quality signal.
The yellow flag: the provision for credit losses rose to $237M from $225M at year-end, and the H1 2026 provision charge was $65M vs $21M in H1 2025 — credit quality on extended markers is deteriorating even as gross balances shrink. That is consistent with the softer Macau high-end environment, not with earnings running ahead of cash. **Not a value-trap signature on current evidence; watch the provision trend.**

### NFLX — RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN
**Verdict: benign / likely screen noise — no earnings-ahead-of-cash signal.**
Netflix runs a prepaid-subscription model with a structurally tiny receivables base; MacroTrends data shows the cash-flow-statement change in accounts receivable was **$0M** for both the quarter and twelve months ended 3/31/2026 — i.e., AR is immaterial and not growing. The screen flag most plausibly reflects growth in "other current assets" (H1 2026: +$593M use of cash), consistent with the ramp of the ad business (ad-tier billing, agency receivables, in-house ad-tech build) — a business-model artifact of a new revenue stream, not channel-stuffing.
Cash conversion confirms it: H1 2026 operating cash flow $7.03B against net income $8.68B (content cash spend is the normal wedge), TTM FCF ~$11B, and a record $4.7B Q2 buyback. **Earnings are not running ahead of cash; flag is not actionable.**
