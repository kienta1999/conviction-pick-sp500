## HSY — The Hershey Company (chocolate / confectionery + salty snacks)

**Snapshot:** ~$160.34 (10/7/26 close) · 52wk $156.16–$239.48 · mkt cap ~$32.2B · 132-year-old company; 19,595 employees.
Finnhub 1-yr return −17.5%.

### 1. Why it's down
2025 was the brutal year: cocoa prices roughly doubled off the supply shock that began in 2024, tariffs added another layer, and full-year adjusted EPS collapsed to $6.31, down about a third (ainvest.com, Sep 2026). The 2026 story is a margin snap-back, but with a catch: Q2 2026 adjusted gross margin hit 41.6% (+350 bps YoY) on lower net commodity costs, a ~$100M productivity push, and higher prices — yet nearly all sales growth was price, not volume: ~12 pts of price vs ~8 pts of volume decline (in core North America confectionery, ~14 pts price vs ~10 pts volume). Hershey conceded it **lost share of the U.S. candy, mint and gum categories** in the quarter (ainvest.com). Public.com (crawled Oct 2026) cites 2Q'26 candy-aisle share down ~115 bps in dollars and ~190 bps in volume. New overhang (Sep 15, 2026): Jefferies flagged a potentially record-strong Super El Niño in 2026–27 as a risk for cocoa input-cost volatility (GuruFocus via ad-hoc-news.de). Earlier: on Apr 30, 2026 Q1 results (sales +10.6% to $3.10B, adj EPS +12.4% to $2.35) the stock still fell 1.8% as management warned elevated cocoa inflation would pressure margins through FY2026 (barchart via jewellgrain.com).
**Verdict: mostly TEMPORARY** — a commodity cost cycle that is already reversing (management projects cocoa cost deflation in 2027), but with a real, watch-worthy impairment in unit volumes and market share that price hikes caused.

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability
Moat = iconic brand (Reese's, Kisses, Kit Kat) + dominant U.S. confectionery scale (>35% of the domestic chocolate aisle per Public.com bull case) + manufacturing scale and snack acquisitions (Dot's Pretzels, Amplify, Pirate Brands, LesserEvil broadening the runway). AI cannot make, distribute, or eat chocolate — zero product-level disruption path; the only adjacent risk is GLP-1 appetite suppressants denting snack volume, which is a demand drag, not a substitution moat-break.
**Irreplaceability: 9/10.** Weak spot is execution (share loss), not moat erosion.

### 3. Rebound catalyst
CEO Kirk Tanner on the Q2 2026 earnings call: "plenty of cocoa supply globally," healthier inventories, diversified sourcing, encouraging 2026 and 2027 West African crop data — and **projected cocoa cost deflation in 2027** (Globe and Mail transcript via fooddrinklife.com, Sep 2026). CFO Dave Hulays at a September conference said Hershey has "good visibility into deflation next year from cocoa" (Motley Fool, Oct 6, 2026). Management guides 2026 organic sales +3–3.5% and adjusted EPS growth +32.5–35% (Motley Fool, Oct 4, 2026). Product-side: Hershey announced (March investor presentation) it will return to traditional milk/dark chocolate recipes across Reese's and others starting 2027 after the crisis-era reformulation backlash (walaw.press, May 2026). Timing: margin tailwind builds through H2 2026 into 2027; Q2 showed EPS beat ($1.90 vs $1.43 consensus, July 30, 2026 per MarketBeat). Note: one source lists next earnings Oct 29, 2026, another Nov 4 — report window is late Oct / early Nov.

### 4. Balance-sheet survival
D/E 1.03, current ratio 1.18 (MarketBeat, 10/7/26); cash ~$972M; TTM free cash flow **$2.1B**, with dividends only ~52% of FCF (Motley Fool, Oct 4, 2026). No distress risk; dividend was frozen ~2 years and restarted earlier in 2026 once cash flow supported it — discipline, not distress.

### 5. Margin of safety / valuation
Forward P/E ~18.9 (MarketBeat, 10/7/26; a Tradestie screen puts it at 16.3 vs ~21.0 historical average) vs trailing P/E ~21.9 — i.e., at or below its own multi-year average multiple of low-20s. Consensus: Hold (2.27/4), average price target **$203.67** across 22 analysts, +27% implied upside at $160 (MarketBeat, 10/7/26). Dividend yield ~3.6–4.5% (varies by source), payout ~78% — manageable but not spacious. Verdict: **genuinely cheaper than its own history**, priced as if earnings will decline (Tradestie: market implying −8.2% annual earnings decline), which looks overly pessimistic given the 32–35% 2026 EPS growth guide.

### 6. Category position
Still #1 in U.S. confectionery with real pricing power (took 12–14 pts of price), but **pricing power is currently costing share** — losing shelf in candy/mint/gum to Mars and leakage to premium, better-for-you, and private-label (Public.com, Oct 2026).

### 7. Value-trap risk
Super El Niño 2026–27 re-ignites cocoa costs and the whole 2024–25 margin collapse replays; volume keeps declining under further price increases (elasticity trap); share loss proves structural, not cyclical; the reformulation backlash (Reese's grandson publicly criticized flavor changes; some products fell below Canadian cocoa-solids standards) shows brand goodwill is spendable. Bear case summary: a price-led "recovery" selling fewer bars at higher prices while rivals take shelf.

### 8. Rebound score & verdict
**7/10** — Classic commodity-cycle dip with the margin snap-back already printing; cheap vs. own history with a 2027 deflation catalyst. Docked for share loss and volume elasticity risk.
**Verdict: the dip is a cocoa-cycle overreaction on a still-#1 brand; buy the margin snap-back, but watch whether 2027 volumes stabilize as prices roll over.**

## AZO — AutoZone, Inc. (auto parts retail, DIY + commercial/pro)

**Snapshot:** ~$2,846 (10/7/26) · 52wk $2,756–$4,293 · mkt cap ~$45.6B · 8,031 stores (FYE 8/29/26).
Finnhub 1-yr return −30.3%; YTD −16.1%; trading ~14% below its 200-day SMA (Stockopedia, Oct 2026).

### 1. Why it's down
Two acts. **Act 1 — misses:** roughly six straight quarterly EPS misses through fiscal Q1 2026 (reported Dec 9): EPS $31.04 vs ~$32.24 consensus, revenue $4.63B (+8.2%, comps +4.7–5.5% vs 5.6% expected), net income −6% to ~$531M; gross margin fell ~203 bps to 51.0% on a ~212 bps **non-cash LIFO inventory charge**, op margin compressed 19.7%→16.9%, and the WSJ highlighted tariff-related cost pressure (ts2.tech roundups, Sep 2026; Barron's). The Dec 2025 selloff was ~7%, the worst S&P 500 day. **Act 2 — Q4 FY2026 beat that didn't stick:** for the 16 weeks ended Aug 29, 2026, EPS jumped 15.1% to **$56.05** (above ~$54–55 consensus), revenue +5.6% to $6.59B (miss), gross margin +182 bps to 53.3% — but ~$96M of a one-time **tariff refund** plus a lighter LIFO charge did a lot of the work, so EPS quality was questioned; comps only +1.5% vs 3.9% TTM pace; DIY visibly softer (Simply Wall St; thestockobserver.com, Sep 23–25, 2026). Analysts cut targets post-Q4 (Goldman $3,917, Roth $3,850, BMO $3,500, DA Davidson $3,500, Baird $3,400, Mizuho $3,000) while mostly keeping Buy/Outperform ratings — a valuation reset after a premium-multiple run, not a business collapse.
**Verdict: mostly TEMPORARY** — the margin compression story is dominated by LIFO/tariff accounting noise; demand is still growing (revenue +5.6% in Q4). The watch item is comp deceleration (1.5% vs 3.9%) and DIY softness.

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability
Moat = store/density network (8,031 locations), parts-availability logistics, Mega Hubs (run ~16% higher commercial sales), and commercial-program relationships with repair shops — a switching-cost moat built on delivery speed ("get the part to the bay in 30 minutes"). DIY loyalty program and private-label add margin. AI threat: near zero for the physical product; cars are physical assets needing parts; e-commerce/AI search can route some DIY discovery (Amazon), but the commercial "fast logistics to the bay" advantage is AI-proof. Long-term EV transition (fewer moving parts) is a slow structural headwind, years away from mattering; meanwhile the aging U.S. vehicle fleet is a tailwind.
**Irreplaceability: 9/10** near-term; long-dated EV transition is the only credible structural risk.

### 3. Rebound catalyst
Management expects growth to **accelerate in fiscal 2027** on commercial + international (opened 374 stores in FY2026; 175 in Q4), per post-earnings coverage (Sep 2026). LIFO/tariff noise is inherently mean-reverting — Q4 gross margin already snapped back +182 bps as the LIFO headwind eased. Buyback engine keeps grinding: $697M repurchased in Q4 (~$697.5M), 2.2% of share count retired in FY2026, and management signaled buybacks could accelerate at low prices (barchart.com, Sep 2026). Commercial comp was +8.6% in Q4 and ~+11% for the year — the pro segment is still gaining share.

### 4. Balance-sheet survival
Total debt $9.02B incl. $1.36B commercial paper, cash $253.7M, $2.2B revolver capacity; adjusted debt-to-EBITDAR 2.5x on a TTM basis (10-Q for 36 weeks ended May 9, 2026 via stocktitan.net; CFO Jamere Jackson on Q3 call). FY2026 36-wk operating cash flow $2.12B; FCF ~$1.8B TTM; $1.3B spent on buybacks in that period. Investment-grade ratings maintained. Leverage is by design (negative-equity buyback machine) — 2.5x EBITDAR is the managed target and cash flow covers it comfortably. No distress or dilution risk.

### 5. Margin of safety / valuation
Trailing P/E ~17.7 (Finnhub) / 16.4–16.6 (Stockopedia) — **near the low end of its historical 18–22x range**. Stockopedia consensus target ~$3,712 (~+30% at ~$2,820–2,860); an older MarketBeat consensus $4,029 (~+33%). Analysts remain constructive (Goldman/BMO/Roth/Raymond James Buy/Outperform/Strong Buy) — targets were trimmed to reflect slower comp growth, not a broken thesis. PEG ~1.55 (MarketBeat). Verdict: **genuinely cheap vs. own history for a compounder of this quality** — the multiple derated because of six misses, while FCF kept growing.

### 6. Category position
**#1 U.S. auto-parts retailer** and gaining in the pro segment: FY2026 domestic commercial sales +10.6% to $5.76B, 6,443 commercial programs (+5.7%), available in 94% of stores; average weekly sales per program +6.0% TTM (tradingkey.com, Sep 2026). Pricing power intact; the softness is DIY, not share loss.

### 7. Value-trap risk
If DIY softness is structural (consumers deferring maintenance or losing the DIY habit) rather than cyclical, and if commercial comp growth (which slowed to 1.5% system comps) keeps decelerating, the "temporary LIFO noise" narrative fails and the premium multiple never returns. A seventh miss would cement the credibility problem. Tariff costs could persist and get passed through as margin drag rather than one-time noise.

### 8. Rebound score & verdict
**7/10** — The market is pricing the EPS-miss streak as structural, but the misses were overwhelmingly LIFO/tariff accounting on still-growing revenue; the FCF machine and commercial-share gains are intact, and the multiple is at its historical floor. Docked for the comp deceleration that needs to reaccelerate in FY2027.
**Verdict: a quality compounder being punished for accounting noise — buy the derating, with comp reacceleration in FY2027 as the confirming catalyst.**

### Earnings-quality flag: AZO RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN — verdict: **benign**
AutoZone's commercial (DIFM/pro) segment is explicitly a credit business: repair shops buy on terms, while retail DIY is cash/card. Commercial is the growth engine — domestic commercial sales +10.6% in FY2026 to $5.76B (~+11% for the year), commercial programs +5.7% to 6,443, commercial Q4 comp +8.6% — so receivables **should** outrun revenue: the mix is shifting toward the credit-bearing segment. The business remains profitable with FCF ~$1.8B and no disclosed collection-quality deterioration in the filings I reviewed. **Watch: allowance for doubtful accounts in the next 10-K (not verified here) — if bad-debt provisions rise, reassess.**

## COO — The Cooper Companies, Inc. (CooperVision contact lenses + CooperSurgical fertility/IUD)

**Snapshot:** ~$54.75 (10/7/26) · 52wk $51.01–$89.83 · mkt cap ~$10.8B · ~15,000 employees.
Finnhub 1-yr −20.7%; YTD −33.2%.

### 1. Why it's down
The Sept 9, 2026 FY-Q3 report (quarter ended Jul 31) was the detonation: revenue $1.066B (+1% YoY, **missed** ~$1.10B consensus); adjusted EPS $1.15 beat the $1.11–1.12 consensus, but GAAP EPS $2.24 was flattered by a $307.2M discrete U.K. tax benefit. CooperVision revenue was **flat at $717M** as the company "proactively reduced U.S. channel inventory," which "weighed on our results and will continue to impact Q4" — CEO Albert White attributed the overhang to H1 buy-in before price increases, distributor consolidation, and IT-upgrade stocking. Guidance was cut for the full year: revenue to $4.229–4.252B (from $4.29–4.32B; −~$59.5M, −1.4%), non-GAAP EPS to $4.51–4.55 (from $4.58–4.66), and Q4 EPS guide $1.05–1.09 vs ~$1.20 consensus (globenewswire, Oct 5, 2026; ad-hoc-news.de, Oct 2–3, 2026). Stock fell 14.67% to $54.17 on Sept 10. Compounding the damage: (a) the strategic review concluded with **no sale/spin of CooperSurgical** — offers "were not sufficiently attractive," disappointing deal-arbitrage holders (MarketBeat roundups); (b) **$272M of litigation expense** in 9M 2026 (net of $53.8M insurance recoveries) tied to CooperSurgical claims cut operating income to $403.8M from $542.5M YoY (stocktitan 10-Q FAQ); (c) PARAGARD faces a new non-hormonal IUD competitor with expected mid-single-digit declines (Stifel, Oct 1); (d) multiple law firms announced securities-fraud investigations; (e) analyst downgrades from Baird, UBS, JPMorgan, William Blair (Zacks holds a Strong Sell).
**Verdict: MIX — the destocking is temporary and management's "growth tied to consumption" reset is credible; but CooperVision flatlining, PARAGARD competition, and litigation are genuine, not one-quarter, impairments.**

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability
Moat = process technology + capital intensity in contact-lens manufacturing (daily-disposable torics/multifocals are precision-manufactured medical devices), prescription/eye-care-professional channel relationships (certification lock-in), and proprietary myopia-control (MiSight) in CooperVision; in CooperSurgical, fertility is a regulation-and-certification-heavy niche — gamete/embryo media, genetic testing, ART devices carry high switching costs for clinics, and PARAGARD is the established copper-IUD brand. AI cannot fabricate a lens, perform IVF, or insert an IUD — the product layer is AI-proof. Threats are adjacent, not substitutive: LASIK/orthokeratology and myopia-management alternatives at the margin; online lens retail (1-800 Contacts) pressures the distribution channel but not the manufacturing moat. This is a contact-lens maker, not an AI target.
**Irreplaceability: 8/10** (docked one point because vision-correction alternatives exist at the margin; fertility exposure is essentially irreplaceable).

### 3. Rebound catalyst
The Q4 reset is the catalyst setup: management says the **entire** guidance reduction was channel inventory — consumption demand, not end demand, which means Q1 FY2027 (quarter ending Jan 2027) laps a clean channel and CooperVision returns to growth (Stifel models 3.1% CooperVision growth for FY2027). FCF is accelerating: **$273M in Q3 (+66% YoY)**, FY2026 FCF guidance raised to $600–625M, with >$2.2B expected FY2026–28. The board upsized the buyback to **$3B (~$1.5B remaining)**; $444.7M repurchased in 9M FY2026 at avg $71.69 (now ~$54–57 — value-destructive in hindsight, but dry powder for accretive repurchase at current prices). Debt refinancing: $950M term loan amended/extended to Feb 2031, remaining $550M (due Dec 2026) to be repaid from FCF/revolver (cerbatgem, Mar 2026). Fertility keeps growing (+3% to $141.2M in Q3). Insider signal: three directors bought ~$1.33M of stock at $53.18–54.29 post-selloff (MarketBeat, Sep 15, 2026). Timing: Q4 FY2026 report (Dec 2026) will show whether the destock cleared as promised — that's the make-or-break event.

### 4. Balance-sheet survival
Total debt $2.54B at Jul 31, 2026 ($1.50B term loans + $966.7M revolver draw; $1.33B revolver capacity remaining), cash $154.7M, D/E 0.23 (stocktitan 10-Q FAQ; MarketBeat). Net debt ~$2.4B. Q3 operating cash flow strong; adjusted operating margin actually **expanded** 30 bps to 26.3% (66.7% adj. gross margin) on cost control. Easily survivable — leverage is modest and FCF covers the maturity wall.

### 5. Margin of safety / valuation
Forward P/E **12.5** (MarketBeat, 10/6/26) vs trailing ~19.4 — versus a historical multiple in the 25–35x range: **profoundly cheap on its own history**. Price/FCF ~9.4. Consensus: Hold (2.16/4), average target **$71.47** across 19 analysts, +25.9% implied upside at ~$56.75 (MarketBeat, Oct 2026); Stifel Buy $70. Divergences: Zacks Strong Sell ($4.53 EPS est). The cheapness is real — the discount is for credibility (guidance cut + investigations + litigation), not for balance-sheet risk.

### 6. Category position
CooperVision is a top-3 global contact-lens maker (behind Alcon, alongside J&J) with a specialty stronghold in torics/multifocals and myopia control; CooperSurgical is a **leader in the fertility/ART supplies niche**. Not #1 in lenses; #1-ish in fertility. Flat CooperVision growth is the share question — management says it was inventory, not demand; unproven until Q4 prints.

### 7. Value-trap risk
If CooperVision's flatness isn't just destocking — i.e., U.S. consumption itself is softening (price-increase buy-in suggests demand pull-forward, and Asia Pacific was down 10% reported / 5% CER in Q3 on portfolio rationalization) — then the "temporary" thesis dies. PARAGARD decline could accelerate with the new non-hormonal competitor. Litigation expenses ($272M in 9M) may recur; the securities investigations add overhang; management credibility is dented. Zacks' Strong Sell reflects a real fear: guidance misses may repeat because the company is now guiding to a channel it doesn't fully control. This is the riskiest of the three.

### 8. Rebound score & verdict
**6/10** — Dirt-cheap on FCF (9.4x) with an identifiable one-off cause (channel destock) and strong insider/board signals ($3B buyback, director buying). Capped at 6 because the impairment stack — flat lens growth, PARAGARD competition, litigation, credibility — is wider than the other two, and the confirming evidence only arrives with the Dec 2026 Q4 report.
**Verdict: the cheapest of the three with a clear Q4 proving event, but demand-verification risk makes it a smaller, staged position — confirm the channel clears before sizing up.**
