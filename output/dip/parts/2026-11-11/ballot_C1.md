## SINGLE-PICK BALLOT

- Top pick: KLAC
- Runner-up: LRCX
- Thesis (3-5 sentences): KLAC is the inspection tollbooth on every advanced wafer, and the dip is a multiple de-rating of a business that kept beating. Fiscal Q1 2027 revenue was \$4.14bn, +28.9% YoY, with adjusted EPS \$1.22 vs \$1.19 expected and a 45.0% operating margin, yet the stock is -49.7% off its 52-week high at a screen forward PE of 21.4. As geometries shrink to 3nm and 2nm and advanced packaging grows, process-control content per wafer rises — AI makes KLAC more necessary, not less. With about \$12.5bn of remaining performance obligations, purchase commitments of \$8.87bn, and \$8.81bn remaining under the repurchase authorisation, the rebound is a re-rating back toward a monopoly that never lost a customer, not a turnaround.
- Why the dip is temporary (not permanent): the single most compelling evidence the drop is about price, not the business
- KLAC beat estimates in all eight of its last reports, yet the share price fell in the first session after six of them — including the 28 July 2026 beat, followed by a 10.80% drop — a pattern the dossier attributes to the October AI-financing / CXMT / rates repricing, not to any print. Nothing in the Q4 FY26 or Q1 FY27 prints caused the sell-off.
- Moat / AI-irreplaceability: can AI or a substitute disrupt it? (low / medium / high risk — and why)
- Low risk. KLAC holds about 55-60% of the overall inspection/metrology market, 75-80% in patterned wafer inspection and 85% in optical inspection, with Morningstar rating it a wide moat, gross margins consistently above 61% and ROIC above 40% in the dossier's cited estimates. AI cannot replace inspection — smaller geometries and advanced packaging raise process-control content per wafer — and a new entrant cannot replicate multi-year qualification, proprietary optics and the installed base. The threats are geopolitical (China has at times been above 40% of revenue) and customer concentration (TSMC about 19% of revenue), not substitution.
- Rebound catalyst & timing: the concrete trigger and rough when
- December-quarter guidance issued 28 October 2026 — revenue \$4.7bn plus or minus \$200m and non-GAAP diluted EPS \$1.44 plus or minus \$0.11 — delivered at the next report (listed as 4 February 2027 by Finnhub / Gekko, with one vendor listing 28 January 2027), plus conversion of the \$12.5bn backlog against about twelve-month lead times. Management also raised its 2030 revenue target to \$26bn and said advanced-packaging process-control revenue will nearly double to about \$1bn in 2026.
- 12-18 month return scenario: rough base / bull case
- Base: re-rating toward the dossier's consensus target of about \$219.62 (+43.6% vs the 10 November 2026 close, screen analyst upside +44.9%) as the December and March quarters confirm the guided ramp. Bull: materially above consensus if the 2030 \$26bn target and the advanced-packaging doubling hold and the market re-prices KLAC back toward a wide-moat monopoly multiple rather than a cyclical equipment multiple.
- Top risk (value-trap case):
- China export controls bite harder than guided — China has been KLAC's largest single country by revenue at times — or the WFE cycle peaks in 2027 and the \$12.5bn backlog and \$8.87bn of purchase commitments turn from an anchor into a liability; insiders sold \$154.2m across 36 sales over the last 18 months with no open-market purchases, so there is no insider buying at a 50% drawdown.
- Conviction (1-10): 8

### Top 3 — compelling temporary-dip + intact-moat data point, and 12–18mo scenario

**1. KLAC.** The single most compelling point is the beat-then-fall pattern on top of a near-monopoly: eight straight beats, six followed by a down session, while patterned-wafer inspection share sits at 75-80% and optical at 85%, with services of about \$3.1bn in FY2026 growing steadily. Base: roughly the +44.9% screen analyst upside toward the \$219.62 consensus target; bull: above that if the \$12.5bn backlog converts and the 2030 \$26bn revenue target gains credibility.

**2. LRCX.** The single most compelling point is a record fiscal Q1 2027 — revenue \$8.22bn, +54.4% YoY, record memory revenue +120% YoY, record 40.2% operating margin and record free cash flow of \$3.24bn — sold off about 11.2% over 22-23 October 2026 on a China cliff and a clean-room-capped guide, while its Aether tools ramp in HBM manufacturing and cumulative Aether revenue is now expected at about \$2.5bn by the end of the decade. Base: roughly the +63.7% screen analyst upside; bull: above that if CY2027 WFE above \$200bn (management preliminary outlook) materialises and the March 2027 quarter catches up delayed shipments, with the \$25bn buyback authorisation approved 5 November 2026 as a floor.

**3. CAT.** The single most compelling point is a record Q3 2026 — sales \$20.68bn, +17% YoY, adjusted EPS \$7.20 vs \$4.95 a year earlier, and a record backlog of \$78.1bn, +96% YoY — sold off 9.25% on the print on a "moderate" Q4 guide and a planned dealer inventory drawdown of over \$1bn, while the dossier scores its moat 9/10 as the number-one heavy-equipment maker whose dealer network, installed base and resale value AI cannot replicate. Base: roughly the +42.7% screen analyst upside toward the \$823-884 average target range; bull: above that if Power \& Energy backlog converts and a 2027 dealer restock follows the Q4 drawdown.

### Deliberately left out of the top 10

Left out: APP, HOOD, BE, EXPE, ADBE. APP — AI is both its product and its disruption vector, the Q2 generative-AI video-tool delay is the concrete evidence the race is not won, and the stock already rebounded 73% in five sessions to 10 November 2026, so the trough entry is gone; also a class action with a 16 November 2026 lead-plaintiff deadline. HOOD — low switching costs, a business model (payment for order flow, crypto spreads) regulators and competitors can squeeze, and a verifiable forward PE of about 42x, not the screen's 23.7x, with prediction-market legality as a binary on about 12% of revenue. BE — a timing arbitrage against turbine lead times, not a fortress, with Q3 free cash flow of -\$181m and about \$5.8bn of supplier commitments against only \$0.72bn of binding customer orders. EXPE — the lowest irreplaceability in the dossier at 5/10, with AI disintermediation unresolved and both OTAs warning in their 10-Ks that agents may become the booking layer. ADBE — the clearest value-trap suspect in the set: AI-native and free tools attack routine creative work, organic net new ARR fell roughly 36-37% YoY in Q3 FY2026, and Citi (4 November 2026) warns of a "hard reset" with ARR growth of only about 5.3% in FY2027; cheap at 8.6x forward PE only if earnings hold, which the December FY2027 guide will decide.
