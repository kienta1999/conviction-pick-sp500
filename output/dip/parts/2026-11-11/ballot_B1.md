# Lens B — Growth / quality compounder — ballot, run date 2026-11-11

## SINGLE-PICK BALLOT

- Top pick: FIX
- Runner-up: KLAC
- Thesis (3-5 sentences): FIX is the cleanest growth-on-sale name in the dossier: Q3 2026 revenue rose 53.7% year over year to \$3.77bn with EPS of \$13.91 beating consensus of \$12.71, its 19th consecutive beat, while the stock sits 46.3% off its 52-week high at a forward P/E of 16.5 on the screen. The growth is contracted, not hoped for — backlog hit a record \$17.81bn at 30 September 2026, up 90% year over year and up 27% sequentially, with \$3.8bn of new bookings in the quarter and backlog now extending 18–24 months. Quality is confirmed by cash, not just accruals: Q3 operating cash flow was \$1.01bn and free cash flow \$806m, with net cash on the balance sheet (cash \$2,158.0m at 30 September 2026 against minimal debt). At roughly 15–17x forward earnings against consensus FY2027 EPS of \$66.81, up 31% on the dossier's figures, this is a durable compounder repriced as if its growth were ending.
- Why the dip is temporary (not permanent): the single most compelling evidence is that demand accelerated while the price collapsed — backlog up 90% year over year, bookings up 27% quarter over quarter, and technology customers rising to 59% of nine-month revenue from 42% a year earlier, all while Q3 free cash flow of \$806m exceeded operating income. The sell-off was priced on margin optics (gross margin 24.8%, a second straight sequential decline on equipment mix) and AI-sentiment, not on lost orders, lost share, or cash shortfall.
- Moat / AI-irreplaceability: low risk — FIX is the largest US mechanical/electrical contractor for data centres, and its work is physical, licensed-craft installation (HVAC, pipefitting, switchgear) that AI cannot do; AI data centres are the demand driver, not the substitute. The constraint is skilled labour, not customer demand, and FIX is expanding modular prefab capacity to over 4.0m sq ft by end-2026 and 6.0m sq ft by end-2027, a capital- and process-intensity edge small contractors cannot copy quickly. The deduction is customer concentration (59% tech revenue) and cyclicality, not substitutability.
- Rebound catalyst & timing: backlog conversion through 2027, with management guiding 2027 same-store revenue growth in the mid-20% range and job maturity normalising in H1 2027, which should stabilise margins; the nearer sentiment reset is Nvidia earnings on 17 November 2026 and hyperscaler capex confirmations, with the hard proof point at the Q4 2026 print expected around 18 February 2027 and 2027 guidance.
- 12-18 month return scenario: rough base / bull case: base roughly +35–45% over 12–18 months as the multiple re-rates toward the \$1,889–1,947 analyst target range cited in the dossier (screen analyst upside +64.0%) on delivered backlog conversion and margin stabilisation; bull roughly +60–70% if H1 2027 margins normalise, the mid-20s 2027 growth guide is met, and AI-capex sentiment resets, taking the stock toward the top of that target range.
- Top risk (value-trap case): hyperscaler capex actually slows — AI monetisation disappoints or data-centre moratoria spread beyond Texas and the local ballot measures — so the \$17.8bn backlog converts slower and bookings dry up, with the 18–24 month backlog duration delaying the signal; meanwhile a widening skilled-labour shortage caps throughput and equipment-heavy job mix keeps gross margin below 25%, turning a temporary margin wobble into a peak-margin story and leaving the multiple compressed.
- Conviction (1-10): 8

### Top 3 — single most compelling data point and scenario

**1. FIX:** The most compelling point is the combination of a record \$17.81bn backlog (up 90% year over year, eighth consecutive sequential increase) with Q3 free cash flow of \$806m — demand and cash both accelerating into a 46% drawdown, and the receivables flag judged benign because receivables rose 49% against revenue up 54%. Rough 12–18 month scenario: base roughly +35–45% on backlog conversion and H1 2027 margin normalisation; bull roughly +60–70% toward the \$1,889–1,947 target range if the mid-20s 2027 growth guide holds.

**2. KLAC:** The most compelling point is a structural near-monopoly — roughly 55–60% of inspection/metrology, 75–80% in patterned wafer inspection, 85% in optical inspection, with a 45.0% operating margin and estimated after-tax ROIC of about 34–44% in each of the last five fiscal years — that beat in Q1 FY2027 (revenue \$4.14bn, up 28.9% year over year) and still fell, backed by about \$12.5bn of remaining performance obligations. Rough 12–18 month scenario: base roughly +30–40% toward the \$219–220 consensus target (screen analyst upside +44.9%) as the December-quarter guide (revenue \$4.7bn plus or minus \$200m) is delivered; bull roughly +50% or more if CY2027 WFE strength and the raised 2030 revenue target of \$26bn re-rate the tollbooth.

**3. LRCX:** The most compelling point is a record fiscal Q1 2027 — revenue \$8.22bn, up 54.4% year over year, record 40.2% operating margin, record free cash flow of \$3.24bn — sold off on a China cliff and a financing scare, with a \$25bn buyback authorisation approved 5 November 2026 putting a floor under the stock and management guiding CY2027 WFE above \$200bn. Rough 12–18 month scenario: base roughly +40–50% toward the \$357–363 analyst target range (screen analyst upside +63.7%) as the March 2027 quarter catches up shipments delayed by clean-room constraints; bull roughly +60–70% if the China normalisation proves smaller than feared and the WFE ramp arrives.
