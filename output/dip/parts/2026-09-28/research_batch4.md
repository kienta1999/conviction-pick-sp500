## 1) SYK — Stryker Corporation (orthopedics / medtech instruments)

### Why it's down — transitory
- **Cyber incident:** Q1 2026 ransomware/cyberattack hit operations; Q2 (reported Jul 30, 2026) was the recovery quarter — sales $6.59B (+9.4% y/y, in line), adjusted EPS $3.69 (+17.9% y/y, beat). CEO Lobo: "significant progress in our recovery from the cyber incident" (StockStory, 2026-07-30).
- **Inari supply disruption:** July 30 call disclosed a supply disruption at the Inari Medical peripheral-vascular plant (acquired Feb 2025) creating a "meaningful backorder situation with lost sales." On Sep 8, 2026 CFO revealed the issue was NOT resolved as investors believed → stock fell 8.8% (−$26.70), wiping out >$10B market cap. Hagens Berman opened a securities investigation on Sep 16, 2026 (GlobeNewswire, 2026-09-16).
- **Sector derating + analyst cuts:** Sep 8, 2026 medtech sector-wide selloff (SYK −8.6%, peers ISRG −4.4%, BSX −4.2% — AlphaStreet); Wells Fargo cut PT $418→$348 (Overweight kept), lowering 2026 organic revenue estimate to $27,209M, below guidance range 8.3–9.3% (CoinCentral, ~Sep 9, 2026).
- **Insider selling:** ~351,000 shares sold by insiders (~$118M) over the prior quarter (CoinCentral, Sep 2026) — a sentiment headwind.
- **Verdict: TEMPORARY.** No product obsolescence, no lost moat — a cyber event plus a manufacturing/supply hiccup on a recently acquired unit, amplified by disclosure questions and medtech rotation.

### Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 9/10
- Global #1 in orthopedic implants + surgical robotics leader: Mako platform with **>2.5M cumulative procedures**, Q2 record installations, launches in shoulders and RPS (Zacks, 2026-09-23). FDA-regulated implants with deep surgeon relationships and hospital switching costs. >150M patients impacted annually.
- AI cannot implant a hip or sterilize a surgical tray; AI/AR is additive (SportSuite Vision on Apple Vision Pro, first hip arthroscopy milestone, Sep 2026 — Simply Wall St). Score: **9**.

### Rebound catalyst

Adjusted operating margin 27.4% (+170 bps) already inflecting. Opportunistic buybacks resumed (~$1B auth remaining). Timing: Q3 print (Oct 2026) and Q4 — any confirmation that Inari backorders normalized re-rates the name.

### Balance-sheet survival
- Jun 30, 2026: cash $3,391M, LT debt $14,192M, equity $23,988M (StockTitan, Jul 2026). H1 2026 operating cash flow $1.84B (up from $1.36B), Q2 FCF margin 16.1%. Manageable leverage for an investment-grade medtech giant — **no distress risk**.

### Margin of safety / valuation
- Trailing P/E ~28x (Finnhub); forward P/E **~17.2x** at $273 (StockStory) — a major derating vs. its historical 25–30x premium multiple. Consensus avg target **$386.28**, "Moderate Buy" (CoinCentral, citing analysts ~Sep 9, 2026); Simply Wall St narrative fair value $311.21 (~12% undervalued at $275). Implied upside to consensus ≈ **40%+**. **Genuinely cheap *because of* the dip.**

### Category position
- Still the #1 orthopedics/medtech franchise (MedSurg + Neurotech + Ortho each growing high-single-digit organic in Q2). U.S. vascular organic −6.7% is the localized wound, not share loss.

### Value-trap risk (honest bear)

Cyber remediation drag could persist; hospital capital budgets and procedure volumes are macro-sensitive; insiders sold $118M into weakness — they may know something about 2027 demand.

### Rebound score: **8/10** — "A cyber one-off plus a fixable supply blip knocked a #1 medtech franchise 30% off its high, and the forward multiple has collapsed to ~17x — a classic temporary-impairment buy-the-dip."

## 2) EFX — Equifax (consumer credit bureau / data)

### Why it's down — transitory (cyclical + regulatory sentiment)
- **Mortgage-cycle arithmetic:** Best quarter on record in Q2 (reported Jul 21, 2026): revenue $1.70B (+11% y/y), adjusted EPS $2.25 (+13%, top of guidance), U.S. mortgage revenue +25–40%. But H1 got a **pull-forward** — borrowers rushed to lock rates during the "interest-rate shock around the Iran conflict" (ainvest, Sep 2026) — and management kept full-year growth guidance near 10–11% even after a 14%-then-11% H1, mechanically forcing a decelerating H2. Market is front-running a mortgage growth stall.
- **Regulatory headline risk:** housing regulator intervention early Sep 2026 hit credit-scoring peers ~6.4% (ad-hoc-news, 2026-09-06); CFPB tightened complaint-portal access, spotlighting EFX dispute handling (Simply Wall St, Sep 2026); EFX −8.4% on Sep 4, 2026 with TRU −9.1% in a sector-wide selloff (AlphaStreet).
- **Settlement:** $100M FCRA settlement accrual in Q2 2026 tied to a credit-score coding issue (net $40M after insurance) — one-off but ugly headline (cnhinews, ~Sep 2026).
- **Verdict: TEMPORARY / cyclical.** Fundamentals strong; drawdown is rate-cycle fear + regulatory noise, not impairment.

### Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 8/10
- One of the Big-3 U.S. bureaus; **Workforce Solutions (The Work Number) — 217M active records, 108M unique SSNs** — is a 52.1% EBITDA-margin verification utility embedded in lender/employer/government workflows (Simply Wall St; AlphaStreet, Sep 2026). Oligopoly data network effects.
- AI is a tailwind here, not a threat: 54 new AI products launched H1 2026; management **doubled AI cost-savings target to $150M by 2028**; EBITDA margin ex-FICO up 120 bps to ~35% on AI productivity (Q2 call, Jul 21, 2026). Score: **8**.

### Rebound catalyst
- $300M in government contract wins/renewals in H1 2026 (~$100M new business, "principally benefit 2027"); **VantageScore mortgage transactions tripled QoQ to 2.2M** with 1,200 lenders testing the $1 score — a pricing/mix engine (Q2 call); **$750M Círculo de Crédito acquisition** (Mexico's fastest-growing bureau) closing Q4 2026, accretive in year one (Morningstar/PRNewswire, 2026-07-21); AI savings ramping to 2028; any mortgage-rate relief unlocks origination comps. Timing: Q3/Q4 2026 prints + deal close.

### Balance-sheet survival
- Jun 30, 2026: cash ~$170M, LT debt ~$4.1B, short-term debt/CP $1.41B (rising; CP up to $1.40B) — increasing reliance on short-term funding (cnhinews). But: leverage **under 3x EBITDA**, expected **>$1B FCF in 2026 with 100%+ cash conversion** (Q2 call), $1.5B capital capacity for the $750M deal. Levered but **no distress risk** — FCF comfortably covers it.

### Margin of safety / valuation
- Trailing P/E ~25.2x (Finnhub); ~30x at the July peak (GuruFocus, 2026-07-21) — the dip has compressed the multiple, though still richer than its own multi-year history (ainvest: "a valuation that never got cheap"). Consensus mean target **$215.16** (MarketBeat via ad-hoc-news, early Sep 2026); Simply Wall St fair value $214.95 — implies **~45% upside** from ~$148. **Cheap for Equifax, not absolutely cheap** — you're paying for the franchise, but the dip has removed the premium-entry penalty.

### Category position
- Still a Big-3 bureau with the **differentiated #1 position in income verification** (Workforce Solutions = 40% of revenue). Diversified markets growth accelerated 300 bps sequentially to 6% in Q2. No share-loss signal.

### Value-trap risk (honest bear)
- If mortgage originations stay depressed and the H2 deceleration is worse than guided, estimate cuts follow. Regulatory risk is the structural one: CFPB/Congress could cap pricing or open the bureau oligopoly; the VantageScore-vs-FICO migration complicates royalty economics; rising short-term debt ($1.41B CP) tightens flexibility if rates stay high; 2017 breach still producing residual costs ($0.4M in Q2).

### Rebound score: **7/10** — "Record fundamentals trapped in a mortgage-cycle narrative; government contracts, VantageScore momentum, and the Mexico deal give it 2027 earnings legs, with ~45% upside to street targets."

## 3) AZO — AutoZone (aftermarket auto-parts retail)

- **Serial post-earnings selloffs:** fell after 7 of the last 8 earnings reports, incl. −8.99% on May 26, 2026, the biggest (TipRanks, ~Sep 2026).

DIY segment soft.
- **New investment cycle:** distribution expansion + accelerated store openings (175 stores in Q4, 8,031 total) + wage/real-estate spend raising SG&A; Mizuho downgraded to Neutral, PT $3,550, warning "the cost of doing business is moving structurally higher" and +DD% annual EPS growth is at risk (Mizuho via BlueMatrix, ~May 2026).
- **Analyst PT cuts post-Q4:** Roth $3,850, DA Davidson $3,500, BMO $3,500, Raymond James $3,700, Baird $3,400, Mizuho $3,000 (MarketBeat roundup, 2026-09-23).
- **Verdict: TEMPORARY.** An investment-cycle margin overhang plus tariff/LIFO noise on top of a stock priced for flawless beats — demand (commercial, international +10.7% comps) is intact.

### Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 8/10
- Scale/distribution moat: 8,031 stores, megahubs, inventory breadth, and the high-service commercial (DIFM) channel — capital-intensive logistics that Amazon has failed to displace in installed-base parts. Negative-equity-by-design buyback compounder (bought back **>100% of inception outstanding shares since 1998**).
- AI can't replace a water pump or a repair; the real substitute threat is e-commerce/DIY software, not AI. Score: **8**.

### Rebound catalyst
- Management: "sales momentum improved later in the quarter" and growth to **accelerate in fiscal 2027**, helped by commercial and international (MarketBeat, Sep 2026); FY2027 free-cash-flow guidance raised on the Q4 call; $1.6B buyback authorization remaining after $2B FY2026 repurchases (−3.2% share count y/y — the EPS engine keeps running); tariff-refund tailwinds. Timing: FY2027 quarterly prints — the bar is now reset low.

### Balance-sheet survival
- Negative book equity (~−$2.8B) is **by design** (decades of debt-financed buybacks), not distress: $9.0B LT debt + $3.3B capital leases vs. **~$1.8B FY2026 FCF** (Q4 FCF $684M vs $511M prior year), leverage 2.5x EBITDAR, "liquidity very strong" per Q4 call (Motley Fool transcript, 2026-09-22). Not found: any covenant stress. **No distress risk** — the machine prints cash.

### Margin of safety / valuation
- Forward P/E **16.76x** (Forbes-Mirror market data, Sep 25, 2026), trailing ~18.2–18.8x; consensus rating **"Moderate Buy"**, consensus target **$3,724.58** (~30% upside; range $3,000–$4,800). Peers: ORLY forward P/E 27.8x (ChartMill) — AZO trades at a ~40% discount to its closest comp, on par-with-better FCF. **Genuinely cheaper because of the dip** — historically ~18–22x forward, now ~16.8x.

### Category position
- Still the **largest U.S. aftermarket auto-parts retailer by store count**, gaining share in commercial/international; the dip reflects margin/investment-cycle worries, not share loss to ORLY or Amazon.

### Value-trap risk (honest bear)
- Structural SG&A inflation is real: if the investment cycle never laps, +DD% EPS growth ends and the 18x multiple isn't cheap. Tariff pass-through could kill DIY demand; miles-driven sensitivity; repeated estimate misses have trained the market to sell rallies; the buyback masks organic EPS weakness.

### Rebound score: **7/10** — "The best compounder in consumer retail is in a self-inflicted investment-cycle trough, now ~17x forward vs. ORLY at ~28x — a classic overpunished quality name if FY2027 acceleration materializes."
