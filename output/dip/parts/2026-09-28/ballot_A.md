# Ballot A — Mean-reversion / catalyst analyst
**Date:** 2026-09-28 | **Lens:** weight the strength and timing of the rebound catalyst and the evidence the drop is *temporary* above all — the name most likely to re-rate fastest.

=== SINGLE-PICK BALLOT ===

- **Top pick:** SYK
- **Runner-up:** BKNG
- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** Stryker is the most classically temporary impairment in the slate: a ransomware incident in Q1 plus a supply disruption at the recently-acquired Inari plant — both fixable, both non-franchise — knocked a #1 orthopedics/medtech franchise ~30% off its high and collapsed the forward P/E from a historical 25–30x to ~17x. Underneath the noise, Q2 grew sales 9.4% with adj EPS +17.9%, adjusted operating margin inflected +170 bps to 27.4%, and management *raised* the FY2026 EPS midpoint. This is a one-off stack being priced as a broken growth story, which is the fastest possible re-rating setup: you don't need a cycle to turn, you need two one-offs to not recur.
- **Why the dip is temporary (not permanent):** The two causes have nothing to do with the moat — a cyberattack (a one-time event with remediation costs already rolling off) and a manufacturing backorder at Inari, where management has explicitly guided backorders to "a manageable level by end of Q3 2026." No product obsolescence, no share loss (MedSurg, Neurotech and Ortho each grew high-single-digit organic in Q2; the wound is localized to U.S. vascular at −6.7%), no reimbursement collapse. Cyber + supply chain are definitionally non-recurring.
- **Moat / AI-irreplaceability:** low risk (9/10). Global #1 in orthopedic implants; Mako robotics platform with >2.5M cumulative procedures and record Q2 installations; FDA-regulated implants, deep surgeon relationships, hospital switching costs. AI cannot implant a hip — Stryker's AI/AR work (SportSuite Vision on Apple Vision Pro, first hip arthroscopy milestone Sep 2026) is additive product, not a displacement threat.
- **Rebound catalyst & timing:** Q3 2026 earnings (~late Oct 2026) confirming Inari backorders normalized and cyber costs rolling off — the exact resolution management already promised. Buybacks resumed (~$1B authorization remaining) into the dip, and narrowed FY2026 guidance (organic 8.3–9.3%, EPS $14.95–$15.10, midpoint raised) anchors estimates. The Sept 8 disclosure scare (−8.8% session, Hagens Berman headline) is the kind of overreaction that evaporates on one clean print.
- **12-18 month return scenario:** base ~$350–370 (+28–36% to a $386 consensus target range) as the multiple drifts back toward ~22x on normalized earnings; bull $400+ (~+47%) if Inari fully recovers and the forward multiple re-rates to its historical 25–28x premium, since nothing about the franchise changed.
- **Top risk (value-trap case):** The bear case is disclosure, not demand: if the Hagens Berman investigation becomes a class action on the Sep 8 reveal, or insiders' $118M of selling into weakness foreshadows softer 2027 demand, the multiple stays pinned as "cheap but unownable." Also hospital capital budgets are macro-sensitive — a capex freeze would delay, not destroy, the thesis.
- **Conviction (1-10):** 8
