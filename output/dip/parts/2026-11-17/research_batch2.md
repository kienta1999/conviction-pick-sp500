Long-term EPS target ~15%. **Genuinely cheap vs its own history for a wide-moat compounder — but the history was written before agentic AI existed**, so the "cheap" verdict assumes the take rate survives. If agents capture the customer relationship, 13x forward is not a floor.

### 6. Category position
Still #1 — the world's largest OTA, #1 in its GICS sub-industry by market cap (screen), dominant in Europe and independent hotels. No share loss is visible in the numbers: room nights +7%, gross bookings +10%, Connected Trip verticals all accelerating. The threat is not a rival OTA (Expedia/Airbnb are the old war) but a new species of competitor: Meta, ChatGPT, Claude, Google as the trip-planning front door. Pricing power: take rate is under pressure from the indirect channel (paid search on Google, where AI overviews are reducing organic traffic) rather than from hoteliers.

### 7. Value-trap risk
The bear case: this is the classic innovator's-dilemma trap — every quarter the operating numbers look fine (Q3 beat on every metric) while the strategic ground shifts underneath. If travelers start trips inside an AI assistant, Booking pays more to acquire each booking (marketing expense already rose 11% in Q2, faster than gross bookings), the direct channel shrinks, and the take rate compresses — a slow, grinding de-rating that a 13x multiple and buybacks cannot outrun. Add: FTC exposure beyond the $1B accrued, a new EU DSA probe, and buybacks funded with debt into a demand slowdown. The Value Thesis (Oct 2026) rates it Hold precisely because "I can't comfortably predict its future anymore."

### 8. Rebound score: 6/10
**Verdict:** A world-class travel compounder at a 5-year-low multiple with a real Q4 air pocket and a real AI question — the rebound case is valuation + buybacks + episodic demand headwinds fading, but the AI-disintermediation debate has no near-term resolution, so size it as a cheap compounder, not a snap-back.
