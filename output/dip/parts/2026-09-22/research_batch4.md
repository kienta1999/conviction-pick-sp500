## 1. EFX — Equifax Inc. (~$158.62; -38.4% off 52w high of ~$271.84; YTD -26.9%)

### 1. Why it's down
Three compounding drivers over 2026:
- **Frozen mortgage market.** After Q2 2026 results (2026-07-21), Equifax cut full-year revenue guidance to $6.71–$6.78B, below the $6.76B consensus (LSEG) — stock fell ~7% that day. Management blamed elevated mortgage rates (~6.6% for 30-yr vs ~4.3% decade average) suppressing purchase and refi volumes. (Motley Fool, 2026-07-21; Sahm Capital, 2026-07-21)
- **Regulatory threat to mortgage data economics.** In April 2026, FHFA said Fannie Mae/Freddie Mac would accept VantageScore 4.0 (breaking FICO's monopoly); on 2026-09-04 FHFA director Pulte warned the agency is "seriously considering bi-merge" — moving from mandatory tri-merge (all three bureaus pulled on every agency loan) to bi-merge would remove ~1/3 of mortgage report volume industry-wide. EFX dropped ~8.6% on Sept 4 with no company-specific news. (QuiverQuant, 2026-09-04; Longbridge, Sept 2026)
- **Debt-funded M&A.** Acquired Mexico's Círculo de Crédito for $750M in 2026 (Sahm Capital, 2026-07-21), raising leverage just as the core cycle weakened.

**Verdict: mostly TEMPORARY (cyclical + sentiment/policy overreaction), with a smaller permanent tail risk from bi-merge.** Mortgage volumes are rate-driven and cyclical; the VantageScore/bi-merge fears hit *revenue per loan* assumptions, but the oligopoly structure remains intact.

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability
Credit-bureau oligopoly (EFX/Experian/TransUnion): the moat is the proprietary data flywheel — 15,000+ lenders contribute loan performance data that no model can recreate without the same contributor network — plus Workforce Solutions' payroll-database monopoly in income/employment verification. AI is a *tool* for credit modeling, not a substitute for the underlying verified consumer data; generative AI cannot conjure 200M verified consumer files. Threats are regulatory (bi-merge, VantageScore acceptance), not technological.
**Irreplaceability score: 8/10.**

### 3. Rebound catalyst
- **Refi pipeline coiled spring:** CEO Mark Begor on the Q1 2026 call (2026-04-21): a "significant pipeline" of borrowers hold mortgages at 5–6%+ and are ready to refinance once rates drop 25–50 bps. Any Fed easing (hawkish stance has been the binding constraint) would release a refi wave — EFX's highest-margin revenue.
- **Next earnings 2026-10-20:** Q3 print could reset expectations; a guidance re-raise or mortgage-uptake signal would re-rate.
- **Policy clarity:** bi-merge is a proposal, not a rule; FHFA walking it back (or bureau lobbying succeeding) removes the September overhang.

### 4. Balance-sheet survival
D/E 0.89, debt-to-capital ~48.7% (Zacks, 2026-06-30 data), ROE 21.6%, net margin ~10.7–11.1%, cash flow $13.90/share. Pays a $2.24 annual dividend (1.27% yield, payout ratio 0.27). Elevated leverage from M&A but investment-grade economics, positive FCF, no dilution risk. **Can easily endure.**

### 5. Valuation / margin of safety
- Forward P/E ~17.9 (MarketBeat, Jun 2026) vs trailing ~27 and a multi-year average in the mid-20s to ~30 — this is the cheapest EFX has been vs its own history in years.
- Consensus mean target $225.28 (19 analysts, "Moderate Buy") → +46.7% upside (MarketBeat, 2026-06-23); Stockopedia mean $214.12 → +34.8% (2026-09-21).
- Analyst 2026 EPS consensus $8.65, 2027 $10.26 — the market is pricing ~18x 2026 adj. EPS for a business that historically commands ~25x in normal rate environments.
**Genuinely cheap because of the dip, not a broken multiple.**

### 6. Category position
Still a co-#1 of the global bureau triopoly with Experian/TransUnion; Workforce Solutions is the dominant income-verification provider. No share loss to any competitor — the drawdown is rate-cycle and policy-sentiment driven, not competitive erosion.

### 7. Value-trap risk
The honest bear case: **bi-merge becomes rule** — permanently removing ~1/3 of mortgage pull volume and compressing EFX's highest-priced data product; **VantageScore 4.0 adoption** erodes FICO-score-linked revenue (EFX co-owns VantageScore, partially offsetting, but economics differ); mortgage rates stay high-for-longer through the Iran-war oil-inflation episode, keeping Workforce Solutions verification volumes depressed as hiring stalls. If US housing structurally freezes for years, EFX is a lower-growth utility re-rating to ~15x.

### 8. Rebound score & verdict
**Rebound score: 7.5/10.** **Verdict: deepest drawdown of the batch on cyclical rates plus policy headlines that punish sentiment more than cash flow — the coiled refi pipeline and ~18x forward P/E make this the best risk/reward of the three.**

## 2. COO — The Cooper Companies, Inc. (~$53.91; -34.4% off 52w high; -23.8% vs 200-day SMA)

### 1. Why it's down
- **Q3 FY2026 miss and guidance cut (2026-09-09):** revenue $1.07B missed; CooperVision revenue fell to $717M on weak contact-lens demand and reduced U.S. channel (distributor) inventory. FY2026 adj. EPS guidance cut to $4.51–$4.55 from $4.58–$4.66; revenue cut to $4.23–$4.25B from $4.29–$4.32B. Stock tumbled ~14.7% that day. (Barchart, 2026-09-18; Motley Fool, 2026-09-10)
- **Asia-Pacific weakness:** CooperVision APAC revenue fell 6% in Q2 2026 on soft Japan/China consumer demand; management expects another decline in Q3 and admits legacy hydrogel rationalization will drag through 2027. (Zacks, 2026-08-13)
- **Deal premium evaporated:** board completed a strategic review and announced it will NOT sell CooperSurgical (Motley Fool, 2026-09-10) — removing the buyout floor investors had priced in.
- **Margin headwinds:** ~$22M of tariff costs for FY2026, unfavorable FX, and Q2 2026 took a litigation charge settling 95%+ of claims from the Dec 2023 CooperSurgical embryo-culture-media recall. Multiple law firms (Rosen, Bragar Eagel, Levi & Korsinsky, Kirby McInerney) have since announced securities investigations — no wrongdoing established, but headline risk. (themarketsdaily, 2026-09-16)
- **Analyst capitulation:** consensus cut to "Hold" (18 analysts); mean target $70.44, +29.1% upside; consensus target lowered 14.83% to $72.27 post-earnings. (Barchart, 2026-09-18)

**Verdict: TEMPORARY (channel destocking + one-off recall/tariff drag + deal-premium removal), with an extended-tail risk in Asia-Pacific/hydrogel.** U.S. distributor destocking is a classic air-pocket that reverses; the China/Japan softness may linger.

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability
Contact lenses are a medical-device franchise: FDA/CE clearance, practitioner fitting habits (switching lenses requires a new fitting), manufacturing precision, and in myopia control (MiSight — CooperVision is the category leader in pediatric myopia management), clinical evidence accumulates over years. AI cannot substitute a physical lens on a cornea. Competition is the traditional four-player oligopoly (J&J, Alcon, B+L, Cooper), not a tech disruptor.
**Irreplaceability score: 9/10.**

### 3. Rebound catalyst
- **Channel restock:** U.S. distributor destocking is finite; management warned Q4 still pressured, implying normalization in early FY2027 (Nov 2026 onward).
- **Asia-Pacific recovery:** management expects the region back to market-level growth by Q4 2026 (Zacks, 2026-08-13); China/Japan re-acceleration would restore the CooperVision growth algorithm.
- **Capital returns as signal:** board authorized a **$3B share-buyback program** (~22.7% of outstanding shares) and three directors bought ~$1.33M of stock at $53–54 (Sept 2026); FY2026 FCF guided $575–$625M (+33–44% YoY). (themarketsdaily, 2026-09-17; AInvest)
- **Tariff refund optionality:** potential refunds on the $22M tariff cost.

### 4. Balance-sheet survival
Net debt $2.39B vs EBITDA ~$928M → ~2.6x leverage — moderate for a device company. Operating cash flow $1.03B, FCF $678M (AInvest statistics); interest coverage 6.83x (stocktitan). D/E 0.31. **No distress; easily funds the $3B buyback from FCF + revolver.**

### 5. Valuation / margin of safety
- P/E 18.44 (AInvest), EV/EBITDA ~17.6 (MarketBeat, Q3 2026) — vs a multi-year premium of ~23–30x EV/EBITDA when compounding well (MarketBeat history table: EV/EBITDA 20.9–23.6 in 2024). The premium has compressed materially.
- Mean target $70.44 (18 analysts, Hold) → +29.1% upside; target range skewed to upside post-cut. (Barchart, 2026-09-18)
**Genuinely cheaper because of the dip — the stock has de-rated from quality-compounder premium to near-market multiples.**

### 6. Category position
#2 globally in contact lenses behind J&J Vision (with Alcon, Bausch+Lomb); the global leader in myopia-management lenses (MiSight). CooperSurgical is a niche leader in women's-health surgical devices. The dip reflects demand/channel, not share loss — no evidence of competitive displacement.

### 7. Value-trap risk
The honest bear case: **hydrogel rationalization drags through 2027** and APAC demand never re-accelerates (Japan/China contact-lens softness proves structural as demographics bite); **CooperSurgical retention** means the conglomerate discount persists without the sum-of-the-parts catalyst; the securities-law-firm investigations escalate into an actual restatement/litigation overhang; tariffs/FX keep gross margin below the 66%→68% recovery path. Then COO is a ~15x med-tech compounder in a low-growth category.

### 8. Rebound score & verdict
**Rebound score: 7.0/10.** **Verdict: a classic channel-destocking air pocket in a defensible med-tech franchise, with a $3B buyback and insider buying providing a floor — but APAC/hydrogel drag through 2027 caps the near-term snap-back.**

## 3. SPGI — S&P Global Inc. (~$407.59; -21.0% off 52w high of ~$529; YTD ~-17%)

### 1. Why it's down
- **February 2026 guidance shock:** 2026 adj. EPS guided $19.40–$19.65 vs $19.96 consensus — a small miss that triggered an ~18% slide as a "Steady Eddie" premium name reset expectations. Management signaled ratings issuance would normalize after the 2024–2025 refi/maturity-wall boom. (financialcontent/marketminute, 2026-02-10)
- **AI disruption narrative:** release of Claude Opus 4.6 (financial-research AI) sparked fears that generative AI commoditizes Market Intelligence's data/analytics, pressuring the whole financial-data sector including SPGI. (Morningstar, ~2026-03; TipRanks)
- **Rate backdrop:** hawkish FOMC through 2026 (July 28–29 meeting: 3 dissents favoring a *hike* over a hold) reduces corporate borrowing/refinancing → less new debt to rate. (Motley Fool, 2026-08-06)
- Despite solid Q2 2026 (2026-07-28): revenue +10% YoY (+11% ex-Mobility), record quarters in Ratings and Indices — the stock stayed de-rated; JPMorgan cut target $555→$530 (kept Overweight). Zacks Rank #4 (Sell) on estimate revisions; 2026 consensus EPS fell 4.5% in 4 weeks. (Zacks, 2026-08-21)

**Verdict: TEMPORARY (guidance reset + sector-wide AI sentiment), not fundamental.** Ratings and Indices posted record revenue in Q2 2026; the earnings power is intact.

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability
The deepest moat in the batch: credit ratings are a regulated duopoly (SPGI + Moody's) embedded in bond covenants, bank capital rules, and ETF mandates — issuers *must* buy ratings; S&P Dow Jones Indices' benchmarks underpin trillions in passive AUM (switching an index breaks tracking); Platts energy benchmarks are the reference price for physical commodities. Morningstar reaffirmed a **Wide** economic moat and $570 fair value (2026-03). AI is a workflow tool for analysts, not a substitute for NRSRO-licensed ratings or contractually referenced benchmarks. Only Market Intelligence faces modest AI workflow risk.
**Irreplaceability score: 9.5/10.**

### 3. Rebound catalyst
- **Mobility spin-off completed 2026-07-01** — pure-play focus on four higher-margin businesses; margin expansion thesis now cleaner.
- **Buyback firepower:** 2026 repurchase target raised to **>$7B** ($1.5B done in H1; ~$5.5B for 2H26) — ~4–5% of shares at current prices, amplifying EPS. (Zacks, 2026-08-21)
- **Q3 earnings 2026-10-29:** any stabilization in estimate revisions + ratings-volume commentary could break the Zacks #4 overhang.
- **Issuance cycle:** rate cuts would restart the refi machine; AI-infrastructure capex (data centers, utilities) is a structural new source of rated debt issuance.

### 4. Balance-sheet survival
Debt/EBITDA 1.91, interest coverage 22.3x, D/E 0.43 (stockanalysis, 2026-09-18); TTM FCF $4.88B (3.8% FCF yield); quarterly dividend $0.97 (~0.95% yield); shares outstanding down 2.79% YoY. **Fortress; zero survival question.**

### 5. Valuation / margin of safety
- Forward P/E 21.5 (stockanalysis) vs ~29–30x at the peak and a 10-year average P/E of 41.2 (AInvest) — a meaningful de-rating, though not "distressed."
- Mean analyst target $523.59 (20 analysts, "Moderate Buy") → +29.4% upside (MarketBeat); Morningstar fair value $570 → +40% (2026-03).
- EV/EBITDA 16.1, PEG 1.72. **Cheaper than its own history on every metric — the dip genuinely opened a margin of safety, though the multiple is still above market.**

### 6. Category position
#1 global credit rater (duopoly with Moody's), #1 index provider by licensed AUM (S&P 500/Dow Jones brands), and the dominant energy benchmark provider (Platts). Pricing power intact — ratings fees are effectively a toll on global debt markets.

### 7. Value-trap risk
The honest bear case: **2024–2025 issuance was pulled forward**, so "normalization" is actually a multi-year earnings plateau and the $19.4–19.65 guide is the new run-rate, not a trough; **AI-native research tools** genuinely compress Market Intelligence pricing over time; passive-fee compression eventually leaks into index licensing. Then SPGI is a low-teens grower stuck at ~20x — dead money, not a disaster.

### 8. Rebound score & verdict
**Rebound score: 7.5/10.** **Verdict: the highest-quality franchise of the three, de-rated by AI sentiment and a guidance reset while record Ratings/Indices revenue argues the moat is intact — a $7B buyback and the clean post-spin portfolio are the catalysts back toward $500+.**
