## SPECIAL FLAG — CPRT RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN

**Facts (FY2026 10-K/8-K, reported 9/10/2026, via Morningstar/Stockopedia):** Net receivables rose to **$859M from $763M (+12.6% YoY)** while FY2026 revenue grew only **+0.4%** to $4.7B. The flag is real on its face. However:
- **Operating cash flow was $1.60B vs net income $1.48B in FY2026** — cash conversion still exceeds 1.0x; earnings are NOT running ahead of cash.
- **Benign business-model explanation fits:** Copart's receivables are overwhelmingly short-cycle auction settlements — the company collects from buyers (often via cash/ACH at auction close) before remitting net proceeds to insurer sellers; timing of quarter-end settlements mechanically swells receivables when vehicle-sales mix shifts. Q4 FY2026 vehicle *sales* (principal-basis, which carry receivables) grew **+8.3%** while service revenue grew only +1.4% — the mix shift toward principal sales inflates the receivables line. International growth (+11.6%) also carries longer settlement cycles.
- **Watch item, not red flag:** Allowance for credit losses jumped from $354K to $5.6M YoY — tiny in absolute terms ($5.6M on $859M receivables) but worth monitoring for buyer-credit deterioration.

**One-line verdict:** Benign — receivables growth is auction-settlement timing and principal-sales mix shift, not channel stuffing; operating cash flow still exceeds net income, so this is not the value-trap signature.
