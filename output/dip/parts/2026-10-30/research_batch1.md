## 1. AMAT — Applied Materials (semi equipment)

### 1. Why it's down — temporary vs permanent
A valuation de-rating on top of a parabolic run, not a demand collapse. Shares ran from a 52-week low of $154.47 to a high of $739.67 within a year, then fell ~42% from the 30 June 2026 high to $430.01 by 15 Oct 2026 (Trefis, updated 16 Oct 2026). The fall continued to $377.24 by 23 Oct 2026, −22% in 30 days from $485.00 on 25 Sep 2026 (TickerOn). Drivers cited: (a) a broader semiconductor-equipment selloff on doubts about AI-infrastructure financing sustainability; (b) a peer trigger — Lam Research's record quarter still sold off after it flagged supply-chain constraints capping its near-term outlook; (c) Citi warning in late October of further downside for equipment names while keeping a Buy on the group; (d) AMAT-specific disappointment at its 13 Oct 2026 investor presentation, which gave 2030 margin targets (gross ~54%, operating ~40%) and a +2pp wafer-front-end share-gain goal plus services revenue "over" $12bn, but no overall 2030 revenue target — read as not ambitious/specific enough for a premium multiple (Motley Fool, 13 Oct 2026). Meanwhile fundamentals kept improving: fiscal Q3 2026 (ended 26 July 2026, reported 13 Aug 2026) revenue $9.12bn, +24.8% YoY, non-GAAP EPS $3.50 (record, beat by $0.10), non-GAAP gross margin 50.4% in one account / 55.4% semiconductor-systems margin in another — see note below — and Q4 guidance of $10.25bn ± $0.5bn (+51% YoY at midpoint) and EPS $4.02. China share fell to ~28% from ~35% a year earlier on US export controls — a real, durable headwind, but partly offset by AI-led demand elsewhere. **Verdict: TEMPORARY (multiple compression + sentiment + China mix), with a structural China cap. Not a permanent impairment.**
Note on margin figures: sources disagree on which margin the 55.4% refers to (segment vs group); the Q3 non-GAAP gross margin of 50.4% and record operating margin of 34% are from the Fool's 16 Aug 2026 report. Do not quote 55.4% as group gross margin without checking the earnings deck.

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 8/10
Largest semiconductor-equipment maker in the world; leadership across deposition, etch, e-beam metrology and advanced packaging (including HBM packaging), plus Applied Global Services (maintenance + yield data). Moat: process-technology/IP, installed-base switching costs (fabs qualify tools for years; service/parts lock-in), and co-innovation at the EPIC Center — May 2026 TSMC partnership and 6 Oct 2026 Intel collaboration on next-gen transistors/advanced packaging. AI does not replace these tools; AI chip complexity (gate-all-around, advanced packaging, HBM) *increases* demand for them. Threat is cyclical capex, export controls and customer concentration, not substitution. A generic AI model cannot deposit or etch a wafer.

### 3. Rebound catalyst
Next print is the catalyst: fiscal Q4 2026 earnings after close Thu 12 Nov 2026, consensus ~$4.06 adjusted EPS (+87% YoY) and ~$10.29bn revenue (Barchart, 28 Oct 2026). A beat plus January-quarter guidance confirming 2027 growth (management already said it expects "another strong year of growth in 2027") would directly test the "AI capex is unsustainable" thesis. Secondary: 2027 capex-as-%-of-sales falling (management, via Trefis 29 Oct 2026), restoring FCF conversion and buybacks; the 26 Oct 2026 move to raise the BESI stake to ~20% from ~9% deepens advanced-packaging exposure.

### 4. Balance-sheet survival
No distress risk. At end-Q3 FY2026 (26 July 2026): cash + equivalents $7.04bn, short-term investments $2.20bn, long-term investments $5.27bn — total cash/investments ~$14.5bn per the Q3 deck — against total debt ~$6.54bn (short-term $1.30bn + long-term $5.25bn); net cash, screen ndE −0.19. Q3 operating cash flow topped $3bn (record) and FCF $2.33bn, up from $2.05bn a year earlier, though TTM FCF slipped to $5.62bn from $5.82bn because capex rose to ~9% of revenue (vs a 2.8% historical norm) on the EPIC Center/Singapore capacity build. Returned $860m in Q3 ($440m buybacks + $420m dividends); TTM buybacks fell to $2.03bn from $5.49bn — a watch item, not a solvency issue. Management still targets returning 80–100% of FCF.

### 5. Margin of safety / valuation
Genuinely de-rated, not obviously cheap on absolute earnings power. Forward P/E compressed from ~49x at the late-June 2026 peak to the low-20s (TickerOn) / screen 22.7x. Analyst mean target ~$636–$647 (Zacks 14 Oct 2026: $647.09, 33 targets, range $515–$900; Barchart 28 Oct 2026: $635.70, 26 Strong Buy + 3 Moderate Buy + 8 Hold of 37; screen upside +48.9%). Targets were being *cut* into the fall (Evercore $700→$590 on 15 Oct, Needham $740→$560 on 16 Oct), so the mean is stale-upward. Precise multi-year median forward P/E: not found in sources checked. Versus peers at 23 Oct 2026 (Apex): LRCX fwd 24.88x, Onto 16.84x — AMAT at ~21x then was the cheaper large-cap equipment name, but still a growth multiple that needs the Q4 guide to hold.

### 6. Category position
Still #1 in materials engineering / wafer-front-end equipment by revenue, and guiding to *gain* 2pp of WFE share by 2030. No evidence of share loss in Q3: Semiconductor Systems guided +62% YoY in Q4. The dip is a multiple story, not a share-loss story — so far.

### 7. Value-trap risk
The honest bear case: AI capex normalises in 2027 and equipment orders — the most cyclical line in semis — fall first and furthest; a $10.25bn Q4 (+51%) sets a brutal comparison base. China export controls tighten further, capping the second-largest market. Capex intensity stays elevated (EPIC, Singapore), keeping FCF conversion below the earnings headline. If the 12 Nov print guides flat/down for Q1 FY27, the "low-20s" multiple can compress further — it did exactly that after a record Q3.

### 8. Rebound score: 8/10
**Verdict: The cleanest "record results, halved multiple" setup in this batch — buy-the-dip works if, and only if, the 12 Nov print confirms 2027 demand; size it as an earnings-event bet, not a blind multiple bet.**

## 2. KLAC — KLA Corporation (semi process control)

### 1. Why it's down — temporary vs permanent
Same equipment de-rating as AMAT, amplified by a higher starting multiple and a sector-wide 22 Oct 2026 selloff. KLAC fell from a split-adjusted 30 June 2026 high of $307.37 to $145.61 by 25 Oct 2026 (~−52.6% on that series; screen −44.4% off high on its own series) and −9.55% on 22 Oct 2026 alone ($163.43→$147.83) on shipment-constraint / AI-spending concerns (InTickers analysis, Oct 2026). Q4 FY2026 (28 July 2026) was strong — FY2026 revenue $13.6bn, +11.7% — but did not stop the slide, because the market questioned whether premium assumptions justified the price. Headwinds named on the July call: memory pricing, tariffs, and longer-lead-time material shortages (lead times ~12 months, 18–24 months on some products); China fell to 29.8% of FY2026 revenue ($4.05bn) from higher levels, with CBP holding some China-bound shipments and customer deposits down to $433.94m from $643.19m on cancelled-order returns, plus a guided $300–350m CY2026 export-control revenue impact (Indra prep, Oct 2026). **Verdict: TEMPORARY (multiple + cycle + export-control friction). No moat break identified.**

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 9/10
Near-monopoly economics in process control: the tools that find defects, measure wafers and manage yield. As nodes shrink and packaging gets more complex, inspection/metrology content per wafer *rises* — AI accelerators are KLAC's tailwind, not its replacement. Moat: process-tech/IP in optics and algorithms, qualification lock-in (a defect missed costs a fab far more than the tool), and a growing services installed base ($850m services revenue in Q1 FY27, +14%). No credible substitute routes around physically inspecting wafers; in-house efforts by chipmakers have historically failed to match KLAC's defect sensitivity at volume.

### 3. Rebound catalyst — ALREADY PRINTING
The catalyst arrived 28 Oct 2026, after the screen date: fiscal Q1 2027 (quarter ended 30 Sep 2026) revenue $4.136bn, +28.9% YoY, above the $4.0bn ± $200m guidance midpoint and above ~$4.04bn consensus; non-GAAP EPS $1.22 vs $1.18 expected; GAAP EPS $1.18; operating margin 46.1% — the first quarter above 46% in company history; gross margin 62.7%. December-quarter guidance: revenue $4.7bn ± $200m (~+42.5% YoY at midpoint, ~+14% sequentially, ~4.8% above consensus) and non-GAAP EPS $1.44 ± $0.11. Management raised the 2026 wafer-equipment market view to the mid-$150bn range and previewed >$200bn for 2027, with advanced packaging on track for ≥$1.1bn in CY2026 (+70%+). Shares rose ~5.5% on 29 Oct to $167.43 (Finnhub snippet). Next print (Q2 FY27) is Jan 2027 per the task screen (2027-01-28). The rebound path is therefore: Q1 beat + Q2 guide → continued H2 CY2026 acceleration (+25% vs H1, per management) → 2027 WFE >$200bn thesis.

### 4. Balance-sheet survival
Strong. Q1 FY27 operating cash flow $1.53bn and FCF $1.34bn in the quarter; LTM OCF $4.51bn, FCF $4.04bn (company PR, 28 Oct 2026). FY2026 FCF was $3.77bn (27.7% margin). Balance sheet at 30 Sep 2026 (company IR): accounts receivable $3,072m, inventories $4,090m, total assets $18,405m, long-term debt $5,723m, total liabilities $11,881m, equity $6,523m. Capital return ~$875m/quarter in FY2026 (~88.9% of FCF) with a 21% dividend hike; no dilution or distress signal.

### 5. Margin of safety / valuation
De-rated but still the premium equipment name. Screen fwd PE 23.9x. Apex (23 Oct 2026) put trailing/forward P/E at 39.78x/26.95x vs 33.44x forward on 31 Dec 2025 (~19% lower) and vs peers AMAT 21.14x, LRCX 24.88x, Onto 16.84x — i.e. KLAC still carries a premium to every peer, so the dip "reduced expectations without making the shares an obvious bargain." Musaffa (pre-print) cited a median 12-month target of $227 vs $149.21 close (~+52%); screen upside +35.7% on its own target set. Precise multi-year median forward P/E: not found. Post-print price (~$167) mechanically lifts the multiple again, so the margin of safety is thinner than the screen snapshot implies — the Q2 guide has to be believed.

### 6. Category position
Still #1 in semiconductor process control, with record revenue and record operating margin in Q1 FY27. Backlog/RPO $12.5bn at end-FY2026 (+58.2% YoY) and deferred system revenue jumping to $932.9m in Q4 FY26 from $620.8m in Q3 — demand, not share loss, is the story.

### 7. Value-trap risk
The bear case: the premium multiple is the trap — if WFE in 2027 disappoints, KLAC falls hardest because it fell least on fundamentals. Memory mix (40% of Dec-quarter process-control systems revenue, 84% DRAM within memory) exposes it to a memory-price downturn. China/CBP friction and the $300–350m export-control drag could widen. Inventory build (see flag) could turn into a write-down if long-lead optical capacity arrives into a downturn.

### FLAG — RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN (KLAC only)
Evidence: Q4 FY26 saw a $584.75m sequential spike in receivables on back-end-loaded June shipments, with inventories +$436.39m YoY, which compressed quarterly FCF margin to 22.3% (from 38.3% in Q2 FY26). But in Q1 FY27 receivables rose only $3,072m vs $2,889m at 30 June 2026 (+6.3%) while revenue rose 13% sequentially ($3,658m→$4,136m) — receivables grew *slower* than sales — and OCF rebounded to $1.53bn / FCF $1.34bn in the quarter, exactly the collection the prep note predicted (>$1,100m). One independent forensic-style review (InTickers) found "no substantiated accounting anomaly" for receivables/inventory. **One-line verdict: Benign timing so far — back-end-loaded shipments collected in Q1 FY27 — but keep watching inventory ($4,090m, +12% QoQ) as the real working-capital tell, not receivables.**

### 8. Rebound score: 8/10
**Verdict: Strongest fundamental confirmation in the batch — a record quarter plus a 42%-growth guide already delivered — but you are paying a peer-premium multiple for it, so the dip is only half a bargain.**

## 3. CAT — Caterpillar (construction machinery)

### 1. Why it's down — temporary vs permanent
Note first: the task screen said "next earnings None" — that is stale. CAT printed Q3 2026 on the morning of Thu 29 Oct 2026, and that print *is* the drawdown story. The stock had already tumbled 33% from its record close of $1,064.90 on 30 June 2026 through 28 Oct (Morningstar/MarketWatch, 29 Oct 2026), on cooling AI data-center sentiment, then fell a further ~8–10% on 29 Oct (premarket −9.6% to ~$644; close $647.39, −9.25%; worst single day since March 2009 on some measures) despite a beat. Q3: revenue $20.68bn, +17.3% YoY (consensus ~$20.54–20.76bn depending on vendor — essentially in line to a slight miss); adjusted EPS $7.20 vs ~$7.06–7.12 expected, +45.5% YoY; GAAP EPS $7.13; operating margin 20.4% (from 17.3%), adjusted 20.6%. Why the selloff on a beat: (a) the beat narrowed from ~31% in Q2 to ~1%; (b) guidance was only *maintained* (2026 sales growth mid-to-high-teens, below the ~18% analysts expected) with adjusted margin now expected near the *bottom* of the target range ex-tariff-recoveries; (c) Q4 guided only "moderate" growth vs ~12% consensus, plus a >$1bn Construction Industries dealer-inventory drawdown (~$1.6bn gross, ~$1bn volume headwind); (d) tariff costs ~$600m in Q3 and ~$600m expected in Q4, ~$2.2bn for the year ex-IEEPA recoveries, with recoveries shrinking to $128m in Q3 from $392m in Q2 — raising questions about repeatability; (e) segment soft spots: Construction sales $7.98bn missed the $8.12bn consensus, Asia-Pacific weak, rail retail sales down sharply, and Cat Financial credit-loss allowance rising. **Verdict: TEMPORARY (expectations reset + tariff/margin noise + Q4 inventory headwind), not a demand break — backlog is at a record. The permanent risk is only if data-center power demand proves debt-fuelled and unsustainable.**

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 7/10
Moat: brand, the 150-independent-dealer network across ~190 countries (distribution + service that Komatsu/Deere cannot replicate overnight), scale in engines/turbines, and aftermarket parts. AI does not replace a bulldozer or a 10MW gas engine; it *increases* demand for CAT's power products (data-center backup/prime power) and, via autonomy, for its machines. Disruption threats are real but slow: electrification of smaller machines, autonomous haulage (where CAT itself is a leader), and Chinese competitors on price in emerging markets. No software substitute commoditises a mining truck. Score capped at 7 because the moat is execution/distribution, not IP monopoly, and construction/mining demand is deeply cyclical.

### 3. Rebound catalyst
Backlog conversion, not a single product. Record backlog $78.1bn at end-Q3, +$38.2bn YoY and +$6bn QoQ (slower growth than prior quarters — the market's complaint), with large reciprocating-engine orders extending into 2029 and turbine orders into 2030; Power Generation sales +30% in Q3 ($3.4bn), Power & Energy revenue $8.78bn (+18%), segment profit +31%. Management is adding capacity (restarting a 10MW gas-engine platform, ~1.5GW; Wamego, Kansas turbine facility shipping PGM130). Path back: Q4 prints through the inventory drawdown, 2027 guidance that quantifies backlog conversion, tariff relief/recovery clarity, and any re-acceleration in data-center orders. Timing: Q4 2026 print (Jan/Feb 2027) is the first clean test; backlog visibility runs to 2029–30, so the thesis is 2–4 quarters, not 2–4 weeks.

### 4. Balance-sheet survival
No distress. Screen ndE 2.41x flatters the risk because it consolidates Cat Financial's financing debt; the machinery business itself is a cash machine. Q3: enterprise operating cash flow $4.4bn, MP&E FCF $3.8bn in the quarter; 9-month operating cash flow $10.6bn (vs $8.1bn); enterprise cash $7.9bn at quarter-end; returned ~$2bn in Q3 ($1.2bn buybacks + ~$750m dividends). Full-year MP&E FCF guided to the upper half of $6–15bn. Cat Financial past-dues 1.31% — lowest since 1998 — despite the rising loss allowance. FY2025 FCF was ~$7.5bn on revenue ~$67.6bn. Leverage is manageable for a company generating this cash; dilution risk is nil.

### 5. Margin of safety / valuation
The most "reset" multiple in the batch. Screen fwd PE 19.9x. Pre-print, CAT traded ~23x expected earnings vs ~17x a year earlier (MarketWatch); Barchart put it at ~22x on the premarket price using FY2026 EPS estimates of $27.74 (+45.5%). Post-Q3 target cuts are the tell: Wells Fargo $1,155→$840 (still Buy), Bernstein $1,002→$749 (Hold), UBS $925→$750 (Hold) on 29 Oct 2026 (TipRanks), while pre-print consensus was ~$947–$948 (30 analysts: 16 Buy, 12 Hold, 2 Sell) and Oppenheimer held $1,009 Outperform. Screen upside +43.2% uses a stale, pre-cut mean — treat it as overstated; the post-cut mean (~$930 per TipRanks' 8 Buy/7 Hold set, or lower once all cuts flow through) still implies ~+40% from ~$647 only if earnings hold. Precise multi-year median P/E: not found. On 19.9–22x forward earnings for a cyclical at peak backlog, CAT is fairly priced for the cycle, cheap only if the data-center power cycle extends into 2027–28.

### 6. Category position
Still #1 globally in construction/mining equipment and a top-tier large-engine/turbine supplier; best-performing Dow component in H1 2026 (+80% at peak). No share-loss evidence in Q3 — Construction missed consensus on timing/inventory, not on competitive loss; Power & Energy beat ($8.78bn vs $8.22bn consensus). Pricing held (volume + price both up).

### 7. Value-trap risk
The bear case: CAT is a cyclical at a record backlog priced for AI power demand that may be financed with debt — if hyperscaler capex pauses, Power & Energy orders (the growth engine) fall fastest, while construction/mining roll over simultaneously. Tariffs are a $2.2bn annual tax that recoveries may not fully offset. Q4's dealer drawdown could extend into 2027 if dealers stay cautious. At 19.9x forward, a normal industrial downturn (earnings −30–40%) would still leave the stock down double-digits from here.

### 8. Rebound score: 7/10
**Verdict: Record backlog and record Q3 cash flow against a post-print expectations reset — a real dip, but the Q4 inventory headwind and tariff overhang mean the rebound is a 2027 story, not an immediate snap-back.**

## 4. HWM — Howmet Aerospace (aero castings) — POST-PRINT DOSSIER

### 1. Why it's down — temporary vs permanent (use post-print facts)
A premium-multiple de-rating on competitive *fears*, repeatedly contradicted by prints. From the 52-week high of $310 on 6 Aug 2026 (after Q2: revenue $2.55bn, +24%, adjusted EPS $1.33, +46%, guidance raised), HWM fell ~35% over the quarter to ~$186–$210 by late October (TickerOn: $232.48→$185.91 in 30 days; ~$287 late July → ~35% pullback). Triggers: late-August reports of SpaceX bringing gas-turbine blade/vane casting in-house (−7.5% in a day); GE Aerospace's $11.75bn agreement to acquire castings supplier CPP (vertical-integration signal); defence-budget uncertainty and rotation out of premium aerospace suppliers; pre-earnings de-risking and target trims (RBC, TD Cowen, Bernstein) ahead of Q3. No operational miss preceded the fall — Q2 beat and raised guidance. Then Q3 2026 (reported Thu 29 Oct 2026) beat again (see §3) and the stock rose +5.51% to ~$200+ on the day — but the screen still shows −29.6% off the high, i.e. the rebound has barely started. **Verdict: TEMPORARY (sentiment + vertical-integration fears + multiple compression). The fears are medium-term watch items, not realised share loss — see §7.**

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 9/10
Moat: certification lock-in + metallurgy/process IP + capital intensity. Turbine blades, structural castings and fasteners for commercial aerospace, defence and industrial gas turbines must be qualified part-by-part with engine OEMs and airworthiness authorities — a multi-year, capital-heavy barrier that no AI tool or software substitute can bypass. You cannot 3D-print or algorithm your way around a certified single-crystal turbine blade at volume (yet). AI helps Howmet's own yields and design, it does not replace the casting. Score capped at 9 (not 10) only because a determined, well-funded customer (SpaceX, GE) *can* in-source over years — the exact fear driving the multiple.

### 3. Rebound catalyst — DELIVERED 29 Oct 2026, with more to come
Q3 2026 (29 Oct 2026, PRNewswire via Morningstar): revenue $2.703bn, +29% YoY (+25% organic, record organic growth); GAAP EPS $1.42 (+49%), adjusted EPS $1.46 (+54%) vs $1.37 consensus; adjusted EBITDA $879m, +43%, margin 32.5% (+310bps); FCF $562m, a quarterly record (after $147m capex), +33%; Q3 buybacks $500m (1.9m shares at $264.77 avg); dividend raised 17% to $0.14. Segment: Engine Products $1.45bn (+33%), Fastening Systems $630m (+41% incl. CAM/Brunner acquisitions), Commercial Aerospace $1.29bn (+32%), Gas Turbines +45% (share gains + output adds), Spares $570m (+30%, 21% of revenue). Guidance raised across the board: FY2026 revenue $10.29–10.31bn (from ~$10.05bn), adjusted EPS $5.49–5.51 (from $5.27), FCF $1.95–2.05bn (from ~$1.9bn), Q4 EPS guided $1.49–1.51; *and* first FY2027 guidance: revenue ~$11.5bn (+~12%). Board added $5bn to the buyback authorisation on 28 Oct 2026 (total remaining $5.397bn). Management now expects to *double* industrial gas-turbine sales within three years from a 2025 base (accelerated from 3–5 years). Catalyst path: this print re-rates the multiple; Q4 delivery + 2027 guidance confirmation is the second leg. Timing: immediate (post-print momentum) through the Q4 print in early 2027.

### 4. Balance-sheet survival
Comfortable. Q3-end cash $507–508m (down from $742m at end-2025 after the $1.9bn net acquisition spend on CAM/Brunner in H1), long-term debt $4.052bn (up from $2.859bn at end-2025 on CAM debt), $390m commercial paper outstanding of a $1bn programme (repaid $60m in Q3), net debt/trailing EBITDA improved to ~1.3x (from 1.4x in Q2, 1.6x post-CAM in Q1; management targeting ~1.0x by year-end). Screen ndE 1.47x is consistent. FCF: $1,400m in 9M 2026 (+55% YoY); FY2026 guide $1.95–2.05bn. Undrawn $1bn revolver (per call). No distress or dilution risk; buybacks are funded from FCF, not debt.

### 5. Margin of safety / valuation
The least cheap in the batch — the dip repaired a premium, it did not create a value stock. Screen fwd PE 31.7x; MarketBeat (pre-print) showed trailing P/E 42.90x and next-year EPS growth +20.4% ($5.34→$6.43 on its series, which differs from company guidance — use company guidance, not that series, for forward EPS). Analyst targets pre-print averaged ~$317.68 (TradingKey, Oct 2026; range $214.92–$375.00) — screen upside +57.6% is on a higher target set and pre-dates the post-print move; treat with caution until targets are refreshed post-29 Oct. Precise multi-year median forward P/E: not found. At ~32x forward earnings even after a ~30% fall, HWM needs flawless execution and the gas-turbine doubling thesis to justify the price — the margin of safety is the *growth*, not the multiple.

### 6. Category position
Still the leading independent supplier of engine castings/fasteners/airframe structures; gas-turbine share *gains* cited in Q3 (+45% segment growth "attributed to increased market share and output additions"). No lost-programme disclosure in Q3; spares (21% of revenue, +30%) is a high-margin annuity that in-sourcing by one customer does not touch. GE's CPP deal may actually reflect constrained casting capacity industry-wide — a tailwind for pricing — rather than a Howmet displacement.

### 7. Value-trap risk
The honest bear case: the multiple never re-rates because the market permanently prices in customer in-sourcing — SpaceX qualifies its own castings, GE favours CPP, and Howmet's growth converges to the aero cycle rather than beating it. Defence visibility post-FY27 is constrained. Capacity investment (new furnaces/plants) is capital-heavy; if aero or gas-turbine demand cools, fixed costs bite and FCF conversion falls. At 31.7x forward, any guide-down in Q4 or 2027 would re-open the drawdown quickly. This is the one name in the batch where "expensive after the dip" is a legitimate reason to wait for a second confirmation print.

### 8. Rebound score: 7/10
**Verdict: Best print in the batch — record revenue, record FCF, raised 2026 and first 2027 guidance plus a $5bn buyback — but at 31.7x forward the dip is a growth re-rating story, not a margin-of-safety bargain; the post-print bounce is the first leg, not proof the de-rating is over.**
