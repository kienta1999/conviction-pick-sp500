# Panelist D — Falling-knife / value-trap skeptic (veto lens)

## SINGLE-PICK BALLOT

- Top pick: CPRT
- Runner-up: KLAC
- Thesis (3-5 sentences): Copart's dip is a margin-and-one-contract wobble in an otherwise intact, high-margin network, not a broken business. The stock fell on Q4 FY2026 net income down 17.4% and US insurance units down 7.5% after a lost major account, yet revenue still grew 2.4%, pricing power held with average selling prices up, and international units rose 10%. With a fortress net-cash balance sheet and a de-rating to EV/Sales of about 4.82x versus its own 5-year average of about 8.58x, a stabilisation in US volumes and cost per car re-rates a compounder rather than rescuing a distressed name. That is the veto lens's preferred shape of upside: recovery of a proven margin, not a bet on a new story.
- Why the dip is temporary (not permanent): the single most compelling evidence the drop is about price, not the business: Copart remains the scale salvage network with pricing power intact — global ASP up 3.5% in Q4 and 5.5% in FY2026 — while the volume hit is traceable to one lost insurance customer plus a 12.7% rise in operating expense per car, both fixable under a returning founder CEO, rather than evidence that insurers or bidders have abandoned the network.
- Moat / AI-irreplaceability: low risk — AI cannot replicate zoning-scarce yards near major cities, title transfer, transport, or the two-sided insurer-buyer liquidity network, and AI instead helps Copart on pricing and damage assessment; the real long-run substitute threat is safer/autonomous vehicles shrinking wreck supply, a decades-scale headwind, not an AI disruption.
- Rebound catalyst & timing: Q1 FY2027 print in the 18–19 Nov 2026 window — the first test of Jay Adair's cost-per-unit priority and whether US insurance volumes have stabilised — with international expansion and the announced all-cash ACV acquisition as medium-term legs.
- 12-18 month return scenario: rough base / bull case: base roughly +24% to +32% toward the StockEvents average target of $36.63 (versus about $29.55 close) and the screen upside of +32.0%; bull roughly toward the $43 high cited in that target set (about +45% from about $29.55) if US volumes and cost per car bottom and margins stabilise.
- Top risk (value-trap case): the lost insurer contract is the first of more as competitors bid aggressively, cost per car stays elevated because yard capacity was overbuilt for volumes that do not return, and safer cars plus ADAS shrink total-loss supply faster than record total-loss frequency offsets it — in which case a 16.3x forward multiple re-rates toward a no-growth multiple and the 52-week low of $25.72–$26.27, only about 12–13% below the 29 Oct close, is reached rather than held as a floor.
- Conviction (1-10): 7
