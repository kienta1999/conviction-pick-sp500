## SINGLE-PICK BALLOT

- Top pick: KLAC
- Runner-up: LRCX
- Thesis (3-5 sentences): KLA is the highest-quality business in the triaged field, and the dip is a multiple reset on a business that is accelerating, not deteriorating. Revenue growth quickened three straight quarters (7.2 percent, then 11.5 percent, then 15.2 percent, then 28.9 percent year over year in fiscal Q1 2027), with an eighth consecutive EPS beat, a 45.0 percent operating margin, 91.4 percent ROE, and record quarterly free cash flow of USD 1.34 billion. Demand ran ahead of supply all through 2026 with lead times averaging about 12 months, and purchase commitments jumped to USD 8.87 billion from USD 2.50 billion a year earlier. At a forward P/E of 20.3 the stock is no longer cheap in absolute terms, but it is a near-monopoly compounder bought roughly 52 percent below its June peak after the multiple, not the business, broke.
- Why the dip is temporary (not permanent): the single most compelling evidence is the order book against the price action — purchase commitments of USD 8.87 billion at 30 September 2026, up from USD 2.50 billion a year earlier, with demand ahead of supply and 12-month lead times, while the stock fell 30.0 percent from 8 August to 6 November versus 4.7 percent for the S and P 500. A business losing its franchise does not see customers commit three-and-a-half times more money a year in advance. The two company-specific drags named on the call — persistently high memory costs trimming gross margin to a guided 62.5 percent, and CEO share sales of over USD 10.7 million — are a small input-cost headwind and a sentiment item, not a demand or share signal; KLA's process-control share is rising, not falling.
- Moat / AI-irreplaceability: low risk. KLA holds 56.8 percent of the global process-control market in 2025, 6.5 to 7 times its nearest competitor (Applied Materials at 11.2 percent), and 85.9 percent of defect inspection, the largest sub-segment. Its tools are the measurement layer of the fab: at 3nm/2nm and in HBM stacks, you cannot fabricate what you cannot measure, and decades of defect-classification data plus optics, laser and software integration make KLA the default reference platform at TSMC, Samsung and Intel. AI makes KLA more necessary, not less, because AI chips are the most defect-sensitive products ever built; advanced-packaging inspection revenue is expected to reach USD 1.1 billion in calendar 2026. No AI substitute exists for measuring a wafer.
- Rebound catalyst & timing: the December-quarter print in late January 2027 (expected about 28 January). Management guided revenue of USD 4.7 billion plus or minus USD 200 million (about 42.5 percent year-over-year growth at the midpoint) and non-GAAP EPS of USD 1.44 plus or minus USD 0.11. Hitting that guide confirms the acceleration and the gross-margin story, with management expecting to be back inside its 63 to 64 percent Investor Day gross-margin model by mid-to-late 2027 into 2028. The USD 8.81 billion of buyback authorisation remaining at 30 September 2026, after USD 936 to 943 million of Q1 buybacks plus USD 305 million of dividends (about 93 percent of quarterly free cash flow returned), is a steady floor under the stock while the print approaches.
- 12-18 month return scenario: rough base / bull case. Base case, roughly plus 30 to 40 percent: the December and March prints land on guide, revenue keeps compounding at a high-teens to 20 percent-plus rate, and the forward multiple holds near 20 times rather than re-rating — the gain comes mostly from earnings growth plus buybacks. Bull case, roughly plus 55 to 70 percent: the guided acceleration is delivered, margins return to the 63 to 64 percent model, and the market pays something closer to KLA's historical premium for a near-monopoly, moving toward the screen's plus 52 percent analyst upside and beyond it. These are rough scenarios anchored on the dossier's analyst upside and guided growth, not forecasts.
- Top risk (value-trap case): the trap is paying a premium multiple for a cyclical. If wafer-fab equipment rolls over, KLA's revenue (91 percent process control, tied to fab spending) falls with it, and a trailing P/E of about 35 times compresses toward 20 times at the same time earnings fall — a double de-rating. China is about 26 to 31 percent of revenue and subject to export-control rules that already capture inspection tools able to find defects of 21nm or smaller, so a further tightening could take a permanent slice out of the addressable market. The memory-cost margin drag management flagged is small (each gross-margin point is about USD 145 million of annual gross profit), but it shows input-cost inflation can bite even a monopolist.
- Conviction (1-10): 8

### Top 3 detail

**1. KLAC.** The single most compelling data point is the combination of a 10/10 irreplaceability score with accelerating revenue and a surging order book: 56.8 percent of process control, 85.9 percent of defect inspection, revenue growth quickening to 28.9 percent, and purchase commitments of USD 8.87 billion versus USD 2.50 billion a year earlier — while the stock sits 52 percent off its June high on a multiple that was 48.4 times trailing earnings at end-September. Rough 12–18 month scenario: base roughly plus 30 to 40 percent on earnings delivery at a stable multiple; bull roughly plus 55 to 70 percent if margins return to the 63 to 64 percent model and the market re-rates the monopoly.

**2. LRCX.** The single most compelling data point is a record quarter that the market sold anyway: revenue USD 8.22 billion, up 54 percent year over year and 22 percent sequentially, the fifth consecutive quarterly revenue record, with operating margin above 40 percent for the first time (40.2 percent) and record quarterly free cash flow of USD 3.24 billion — followed by a fresh USD 25 billion buyback authorisation on 5 November, roughly 9 to 10 percent of market value at current prices. The "miss" was against elevated buy-side expectations, not consensus: December-quarter guidance of USD 8.60 billion plus or minus USD 0.40 billion narrowly cleared consensus, with component shortages merely deferring shipments into the March quarter. Rough 12–18 month scenario: base roughly plus 35 to 45 percent as the deferred shipments land and the 2027 WFE outlook (above USD 200 billion, from the mid-USD 150 billion range in 2026) is confirmed; bull roughly plus 60 to 75 percent toward the screen's plus 72 percent analyst upside if WFE re-rates on customer capex confirmations.

**3. FIX.** The single most compelling data point is the backlog against the balance sheet: a record USD 17.81 billion backlog, up 90 percent year over year and 26.7 percent sequentially (the eighth consecutive quarterly increase), with technology projects now 59 percent of revenue, paired with net cash of about USD 2.16 billion and quarterly free cash flow of about USD 806 million — yet the stock fell 9.26 percent the day after a Q3 beat on every line (revenue plus 53.7 percent, EPS plus 68.6 percent, operating margin 16.6 percent versus 15.5 percent) because no forward guidance was issued and the CFO announced retirement. Rough 12–18 month scenario: base roughly plus 30 to 40 percent as the backlog converts over the next 6 to 12 months at a forward multiple back toward its five-year median of 23.64 times (versus 16.3 times on the screen); bull roughly plus 55 to 65 percent toward the plus 61 percent analyst upside if guidance resumes at the February print and modular capacity expansion supports the 2027 growth leg.

### Lens notes on the rest of the top 10

AMAT at 4 is the broadest equipment play with net cash of USD 7.56 billion, a record USD 10.41 billion quarter, 53 percent revenue growth and a 14.2 times forward P/E, but flat free-cash-flow conversion (Q4 free cash flow flat year over year while revenue grew 53 percent) keeps it below the two purer compounders. GOOGL at 5 pairs 25 percent revenue growth and a Cloud arm doubling (plus 103 percent, USD 560.8 billion backlog) with about USD 123 billion of net cash, but two straight quarters of negative free cash flow and a search-monetisation transition keep it a show-me story until the February print. VRT at 6 has the strongest operating print in the field (sales plus 61 percent, organic orders up more than 100 percent, adjusted operating margin 25.2 percent, up 290 bps) but a thinner moat — the open 800 VDC power spec lets 80-plus vendors compete for the same socket — and an inventory pile that more than doubled. CAT at 7 owns the least AI-disruptable moat in the field (9/10) and a record USD 78.1 billion backlog, but it carries net debt of 2.25 times EBITDA, a 46 percent peak-to-trough fall that is mostly a give-back of a 46-times peak multiple, and no catalyst before the Q4 print. TPR at 8 is a genuine compounder on Coach (revenue plus 24 percent in FY2026, gross margin 77 to 79 percent, guidance just raised) but is effectively a single-brand bet with Kate Spade still shrinking and a scheduled H2 FY2027 tariff test. ADBE at 9 is the cheapest quality name in the field on a forward P/E of 8.6, with a 66.8 percent ROIC and about USD 9.35 billion of free cash flow, but net new ARR fell roughly 36 to 37 percent and the AI disruption thesis aims directly at its core product, so the 9 December FY2027 guide decides it. META at 10 has the strongest ad engine in the world (revenue plus 26 percent, 3.64 billion daily actives) at a below-average multiple, but the historic net-cash position is gone, free cash flow was negative USD 2.12 billion in Q3, and the up-to-USD 97.5 billion equity package makes per-share dilution — not demand — the live question for a compounder lens.

### Left out of the top 10

Left out: MU, INTU, HOOD, BE. MU is the most likely value trap in the field for this lens — a forward P/E of 3.4 and an 87.0 percent gross margin are peak-cycle prints on a commodity product where Micron is third in HBM (18 percent share, behind SK Hynix at 50 percent), and the dossier itself warns earnings could fall 50 to 70 percent if memory prices normalise, turning 4 times earnings into 12 times overnight; a compounder lens cannot underwrite peak-cycle earnings as durable. INTU is left out because it reports earnings TODAY (2026-11-19 after close), the print is binary (average post-earnings move about 7.8 percent), and it carries the most credible structural AI-disruption thesis in the field — an agent that files taxes and runs books from a bank feed attacks the per-seat model itself — so its 11.9 times forward P/E is not a margin of safety until tonight's print and then tax season answer the franchise question. HOOD is left out because growth has decelerated to 12 percent revenue growth with EPS down 16 percent, crypto revenue down 55 percent, take rates compressing, and the rebound depends on crypto stabilising and prediction markets surviving live court fights. BE is left out because, despite 138 percent revenue growth, it still trades at a 32.6 times forward P/E (about 98 to 112 times trailing) with negative quarterly operating cash flow, about 70 percent of named backlog concentrated in one customer (Oracle, which just issued a force-majeure notice on Project Jupiter), and a price that assumes roughly 47 percent revenue growth a year for five years — no margin of safety for a quality buyer.
