## KLAC — KLA Corporation

### KLAC 1. Why-it's-down: multiple compression, not a business break — CONFIRMED

- Revenue: fiscal Q1 2027 (quarter ended 30 September 2026), reported 28 October 2026: revenue USD 4.14 billion (USD 4,136 million), up 28.9% year over year and 13% sequentially, a record; non-GAAP EPS USD 1.22 vs about USD 1.18 expected; Semiconductor Process Control USD 3.75 billion, 91% of total. Source: KLA earnings release / call transcript, 28 October 2026 (AlphaStreet transcript; VLSI.kr fact-check of the release).
- Trailing P/E about 48.4x at end-September 2026: CONFIRMED. Trefis, 4 November 2026: "at the end of September 2026, KLA's P/E of 48.4 was higher than at 38 of those 40 quarter ends." YCharts shows 48.71 on 30 September 2026 on a slightly different EPS basis — same picture.
- Growth acceleration: Trefis, 7 November 2026, confirms revenue growth accelerated three straight quarters, from 7.2% (fiscal Q2 2026) to 28.9% (fiscal Q1 2027).
- Drawdown arithmetic: 52-week high USD 307.37 on 30 June 2026 (Finnhub); screen price USD 144.56 is 53.0% below that high, so the dossier's "-52%" is correct to rounding. The multiple then compressed: same trailing earnings, P/E 48.4x on 30 September to 38.8x on 3 November and about 34.4x by 6 November (Trefis, 4 and 7 November 2026); Finnhub trailing P/E 35.92x at the 18 November 2026 close.
- The dossier's supporting detail that the stock rose 5.45% the session after the print was not independently re-checked and is not load-bearing for this verdict.

### KLAC 2. Rebound catalyst and date: December-quarter guide CONFIRMED; date UNVERIFIED

- Guide: management guided December-quarter (fiscal Q2 2027) revenue USD 4.7 billion plus or minus USD 200 million, non-GAAP EPS USD 1.44 plus or minus USD 0.11, gross margin 62.5% plus or minus 1pp. Source: KLA earnings call, 28 October 2026.
- The +42.5% year-over-year figure: CONFIRMED by arithmetic. December-quarter 2025 (fiscal Q2 2026) revenue was USD 3.30 billion (USD 3,297 million), per KLA's release of 29 January 2026. USD 4.7 billion / USD 3.297 billion − 1 = +42.6%.

No company-confirmed date was found as of 2026-11-19. The catalyst (the print itself) is real; the dossier's "~28 Jan" should be treated as an estimate, not a confirmed date. The ballots' "~28 January" inherits the same caveat.

### KLAC 3. Forward P/E about 20.3x vs history — CONFIRMED on screen-provider basis; provider-dependent

- The 20.3x figure: CONFIRMED on the Yahoo-finance-family basis the screen appears to use. Kinfo, prices as of 18 November 2026: trailing P/E 36.23, forward P/E 20.35, estimated forward EPS USD 7.12. MarketBeat, 18 November 2026: forward P/E 20.67. StockAnalysis (TSX listing): forward P/E 23.31.

Other providers give materially higher forward multiples on different EPS bases: FinanceCharts forward P/E 34.84 (13 November 2026); ValueInvesting.io forward P/E multiples 39.7x–50.4x, mean 41.3x (17 November 2026); MCH Research 25.1x–26.5x on consensus forward EPS (17 November 2026). But forward 20.3x cannot be compared directly with that trailing history; the dossier does not make that error explicitly, but a reader should not infer "forward 20x is below its historical median" from the trailing series. Correct reading: KLAC is cheaper than at its June peak and than at end-September, still a premium multiple.

### LRCX 4. Moat: duopoly in etch/deposition, large installed base, high switching costs — PARTIALLY CONFIRMED

- Substance CONFIRMED: Lam is a leading etch and deposition equipment maker; every advanced chip passes through etch/deposition steps; its business description (deposition, etch, clean, Customer Support Business Group spares/upgrades) and its record quarter on AI/memory demand are consistent with a leading position. No source found in this check quantified Lam's etch or deposition share, so no share figure is endorsed here.
- Wording overstated: "duopoly" is NOT supported. The dossier itself describes the market as a three-player oligopoly (Lam, Applied Materials, Tokyo Electron) in etch/deposition, and the ballots' "duopoly" wording narrows that without a source. Correct wording: oligopoly, with Lam co-leading etch.
- Switching costs: the claim that re-qualifying a new vendor's tool takes quarters and risks yield is standard industry logic and is asserted in the dossier without a primary source; no independent primary source was found in this check to confirm or contradict the "quarters" figure. Treat as plausible but UNVERIFIED as a quantified claim. It does not change the verdict on the moat's direction, but the panel should not cite it as a sourced fact.

## FIX — Comfort Systems USA

### FIX 1. Why-it's-down: record Q3 met with about -9.3% on no guidance + CFO retirement + AI-capex fear — CONFIRMED

- Record Q3: reported 22 October 2026 (quarter ended 30 September 2026): revenue USD 3.77 billion (USD 3,766 million), up 53.7% year over year from USD 2.45 billion; net income USD 489.0 million, up 67.7%; diluted EPS USD 13.91, up 68.6% from USD 8.25, beating consensus of about USD 12.71–12.85; backlog USD 17.81 billion (USD 17,815 million), up 90% year over year from USD 9.38 billion and 26.7% sequentially from USD 14.06 billion. Source: FIX Q3 2026 release (8-K Exhibit 99.1), 22 October 2026, via Zacks, 23 October 2026, and SEC filing summary.
- The drop: shares fell 9.26% on 23 October 2026 to USD 1,160.86 (Zacks; Investomine earnings radar). The dossier's "about -9.3%" is exact. Shares also fell 6%+ after hours on 22 October and 8.70% in the regular session on 22 October — before the release, which came after the close — so the 22 October fall cannot be attributed to the print; only the 23 October fall is the post-print reaction.
- No forward guidance: CONFIRMED. Management gave same-store revenue growth expectations (mid-40% range for 2026, mid-20% for 2027) but no numerical Q4 EPS or revenue range (Investomine; Tickeron, November 2026). Multiple contemporaneous reports attribute the selloff to the withheld guidance.
- CFO retirement: CONFIRMED. CFO Bill George announced retirement after the filing of the 2026 annual report, ending a 30-year tenure; Chief Accounting Officer Julie Shaeff to succeed him. Source: Zacks, 23 October 2026; FIX release, 22 October 2026.
- AI-capex fear: supported as a sentiment attribution, not a company disclosure. Contemporaneous coverage (MarketFlux, 26 October 2026; Tickeron) cites growing investor concern about AI data-centre spending alongside the guidance/CFO items. One additional driver the dossier underweights: margin softness — gross margin 24.8% in Q3 vs 26.1% in the first half (electrical gross margin 24.3% vs 26.2% a year earlier), cited by TradingKey, Tickeron and the 26 October analyst brief as part of the selloff. The "sentiment, not results" verdict still holds — revenue, EPS and backlog all beat — but the margin point is a real, if secondary, results-based contributor and should not be omitted.
- Technology share: technology customers were 60.8% of Q3 revenue (Zacks, 23 October 2026), up from 45.8% a year earlier — consistent with the dossier's "technology projects now 59% of revenue" on a nine-month basis (Trefis, 6 November 2026: 59% of nine-month revenue). The dossier's "59%" is the nine-month figure; the Q3 figure is 60.8%.

### FIX 4. Moat: leading mechanical/electrical contractor for data centres, record backlog, net cash — CONFIRMED (with a sourcing caveat on "leading")

- Record backlog: CONFIRMED — USD 17.81 billion at 30 September 2026, up 90% year over year, eighth consecutive quarterly increase (see FIX 1).
- Net cash: CONFIRMED. Company investor presentation (SEC, 28 October 2026): USD 2.16 billion cash at 30 September 2026, USD 49.6 million total debt, no borrowings under the USD 1.10 billion senior credit facility; Debt/TTM EBITDA 0.02x. The scale facts (locations, headcount, backlog, modular capacity of 4.0 million sq ft) support a scale-moat reading, but "leading" should be attributed to the company, not stated as an independently ranked number-one position. The physical-trade point (AI cannot install a chiller or commission a data hall) is a logical claim, not a sourced fact, and is not disputed.

## Cross-cutting notes for the consolidator

- Forward P/E is provider-dependent for all three names. The screen figures (KLAC 20.3x, LRCX 17.2x, FIX 16.3x) all reproduce on the Yahoo-finance-family basis at the screen dates, but other reputable providers give materially higher forward multiples (KLAC up to about 35–41x, LRCX up to about 26x, FIX up to about 26x on FinanceCharts' basis). The consolidation should keep the screen figures and add one sentence naming the provider basis, rather than presenting any of them as the single true multiple. The valuation *rankings* (FIX cheapest vs its own history, KLAC still a premium) survive across providers; the exact levels do not.
- Earnings dates: LRCX about 27 January 2027 is the only one of the three where listings agree. KLAC about 28 January 2027 conflicts with listings of 27 January and 4 February 2027; FIX about 18 February 2027 conflicts with a 17 February 2027 listing. Neither KLAC nor FIX date should be stated as confirmed.
- The ballots' load-bearing claims survive this check: KLAC's accelerating revenue into a multiple reset, LRCX's record quarter plus USD 25B buyback, and FIX's record backlog plus net cash are all confirmed from primary sources. The two wording corrections are: (a) LRCX is an oligopolist, not a duopolist, in etch/deposition; (b) FIX's "leading" position is the company's self-description, not an independently sourced ranking.
- One omission to carry forward: FIX's Q3 gross-margin softness (24.8% vs 26.1% in H1) was a cited contributor to the 23 October selloff alongside no-guidance and the CFO retirement; the dossier's "sentiment, not results" framing should acknowledge it.
