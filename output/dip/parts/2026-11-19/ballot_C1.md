## SINGLE-PICK BALLOT

- Top pick: KLAC
- Runner-up: LRCX
- Thesis (3-5 sentences): KLA is the measurement layer of the fab — 56.8% of the global process-control market in 2025, 6.5–7x its nearest competitor, and 85.9% of defect inspection — and at 3nm/2nm and in HBM stacks you cannot fabricate what you cannot measure. The dip is a multiple reset, not a business break: revenue growth accelerated three straight quarters (7.2% → 11.5% → 15.2% → 28.9%), Q1 FY2027 was its eighth consecutive EPS beat, and purchase commitments jumped to \$8.87B from \$2.50B a year earlier with lead times averaging ~12 months. AI makes KLA more necessary, not less, because AI chips are the most defect-sensitive products ever built. With the December-quarter guide at \$4.7B revenue (+42.5% year over year at the midpoint), the rebound is earnings delivery on a near-monopoly, not a hope trade.
- Why the dip is temporary (not permanent): the single most compelling evidence the drop is about price, not the business
- The stock fell because the multiple was extreme — investors were paying 48.4x trailing earnings at end-September, the highest P/E at 38 of the last 40 quarter-ends — while the business itself accelerated into the selloff, with demand running ahead of supply throughout 2026 and Semiconductor Process Control revenue at 91% of total.
- Moat / AI-irreplaceability: can AI or a substitute disrupt it? (low / medium / high risk — and why)
- Low risk. There is no AI substitute for metrology and inspection: no model can measure a wafer, and AI chips raise defect sensitivity, so AI increases demand for KLA's tools. The moat is decades of defect-classification data plus optics/laser/software integration that make KLA the default reference platform at TSMC, Samsung and Intel. The only erosion channel is a WFE down-cycle delaying tool purchases — a timing hit, not a substitution.
- Rebound catalyst & timing: the concrete trigger and rough when
- The December-quarter print in late January 2027 (expected ~28 January): hitting the guided \$4.7B revenue and \$1.44 non-GAAP EPS confirms the acceleration and the gross-margin story (guide 62.5%, back inside the 63–64% Investor Day model by mid-to-late 2027 into 2028). A secondary, nearer support is the \$8.81B of remaining buyback authorisation plus Q1's \$936–943M of buybacks.
- 12-18 month return scenario: rough base / bull case
- Base: +35–50% — earnings grow into the guided ramp and the forward multiple holds near 20x, roughly tracking the +52% analyst upside (mean target \$219.62 vs screen price \$144.56). Bull: +60–80% — the December guide is hit, margins return to the 63–64% model, and the market re-rates the near-monopoly back toward its historical premium.
- Top risk (value-trap case):
- The trap is paying a premium multiple for a cyclical: trailing P/E is still ~35x, so if WFE rolls over, revenue falls and the multiple compresses at the same time — a double de-rating. China is ~26–31% of revenue and export controls already capture inspection tools able to find defects of 21nm or smaller. CEO Richard Wallace sold over \$10.7M of shares, a sentiment headwind, and the memory-cost margin drag shows even a monopolist can be squeezed on inputs.

### Top 3 — the single most compelling temporary-dip + intact-moat data point, and a rough 12–18mo scenario

**KLAC:** Demand ran ahead of supply all through 2026 with ~12-month lead times and purchase commitments of \$8.87B (from \$2.50B a year earlier) — customers are queuing for a near-monopoly product while the stock halved on multiple compression alone. Base +35–50% (toward the +52% analyst upside); bull +60–80% if the December guide is hit and margins return to the 63–64% model.

**LRCX:** A record September quarter — revenue \$8.22B, +54% year over year, fifth consecutive quarterly record, operating margin above 40% for the first time — sold off because December guidance only narrowly cleared consensus, and the board then authorised a further \$25B buyback, roughly 9–10% of market value at ~\$210. Base +50–60% (toward the ~\$363 mean target, +64–72% upside); bull +80–100% if management's preliminary 2027 WFE outlook above \$200B is confirmed by customer capex.

**AMAT:** The broadest WFE vendor reported a record quarter (revenue \$10.41B, +53%; non-GAAP EPS \$4.16, +92%) with guidance above consensus, and fell on a "not enough" reaction while at least 10 firms cut targets yet kept Buy-equivalent ratings; it is net cash with the largest quarterly buyback in eight years (\$2.0B in Q4). Base +45–55% (toward the \$565 median target, +60%); bull +75% or more if the \$11.1B Q1 guide lands and the plan to double systems output by 2028 holds.

### Left out of the top 10

HOOD, BE, ADBE and INTU were deliberately left out. HOOD's front end is directly contestable by agentic trading and its take rates are compressing. BE is the clearest value-trap suspect in the field: Oracle is ~70% of named backlog, Q3 free cash flow was −\$181M, and even after halving it trades at 32.6x forward earnings on a price that assumes ~47% revenue growth a year for five years. ADBE is a value-trap suspect too: generative AI attacks its core product directly, net new ARR fell ~36–37% year over year, and the 9 December FY2027 guide is the verdict — until then the 8.6x forward P/E is a discount for a structural question, not a margin of safety. INTU faces the same agent-interface threat — Claude Personal Finance coincided with a 7.17% single-day fall — and it reports earnings TODAY (2026-11-19 after close), so any rebound thesis is a binary coin-flip until tonight's print and tax season resolve whether the franchise holds.
