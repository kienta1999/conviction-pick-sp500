### Claim 6 — HIGH_ACCRUALS flag explained by ~$99B unrealised gain on private investments (SpaceX/Anthropic marks); core operating income converted to operating cash at ~96% — flag benign for core earnings quality
**Verdict: CONFIRMED**

- Q2 2026 net income was $112.1B (+298% YoY), driven by a **$99.0B gain on equity securities**, described by Alphabet as primarily unrealised gains on SpaceX and one unnamed private company. The press (Fortune, Motley Fool) identifies the private company as Anthropic; note Alphabet itself did not name it on the call — the Anthropic attribution is well-sourced press inference, not a company disclosure. Alphabet disclosed its SpaceX stake at $94.1B at 30 Jun 2026.
- Cash conversion arithmetic verified from primary figures: Q2 operating income $40.77B (call transcript) vs Q2 operating cash flow $39.1B (CFO on the call: "We generated strong operating cash flow of $39.1 billion in the second quarter") → 39.07 / 40.77 = **95.8% ≈ 96%**. Correct.
- Interpretation holds: the accrual gap (net income >> operating cash flow) is created by a non-cash, non-operating mark-to-market gain sitting **below** operating income, not by aggressive operating accruals. Core operating earnings quality is therefore not impugned by the flag. Separate, real caveat (not part of the claim): Q2 free cash flow was −$5.9B because capex was $44.9B — a capex-intensity issue, not an earnings-quality issue.
- Sources: Motley Fool, 23 Jul 2026 (https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/07/23/alphabet-just-revealed-a-94-billion-stake-in-space/); Fortune reporting via Startup Fortune summary (https://startupfortune.com/big-tech-booked-160-billion-in-paper-gains-from-ai-bets-last-quarter/); Q2 call transcript via Longbridge (https://longbridge.com/en/news/299285143).

## Premise caveat — dossier prices labelled "2026-10-20 closes"

Not one of the 10 assigned claims, but flagged because every multiple in the dossier keys off these prices: the stated prices do **not** match the 20 Oct 2026 closes found in public sources. FinanceCharts/FullRatio both give SYK's 20 Oct close as **$304.40** (the dossier's $293.38 matches SYK's 21 Oct intraday trading, ~$294 per Finnhub); StockAnalysis gives AMAT's 20 Oct previous close as **$404.98** (the dossier's $410.55 matches 21 Oct intraday ~$410.66); DailyIQ reports Alphabet fell to $339.57 on 20 Oct (dossier: $327.78). The dossier prices therefore look like a 21 Oct intraday snapshot mislabelled as 20 Oct closes. Impact: multiples computed off the dossier prices are ~1–4% lower than off true 20 Oct closes — direction of every valuation claim is unchanged, but the panel should relabel the price date or restate the closes before the memo ships.

## Overall assessment

The dossier's load-bearing facts hold up unusually well: all 10 claims are confirmed against primary or reputable secondary sources, including the two most falsifiable ones (Alphabet's 96% operating cash conversion, which reproduces exactly from call-transcript figures, and AMAT's net-cash position, which reproduces from the filed Q3 balance sheet). The corrections the panel should absorb are presentational, not thesis-level: (1) Claim 2's zero-cancellations evidence is about the capital order book, not Inari; (2) Claim 3's historical comparison is forward-to-forward (~26x) — trailing averages are ~41x; (3) Claim 9's 22.2x is an NTM, provider-dependent figure (live read ~23.2x; other bases 32–42x) and needs a basis footnote; (4) Claim 10's $600M was disclosed in Oct 2025; (5) the "2026-10-20 close" price labels appear to be 21 Oct intraday prices.
