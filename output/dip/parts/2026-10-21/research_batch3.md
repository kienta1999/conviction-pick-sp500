## GE — GE Aerospace — $303.15, −20.4% off 52w high, −6.1% vs SMA200

**Earnings-date check (asked explicitly): the screen's 2027-01-21 is NOT a missing October print — the October print already happened.** GE reported Q3 2026 **before the open on Tue 2026-10-20** (the screen's own close date): adj. EPS $2.25 vs ~$2.01 consensus, GAAP revenue $14.1B (+16%), orders +49% to ~$19B (book-to-bill 1.35), and a **raise** — FY26 adj. EPS guided to $8.00–8.10 (from $7.65–7.85), operating profit $10.9–11.0B, FCF $9.1–9.4B (Motley Fool / TradingView-Ben­zinga, 2026-10-20; MarketBeat earnings calendar). The stock still fell ~4.4% that day to ~$297–299 in intraday reports, i.e. the screen price was set on a sell-the-news day. Next print would be Q4, plausibly the screen's 2027-01-21 — that specific date itself was **not independently confirmed** in search results.

**1. Why it's down (3–12m) — TRANSITORY / valuation reset, not impairment.** Three stacked causes, none a demand break: (a) Valuation de-rating — Morningstar (pre-earnings, Oct 2026) noted the stock had "declined 19% since fuel prices spiked in early August," with high fuel threatening airline departures/services demand; (b) cost/supply friction — Q2 2026 operating margin fell 130bp to 21.7% on inflation, engine mix and investment, and spare-parts delinquencies rose 20% Q2 vs Q1 (Trefis, 2026-09-24); (c) deal + target-cut overhang — the $11.75B Consolidated Precision Products (CPP) castings acquisition announced 2026-09-08 drew valuation/integration questions, followed by target cuts: Wells Fargo $390→$380 (2026-09-30), Jefferies $455→$435 (2026-09-23), Deutsche Bank $450→$377. The Oct 20 leg down was specifically FCF −14% YoY to $2.04B, higher sales discounts/allowances and LEAP durability reserves, and GE9X revenue deferred pending midseal certification (HDFC Sky / Schaeffer's, 2026-10-20). Against that: orders +49%, backlog >$210B at Q2, guidance raised. **Verdict: transitory.**

**2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 9/10.** The moat is the installed base and its aftermarket annuity: ~80,000 engines in service (CEO count, via Trefis), engines on nearly three-fourths of global commercial flights (Morningstar), a commercial-services backlog >$170B, and the CFM/LEAP franchise across A320neo and 737 MAX. Certification, metallurgy and a global MRO network take decades to replicate; AI is a tool inside MRO (management cited AI cutting maintenance time in Q3 coverage), not a substitute for a certified turbine. Threat is execution/supply chain, not disruption.

**3. Rebound catalyst.** (i) GE9X upgraded-midseal FAA certification targeted **by end-2026** — engines with upgraded midseals have shipped since August; certification unlocks deferred GE9X revenue and removes a named overhang. (ii) Services compounding: management guides low-double-digit commercial-services growth in 2027 on the >$170B backlog; Q3 services revenue +17%, spare parts +20%+. (iii) Q4 print (Jan 2027, date unconfirmed) with FY27 outlook. (iv) CPP closing — timing **not found** in sources checked; deal is framed as securing scarce casting capacity, the binding constraint on the ramp.

**4. Balance-sheet survival — solid, watch the CPP funding.** End-Q3 2026: cash $8.8B, borrowings $19.3B (TradingView/Benzinga, 2026-10-20); screen net debt/EBITDA 0.90. YTD FCF $6.7B, +13%, 105% conversion; FY26 FCF guided $9.1–9.4B. $20B buyback authorized Dec 2025 ($2B repurchased in Q3 alone; $4.2B in H1). Note: CPP's ~$11.75B price is "a large portion cash, remainder new debt" (Kalkine/press coverage, Sep 2026), so leverage steps up on close — manageable against ~$9B+ annual FCF, but it consumes the net-cash cushion narrative.

**5. Margin of safety / valuation — real upside to target, thin safety on the multiple.** Screen fwd P/E 32.8 checks out only on a **next-year** basis: $303.15 / ~$9.05 FY27 consensus EPS (MarketBeat) ≈ 33.5×. On the just-raised FY26 guide, the stock is ~37–38× the $8.05 midpoint — Evergreen/Fool made the same point Oct 20 ("north of 36× FCF"). Trailing P/E ~35.3. That is the richest multiple in the aero-engine peer set (peer forward multiples not independently verified — **not found** in a single clean source). Analyst targets corroborate the screen: consensus $386.67 (DefenseWorld, 2026-10-15) to $390.96 (Schaeffer's, 2026-10-20) vs screen $388.95 (+28.3%). Morningstar fair value is lower at $347 (+~14%). Margin of safety therefore comes from earnings growth/backlog visibility, not from a cheap multiple.

**6. Category position — still clear #1, with pricing power.** Sixth consecutive quarter of ≥20% CES revenue growth in Q3 (+21%; services +17% on volume, heavier widebody scopes **and pricing**, per the Q3 call summary); equipment orders more than tripled; >1,800 engine commitments at Farnborough including >1,000 LEAP-1A for IndiGo. No share-loss evidence found.

**7. Value-trap risk — low trap risk, real "expensive quality" risk.** Bear case: durability costs on LEAP/GE9X keep eating the services margin (CES margin already slipped 0.1pp to 27.9% in Q3); sustained high fuel prices cut departures and shop visits; CPP is a full-price, debt-part-funded deal at the cycle peak; and at ~37× current-year earnings any guidance slip de-rates hard — exactly the Oct 20 pattern (beat + raise, stock −4.4%).

**8. Rebound score: 7/10.** Verdict: A valuation-and-costs dip in the sector's strongest franchise — fundamentals (orders, backlog, raised guide) contradict impairment, but the entry multiple leaves the rebound dependent on flawless execution and the GE9X certification landing on time.

## CPRT — Copart — $28.11, −38.6% off high, −14.1% vs SMA200

Screen: fwd P/E 15.5, target $39.00 (+38.7%), rev growth TTM +0.4%, op margin 32.0%, net debt/EBITDA −2.36 (net cash), next earnings 2026-11-19 (confirmed by Zacks/StockAnalysis listings).

**EARNINGS-QUALITY FLAG — RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN, explained.** FY2026 (ended 2026-07-31, 10-K cash-flow statement): accounts receivable consumed **$80.6M of cash vs $34.0M in FY2025**; total receivables rose $763.4M → $859.1M (**+12.5%**; accounts receivable alone $728.8M → $809.2M, +11.0%) against revenue growth of just **+0.4%** — receivables growing ~30× faster than sales. Allowance for credit losses also jumped to $5.6M from $0.35M. Mitigants: operating cash flow was $1.60B vs net income $1.48B — cash conversion still >100%, so earnings are **not** running ahead of cash in aggregate; an independent forensic review (InTickers FY26 analysis) scores accounting anomalies 8/8 clean and calls receivables "a monitoring item rather than evidence of material accounting distortion." The OCF decline (−10.8% YoY) is also partly mechanical: lower interest income and cash deployed into the $1.63B buyback.
**One-line verdict: Mostly benign/timing — a yellow flag, not earnings ahead of cash — because OCF ($1.60B) still exceeds net income ($1.48B), but receivables +12.5% on +0.4% sales must shrink at the Nov 19 print or the flag turns red.**

**1. Why it's down — MIXED: cyclical trough PLUS one genuine share loss; not yet proven permanent.** FY26 revenue +0.4% to $4.7B, net income −4.4% to ~$1.48B; Q4 net income −17.4%, EPS $0.35 vs $0.41 (company release, 2026-09-10). Volume mechanics: Q4 global units sold −2.9%, US insurance units −7.5%, US assignments −5%, collision claim frequency −3.4% YoY; Q2 was worse (global insurance units −9.3% reported, −4.1% ex-CAT). Drivers management/analysts cite: fewer accidents (ADAS/safety), drivers downgrading from comprehensive to damage-only cover as premiums rose (fewer total-loss claims), tough CAT comps, and faster insurer cycle times shrinking inventory. **The share-loss datum: management said Q4 domestic insurance assignments would have risen +2.3% excluding "one customer loss"** (Q4 call, via AInvest summary) — i.e. most of the US insurance decline is a single large insurer moving volume. Corroboration that the rival is gaining: RB Global's IAA grew auto GTV ~+13% / units ~+11% in Q2 2026 while Copart's global units fell 2.4%, and secondary research reports a major carrier (Progressive named in one deep-dive — single-source, treat as reported-not-confirmed) shifting volume to IAA for service-level transparency. Governance added a leg down: CEO Jeff Liaw's departure announced 2026-06-29 knocked the stock −8.0% to $28.10 that day, with securities-law investigations opened and the new CEO being the chairman's son-in-law (ChartsView research summary). HSBC downgraded to Hold, target $46→$36, on 2026-09-17. **Central-question answer: the near-zero growth is mostly a trough (claims frequency, coverage mix, CAT comps — with the total-loss rate actually at a record ~22.8% as repair costs/ADAS complexity push cars to salvage), but it is not share-neutral: one large-insurer loss plus IAA's multi-quarter volume gains are real share leakage Copart must win back. Trough-dominant, share-loss-real — call it 70/30.**

**2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 8/10.** Scarce, permitted salvage-yard land near metros bought years ahead, exclusive whole-volume insurer contracts, and a global buyer network that maximises salvage proceeds — "close to a toll booth" (Insider Monkey moat review, 2026-10-12). Op margins ~32% (screen; FY26 gross margin 44.7%) are structurally above peers. AI helps Copart (pricing, title/claims automation — management discussed AI productivity on the Q2 call) more than it threatens it; the true long-run threat is autonomy/ADAS shrinking accident supply, partly offset because sensor-laden cars are totalled more often when they do crash.

**3. Rebound catalyst.** (i) **Q1 FY27 print, 2026-11-19** — the test: US assignments ex-CAT and receivables direction; Zacks consensus already pencils revenue +2.4% / EPS $0.41 flat YoY, a low bar. (ii) **ACV Auctions acquisition** — $10.50/share cash, ~$1.9B equity value, announced 2026-09-10, expected to close by end-CY2026, accretive in first full year (company); tender was extended to 2026-10-07 with 47.78% tendered by Sep 30 and the HSR waiting period set to expire 2026-10-13 (Business Wire via press summaries) — close status after Oct 13 **not found**; ACV adds ~800k whole-car units/yr and dealer volume (dealer units already +5.8% in Q4; BlueCar +~20%). (iii) International compounding (Q4 intl service revenue +15.5%, intl insurance units +11.2%). (iv) Any major CAT event season. (v) Continued buyback ($1.63B in FY26, share count −4.7% in Q4).

**4. Balance-sheet survival — fortress.** Zero debt (total liabilities $913.9M against $10.03B assets; equity $9.10B); cash $1.91B plus $2.6B short-term investments at FY-end; current ratio ~7.9 (StockAnalysis). FCF FY26 ≈ OCF $1.60B − capex $337M ≈ **$1.27B**. Screen net debt/EBITDA −2.36 (some aggregators show −1.45 counting cash only — direction identical: deeply net cash). Survival is not a question in any scenario.

**5. Margin of safety / valuation — the cheapest it has been in a decade, on live multiples slightly above the screen's.** Trailing P/E 17.95 (Oct 20) vs 10-year average **29.2** (−39%; FullRatio/Wisesheets histories agree, ~29.1–29.4). Live forward P/E is **17.1–17.15** (StockAnalysis/FinanceCharts/Zacks 17.06) — i.e. the screen's 15.5 is ~10% more flattering than live near-year forwards; note the gap. Peer contrast: RB Global trades at ~34.7× trailing. Target: screen $39.00 vs MarketBeat consensus ~$37.8; HSBC's post-downgrade $36 is the bear anchor. Even at live 17×, the stock prices in near-zero growth forever — historically it has never sustained a sub-20× multiple while growing.

**6. Category position — still #1, but the lead is narrowing at the margin.** ~50% US insurance-salvage share vs RB Global/IAA ~35% (industry deep-dive estimate, Mar 2026). Pricing power intact and doing the work: US ASPs +4.2% in Q4, +5.5% FY26, which is what kept revenue positive while units fell. But #1 is now contested: IAA's volume growth and the one-customer loss mean Copart is defending, not extending, share in US insurance; its growth edge has moved to international and non-insurance (dealer/bank/fleet).

**7. Value-trap risk — the honest bear case is real.** If accident frequency falls structurally (ADAS → autonomy) faster than total-loss rates rise, the unit pool shrinks permanently and 17× is a trap, not a discount. Supporting bear facts: SG&A rose from 7.8% to 10.2% of revenue FY23–FY25 (cost inflation against flat volume); FY26 EPS was propped by buybacks while net income fell; capex was cut ~46% in 9M FY26, which one deep-dive reads as management itself planning for stagnant volumes; governance (abrupt CEO exit, related-party succession, law-firm investigations) deserves a discount until disproven; ACV at $1.9B for a loss-making marketplace could be buying growth Copart used to get free.

**8. Rebound score: 6/10.** Verdict: A net-cash toll booth at a decade-low multiple whose dip is mostly a claims trough — but the single-insurer volume loss to IAA and the receivables flag mean the Nov 19 print, not the valuation, decides whether this is a dip or the start of share-loss compounding.

## GNRC — Generac — $201.15, −31.9% off 52w high ($296.44)

Screen: fwd P/E 15.5, target ~$287.76 (+43.1%), rev growth 5.0%, op margin 17.9%, next earnings **2026-10-28** (confirmed, multiple listings).

**1. Why it's down — TRANSITORY: cyclical residential softness + a hype round-trip, not impairment.** The 52w high was a spike, not a plateau: the stock ran on data-center excitement, then surged again on the Amazon deal (SEC filing 2026-09-16), then gave it back — Quiver (2026-10-12) attributes the October slide to profit-taking and valuation caution, Citi cut its target $300→$232 (Neutral, 2026-09-30), Cantor trimmed $333→$297 citing a sector valuation reset, and Zacks downgraded Strong Buy→Hold (2026-09-28). Underneath, the residential engine has been soft for a year: Q4 2025 net sales −12% YoY to $1.09B with adj. EPS $1.61 vs $1.81 consensus, on a weak outage environment; Q2 2026 residential revenue −2% to $621.3M (portables down on low outage activity; energy storage down after Puerto Rico's DOE grant program ended in 2025), and management **cut** 2026 residential growth guidance to high-single-digits from ~10% (affordability, low outages, a small divestiture). Also discount Q2's headline EPS beat ($2.91 vs $2.01): gross margin got a ~6pp lift from **tariff refunds** — a one-off the market was right to fade. **Verdict: transitory/cyclical — the dip cause (outage lull + post-spike multiple reset) is not a demand or share break; the structural business is actually mix-shifting toward faster-growing C&I.**

**2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 7/10.** Residential: the dominant home-standby brand with the largest dealer/installer and service network in the US (exact share figure **not found** in a primary source during this search — leadership itself is uncontested in coverage) plus a growing connected installed base (5.25M+ connected homes, Q2 2026). C&I/data-center: moat is thinner — qualification with hyperscalers (two multi-year supply agreements; Amazon the landmark one), scarce large-MW manufacturing capacity now being doubled, and lead times. Caterpillar and Cummins are bigger, better-capitalised rivals in large gensets (CAT's Q2 2026 power-generation sales to users +72%). AI is a demand *driver* here (data-center backup), not a disruptor; the disruption threat is technology substitution (turbines, fuel cells, grid-scale batteries) at the very large end. Score reflects: strong consumer franchise, contestable industrial one.

**3. Rebound catalyst — dated and concrete.** (i) **Q3 print 2026-10-28** — first chance to confirm C&I momentum and residential stabilisation; consensus FY26 EPS ~$9.73–9.74, KeyCorp Q3 est. $2.46. (ii) **Amazon agreement**: initial **$2.4B of deliveries scheduled 2027–2028**, up to ~$8B total potential, with Amazon receiving warrants for up to 1,693,745 shares at ~$200.93 (SEC filing, 2026-09-16) — Wells Fargo sees data-center revenue >$3B by 2028 (via press summaries). (iii) Backlog visibility already in hand: data-center backlog ~**$1.6B** at July 2026, ~$450M data-center revenue expected in 2026, C&I 2026 growth guidance raised to low-30% (from ~30%, and mid-to-high-20% before that). (iv) Weather optionality: any return to baseline-or-worse outage activity directly re-rates the residential/portable lines management has guided to ~10% growth on normalised outages.

**4. Balance-sheet survival — adequate, not a fortress; leverage is the watch item.** At 2026-06-30 (10-Q): cash $264.9M; borrowings ≈ $48.3M short-term + $32.1M current LTD + $1,249.0M long-term ≈ **$1.33B gross debt**, so net debt ≈ $1.06B — roughly ~1.2× a guided 18–19% adj. EBITDA margin on ~$4.7B-ish sales (analyst-style estimate, not a company disclosure). Interest coverage ~7.1× (secondary source). FCF: guided ~$350M for 2026 despite capex rising to 4.5% of sales for data-center capacity (payback <12 months claimed); Q2 FCF $63M vs $14M a year earlier, flattered by tariff refunds. Debt-funded expansion (incl. the $211.8M Allmand acquisition in H1) plus the Amazon capacity build means survival is comfortable but the balance sheet no longer funds mistakes — execution must convert backlog to cash in 2027 as scheduled.

**5. Margin of safety / valuation — cheap only on next-year earnings; be explicit about which year.** This is the key nuance: the screen's 15.5× matches **next-year** forwards (live next-year forward P/E 14.98, Tradestie, Oct 20–21). On **near-year** FY26 consensus (~$9.74), $201.15 is ~20.6×; Zacks' forward-12-month figure was 18.78× in August vs a 5-year median of 19.25× — i.e. only at/slightly below its own history on near-term numbers, and trailing P/E is a meaningless ~44–45× on trough TTM EPS ($2.69–4.36 depending on source/method). Targets strongly corroborate the screen: MarketBeat consensus **$290.55** (Moderate Buy; 13–15 Buys, 7 Holds) vs screen $287.76 — but the range is wide: Citi $232 to Canaccord $375 (raised +$100 on the Amazon deal, 2026-09-17); Stephens $335, Baird $305. Margin of safety = real if FY27 EPS (~$13.90 KeyCorp est.) materialises; thin if you only underwrite FY26.

**6. Category position — #1 where it has pricing power (residential), challenger where the growth is (data-center).** Home standby still converts pricing: Q2 home-standby sales returned to growth "primarily due to increased price realization and slightly higher volumes" despite the outage lull — that is pricing power. In large data-center gensets Generac is a fast-growing challenger (C&I +29% to $556.5M in Q2) against CAT/Cummins scale, winning on capacity availability and hyperscaler qualification rather than incumbency.

**7. Value-trap risk.** (i) Data-center digestion: one detailed model (Rational Investment Management, 2026-10-07) has domestic C&I sales more than tripling to 2028 then **giving back ~40%** as the build-out normalises — if the market starts pricing the give-back, the multiple compresses into the growth. (ii) Mix shift dilutes quality: C&I/data-center margins are below residential, and Q2's margin optics leaned on non-recurring tariff refunds. (iii) Residential may be structurally, not cyclically, impaired by affordability and the post-2021 pull-forward overhang — the stock is still −60%+ from its 2021 peak of ~$525. (iv) Customer concentration: the story now hinges on a handful of hyperscalers, one of which (Amazon) holds warrants at ~$200.93 — dilution priced almost exactly at the current quote. (v) Capacity is being built ahead of the 2027–28 revenue; a hyperscaler capex pause strands it.

**8. Rebound score: 7/10.** Verdict: A cyclical outage-lull plus post-Amazon-spike reset in a business whose growth engine has credibly rotated to data centers — transitory dip, with the Oct 28 print and 2027 Amazon deliveries as the proof points, provided you underwrite it on FY27 earnings rather than the flattering headline multiple.
