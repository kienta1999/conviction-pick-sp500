## HSY — Hershey Company — $169.22, −27.3% off high, −9.8% vs SMA200

Screen: fwd P/E 17.2, target $198.00 (+17.0%), rev growth TTM 4.9%, op margin 23.1% (screen; note Q2 adj. operating margin printed 20.2% and TTM operating margin in some sources is far lower after the 2025 cocoa trough — the 23.1% looks like a forward/normalised figure), net debt/EBITDA 1.89, next earnings 2026-10-29 (confirmed, before open; consensus EPS ~$2.11–2.12).

**1. Why it's down — cost shock was transitory; the volume/share damage it caused is the open, semi-permanent question.**
- Cocoa: futures hit a record $12,906/t in Dec 2024 (West Africa crop failure/disease), roughly tripled in 2024. Hershey hedges 3–24 months forward, so peak-cost cocoa flowed through 2025–H1 2026 P&L; 2025 EPS collapsed (Q3 2025 net income −38% YoY, Q4 −60% YoY per MarketBeat summaries) and the stock de-rated from its $239.48 high (Oct-2025/2026 range) to a $156.16 low, $160.19 close on 2 Oct 2026.
- The response created the second problem: double-digit list-price increases (announced Jul 2025, rolled out from mid-Sep 2025). Q2 2026 (30 Jul): net price realisation ~+12 pts company-wide, ~+14 pts in North America Confectionery, but **volume −8 pts overall and ~−10 pts in NA Confectionery**; International volume also ~−8%. Management said elasticity was *slightly better than its full-year assumptions* in Q2 — evidence against a demand cliff, not for volume health.
- Share: Trefis (updated 15 Oct 2026), citing Jefferies, reports Hershey lost ~135bp of dollar share and ~145bp of volume share in core chocolate in Q2 2026, with a mid-teens price premium vs Mars "intensifying volume losses." That is concrete share erosion, not just category elasticity.
- Overhangs layered on top: GLP-1 appetite-suppression narrative (cited in Motley Fool, 23 Sep 2026), value-seeking US consumer, reformulation backlash (some Reese's products shifted to compound coatings; Brad Reese public criticism) — company said in March 2026 investor presentation it will return to traditional milk/dark recipes from 2027.
- **Central question — transitory or permanent margin reset? Mixed, leaning transitory on cost, unresolved on volume.** Cost side is demonstrably reversing: cocoa ~$5,613/t on 8 Oct 2026, **−57% from the peak** (low ~$3,100 in Mar 2026); Q2 2026 adj. gross margin already recovered to **41.6% (+350bp)**, adj. operating margin 20.2% (+450bp), adj. EPS $1.90 vs $1.43 consensus, sales $2.79B (+6.6%). Management's framework (Barclays conference, 9 Sep 2026): restore profit in 2026–27, return to balanced growth in 2028; FY26 guide maintained — sales +4.5–5.0%, adj. EPS $8.36–$8.52. But the CFO has acknowledged cocoa's new equilibrium is *above* historical norms, and shelf prices are staying up while hedged peak-cost stock clears through end-2026, with real deflation benefit weighted to **2027**. So: margins recover toward ~41%+ gross, plausibly not to the pre-crisis mid-40s at the same volumes; whether the ~8–10% volume surrendered comes back — or was permanently handed to Mars/private label — is the true impairment test, and it is not yet answered.

**2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 8/10.** Iconic, non-replicable brand portfolio (Reese's — the #1 US chocolate brand franchise, Hershey's, Kisses — >70M produced/day), dominant seasonal (Halloween/Easter) shelf position, scale distribution, plus a growing salty-snacks arm (SkinnyPop, Dot's) that outperformed in Q2 and diversifies cocoa exposure. AI disruption risk is negligible; the real disruptors are GLP-1 drugs, private label and Mars/Nestlé competition. Docked points because the 2024–26 episode proved the moat could not pass through a 3x input spike without volume and share damage — pricing power exists but is elastic at current price gaps.

**3. Rebound catalyst.** (i) **Q3 print 29 Oct 2026** into the Halloween season (NRF: $4.1B Halloween candy spend forecast) — watch volume trend vs Q2's −8/−10 and gross margin path. (ii) **Cocoa deflation mechanically reaching the P&L in H2 2026 and mainly 2027** as 3–24-month hedges roll off peak levels — CFO has indicated hedges sit above spot, pointing to further 2027 deflation; management nudged gross-margin guide only slightly down in Q2 (salty-snacks throughput/logistics, "Dots" capacity) despite that tailwind. (iii) Return to real-cocoa recipes in 2027 as a brand-repair lever, innovation/marketing tentpoles (Reese's × Oreo etc.), and salty capacity automation online in 2027. Timing: earnings recovery is a 2027 story; the stock can re-rate earlier on two consecutive quarters of stabilising volume.

**4. Balance-sheet survival — no issue.** Screen net debt/EBITDA 1.89x; TIKR (7 Oct 2026) 1.84x; total debt ~$5.9B vs cash ~$0.8B; interest coverage 9.64x; FCF $1.82B in FY2025, cash dividend payout ~50% TTM to Jun 2026 (dividends $1.09B in 2025), dividend yield ~3.4–3.6% at screen price. Current ratio is thin (1.18) as normal for staples. No solvency or dividend-coverage concern.

**5. Margin of safety / valuation.** Screen fwd P/E 17.2 — live: 17.3x NTM (TIKR, 7 Oct), 18.29x forward (StockAnalysis, 21 Oct), 19.9x on 2026 / 17.4x on 2027 EPS (Trefis); **live near-year multiple is ~1 turn higher than screen, not a thesis-changing gap** — the screen figure is closer to a 2027 multiple. Vs own history: 5-year average P/E ~24.7x (TIKR), year-end P/Es of 28.9x (2022) and 20.5x (2023) — HSY is at a genuine multi-year discount *if* $8.4–8.5 EPS (2026) and growth to 2027 land. Precise peer fwd P/E for Mondelez/Mars (private) — **not found** in a directly comparable form; sector-average P/E shown by Trefis is 12.4x, but that is a broad food sector, not branded chocolate. Analyst target: screen $198.00 (+17.0%); direction confirmed by BofA's cut to $200 (from $220) on 25 Jun 2026 — i.e., targets have been *falling toward* the price, a caution flag.

**6. Category position — still the US chocolate leader, but currently losing share.** Hershey remains #1 in US chocolate/confectionery by sales with pricing power demonstrated (+12–14 pts price realisation held in Q2), yet it is the share *donor* right now: −135bp dollar / −145bp volume share in Q2 (Jefferies via Trefis) at a mid-teens price premium to Mars. Salty snacks is gaining. Verdict: #1 with a narrowing lead; the pricing-power claim survives, the share claim does not — yet.

**7. Value-trap risk (honest bear case).** The classic trap here: cocoa relief arrives in 2027 but Hershey keeps shelf prices high to harvest it, volumes never recover, retailers/Mars take the space permanently, and "margin recovery" is achieved on a smaller, ex-growth volume base — while GLP-1 penetration quietly lowers per-capita chocolate demand. Add El Niño risk to the 2026/27 West Africa crop (Reuters-reported Oct 2026 warnings that El Niño historically cuts cocoa output; ICE inventories at a 2.25-year high cut the other way), a reformulation trust deficit with its own founding family, and targets still being cut (BofA). If Q3 shows volume worsening beyond −10, the 17x is 17x on a shrinking franchise.

**8. Rebound score: 7/10 — Verdict:** The cost half of the dip is provably transitory (−57% cocoa, gross margin already +350bp) and the balance sheet/valuation support the dip — but the share-loss half is real, so size it as a recovery-in-progress that must prove volume stabilisation on 29 Oct, not as a clean mean-reversion.
