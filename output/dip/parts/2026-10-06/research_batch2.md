## 1. TPR — Tapestry, Inc. (Coach, Kate Spade)

### 1. Why it's down — TEMPORARY (expectations reset), with one real wound
- **Trigger:** Aug 13, 2026 — FY26 Q4 report was a beat (Q4 revenue $1.876B +9%, adj EPS $1.32 +28% y/y; FY26 revenue $8.004B +14%, adj EPS $7.05 +38%) but FY27 guidance (revenue $8.4–8.5B, EPS $7.80–7.90) landed a hair below consensus ($8.45B midpoint vs $8.46–8.47B). Stock fell 16.5% in a single session and is down ~30% over the following month (Cramer, Mad Money Sep 9, 2026).
- **Mechanism:** a multiple reset, not an earnings cut — forward multiple compressed ~19.6x → ~16.4x because the *growth rate* collapsed: revenue growth +14% → ~+5.8%, adj EPS growth +38% → ~+11.3%, margin expansion 340 bps → ~50 bps (BestStocks, Aug 2026).
- **Kate Spade is the real wound:** FY26 revenue fell 10% to $1.075B; guided to a further high-single-digit decline in FY27 with a continued operating loss; the company took an **$855M impairment** on Kate Spade intangibles/goodwill in FY25. Coach supplies >100% of net growth (~88% of revenue) — Tapestry is effectively a one-brand company with a troubled second brand attached.
- **Coach itself is still compounding:** FY26 revenue +24% (23% constant currency), Q4 +15% to $1.64B on Tabby handbag strength, rising average unit retail, broad new/existing customer demand (CEO Crevoiserat, Aug 13 earnings call). Q1 FY27 EPS guide ~$1.55 beat the $1.48 consensus.
- **Verdict: TEMPORARY.** A guidance-treadmill derating after three FY26 outlook raises; the core engine (Coach) is healthy. The permanent-impairment tail risk sits entirely in Kate Spade.

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 8/10
Accessible-luxury handbag demand is brand- and trend-driven; no AI or substitute technology can replicate Coach's brand equity, design desirability, or DTC distribution. GenAI creates marketing imagery, not $400 handbags people carry. Moat = brand power + scale + pricing power (rising average unit retail). Threat is fashion-cycle risk, not tech disruption.

### 3. Rebound catalyst
- **Holiday season + Q1 FY27 print (~Nov 5, 2026 est.):** Coach guided to high-single-digit growth; Q1 EPS guide already ahead of consensus — another beat would re-anchor the story on Coach compounding.
- **Capital return:** dividend raised in FY26, fresh buyback authorization committed (MarketBeat, Sep 2026).
- **Kate Spade stabilization:** any sign the revenue decline is bottoming (added ~400k new customers in a recent quarter) re-rates the "second brand" discount.

### 4. Balance-sheet survival — strong
FY26 free cash flow ~$1.8B, up 66% y/y (ainvest, Aug 2026). Net margins 19.09%, operating margin >23%. MarketBeat (Sep 2026) shows D/E 3.44 — elevated on paper, but cash generation is massive and the dividend was raised, not cut. No distress or dilution risk.

### 5. Valuation / margin of safety
- Fwd PE ~13.1 (screen); MarketBeat fwd PE 14.59 (Sep 2026). Historically a mid-teens multiple name — now at the low end, below luxury peers.
- Analyst mean target **$175.12–175.13** (21 analysts, Moderate Buy: 15 buy / 2 strong buy / 4 hold; Morgan Stanley $159 OW, Bernstein $185 MP, Wells Fargo $160 OW — all post-earnings Aug 13–14, 2026). Vs ~$115.69 → **~+51% implied upside** (MarketBeat/American Market News, Sep 30–Oct 4, 2026).

### 6. Category position
Coach is the #1 accessible-luxury handbag brand in the US, with demonstrated pricing power (rising unit retails, margin expansion). The dip is an expectations reset, not share loss — Kate Spade's contraction is the only share-losing asset.

### 7. Value-trap risk (honest bear)
The Tabby cycle ends and Coach growth normalizes to low-single-digits; China consumer stays soft; Kate Spade requires another impairment and perpetual reinvestment; the mid-single-digit guide proves the *ceiling*, not the floor — and the multiple stays at 13–14x forever.

### 8. Rebound score: 7/10
**One-sentence verdict:** A 16.5%-in-a-day multiple reset on a company printing record revenue, $1.8B FCF, and 24% Coach growth — the dip is sentiment, not franchise damage, and +51% analyst upside prices in the fear.

## 2. VRSK — Verisk Analytics (P&C insurance data & analytics)

### 1. Why it's down — TEMPORARY (cyclical + derating), growth deceleration is the watch item
- **Cyclical/insurance-cycle:** softer-than-expected Q3 2025 revenue; demand for property-claims solutions cooled amid **low severe-weather activity** (quiet hurricane season); insurance premium growth moderating — a softer rate cycle means less transaction volume (Barchart via FinancialContent, Feb 2026; ad-hoc-news, Sep 30, 2026).
- **Growth deceleration:** Q2 2026 (Jul 29, 2026) revenue $806M (+4.3% y/y, +5.8% organic constant currency vs 7.9% a year earlier). Subscription revenue (83% of total) still +8% OCC; transactional revenue −4.2% OCC — the mix is resilient, the weather-linked part is weak.
- **Multiple derating:** growth expectations were "too ambitious," margin pressure from acquisitions, elevated debt (same Barchart note). Plus a Sep 2026 sector-wide rotation out of data/analytics names (AlphaStreet, Sep 2026: VRSK −5.2% in lockstep with peers on no company news). Generative-AI disruption fears repriced the whole group — BMO upgraded to Outperform arguing the market was "overly harsh in pricing in worries about… the potential impact of generative AI" (Feb 2026).
- **Verdict: TEMPORARY.** No lost moat, no secular demand decline — weather and the insurance cycle are classic mean-reverting drivers. The derating (from ~30x+ to high-teens fwd) already prices in slower growth.

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 8/10
Mission-critical P&C insurance infrastructure: ISO statistical agent data, Xactware claims, underwriting platforms — embedded in carrier workflows with 83% subscription revenue and pricing power. The AI threat is real but indirect (genAI could commoditize pricing data over time); Verisk is itself the AI adopter here — XactAI licenses up 10x to 7,000, Premium Audit AI in renewal growth, MCP connectors with Anthropic Claude in pilot (Q2 2026 call summary). Data network effects, not easily routed around.

### 3. Rebound catalyst

- **Catastrophe reversion:** a normal hurricane season restores transactional claims volume — the single biggest swing factor for H2 2026/H1 2027.
- **Q3 2026 print (late Oct 2026 est.):** a subscription-driven beat would confirm the "weather, not franchise" thesis. Institutional dip-buying already visible: Confluence Investment Management opened a 100k-share position (disclosed Oct 1, 2026).

### 4. Balance-sheet survival — strong cash machine, levered but covered
Q2 2026: operating cash flow $366M (+49.7% y/y), FCF $298M (+57.9%), FCF margin 37% (company press release, Jul 29, 2026). Dividend $0.50/qtr ($2.00 annualized, ~1.1% yield), payout 30.7%. D/E 10.42 per MarketBeat (Sep 2026) — optically high, an artifact of buybacks shrinking equity (negative ROE print); debt is investment-grade and easily serviced by ~$1.2B+ annual FCF. FY26 guidance reaffirmed: revenue $3.19–3.24B, adj EBITDA $1.79–1.83B (56.0–56.5% margin), adj EPS $7.45–7.75. No distress.

### 5. Valuation / margin of safety
- Fwd PE ~18.8 (screen); MarketBeat fwd PE 23.83 (Sep 15, 2026). Either way far below its historical ~28–35x range — a structural derating of a compounder.
- Consensus **Moderate Buy** (15 analysts); mean target ~**$226.71** (MarketBeat, Sep 2026; others cite $230–237). Vs ~$164.82 → **~+38% implied upside**.

### 6. Category position
Still the #1 P&C insurance data/analytics provider — no evidence of share loss; the volume softness is industry-wide (weather + rate cycle), not competitive.

### 7. Value-trap risk (honest bear)
Organic growth is structurally stuck at 5–6% and never re-accelerates; genAI actually does commoditize pricing data over a decade; leverage limits buyback capacity; the market permanently assigns a 18–20x multiple and the stock dead-moneys.

### 8. Rebound score: 6.5/10
**One-sentence verdict:** A weather-and-rate-cycle air pocket plus an AI-fear derating on an 83%-subscription, 37%-FCF-margin franchise — cyclical, not structural, with a reaffirmed 6–8% growth target and ~38% analyst upside.

## 3. ADBE — Adobe Inc.

### 1. Why it's down — TEMPORARY fear with a genuine structural question
- **The core fear (structural, but not yet in the numbers):** generative AI commoditizes creative work; AI-native tools (Midjourney-class image models, video models, Canva, Figma) pull creative work away from Creative Cloud. Net new ARR has weakened (secondary analysis cites −36%); RPO $22.16B in Q3 was +8.4% y/y — the **first single-digit RPO growth since early FY23**, and down sequentially (Sep 10, 2026 data sheet). Management is *choosing* slower growth: Firefly price cut ~30%, hard freemium pivot (100M+ free Creative MAU, 900M+ Acrobat/Express MAU, >1B total MAU) — trading near-term recurring revenue for a bigger future base (ainvest, Sep 2026).
- **Beat-and-fell pattern:** Q1 and Q2 FY26 both beat estimates and the stock fell both times (Mar & Jun 2026). Q3 (Sep 10, 2026): revenue $6.76B +13% (beat $6.69B), non-GAAP EPS $6.13 (beat $6.08), record op cash flow $2.52B — but Q4 revenue guide $6.80–6.85B (midpoint just under $6.85B consensus) and the stock dropped ~3% after hours. Morgan Stanley downgraded to Underweight ($240 target) on AI-substitution risk to the recurring base (summer 2026).
- **Leadership transition:** longtime CEO Shantanu Narayen steps down to Executive Chair Dec 1, 2026; Anil Chakravarthy (Experience Cloud, not Creative Cloud) named CEO; Creative & Productivity president David Wadhwani resigned the same day (8-K, Sep 3, 2026) — stock fell 6.7% on Sep 4. Jefferies: Wadhwani was "the rational choice"; expects more departures.
- **Verdict: TEMPORARY — sentiment and transition, not realized impairment.** Record revenue, 88.7% gross margin, >40% FCF margin: this is a company *discounted as if disrupted*, not one being disrupted. But the pricing-power-erosion question is legitimate and unresolved — the one real canary is Firefly cannibalizing Adobe's own stock-photo library "faster than expected" (Q1 FY26 call).

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 7/10
Entrenched creative workflows, file/asset formats (PSD/PDF), cross-app integrations, enterprise contracts, and commercially-safe Firefly IP. The abundance-scarcity logic: genAI makes content creation abundant and near-free; the scarce layer is the trusted professional-grade tool stack everyone already opens — which Adobe owns. Counter-evidence: AI-influenced ARR is already >1/3 of the book and AI-first ARR is $650M+ (+150% y/y) — Adobe is monetizing the disruption it's accused of being a victim of. Deduct for the real cannibalization vector (stock library) and credible AI-native substitutes.

### 3. Rebound catalyst
- **Paid conversion proof:** the freemium bet converts 1B+ MAUs into paid ARR — Q4 FY26 (Dec 2026) ending ARR vs the 10.2% growth target (~$28.28B implied) is the single deciding variable.
- **Adobe MAX (Nov 2026):** agentic-AI innovation showcase; CX Enterprise Coworker and agent workflows across ChatGPT/Claude/Copilot.
- **New CEO settling in** (Dec 1) + **$25B buyback** (~$24.5B remaining per Fool, Oct 4, 2026 — about a quarter of the ~$93B market cap) creating a mechanical EPS floor: ~9.5M shares repurchased in Q3 alone.

### 4. Balance-sheet survival — fortress
Net debt/EBITDA ~0.07; D/E 0.42 (Finimize/MarketBeat); Q3 record operating cash flow $2.52B; FCF margins >40%; $24.5B buyback authorization remaining. Zero distress — the question is growth, never survival.

### 5. Valuation / margin of safety
- Fwd PE ~8.6 (screen); ~10x FY26 adj EPS guide midpoint ($24.45–24.50) at ~$240 (Fool, Oct 4, 2026). Historically 25–40x — a decade-low multiple on intact double-digit growth.
- Consensus: **Hold** (MarketBeat rating 2.03, 34 analysts); Nasdaq panel mean target **$267.77** (8 buy / 11 hold / 4 sell, Sep 17, 2026); MarketBeat $282.68 (Sep 2026). Vs $237.57 → **~+13–19% implied upside**. Wide dispersion (HSBC $308 bull vs BofA $190 bear) = the debate is fully priced.

### 6. Category position
Still the #1 professional creative software franchise with pricing power — but competitive intensity is genuinely rising (Figma, Canva; Microsoft Copilot cited at 30M paid seats in secondary analysis). Share is *contested*, not yet lost.

### 7. Value-trap risk (honest bear)
Freemium MAUs never convert and train a generation to expect free; net new ARR keeps declining; AI substitutes hollow out willingness to pay for seats; leadership churn delays product decisions; FY27 guide disappoints and the 10x multiple proves to be the fair price for a melting franchise.

### 8. Rebound score: 7.5/10
**One-sentence verdict:** A decade-low ~9–10x multiple on a record-revenue, 40%+-FCF-margin franchise where the bear case (AI substitution) is entirely in the price and the bull case (Adobe monetizing the disruption) is already showing in $650M+ of 150%-growth AI-first ARR.

## 4. CPRT — Copart, Inc.

### 1. Why it's down — MIXED: cyclical insurance air pocket + genuine cost/share pressure
- **Cyclical (temporary):** insurance salvage volumes have declined for a year — global insurance units −2.7% in Q3 FY26 (1.9% ex-cat; prior quarters down 8.4–9%). Cause per management (Q2 & Q3 FY26 calls): after the 2022–24 inflationary period, insurers hiked premiums to restore profitability; consumers responded by raising deductibles, dropping collision coverage, moving to liability-only. Evidence: earned car years −4% y/y while vehicles in operation +1.4% (ISS Fast Track); ~25% of repairs now self-pay (CCC). Management notes some insurers are "already becoming more aggressive again" — i.e., the cycle is turning.
- **Genuine (not clearly temporary):** margin squeeze — Q4 FY26 (ended Jul 31, 2026): revenue +2.4% to $1.15B, but net income −17.4% to $327M, EPS $0.35 vs ~$0.38 est; **operating expense per car +12.7% y/y** against flat revenue per car — negative operating leverage, the opposite of the old compounding story. Full-year revenue +0.4% to $4.67B. Domestic insurance volumes fell 7.5% in Q4 **after a lost customer** — real share/allocation loss to IAA/RB Global at the margin (Simply Wall St, Oct 1, 2026).
- **Secular overhang:** autonomous driving → gradually fewer accidents (Alluvium Global Fund Q2 2026 letter: expects accident rates to "gradually decline" with AV adoption). Distant but real.
- **The ACV deal (Sep 10, 2026):** $1.9B all-cash acquisition of ACV Auctions at $10.50/share (~45% premium) — ~27.5x ACV's 2026 guided adjusted EBITDA (vs Copart's own ~12x), for a GAAP-loss-making business; EPS-neutral year one, accretive only from FY28. Market read it as "buying the growth the core isn't delivering" (ainvest, Sep 2026).
- **Verdict: MIXED, leaning temporary.** The insurance cycle is the dominant driver and it is mean-reverting; total-loss frequency keeps ratcheting up (23.3–24.2%, multi-decade highs on expensive repairs). But cost-per-car inflation and the lost customer are execution/share issues that must prove out.

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 8/10
Wide-moat duopoly: Copart ~50% of US salvage, IAA/RB Global ~35%, top two absorb ~85% of insured salvage. The moat is physical and unreplicable — entitled land, 250+ locations, owned tow fleet, multi-state title infrastructure, 750k–1M global buyer base, VB3 platform ("nobody else can come in and open up 180 locations" — former IAA GM). AI/substitute threat is minimal near-term; the long-run substitute is autonomous driving reducing accident frequency — decades, not quarters.

### 3. Rebound catalyst
- **Insurance cycle normalization** — premium-hike digestion ending; insurers getting aggressive again → assignment volumes recover (H2 FY27).
- **ACV deal close (expected end of calendar 2026)** — removes the overhang; dealer-to-dealer channel (~20M vehicles/year addressable) adds a growth leg; synergies via Copart yards as ACV staging areas.
- **Total-loss frequency tailwind persists** (23%+ and rising on repair-cost inflation) — the structural conversion multiplier keeps working.
- **Q1 FY27 print (late Nov 2026 est.)** — operating expense per car must inflect down to re-rate.

### 4. Balance-sheet survival — fortress, no distress
~$4.5B cash, ~$5.7B total liquidity ($2.05B revolver), **no debt outstanding** (Q4 FY26 call summary, Sep 2026); ~$1.3B trailing FCF; FY26 operating cash flow $1.60B; repurchased $1.6B of stock in FY26. The $1.9B ACV deal is funded entirely from cash on hand with no financing condition — management expects "significant flexibility" to remain afterward. Zero dilution/distress risk.

### 5. Valuation / margin of safety
- Fwd PE ~15.8 (screen); ~17–18x trailing at ~$31 in Sep 2026 (ainvest) — vs its own historical ~30x+ compounder multiple. Stockmarketwatch (Oct 2, 2026): "Attractively Valued" vs peers; 52w range $26.74–45.89.
- Analyst mean target: **not found** in sources checked (no current consensus target located; institutional tone is mixed — Alluvium initiated a position in Q2 2026 calling it a "top notch business operating in a duopoly").

### 6. Category position
Still the #1 salvage auctioneer with pricing power (ASP holding up even as units fell). But the dip partly reflects *real* share pressure — the lost customer and −7.5% domestic insurance units show IAA/RB Global competing harder at the margin.

### 7. Value-trap risk (honest bear)
Operating expense per car keeps rising while insurance volumes structurally shrink (not just cycle — AV-driven accident decline arrives faster); more carrier-allocation losses to IAA; the ACV acquisition proves to be a 27x-EBITDA distraction that consumes management attention and cash; the multiple never re-rates above 18x.

### 8. Rebound score: 6.5/10
**One-sentence verdict:** A wide-moat duopoly with a fortress balance sheet suffering a cyclical insurance air pocket plus a fixable cost-per-car problem — the cycle will turn, but the lost customer and margin squeeze keep this a "prove it" story, not a slam dunk.

## Earnings-quality flags

- **CPRT — RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN:** At FY26 year-end (Jul 31, 2026), accounts receivable grew 6.1% and inventories +29.6% y/y against full-year revenue growth of just 0.4% (signalbloom, Sep 2026) — the screen's flag. **Verdict: mostly benign business-model mechanics, watch not alarm.** Copart is a consignment auctioneer — it has no product channel to stuff; receivables are insurer/buyer settlement balances, and the FY26 cash-flow statement shows the AR build ($80.6M use of cash) alongside $1.60B of operating cash flow, i.e., earnings are *not* running ahead of cash. The inventory build reflects vehicle-pooling balances in a soft-demand environment rather than obsolescence. The real earnings-quality issue at CPRT is the margin/cost-per-car squeeze, not the receivables.
