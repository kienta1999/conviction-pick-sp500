## 1. ISRG — Intuitive Surgical (~$406; 52w $328.57–$603.88; YTD −28.5%, per Finnhub/OrtEx data early Oct 2026)

## 2. EFX — Equifax (~$140; 52w $137.07–$244.37; −42% from high; YTD −35.5% per Zacks, Oct 2026)

### Why it's down
A rate-cycle slump compounded by an active **regulatory attack on bureau economics**:
- **FHFA campaign vs. bureau fees**: Director Bill Pulte announced a unified Fannie/Freddie pricing grid formally incorporating **VantageScore alongside FICO** (ending FICO's mortgage monopoly) and said FHFA is studying a move from tri-merge to **bi-merge/single-bureau** reports to cut closing costs (Bloomberg Law/Reuters via financialcontent, Sep 29 2026). Stock fell 4.1% premarket that day to a 52-week low.
- **Price war**: TransUnion announced it will hold standalone VantageScore 4.0 pricing at **99 cents through Dec 2028** — EFX co-owns VantageScore, so "any volume gains in VantageScore yield negligible fee income" (financialcontent, Sep 29 2026).
- **Higher-for-longer rates**: 30-yr Treasury at its highest since 2004 (Insider Monkey, Sep 25 2026); the Fed hiked to 3.75–4.0% with more expected; MBA and Fannie Mae **cut 2027 origination forecasts** (MBA $2.101T; Fannie $2.279T, HousingWire Sep 2026). Mortgage is EFX's highest-growth engine.
- Irony: the business itself is fine — Q2 2026 revenue +11% to $1.70B, adj. EPS +13% to $2.25 (top of guide), **US mortgage revenue +40%** — but H1 benefited from pull-forward before rates rose, forcing a mechanically slower H2 (ainvest, Sep 2026).
- Sentiment: short interest more than doubled (3.6M in Jan → 7.75M in Sep 2026); 14 insider sales, 0 buys in 6 months, incl. CEO Begor selling 37,791 shares Jul 2026 (MarketBeat); BMO cut target to $160, Market Perform (Oct 3 2026).
- **Verdict: TEMPORARY with a structural overhang.** The rate cycle is cyclical; the FHFA tri-merge/bi-merge agenda is a genuine semi-permanent fee risk, but it is regulatory jawboning so far, not enacted economics. Moat not lost.

### Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 8/10
One of three US credit bureaus — a regulated data oligopoly with Experian and TransUnion; #1 in income/employment verification via Workforce Solutions; **co-owner of VantageScore**, which ironically benefits from the FICO-monopoly ruling. GenAI cannot replace the underlying credit file or the regulatory incumbency; it can only change the interface. Fee compression is the threat, not disintermediation.

### Rebound catalyst
Path: (1) eventual mortgage-volume normalization — Q2 proved operating leverage (mortgage rev +40% on a rate dip); (2) clarity/resolution on the FHFA agenda — any walk-back or benign final rule re-rates the multiple; (3) **Q3 2026 earnings ~Oct 20, 2026** (Zacks). Timing is rate-dependent: Fannie projects ~6.7–6.8% 30-yr rates through 2027, so the rebound is likely a 2027-H2/2028 story unless the Fed pivots sooner (Morningstar expects cuts in H2 2027). Workforce Solutions (less rate-sensitive) keeps growing meanwhile.

### Balance-sheet survival
Debt >$5B (TipRanks), D/E 0.92, current ratio 0.60 — leveraged but **strong, stable free cash flow with cash conversion >100%** (TipRanks); dividend $2.24/yr (1.6% yield), payout ratio 0.27. No distress; FCF funds tech, debt service, and buybacks. Leverage is the main constraint if growth stalls.

### Margin of safety / valuation
Forward P/E **~16.7** (MarketBeat, Oct 6 2026; screen ~14.1); PEG 1.36; price/FCF ~10x. That is a multi-year-low multiple for a bureau triopoly. Analyst consensus: **Moderate Buy** (21 analysts: 15 Buy, 6 Hold), mean target **$204.52–206.90** (Zacks; MarketBeat Oct 6 2026) → **~+45% upside** from ~$140; range $148–266. UBS Buy $220 (Jul 8); Citi Buy $220; JPM $210; MS Overweight $225 (all Jul 22); BMO the outlier at $160 Market Perform (Oct 3).

### Category position
Still a big-three bureau with pricing power in verification; the dip reflects **regulatory/political risk and the rate cycle**, not share loss to TransUnion/Experian. If anything, the VantageScore win structurally favors its co-ownership.

### Value-trap risk
The honest bear case: if FHFA follows through on bi-merge/single-bureau, mortgage credit-reporting revenue structurally shrinks and the 99-cent VantageScore lock compresses scoring margins — a permanent impairment of the highest-margin product; rates staying higher-for-longer (Fed funds projected flat through 2027, MarketWatch Sep 2026) keeps origination volumes frozen; 30-yr at post-2004 highs. Short interest doubling means the market is actively betting the regulatory story wins. Fair value may simply be lower than the old multiple.

### Rebound score: **7/10**
**One-sentence verdict:** A triopoly data franchise at a multi-year-low ~15x forward multiple on a rate freeze plus FHFA fee-scrutiny — proven operating leverage (mortgage rev +40% in Q2) argues the dip is cyclical, but the bi-merge agenda is a real structural ceiling until resolved.
