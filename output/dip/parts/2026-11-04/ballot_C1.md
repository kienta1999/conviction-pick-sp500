## Single-pick ballot

- Top pick: LRCX
- Runner-up: KLAC
- Thesis (3-5 sentences): LRCX fell roughly 47% from its 52-week high and about a third in a month despite printing a record fiscal Q1 2027 — revenue $8.22bn, up 54% year over year, with operating margin above 40% for the first time. That combination, a record print meeting a halved multiple, is the classic moat-on-sale setup this lens exists to buy. The business sits at the hardest-to-replicate choke point in chipmaking, and every AI-driven layer, advanced-packaging step and HBM stack makes that choke point more valuable, not less. With net cash, record free cash flow and a supply-capped rather than demand-capped guide, the dip prices a demand collapse that the quarter itself refutes.
- Why the dip is temporary (not permanent): the single most compelling evidence the drop is about price, not the business: management said the December-quarter guide would have been higher absent component and clean-room supply constraints — supply, not demand, capped the outlook, on top of a record quarter with memory revenue up 120% year over year.
- Moat / AI-irreplaceability: low risk — Lam is the etch leader and a co-leader in deposition, with tools qualified fab-by-fab over years, embedded in customer process recipes, and serviced through a Customer Support Business Group that did $2.49bn in the quarter, up 40% year over year. AI does not substitute for etch or deposition; it increases their intensity through more layers, advanced packaging and HBM. The genuine threats are customer concentration and export controls, not a substitute technology.
- Rebound catalyst & timing: December-quarter delivery at or above the $8.60bn guide, reported late January 2027, with delayed shipments slipping to the March quarter rather than lost, plus a sharper calendar-2027 wafer-fab-equipment view on that call — management already gave a preliminary view above $200bn, led by DRAM, foundry logic and NAND, with about 10 new Tier-1 fabs in 2027 — and easing of the clean-room/component constraints.
- 12-18 month return scenario: rough base / bull case: base roughly +30–40% as the multiple partially re-rates on January delivery and the 2027 WFE outlook holding; bull roughly +55–60%, toward the dossier's analyst mean target of about $360 (about +56–58% from the verified $230.72 close), if supply eases and the above-$200bn WFE view is confirmed.
- Top risk (value-trap case): the WFE cycle has peaked rather than paused — if AI-capex financing tightens or the calendar-2027 above-$200bn view proves optimistic, orders roll over and a still-premium forward multiple compresses toward its historic median on falling earnings, the double-compression path. China export-policy risk is live alongside it, with the US foreign-affiliate rule suspension facing renewal 2026-11-10 and the US–China trade truce expiring 2027-01-10.
- Conviction (1-10): 8

### Top 3 — most compelling temporary-dip + intact-moat data point, and 12-18mo scenario

**LRCX:** The record quarter itself is the point: $8.22bn revenue, up 54% year over year, 52.4% gross margin and 40.2% operating margin, followed by a fall driven by a guide that was in line rather than above the $9bn-plus some wanted — and explicitly supply-capped. No share loss is evidenced; memory drove the record. Rough 12–18mo: base +30–40%, bull +55–60% toward the about-$360 analyst mean.

**KLAC:** The twin to the top pick: record fiscal Q1 2027 revenue of $4.14bn, up 28.9% year over year, a record 46.1% non-GAAP operating margin and 62.7% gross margin, a $12.57bn backlog up 60% year over year, and a December-quarter guide at or above expectations — yet the stock fell on sector rotation, with target trims reflecting tempered multiples, not deteriorating fundamentals. Rough 12–18mo: base +25–35%, bull +40% or more toward the about-$220 analyst mean, held back only by its remaining premium to its fellow equipment names.

**META:** Core demand did not break while the price did: Q3 2026 revenue up 26% year over year and ad revenue up 26%, with the dossier noting Meta gaining share from Google, across a 3.60bn daily-active-person family — yet the stock fell about 20% from its October high on a raised $135–145bn capex range, a first quarterly cash burn since 2012, and a new Class C share class read as dilution. No substitute replicates the social graph, and Meta applies AI with distribution to billions rather than being displaced by it. Rough 12–18mo: base +20–30% on a slow sentiment reset, bull +34% or more toward the about-$833 analyst mean, but only if a quarter of FCF recovery or capex discipline arrives — this is quarters, not weeks.
