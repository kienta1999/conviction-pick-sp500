## SCHW — Charles Schwab Corporation

### 1. Why it's down — temporary vs permanent

The drawdown is, on the evidence, a high-bar / guidance disappointment layered on a round-trip from an August peak — not a franchise break.

- SCHW hit a 52-week high of $114.53 on 24 Aug 2026, then gave it back through September–October; it fell from ~$105.25 to ~$90.85 over the 30 days to ~20 Oct 2026 (−13.7%), per Tickeron (crawled ~20 Oct 2026).
- Q3 2026, reported 15 Oct 2026 before the open, was a record print that still disappointed: net revenue $7.17bn (+17% y/y), GAAP EPS $1.62 (+29%), adjusted EPS $1.69 (+29%, vs ~$1.66 consensus), but revenue a touch below the ~$7.18–7.22bn consensus range. Shares fell ~5% on 15 Oct and >7% over the next three sessions (Trefis, updated 27 Oct 2026; Zacks, 15 Oct 2026).
- The details the market punished: net interest revenue $3.48bn (+14% y/y — its slowest pace in at least five quarters, per Trefis), net interest margin 3.04% vs ~3.09% expected, and bank deposits $244.1bn vs ~$250.7bn expected (Tickeron recap, Oct 2026). CFO Mike Verdeschi guided full-year 2026 revenue growth and NIM expansion toward the lower end of the prior ranges (17.5–18.5% revenue growth; 3.00–3.10% NIM), per Trefis.
- Offsetting strength in the same print: record core net new assets $190.4bn (+38% y/y, 5.8% annualised organic growth), total client assets $13.33tn (+15%), 1.2m new brokerage accounts, asset-management fees $1.91bn (+14%) and trading revenue $1.16bn (+16%) (Schwab Q3 release, 15 Oct 2026, via Zacks/securities.io summaries).
- A further Q3-call wrinkle, reported by Biggo's call summary (15 Oct 2026): Schwab deliberately throttled some long/short growth in mid-September to preserve operational capacity and is building a proprietary long/short solution — demand-constrained, not demand-absent. Management still guided Q4 NIM +25–30bp y/y and ~800bp of full-year positive operating leverage, double initial expectations.

**Verdict: TEMPORARY (sentiment + guidance reset + rate/NIM sensitivity).** Earnings, flows and margins are all still growing y/y; the stock de-rated because the bar was set at the August high, not because flows reversed.

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — score 8/10

Moat: scale + switching costs + regulated custody/banking + RIA ecosystem. Schwab is the #2 US brokerage by client assets ($13.08tn at 30 Jun 2026, per Motley Fool's 2026 ranking from company disclosures; $13.33tn at 30 Sep 2026 per its Q3 release) and #1 by client accounts (48.0m at 30 Jun 2026; 48.6m at 30 Sep 2026). Moving a brokerage, retirement, banking, lending and advisor-custody relationship is high-friction, and the RIA custody business embeds Schwab in thousands of advisors' workflows. Capital intensity and bank regulation are barriers, not just brand.

AI threat: real but partial, not existential. AI can commoditise stock-picking, research summaries and basic advice, and could pressure advisory fees over time; it cannot route around custody, clearing, FDIC-insured sweep, margin lending or regulatory reporting, and Schwab itself can deploy the same tools. The durable risk is fee compression on advice/asset management, not disintermediation of the account itself.

**Irreplaceability: 8/10.**

### 4. Balance-sheet survival

Strong, with one watch item. Preliminary consolidated Tier 1 leverage 8.9% at 30 Sep 2026 (adjusted for AOCI: 7.0%), at the top end of Schwab's 6.75–7.0% adjusted target range (Q3 release; Q3 call). Transactional sweep cash $492.8bn at end-September (+$7.1bn q/q). Return on tangible common equity 44% annualised in Q3 (vs 38% a year earlier). GAAP pre-tax margin 52.7%, adjusted 54.8%.

Watch item, not distress: client leverage is surging — margin loan balances $187.9bn at quarter-end (+14% q/q, including $60.7bn long/short-related), net receivables from brokerage clients $128.1bn (+37% y/y), Pledged Asset Line balances $37.3bn (+59% y/y), while bank deposits grew only 2% y/y to $244.1bn (Q3 release via Block2Learn/TradingPedia, 15 Oct 2026). That leverage is collateralised client borrowing, not Schwab corporate debt, but it is the transmission channel if markets fall sharply: forced deleveraging would hit trading revenue and NIM at once. No dilution risk is visible at current capital levels.

### 6. Category position

Still a top-tier player with pricing power in the ways that matter, but not #1 on every metric. #1 by client accounts, #2 by client assets behind Fidelity ($18.0tn administered at 31 Dec 2025, per Motley Fool). AAII's 2026 member survey: 39% use Schwab as primary broker vs Fidelity 32%, Vanguard 13% — Schwab leads that self-selected sample. The dip is not a share-loss signal: record net new assets and 5.8% annualised organic growth in Q3 argue share is still being gained; the share-loss risk to watch is at the margin — younger/active traders drifting to Robinhood/Interactive Brokers, and fee pressure from Fidelity/Vanguard on advice. ### 7. Value-trap risk — honest bear case

1. NIM/deposit disappointment repeats: if deposits keep drifting down despite asset growth (cash sorting into money funds / higher-yield alternatives), the lower-end guidance becomes the new run-rate and the forward EPS step-up behind the "cheap" multiple never arrives.
2. Margin-loan unwind: $187.9bn of margin balances is a pro-cyclical earnings kicker; a market drawdown forces deleveraging, hits trading and interest revenue simultaneously, and turns the leverage that flattered Q3 into a headwind.
3. Capital constraint: adjusted Tier 1 leverage at 7.0% is at the top of target, but the unadjusted-vs-adjusted gap (8.9% vs 7.0%, a ~$10.4bn AOCI drag) shows how much rate-driven unrealised loss sits in the bond book; a sharp rate rise reopens the 2023-style capital debate and caps buybacks.
4. The stock has already round-tripped a 52-week high and broken below both key moving averages — momentum funds are sellers until $97–100 is reclaimed, so "cheap" can stay cheap through Q4.

### 8. Rebound score and verdict

**Rebound score: 7/10.**
**Verdict: A record-print stock punished for guidance — the dip looks like a genuine buying opportunity if Q4 NIM and deposits confirm, with margin-loan leverage as the risk to size around, not a reason to avoid.**

It is not, on current evidence, the value-trap signature of earnings running ahead of cash.

## HSY — The Hershey Company

### 1. Why it's down — temporary vs permanent

A commodity-cost shock plus a pricing-volume trade-off, now two years into working through — cyclical with a structural overlay, not yet a broken brand.

- The drawdown's origin: cocoa futures peaked at ~$12,906–12,931/tonne in Dec 2024 (Trading Economics via BusinessModelAnalyst, Oct 2026; Noozify, 28 Oct 2026), >2.5x the prior record from 1977. Hershey's gross margin collapsed from 47.3% in 2024 to 33.5% in 2025 (−~1,380bp); 2025 cost of sales rose 31.7% to $7,769.9m including $736.6m higher commodity costs, $287.2m higher supply-chain costs including tariffs, and $491.0m unfavourable commodity-derivative mark-to-market; net income fell from $2,221m (2024) to $883m (2025) (Drillr summary of filings, Oct 2026). Quarterly net income fell from $797m in Q4 2024 to $224m in Q1 2025 and $63m in Q2 2025 (MarketBeat, Jul 2026).
- The price response that created the second leg: Hershey announced a double-digit price increase in Jul 2025, rolled out from mid-Sep 2025. That restored margin but crushed volume — Q2 2026: ~12 points of price vs ~8 points volume decline; Q3 2026: ~11 points price vs ~10 points volume decline (Zacks, 29 Oct 2026).
- The stock path: 52-week high $239.48 (Finnhub) / $236.28 in Feb 2026 (StockStory, 30 Oct 2026); −31.1% off high per screen; −9% YTD and −2.37% over 1 year at 30 Oct 2026 (Finnhub); it lagged the S&P 500 (+19.2%) and staples ETF XLP (+8.1%) over the prior 52 weeks (Barchart via Farmerseande summary, pre-Q3).
- The fresh overhang into Q3: tariffs (a $57m tariff-refund benefit flattered Q3 — see §3), cautious consumers, higher gas prices/inflation/rates, and convenience-store weakness — the US candy/gum/mint category in that channel fell nearly 5% in September 2026 (StockStory, 30 Oct 2026); NielsenIQ Halloween candy sales fell 6.7% in the month to 17 Oct 2026 (Dispatch Times, 30 Oct 2026); Reuters (30 Oct 2026) reported Halloween chocolate sales down $33.1m in the nine weeks to 17 Oct, the biggest drop in at least three years, led by milk chocolate.

**Verdict: PREDOMINANTLY TEMPORARY (cocoa cycle + tariff noise + price-elasticity hangover), with a STRUCTURAL WATCH ITEM (health/GLP-1-driven smaller packs and lower-sugar preferences — see §7).** The cost shock is demonstrably reversing; the volume question is not yet answered.

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — score 7/10

Moat: brand + distribution + scale in US chocolate. Hershey holds ~45% of the US chocolate market (Canvas Business Model, citing 2025 data) — Reese's, Hershey's, Kisses are the mass-retail default — with North America driving >85% of revenue and ~$11–11.5bn net sales. Shelf space in grocery/convenience, seasonal (Halloween/Valentine's) execution, and a 132-year brand are not replicable by a startup, and AI does not eat chocolate: no substitute technology replaces the product itself.

Limits: the moat is North-American, not global (international share ~3% vs Nestlé ~11% in one BCG-style analysis; global chocolate share only ~6–9% vs Mars ~12–15% and Mondelēz ~10–13% per MRFR, Oct 2026). Salty snacks (SkinnyPop, Dot's, Pirate's Booty, LesserEvil) diversify but put Hershey up against Frito-Lay (~60% US savoury share) where it has no moat. Private-label and better-for-you insurgents can nibble at the edges, especially as pack sizes shrink.

**Irreplaceability: 7/10 — AI-proof as a product, but not immune to taste/health substitution.**

### 3. Rebound catalyst — concrete path and timing

The recovery is already printing — the question is whether it is durable or hedge-timing flattery:

- Q3 2026 (reported 29 Oct 2026, quarter ended 27 Sep): net sales $3,275.5m (+3.0% y/y, vs $3,254m consensus), adjusted EPS $2.64 (+103.1% y/y, vs $2.12 Zacks consensus; GAAP EPS $2.88, net income $580.8m, +110%). Adjusted gross margin +1,090bp to 42.7%; adjusted operating profit +78.5% to $754m (23.0% margin) (Zacks, 29 Oct 2026; Baking Business, 30 Oct 2026). Shares rose ~2.8% on the day to ~$164.67 (StockStory, 30 Oct 2026).
- But management was explicit that Q3 ran ahead of expectations: cocoa-hedge gains expected in Q4 arrived in Q3, plus ~$57m tariff refunds ($0.22 of the $2.64 EPS, per CEO Kirk Tanner via Baking Business). Full-year 2026 guidance was raised to adjusted EPS $8.58–8.74 (+36–38.5% growth) *including* tariff refunds received so far — excluding refunds, the outlook is unchanged (StockStory/Zacks, 29–30 Oct 2026). Organic sales guidance was *narrowed down* to ~3% (from 3–3.5%), reported sales ~5%.
- The structural leg: cocoa has fallen ~57% from peak to ~$5,613/tonne on 8 Oct 2026 (Trading Economics), after touching ~$2,846–3,241 in Mar 2026; Hershey hedges 3–24 months forward, so booked costs lag spot by a year+ (BusinessModelAnalyst; Noozify). CFO Dave Hulays/Steve Voskuil commentary (via Noozify, Jul-call reporting): the company's highest cocoa cost was Q3 2025, and it has "good visibility into cocoa deflation next year, even if futures remain around current levels." That makes 2027 — management's own frame is 2027 sales growth 2–4% (CEO on Q3 call, via StockStory) and a low-double-digit earnings recovery in 2027 excluding the $66m / $0.25 tariff-refund benefit (CFO on Q3 call, via Defense World, 31 Oct 2026) — the year the margin recovery should become self-sustaining rather than hedge-timing-driven.
- Other levers: $100m savings from the Advancing Agility & Automation programme in 2026 (Zacks), innovation (Hershey Crème Filled Bars, Reese's Oreo called out on Q3 call), and salty-snacks growth (segment +22.3% in Q3 including ~20 points from LesserEvil; organic +2.3% with volume +~1% — the one segment growing volume, per Q3 call transcript via Roic AI).

Rough timing: Q4 2026 (Feb 2027 print) tests whether the pulled-forward hedge gains leave a hole; 2027 is when cocoa deflation should flow through the P&L cleanly. Volume stabilisation — unit declines already moderating across nearly half of chocolate brands vs H1, including Hershey's, Kit Kat, Cadbury and Kisses (Q3 call via Defense World) — is the monthly tell.

### 6. Category position

Still the US chocolate leader by a wide margin, but the dip coincides with real share pressure at the margin, not just costs.

- ~45% US chocolate share (2025, Canvas Business Model) — the #1 US player, ahead of Mars Wrigley in the US even though Mars leads globally. North America Confectionery is the profit engine; International fell ~8% in Q3 (organic −~10%: +4% price, −14% volume/mix, per Q3 call) — that segment is losing volume, not just cycling price.
- Pricing power is proven (double-digit price taken with volume elasticity, not outright rejection — US non-seasonal confectionery retail sales still rose 2% in Q3, with 2-year growth accelerating to ~4% from 2.5% in H1, per CEO on Q3 call). But power has limits: the category is shrinking in units — Halloween chocolate sales down $33.1m y/y (NIQ via Reuters, 30 Oct 2026), small packs +24% in dollars while every larger size declines (NIQ via Reuters), and Mondelēz chose volume over price in Q3 (flat North America volumes, +1.6% price — its first volume-led quarter since late 2021, per Reuters) while Hershey chose price. If Hershey keeps choosing price, share drifts to Mondelēz/private label; if it pivots to volume, margin recovery slows. That tension is the investment debate.

### 7. Value-trap risk — honest bear case

1. Volume never comes back: Q3's −10% volume (and International's −14%) may not be pure elasticity that fades as prices lap — if the Jul 2025 price rise permanently shrank the category (smaller packs, fewer occasions), 2027's "low-double-digit earnings recovery" arrives on price/mix alone and eventually exhausts.
2. Health/GLP-1 structural drift: Reuters (30 Oct 2026) documents healthier choices reshaping the aisle — GLP-1 use plus the "Make America Healthy Again" campaign, growth in no/low/less-sugar claims, rice/popcorn cakes and popcorn, small packs +24% while larger sizes fall. Hershey's own response (salty snacks, portion-controlled packs, protein-powder exploration reported 30 Oct 2026) concedes the direction. This is a slow bleed, not a cliff — which is exactly what a value trap looks like.
3. Cocoa re-spikes or tariffs return: cocoa already bounced from ~$2,846–3,241 (Mar 2026) to ~$5,550–5,613 (Oct 2026) on African fertiliser disruption and El Niño warnings (Dispatch Times/Noozify, Oct 2026); Hershey's CFO said "we don't expect cocoa to remain at current levels long term" (i.e. it could rise again). Tariff refunds ($66m in 2026) are one-off by definition — 2027 guidance explicitly excludes them.
4. The Q3 beat was flattered: $0.22 of tariff refunds + hedge timing pulled from Q4 means Q4 (Feb 2027 print) could disappoint sequentially and the "raised guidance" (ex-refunds unchanged) is less bullish than the headline +36–38.5% suggests. Analysts noticed: JPMorgan's $166 target implies ~zero upside.
5. Halloween read-through: if the "critical last few days" before Halloween (CEO's phrase on the Q3 call) disappointed, Q4 seasonal shipments — the year's biggest — could soften further, and NielsenIQ's −6.7% October print points that way.

### 8. Rebound score and verdict

**Rebound score: 6/10.**
**Verdict: The cocoa-cost cycle is genuinely turning and Q3 proves margin can snap back violently — but volume is still falling double-digits, the latest analyst cuts price in ~zero upside, and 2027 has to prove the recovery is volume-inclusive, not just price-plus-hedges.**
