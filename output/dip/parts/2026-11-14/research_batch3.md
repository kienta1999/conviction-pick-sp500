## HWM — Howmet Aerospace ($220.82, −24.5% off 52w high)

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 9/10
- Moat: single-crystal investment casting of hot-section turbine blades/vanes — one of only four qualified foundries globally (Howmet, Berkshire's Precision Castparts, Consolidated Precision Products, Doncasters); Howmet holds >50% of industrial gas-turbine blades and a technology edge in proprietary single-crystal casting (AInvest, Sep 2026). A blade set costs >$600,000 and takes 60–90 weeks to produce; qualification takes years. Long-term supply agreements with GE Vernova and Siemens Energy run through 2030. Commercial aerospace is >53% of revenue (Q2 2026 commercial aero +28% YoY).
- AI angle cuts **for** Howmet: AI data-centre power demand is driving the gas-turbine shortage that gives Howmet scarcity pricing; SpaceX's move validates the bottleneck rather than removing it. The exposed IGT slice is only ~10–15% of total revenue; 85% (commercial/defence aerospace castings) is untouched by the SpaceX announcement (AInvest). Jefferies industry checks put a realistic SpaceX timeline at ~4 years (12 months negotiation + 24 months industrialisation + 24 months ramp/yield).
- Counter-signal: GE Aerospace agreed 8 Sep 2026 to buy castings maker CPP for $11.75B (~18x 2027E EBITDA with synergies, ~26x without — in Howmet's own multiple range, per Barron's via Turbomachinery Mag). - **Irreplaceability: 9/10.** Certification lock-in + process tech + a 4-player global oligopoly. The threat is real but multi-year and partial.

### 8. Rebound score: 6/10
**Verdict:** A real beat-and-raise with a buyback bid under it, but the rebound is a Coach-momentum bet at a still-above-history multiple, with a loss-making second brand and tariff exposure — the weakest risk/reward of the three.
