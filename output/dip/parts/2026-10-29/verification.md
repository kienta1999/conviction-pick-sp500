## AMAT — Applied Materials (tentative single pick, Borda #1)

### AMAT-1. Fiscal Q3 print: revenue $9.12B, +25% YoY, EPS +41% (mid-August 2026, record quarter)
**CONFIRMED.**
Applied Materials' own Q3 2026 release (Aug 13, 2026, quarter ended Jul 26, 2026) states:

Source: Applied Materials Q3 2026 earnings release, Aug 13, 2026 (via Quartr filing copy).

### AMAT-2. Why-it's-down cause: Oct 2, 2026 export-control filing implying ~$600M FY26 revenue hit (~2% of revenue), management insisting China softness is not share loss
**CONFIRMED.**
Multiple Oct 2026-dated reports of the filing agree: the expanded BIS "Affiliates Rule"
(effective Sep 29, covering entities ≥50%-owned by Entity List firms) would cut Q4 revenue by
~$110M and fiscal 2026 revenue by ~$600M — Data Center Dynamics (Oct 2026): "expanded curbs
on exports to China would cost the company $600 million in lost revenue during FY2026";
AInvest: the impact is "around 2% of total revenue." On the share-loss point, Reuters quotes
Jefferies directly: "The big debate remains if the departure from peers is a sign of share
loss in China, but AMAT remains adamant that it is not," and CEO Gary Dickerson is quoted
saying foreign rivals were still selling to Chinese companies Applied cannot serve.
Caveat for the panel (not a contradiction of the claim as of Oct 29): the same Reuters item
also discusses a later suspension of the Affiliates Rule and Q4 results, i.e. developments
that post-date this dossier; the panel should re-check the rule's status before the Nov 12
print rather than assuming the $600M hit is still fully live. Separately, a genuine
counterweight exists on overall share: The Information Network data (via The Inference, Oct
2026) puts Applied's WFE share at 20.0% in 2024 falling to 17.9% in 2025, with H1 2026 equipment
revenue +1% vs Lam +23.3%, KLA +12.6%, ASML +13.3% — so "not share loss in China" (the
management/Jefferies position on the China debate) must not be read as "no share loss
anywhere."
Sources: Data Center Dynamics, Oct 2026 (workforce/export-curb report); AInvest export-
restriction coverage; Reuters, "Applied Materials' shares drop as stringent US export curbs
weigh on China business" (Jefferies quote).

### AMAT-4. Key valuation claim: forward P/E ~21.4, a discount to its own multi-year history
**CONTRADICTED as a combined statement — material variance, see below.**
The 21.4 figure itself is reproduced: TIKR (Oct 2026) writes Applied "now trades at
approximately 21.4x forward earnings." But on TIKR's own like-for-like forward history that
21.4x sits against "a five-year average of 21x" — i.e. essentially AT its history, not at a
discount ("valuation has returned close to historical levels"). The discount readings come
only from mixed-basis comparisons: FinanceCharts (Oct 26–28) forward P/E 23.42 vs a 3y
average 30.80 and 5y average 28.20, and Tradestie (Oct 28) forward 22.5 vs historical average
28.0 — those averages are trailing-P/E averages, not forward-P/E averages. On a trailing
basis the discount reverses outright: FinanceCharts trailing P/E 35.89 vs 3y 30.80 / 5y 28.20,
and FullRatio (Oct 28) trailing 35.65, "79% above its 10-year historical average." So the
dossier sentence cannot be carried forward as written: either cite forward ~23.4 vs trailing
history ~28.2 (mixed basis, labelled as such), or cite forward ~21.4 vs forward history
~21 (no discount). The dossier's own §5 was more careful than the brief's shorthand
("genuinely cheaper than its own recent history on forward earnings ... but NOT cheap in
absolute terms"); the brief's shorthand drops that qualification.
Sources: TIKR, Oct 2026 ("Applied Materials Sinks 17% in a Week..." and "Now in Full
Force..."); FinanceCharts AMAT P/E averages, Oct 2026; Tradestie AMAT, Oct 28, 2026;
FullRatio AMAT P/E, Oct 28, 2026.

### AMAT-5. Moat's central factual claim: deposition/etch tools are physically qualified into customer fabs, so AI demand pulls (not displaces) Applied's equipment
**CONFIRMED (qualitative claim, with a scope caveat).**
The qualification mechanism is industry-standard and documented: Kristal's Lam analysis
notes a vendor "holds valuable positions in qualified etching and deposition processes"
and that "once [a vendor] wins a process position, customers face meaningful switching
costs." Applied's own Q3 release ties that installed demand to AI: "As the rapid global
adoption of AI drives unprecedented demand for our materials engineering solutions," with
DRAM and advanced-packaging growth cited as AI-driven. The pull-vs-displace direction is
therefore supported. Caveat: the same Kristal analysis warns "the next technology
generation can reopen competition for the next position" — qualification protects an
existing process position, it does not permanently lock the next node. And per AMAT-2's
counterweight, AI pull has not prevented WFE-share erosion (20.0% → 17.9%, 2024 → 2025).
Sources: Applied Materials Q3 2026 release, Aug 13, 2026; Kristal/Lam 1QFY27 analysis
(qualified etch/deposition positions and switching costs).

## CPRT — Copart (Borda #3)

### CPRT-1. Why-it's-down cause: U.S. insurance-volume slump (~8% in FY2026) plus one lost major carrier account, layered over a cyclical coverage pullback — not a broken network
**CONFIRMED (factual components); the "cyclical" label is management framing, flagged below.**
Copart's Q4 FY2026 earnings-call transcript (Sep 10, 2026) states verbatim: "U.S. insurance
volumes decreased 7.5% in the quarter, and 8% for the fiscal year," and "excluding a single
customer loss, domestic insurance assignments would have increased 2.3%." Independent
coverage identifies the winner as IAA/RB Global (e.g. Tickeron: "the loss of a major
national insurance account to a competitor"; BizModelMastery: "a major insurer could leave
despite Copart's established network... IAA, owned by RB Global, already operates salvage
yards, bidder relationships and insurer processes"). "Not a broken network" is consistent
with the same print: international units grew ~11% and dealer units +5.8% in Q4, and U.S.
insurance ASP rose 3.7% in Q4 / 5.5% for the year — volume, not pricing or network function,
is the problem. Flag: the dossier's "cyclical, not secular" characterisation of the coverage
pullback is management's framing; Speedwell's Q4 memo notes this was "the 6th consecutive
quarter of shrinking volumes," so the panel should not present cyclicality as independently
proven — it is the thesis to be tested on Nov 19, not a verified fact.
Sources: Copart Q4 FY2026 earnings-call transcript (Motley Fool / MarketBeat, Sep 10–11,
2026); BigGo Finance Q4 call summary, Sep 10, 2026; Speedwell Copart 4Q26 memo; Tickeron
CPRT comparison.

### CPRT-2. ACV dealer-wholesale deal closed Oct 16, 2026
**CONFIRMED.**
Copart completed the ACV Auctions acquisition on October 16, 2026: cash tender offer at

Sources: Copart/ACV 8-K (Schedule TO exhibit), filed Oct 19, 2026, via secwatch.observer;
Markets Daily, Oct 16, 2026.

### CPRT-3. Balance-sheet claim: net cash position (screen net debt/EBITDA -2.36), ~$885M trailing free cash flow

panel must correct ~$885M → ~$1.27B before any ballot relies on it; the correction
strengthens, not weakens, the "fortress" balance-sheet point, but the number as written
is wrong.
Sources: Copart FY2026 10-K cash-flow statement (operating cash $1.604B, year ended
Jul 31, 2026); FinanceCharts CPRT free cash flow, Oct 2026; AlphaSpread CPRT FCF FY2026;
TipRanks CPRT financials (cash $4.49B, debt $88.37M).
