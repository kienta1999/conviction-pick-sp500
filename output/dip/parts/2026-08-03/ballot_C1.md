# BALLOT C1 — Agent C: Moat & AI-irreplaceability
Dip run 2026-08-03 · prices are the screen's 2026-08-03 close

**Lens preamble — how I graded the 9/10s.** The brief hands out five 9/10 irreplaceability
scores (BR, VRSK, ROL, VST, NEM). They are not the same asset class. I score a moat on three
tests: (1) can the asset be *rebuilt or permitted* by a competitor with capital? (2) does the
owner *set price*, or take it? (3) is AI the *customer* of the asset, the *substitute* for it,
or *neutral*?

- **VST** — cannot be rebuilt (NRC licences, interconnection queue), sets price into scarcity,
  and AI is the **customer**. Passes all three.
- **BR** — cannot be replicated (SEC mandate + NYSE-regulated fee schedule), price is set by
  regulation rather than by BR, AI is the *alleged* substitute and the evidence says it is not.
  Passes (1) and (3), (2) is neutral-by-regulation, which is fine — regulated pricing is a floor.
- **VRSK** — contributory data pool nobody can assemble, plus the killer detail that insurers
  **cannot file unexplainable black-box models with 50 state regulators**. Passes, but the
  business only grows 5–6%, so the moat protects a slow stream.
- **ROL** — genuinely 9/10 on the *delivery* side (route density, AI cannot kill a termite), but
  the July dip landed precisely on the **one layer that is AI-exposed**: residential lead
  generation through search. Organic cut to "at least 6%" while commercial (+7.2%) and termite
  (+8.9%) held. The dip located the erosion channel exactly. Still 9/10 on service, but the
  *demand-capture* layer is a 6, and 29.1× forward pays for a 9.
- **NEM** — irreplaceable orebodies, **zero pricing power**. This is an ownership moat, not an
  economic moat. It protects the *asset*, not the *return on the asset*. A price-taker with a
  fortress balance sheet is a good hold and a bad conviction pick, because nothing management
  does causes the rebound. Demoted accordingly.

**On ISRG — monopoly or merely a lead?** The decisive number is not the 58% share of US robotic
*systems*; it is the **86% share of accessories**. Accessory share sitting 28 points above system
share is the signature of switching-cost lock-in, not of a lead: hospitals that own competitor
iron still buy Intuitive's annuity. Add **procedures +17% against a 12% installed-base increase**
(utilization per system rising) and instrument pricing **up to ~$1,830 from ~$1,800** *in the
quarter Ottava cleared*. A monopoly losing its moat does not raise consumable price. OTTAVA's
De Novo (Jul 22 2026) covers ~10 procedures against Intuitive's full range and roughly two decades
of trained, credentialed surgeons. Verdict: still a **moat** (certification + installed base),
but the *lead* component has narrowed and I will not pay 31× for the moat plus the lead as if both
were intact.

**On the correlation warning.** BR, VRSK, ROL and ADBE are one bet. I hold **two** of them (BR at
#2, VRSK at #4), reject ADBE entirely, and demote ROL to #9 — and my top pick is drawn from the
genuinely uncorrelated set (VST, ISRG, DECK, NEM).

## BALLOT 1 — Single pick

- **Top pick:** VST
- **Runner-up:** BR

**Thesis (3-5 sentences):**
Vistra is the only name in this field where AI is the *customer* of the moat rather than the
threat to it — every other 9/10 here has to survive AI, VST gets paid by it. The moat is the
hardest kind on offer: **NRC nuclear licences and interconnection-queue position backed by $19.9B
of net PP&E**, assets that cannot be permitted, financed or rebuilt on any timeframe a competitor
cares about, and **PJM's capacity auction proved it empirically — only 525 MW of new supply showed
up at the $325/MW-day price cap.** When a market offers the maximum legal price and almost no new
supply appears, that is not an opinion about irreplaceability, it is a measurement of it. The
screen was wrong twice *against* VST and both corrections are favourable: true TTM FCF is
**+$1.80B, not −$0.16B** (the negative figure was nuclear-decommissioning-trust gross flows), and
"net debt $19.91B" was **gross** debt — true net debt $19.28B, **2.54–2.84× leverage, IG-rated,
~9× interest coverage, termed out and falling**. So the one thing that would disqualify it under a
durability lens — the balance sheet — is a screen artefact, and at **~15.1× forward, 9.5–10.6×
EV/EBITDA and a 7.5–9.0% FCF yield** you are paying a utility multiple for an unbuildable asset in
a structural shortage.

**Why the dip is temporary (not permanent):**
The single most compelling item is that **guidance excludes 5,500 MW of acquired (Cogentrix)
capacity and ~3,800 MW of signed hyperscaler PPAs.** The de-rate was a duration/sentiment reset on
the AI-power trade plus self-inflicted ~98% hedging that capped participation — a *hedging book*
problem, which rolls off, not an *asset* problem. Nothing in the drawdown touched megawatts,
licences, or contracted demand; the contracted demand actually grew while the multiple fell. The
market has already begun conceding this: **VST has rallied 9.2% since 2026-07-30 on ZERO change in
analyst targets ($222.83 → $221.94)** — pure multiple repair against a static fundamental estimate,
which is the exact signature of a sentiment dip closing.

**Moat / AI-irreplaceability — LOW risk:**
This is the lowest AI-disruption risk in the 14, and it is a different *category* of low from
ISRG's or IDXX's. Those two are AI-*resistant* (a model cannot draw blood or hold a scalpel).
VST is AI-*advantaged*: the scarcity that prices its output is created by the same capex wave that
threatens ADBE, NFLX and APP. There is no substitute technology in the window — you cannot software
your way to a licensed 24/7 nuclear megawatt inside PJM, and the 525 MW auction result says capital
cannot either. The one real substitution channel is **data centers self-supplying and bypassing
merchant generators**, and the same auction is the rebuttal: self-supply faces the identical
permitting, queue and supply-chain wall. Behind-the-meter gas at a hyperscaler is a 3–5 year
project competing against a plant that already exists.

**Rebound catalyst & timing:**
Three stacked, all near-dated. **Q2 print Aug 7 (4 days)** — the first opportunity to reset the
hedge disclosure and mark the PPA book. **PJM backstop capacity auction, September 2026** — a
direct read on scarcity pricing after the 525 MW result. **Cogentrix close in 2H26**, which
mechanically moves 5,500 MW from "excluded from guidance" into guidance. The re-rate window is
Aug 2026 → mid-2027.

**12-18 month return scenario:**
- **Base (+25–35%, ~$195–210):** ~17× FY27 as hedges roll to market and Cogentrix enters guidance.
- **Bull (+55–70%, ~$240–265):** September auction clears tight, the ~3,800 MW of signed PPAs plus
  incremental hyperscaler contracts get capitalised into estimates, and the multiple re-rates
  toward the analyst mean of **$221.94** and past it. Consensus already implies **+42.3%** with no
  contribution from the excluded capacity.

**Top risk (value-trap case):**
Regulatory clawback of scarcity rent. The moat is a *licence*, and licences are granted by bodies
that can also cap what the licence earns — PJM's $325 cap is itself an example, and political
pressure over data-center-driven consumer bills is real and rising. Coupled with 2.54–2.84×
leverage in a rate-sensitive, capital-intensive business, a scenario where FERC/PJM re-designs
capacity pricing while rates stay high is how this goes nowhere for two years. Secondary risk:
entry quality has already deteriorated 9.2% in four days — **thesis quality improved, entry quality
worsened**, and I am buying after the first leg.

**Conviction: 8/10.**

### Top 3 — the load-bearing data point and the scenario

**1. VST.** The single most compelling fact is **PJM's capacity auction drawing only 525 MW of new
supply at the $325 price cap** — a market offering the maximum legal price and receiving almost
nothing. That is a *measured* irreplaceability, not an asserted one, and it is why I rank a
9/10-with-pricing-power above four other 9/10s. Layer on the two screen corrections that both cut
in VST's favour (**true FCF +$1.80B not −$0.16B; true net debt $19.28B at 2.54–2.84×, IG-rated,
~9× coverage**) and the durability objection evaporates.
*Base +25–35% (~$195–210) at ~17× FY27 with Cogentrix in guidance; bull +55–70% (~$240–265) if the
September backstop auction clears tight and the ~3,800 MW of excluded PPAs get capitalised.*

**2. BR.** The moat test is whether a client can leave, and here they legally cannot: **SEC rules
require proxy distribution to beneficial owners at NYSE-regulated fees**, and in-sourcing invites
compliance review. The two bear narratives are not merely unproven, they are **affirmatively
contradicted** — the **SEC has confirmed tokenized securities carry identical governance
obligations**, most tokenized flow runs through broker-dealers that are already BR clients, and
**BR went live with on-chain governance for tokenized equities**, monetizing the disruption instead
of being bypassed. Meanwhile it was **RAISING guidance through the 40% decline** (Q3 FY26 recurring
revenue +6% cc, adjusted EPS +11% to $2.72, FY26 raised to ≥7% cc and 10–12% EPS growth) and now
trades at **15.1× forward against a 10-year average of 30.85 and a 10-year *trough* of 21.93** — it
is below its own decade-low multiple while compounding EPS at 10–12%; it was 23.2× on Jan 8 2026.
Not the top pick for one reason only: **earnings tomorrow pre-open with initial FY27 guidance is a
coin flip**, and the closed-sales cut ($290–340M → $240–290M, YTD $147M, −16% y/y) is the one line
where "timing" could become "demand." I would rather buy the mandate the day after the print than
the day before it — but the pipeline **>$1B, +20% y/y with deal origination +25%** says timing.
*Base +27–33% (~$200–210) at ~19×; bull +53–59% (~$240–250) toward the trough multiple of 21.9×
on 10–12% EPS growth, near Needham's cut-but-still-Buy $230.*

**3. ISRG.** The moat datum that survives OTTAVA: **instruments $1.73B +18% at ~$1,830 per
procedure, up from $1,800**, i.e. Intuitive *raised* consumable pricing in the same quarter J&J got
cleared, while **86% of accessories** against only 58% of systems shows the annuity is stickier
than the iron. **Procedures +17% on a +12% installed base** means utilization per system is rising,
and the **dV5 refresh is only ~15% complete (~1,700 of 11,710 systems)** — a multi-year capital
annuity barely started. The balance sheet is the field's best (**$8.63B cash/investments, no debt,
net cash $5.22B, cash rose $0.65B in the quarter after buybacks**): it can fund a five-year
competitive war from the bank account, which is exactly what you want when a competitor arrives.
Held to #3 because **31.1× forward is a moat price plus a monopoly price**, and I am only willing to
underwrite the moat.
*Base +18–22% (~$445–460) on 14–15% procedure growth and an unchanged multiple with the Oct 20 bar
pre-signaled and de-risked; bull +38–44% (~$520–540) if FY27 procedure guidance comes back with a
1-handle above 15% and the multiple re-rates toward 35×.*

### Deliberately left out of the top 10 — and why

**ADBE, APP, NFLX, GNRC — all four fail the irreplaceability test, and three of them are cheap for
that exact reason.** **ADBE is the field's defining value trap and my strongest reject**: 5/10 moat,
**9.1× forward is a forecast, not a discount** — Canva at ~$4B ARR growing 30–35% and Figma at
~$1.2B growing ~40% compound 3–4× faster in the adjacencies Adobe never won, the prior 17% price
increase **accelerated churn** so the pricing lever is now dangerous to pull, total ARR growth is
guided down to 10.2% **including ~$480M of acquired Semrush ARR** (organic meaningfully below 10%),
the first goodwill impairment has appeared, and **both CEO and CFO seats are vacant mid-pivot**. On
top of which the dip is already harvested — **+32% off the ~$190 June low, +22.1% in July alone.**
**APP:** the moat is an algorithm, not a licence, and the SEC data-collection probe targets the
*mechanism* that produces the 78–85% margin — an uncapped tail with two soft channel checks into a
binary Aug 5 print. **NFLX:** switching costs are "cancel anytime," growth has stepped down four
quarters running (+17.6% → +16.2% → +13.4% → +12% guided), YouTube takes three incremental daily
minutes to Netflix's one, and management moved engagement to **annual reporting from 2027** — a
moat you can no longer measure while H1'26 view hours grew ~2% is a moat I will not underwrite.
**GNRC (value trap):** no licence moat, the "beat" was **$71M of one-off tariff refund worth ~$0.90
of $2.40 EPS** with gross margin actually *falling* ex-refund, the data-center growth vector trades
**34.7% residential margin for 14.6% segment margin** against Caterpillar and Cummins, backlog
carries cancellation rights — and at **19% below its 50-day MA the knife is still falling.**
Also flagged inside my ten: **ROL at #9 is a great business at a disqualifying price** (29.1×
forward after a 41% fall, lowest upside in the field at +19.4%; a de-rate to 20× is another ~30%
down with nothing new going wrong), and **IDXX at #6 carries the field's worst risk/reward on
price** — 34.4× for 7.5% growth, into binary earnings tomorrow — held that high only because the
**~1,100bps of quantified outgrowth over a US clinical-visit decline of −1.5%** is the most
rigorously *measured* moat in the entire brief.
