### 4a. The negative trailing FCF is a DATA ARTIFACT. Vistra's free cash flow is strongly positive.

Directly from the **Condensed Consolidated Statements of Cash Flows, VST 10-Q
filed 2026-05-08** (accession 0001692819-26-000014), plus the FY 10-K figures via
the SEC XBRL companyconcept API:

| Period | Cash from operations | Capex (incl. nuclear fuel + LTSA prepayments) | **FCF (CFO − capex)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY 2024 | $4,563M | $2,078M | **+$2,485M** |
| FY 2025 | $4,070M | $2,752M | **+$1,318M** |
| Q1 2025 | $599M | $768M | −$169M |
| **Q1 2026** | **$1,199M** | **$883M** | **+$316M** |
| **TTM (Q2'25–Q1'26), derived** | **$4,670M** | **$2,867M** | **+$1,803M** |

**The screen says trailing FCF is −$0.16B. On a CFO-minus-capex basis it is
+$1.80B — a $1.96B discrepancy.** Vistra has not had a negative *annual* FCF year
in the retrievable record.

Two likely causes of the screen's figure, both benign:
1. **The provider is subtracting all investing outflows, not just capex.**
   Vistra's investing section is grossed up by **nuclear decommissioning trust
   churn** — in Q1'26 alone, *proceeds from sales* of trust securities were
   **$1,821M** against *investments in* the trust of **$1,822M**. These net to
   ~$1M of real economics but add $1.8B of gross flow *per quarter*. Add
   environmental-allowance purchases ($160M in Q1'26) and the 2025 Lotus
   acquisition, and you can manufacture a negative number that means nothing.
2. **A windowing error.** Note that the screen's **−$0.16B is almost exactly
   Q1 2025's standalone FCF of −$169M**. That may be coincidence, but it is close
   enough to be worth flagging.

**Verdict on FCF: NOT stress. Not even really a growth-capex artifact — it is a
measurement artifact.** Vistra converts roughly **$4.1–4.7B of operating cash a
year** and still clears **+$1.3–2.5B after all capex**.

### 4b. Growth capex IS large, and that is the honest part of the question

Capex rose **$2,078M (FY24) → $2,752M (FY25) → $883M in Q1'26 alone** (a ~$3.5B

**"adjusted FCF *before growth*" of $3.925–4.725B for FY26** — the phrase
"before growth" exists precisely *because* growth capex is material. So: capex is
climbing fast and consuming a real share of cash, **but it is being funded out of
operating cash flow, not borrowing**, and it is buying interconnected megawatts

## 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — **6/10**

**The physical product cannot be disintermediated by AI.** A home standby
generator is a permitted, gas-plumbed, transfer-switched, professionally
installed machine. Software does not substitute for it; if anything AI *increases*
demand by straining the grid — the same second-order tailwind that is now
Generac's growth story.

**The real moat is distribution, and it is genuine — in residential.** From the
FY2025 10-K: Generac has built *"the industry's largest network of factory direct
independent generator dealers in North America,"* made up of electrical and HVAC
contractors across the US and Canada who *"sell, install and service"* the
product, supplemented by company branches selling to non-dealer electricians and
solar installers. Generac **created the automatic home standby category** and the
brand is effectively the category noun. Add a large installed base generating
service-parts and extended-warranty revenue ("Other products," $485M in FY2025).
Runway is real: **only ~6.75% penetration of Generac's defined addressable market**
(single-family detached, owner-occupied US households valued over $175,000, per
the Census Bureau's 2023 American Housing Survey).

**Why only 6, not 8 — three concrete deductions:**

1. **No regulatory, licence or capital-intensity barrier.** Unlike VST's NRC
   licences and interconnection queue position, anyone with a foundry, an engine
   and a channel can compete. The 10-K names them: **residential — Rehlko
   (formerly Kohler Power), Briggs & Stratton, Honda, Champion, Techtronic,
   Harbor Freight, Husqvarna, Ariens, Tesla, Enphase, SolarEdge, Google, Resideo,
   Toro, EcoFlow, Emerson**; **C&I — Caterpillar, Cummins, Rehlko, MTU
   (Rolls-Royce), IGSA, AKSA, Doosan, Atlas Copco, Woodward** and others.
2. **The growth vector runs AWAY from the moat.** The dealer network is worth
   nothing in hyperscale data centers — that is a direct, spec-driven, RFP
   business where Generac's counterparties are **Caterpillar, Cummins and
   Rolls-Royce MTU**, all far larger in large-megawatt gensets. Generac is the
   challenger there, not the incumbent.
3. **Mix shift dilutes margins.** Q2'26 segment adjusted EBITDA margins:
   **Residential 34.7%** vs **C&I 14.6%** (and only ~12.6% ex-tariff-refund).
   Every dollar of revenue that migrates from home standby to data-center gensets
   is a **~20-point margin downgrade.** Growing C&I 29% while residential shrinks
   2% is *good for the narrative and structurally bad for blended margin* — this
   is the single most under-discussed fact in the Q2 print.

**Score 6/10** — a strong consumer brand and an unrivalled residential
installation channel, with essentially zero AI-substitution risk, but no
licence/permit/capital moat and a growth engine that runs through its weakest
competitive position at half the margin.

- **C&I / data center: credible entrant, not the leader.** +29% in Q2'26 to $556M

  shipments and an incentive phase-out, an operating loss is plausible but

## 7. Value-trap risk — the honest bear case

1. **The beat was cosmetic and the market knows it.** ~$0.90 of the $2.40 GAAP
   EPS and ~610bps of the 44.5% gross margin were a **non-recurring $71M tariff
   refund**. Ex-refund, gross margin *fell* year-on-year and adjusted EBITDA
   margin rose only ~100bps. If Q3 laps this without the refund and without
   hurricanes, the print will look materially worse than Q2.
2. **The mix shift is a structural margin headwind.** Residential (34.7% segment
   adj EBITDA margin) is guided to high-single-digit growth and is currently
   *declining*; C&I (14.6%, ~12.6% ex-refund) is guided to low-30s growth.
   Succeeding at the growth plan **mechanically compresses blended margin** — a
   revenue-growth story that is not an EPS-growth story at the same rate.
3. **The FY guide is back-half loaded.** H1 grew 11.5%; the full year needs
   mid-to-high teens, implying ~+20% in H2 on an unproven large-megawatt
   packaging ramp across three newly acquired facilities. Execution risk is high
   and the miss would land on a 1.89-beta stock.
4. **Data-center backlog quality is explicitly caveated by management.** The Q2
   8-K newly flags *"cancellation rights, delivery requirements, and potential
   liability exposure tied to our performance obligations"* and *"uncertainty
   regarding the growth of the data center market."* A $1.6B backlog with
   cancellation rights is not a $1.6B backlog. And Generac is now a **levered
   derivative of hyperscaler capex** — the exact exposure that de-rated VRT and
   VST in July 2026.
5. **The core franchise depends on the weather, and the weather is not a trend.**
   FY2024 had *"multiple major landed hurricanes"*; FY2025 had none and revenue
   fell 2% with operating income down 46%. This is an inherently
   un-compoundable demand driver, and it is why the 12-month chart is a
   round-trip rather than a dip.
6. **Residential macro and rates.** Home standby is a **$10k–15k+ discretionary,
   debt-or-HELOC-financed home improvement** requiring an electrician and a
   permit — squarely exposed to mortgage rates, home-equity availability and
   durable-goods sentiment. The 10-K lists *"changes in durable goods spending by
   consumers"* as a top risk. Residential guidance was already trimmed.
7. **Energy technology is a policy casualty.** The OBBBA solar-incentive
   phase-out is a legislated, multi-year headwind against Tesla/Enphase/SolarEdge
   competition, and the segment's standalone economics are undisclosed.
8. **Tariffs are a two-way risk.** The $71M was a *refund* of previously paid
   tariffs. The 10-K risk factors keep *"changes and volatility with respect to
   the trade policies of various countries"* front and centre, and Generac depends
   on *"a small number of contract manufacturers and component suppliers,
   including single-source suppliers"* with *"risks related to sourcing components
   in foreign countries."* The refund could reverse; the exposure does not go away.
9. **Technical damage is fresh.** 19.2% below the 50-day MA, sitting right on the
   200-day, beta 1.89, 3.27% short interest. Nothing about the tape says the
   selling is finished.
10. **Valuation offers no cushion** at ~20× FY26E adjusted (~10% higher clean of
    the refund) with a 2.4% FCF yield.

**Preference: VST over GNRC, but with a timing caveat on both.**

VST is the better business by a wide margin on the dimension that matters most
here — **irreplaceability 9 vs 6** — and this run **removed its single largest
open risk**: the balance sheet is stronger than the screen represents (true net
debt $19.28B not $19.91B, 2.5–2.8× not 2.93×, IG-rated, ~9× interest coverage,
gross debt *falling*), and **trailing free cash flow is +$1.80B, not −$0.16B**.
It is also the cheaper of the two on every measure, on guidance that explicitly
excludes 5,500 MW of acquired capacity and ~3,800 MW of signed hyperscaler PPAs.
**The caveat is entry: it has already run 9.2% in three sessions with zero
estimate revisions, and it reports in four days.**

GNRC is the more interesting *narrative* — a weather-cursed cyclical genuinely
re-platforming into data-center power with two hyperscale contracts and a $1.6B
backlog on a 1.0× levered balance sheet — but the drawdown is four days old, the
quarter that caused it was flattered by a **non-recurring $71M tariff refund**
that supplied ~$0.90 of $2.40 in EPS, the high-margin residential core is
shrinking and was guided down, and the growth it is winning carries **14.6%
segment margins versus residential's 34.7%**. At ~20× forward with the price 19%
below its 50-day moving average and a 1.89 beta, **it is early.** The right
posture on GNRC is to wait for the Q3 print (~late October) to show the ramp
without the refund — with an active hurricane season as the free call option that
could force the issue sooner.

## Screen corrections from batch 3b

1. **VST `fcf_ttm` = −$0.16B is WRONG.** Actual TTM (Q2'25–Q1'26) CFO of $4,670M
   less capex of $2,867M = **+$1,803M**. FY2025 was +$1,318M and FY2024 +$2,485M
   (SEC XBRL, 10-K/10-Q). The screen figure is almost certainly contaminated by
   Vistra's **nuclear decommissioning trust gross flows** (~$1.8B of proceeds and
   ~$1.8B of reinvestment *per quarter*, netting to ~$1M) and/or acquisition
   outflows being treated as capex. **This was flagged as the most important VST
   question and it resolves benignly.**
2. **VST `net_debt` = $19.91B is GROSS debt, not net.** $19,913M is exactly total
   debt at Mar 31, 2026 (ST borrowings $0 + AR financing $750M + LTD current
   $1,899M + LTD noncurrent $17,264M). Cash of $634M is not subtracted. **True net
   debt $19,279M**, and true net debt/EBITDA is **2.54–2.84×** on FY26 guidance,
   not 2.93×.
3. **GNRC `op_margin` = 17.9% is a single-quarter figure, not TTM.** It is
   precisely Q2 2026's GAAP operating margin (17.93% = $210.4M / $1,174M) — the
   best quarter in two years **and one inflated by a $71M one-off tariff refund**.
   **TTM GAAP operating margin is ~9.5%**; FY2025 was 6.9%. The screen materially
   overstates GNRC's profitability.
4. **GNRC `rev_growth_ttm` = 4.6% overstates it.** TTM revenue $4,439M
   (Q3'25 $1,114M + Q4'25 $1,092M + Q1'26 $1,059M + Q2'26 $1,174M) vs the
   prior-year TTM $4,412M ($1,174M + $1,235M + $942M + $1,061M) = **+0.6%**.
5. **GNRC `net_debt_ebitda` = 1.94× overstates leverage.** Net debt is $1,064M at
   Jun 30, 2026 against FY26 guided adjusted EBITDA of ~$0.98–1.03B →
   **~1.0–1.1×**. The screen uses depressed trailing GAAP EBITDA.
6. **GNRC `fwd_pe` = 17.2× is a 2027 multiple.** FY2026 is ~20.4× on adjusted EPS
   (~$9.87) and ~24–27× on GAAP EPS (~$7.5–8.3), and ~10% higher again once the
   non-recurring tariff refund is stripped from guidance.
