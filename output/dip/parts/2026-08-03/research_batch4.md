# Research batch 4 — BR, VRSK, ROL, DECK (run 2026-08-03)

## BR — Broadridge Financial Solutions
**$157.34 | $18B mkt cap | −39.8% off 52w high | −13.2% vs 200d SMA | −35.1% 12m | fwd PE 15.1 | target $206.50 (+31.2%)**

### 1. Why it's down — verdict: TRANSITORY (highest-conviction of the four)
A *narrative* de-rating, not an earnings break. Broadridge has been **raising** guidance through the decline. Q3 FY26 (reported ~Apr 30, 2026): recurring revenues **$1.29B, +6% constant currency**; adjusted EPS **+11% to $2.72**; FY26 guidance *raised* to recurring revenue growth **"at or above 7%" cc** and adjusted EPS growth **10–12%**. Yet the stock fell.

Two fears drove the sell-off, per reporting on the **July 23, 2026** decline (stock $144.40, −43.08% over 52 weeks): (a) **AI eroding BR's technology franchises**, especially proxy processing; (b) **tokenized equities disintermediating the governance business**.

The one *real* negative: **closed-sales guidance cut from $290–340M to $240–290M**. YTD closed sales $147M, **−16% y/y**; quarterly closed sales $58M vs $71M a year ago. Management attributed this to longer sales cycles on larger digital-transformation and wealth-platform deals — "sales-cycle timing rather than demand destruction" — and quantified the midpoint delta at **$40–45M, only a fraction of which converts next year**. Offsetting: CEO Tim Gokey said the **pipeline exceeds $1B, up 20% y/y, with deal origination up 25%**. Needham cut its target to $230 from $255 but **maintained Buy**.

The classic setup: a compounder whose numbers are fine, sold on a story about the next decade.

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 9/10
**Regulatory mandate is the moat.** SEC rules *require* brokers and banks to distribute proxy materials to beneficial owners, with fees regulated by the NYSE subject to SEC review. BR is the near-monopoly utility executing this. The in-sourcing threat is weak: clients face prohibitive compliance risk handling proxy distribution themselves, and any broker-dealer turning proxy distribution into a profit center would invite regulatory review.

On tokenization the evidence **directly contradicts the bear thesis**: the **SEC has affirmed that tokenized securities carry the same governance and compliance obligations as traditional equities**, and the vast majority of tokenized equities flow through broker-dealers and digital platforms that *are already BR's clients*. More decisively, BR isn't being bypassed — it's building the rails. In **April 2026** it extended its governance platform to digital assets, and has since announced it is **live with on-chain governance for tokenized equities**, plus extended proxy voting and disclosure to support *all* models of tokenized securities. The disruption is being converted into a product line.

### 3. Rebound catalyst — near-term and dated
**NEXT EARNINGS: August 4, 2026, before market open (confirmed)** — Q4 FY26. Consensus: revenue **$2.17B (+5.3% y/y)**, EPS **$3.75 (+5.6%)**. Segment consensus: Investor Communication Solutions **$1.69B (+5.8%)**, Global Technology & Operations **$481.53M (+3.6%)**. The catalyst is initial **FY27 guidance** — management explicitly declined to guide at Q3, so this is the first read on whether the closed-sales cut bleeds into FY27 recurring revenue. Given the $40–45M delta and >$1B pipeline, a reassuring FY27 frame is the single cleanest re-rating trigger in this field. Secondary: continued tokenization product announcements converting the bear narrative.

### 4. Balance-sheet survival — strong
Net debt/EBITDA **1.76x** (corroborated ~1.7x). FCF **$1.19B** against an $18B cap — a **~6.6% FCF yield**. Recurring-revenue model with regulated, non-discretionary demand. No distress or dilution risk.

### 5. Margin of safety — genuinely cheap
Fwd PE **15.1**, trailing 16.8. BR's **10-year average P/E is 30.85**, peaking at **39.32 (Dec 2021)** and bottoming at **21.93 (Dec 2015)**. As of **Jan 8, 2026** its forward PE was still **23.2**. The stock has de-rated to roughly **half its decade-average multiple and below its own 10-year trough multiple**, while still compounding EPS at 10–12%. Analyst mean target **$206.50 (+31.2%)**; Needham $230. The widest gap between multiple and fundamental trajectory in the group.

### 6. Category position — #1, intact
Still the dominant proxy/investor-communications processor. Nothing shows share loss; the closed-sales shortfall is timing on large deals, with origination and pipeline both *up* double digits.

### 7. Value-trap risk
The fears are long-dated, so they can't be disproven quickly — a multiple can stay compressed for years on an unfalsifiable story. If closed sales miss *again* on Aug 4, "timing" becomes "demand," and the $40–45M delta compounds into FY27–28 recurring revenue. A genuine structural risk exists if tokenized-equity market structure eventually moves governance on-chain in a way that disintermediates broker-dealers themselves — BR's clients — rather than BR directly. Also: if ICS revenue growth decelerates below 5%, 15x is not obviously cheap for a low-single-digit grower.

### 8. Rebound score: **8.5/10**
*A regulated near-monopoly compounding EPS at 10–12% marked down to half its historical multiple on two unfalsifiable narratives it is actively monetizing — but you must survive tomorrow's print.*

## VRSK — Verisk Analytics
**$193.11 | $25B mkt cap | −28.6% off 52w high | −2.5% vs 200d SMA | −30.1% 12m | fwd PE 22.2 | target $237.12 (+22.8%)**

### 1. Why it's down — verdict: TRANSITORY, with a real growth-rate reset
Four drivers, three non-fundamental:
- **Growth deceleration, now inflecting.** Q1 2026 organic constant-currency growth fell to **3.9%**, which management itself described as a **"trough."** Q2 2026 (reported **July 29, 2026**) came in at **5.8% organic cc** — an acceleration off that trough. Revenue $806M.
- **Transactional weakness.** Transactional revenue **−4.2% organic cc**, management flagging softer commercial property quotes, **low weather losses**, and travel weakness. Low weather losses is a *cyclical* drag that reverses with catastrophe activity.
- **Forced technical selling.** The stock was **removed from the NASDAQ-100 and the Russell 1000 Growth index**, triggering index-fund liquidation — mechanical, price-insensitive selling with zero information content.
- **AI fear.** Concern that AI commoditizes Verisk's data assets and lets insurers build in-house analytics.

Margin pressure is real but modest: trailing net margin compressed ~2.2pp from 30.4% to 28.2%. Critically, **full-year 2026 guidance was reaffirmed** at Q2: revenue **$3.19–3.24B**, adjusted EBITDA **$1.79–1.83B**, margin **56.0–56.5%**. Subscription revenue — **83% of total — grew 8.0% organic cc**. A business with 83% subscription revenue growing 8% at 56% EBITDA margins is not impaired.

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 9/10
Proprietary data pooled from the industry itself (contributory data no single insurer can replicate), plus **regulatory embedding**: insurance is regulated at the *state* level, and regulators and carriers demand **auditability and transparency that opaque GenAI models structurally struggle to provide** — RBC Capital calls the GenAI disruption concern "overblown" on exactly this basis. An insurer cannot file an unexplainable black-box model with 50 state regulators.

Verisk is converting AI from threat to product: a **GenAI Commercial Underwriting Assistant** and **XactAI** (computer-vision property damage assessment from photos). **XactAI users jumped 10x to ~7,000** as of Q2 2026. Caveat: roughly **20–25% of revenue is tied to contracts influenced by net-written-premium P&C pricing growth**, so a soft P&C pricing cycle is a genuine non-AI headwind on a minority of the book.

### 3. Rebound catalyst
**NEXT EARNINGS: not found (unconfirmed).** Q2 was July 29, 2026; historically Q3 lands late October, so ~late Oct 2026 is the likely window — estimate, not fact.

Catalysts: (a) **the growth inflection continuing** — 3.9% trough → 5.8%; a third consecutive acceleration re-rates the stock; (b) **index-deletion selling exhausting itself**; (c) a **$1.5B share repurchase** program; (d) transactional revenue mean-reverting when weather losses normalize; (e) AI reframing from disruptor to margin lever as XactAI adoption scales.

### 4. Balance-sheet survival — adequate, most levered of the four
Net debt/EBITDA **2.68x** — highest here, though unremarkable for a 56%-EBITDA-margin subscription utility. FCF **$1.06B** on a $25B cap (~4.2% yield). Highly predictable subscription cash flows. No distress risk; leverage modestly constrains buyback aggressiveness vs DECK.

### 5. Margin of safety — moderately cheap, not a screaming bargain
Fwd PE **22.2**, trailing 29.7 — a real reset from premium data-monopoly multiples. Mid-2026 comparison: current P/E ~25.0x vs a "fair ratio" of 22.7x, **peer group average 26.5x**, but **Professional Services industry average only 18.7x**. So it screens *cheap vs data/analytics peers, expensive vs the broad services industry*. One DCF puts intrinsic value at **$247.43 (~29.8% undervalued)**; analyst mean target **$237.12 (+22.8%)**. Honest read: the easy multiple compression is done; at 22x this is a fair-to-good price for a 56%-margin monopoly, not a deep-value entry.

### 6. Category position — #1, intact
Still the dominant insurance-data utility. No evidence of customer loss; the deceleration is cyclical (transactional, weather, P&C pricing), not share-driven. Subscription growth at 8% on 83% of revenue is the tell.

### 7. Value-trap risk
The most credible bear case in the field: sustained transactional weakness or subscription growth slipping would push organic cc growth **below 6%**, risking a guidance miss. One bear narrative models revenue *declining* 16% and a fair value of **$69.70** — i.e. the stock trading 149% above fair value — on the view AI genuinely commoditizes the data. An extreme scenario, but it defines the tail. The subtler trap: a "regulatory-grade data utility" growing 5–6% deserves a utility multiple (~18x), not a compounder multiple, in which case there's little upside from 22x. And if the P&C pricing cycle turns soft, 20–25% of revenue faces a multi-year headwind.

### 8. Rebound score: **7.5/10**
*A regulated data monopoly whose growth already troughed in Q1 and is re-accelerating, sold down partly by index funds that had no view on the business — good quality at a fair price rather than a bargain.*

## ROL — Rollins, Inc.
**$38.19 | $18B mkt cap | −41.4% off 52w high | −30.0% vs 200d SMA (deepest gap in field) | −32.5% 12m | fwd PE 29.1 | target $45.59 (+19.4%)**

### 1. Why it's down — verdict: TRANSITORY demand issue, PERMANENT multiple reset
The important distinction. The *business* problem is cyclical; the *valuation* problem is structural and mostly not yet resolved.

The 52-week high was **$65.60 in February 2026**; the stock is now ~$38–39. Most of that is de-rating from an extreme starting multiple, crystallized by one event: **July 23, 2026**, Q2 2026 results. Revenue **+7.9% y/y to $1.1B**, but **organic growth of 5.7% missed**, and the company **cut its 2026 outlook to "at least 6%" organic growth** (from a higher prior) with "at least 10%" incremental margins. Shares fell **~14.3% to $37.24** after hours from a $43.47 close.

Margins compressed alongside: operating margin **18.7% vs 19.8%** a year ago; gross margin **−100bps to 52.8%**; adjusted EBITDA margin **−120bps to 21.9%**.

Cause is specific and narrow: **residential pest control**, particularly brands like **Orkin** that depend on consumer-initiated demand via search, digital media and inbound calls. CEO **Jerry Gahlhoff** located the weakness precisely there. The rest held up well — **commercial pest +7.2% organic, termite and ancillary +8.9% organic**. A consumer-discretionary lead-generation problem, not a franchise problem.

Notably: **management said lead flow worsened through most of the quarter but improved by late June into early July** — a dated green shoot. Management also stated the revised guidance reflects **near-term conditions, not a change to the longer-term framework**, reaffirming a long-term algorithm of **at least 7% organic growth with incremental margins of at least 30%**.

No evidence of a management change, an M&A integration failure, or an accounting problem — acquisition and cash-flow expectations left intact.

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 9/10 (highest business-quality moat here)
Route density plus recurring contracts. A technician must physically visit a property; **AI cannot kill a termite**. The moat is service-route density (drop cost per stop), the recurring contractual base, and brand trust (Orkin) in a trust-mediated purchase. No plausible substitute technology and no AI disintermediation vector; AI is mildly *accretive* to route optimization and lead scoring. The one genuine erosion channel is the one that just bit: if customer acquisition shifts to AI-mediated search, Orkin's paid-search-dependent lead funnel could be structurally disrupted at the *demand-capture* layer — worth monitoring, though it affects acquisition cost, not service delivery.

### 3. Rebound catalyst — weakest near-term visibility
**NEXT EARNINGS: not found (unconfirmed).** Historically Q3 lands in the third or fourth week of October (Q3 2024 was Oct 23, 2024), so ~late Oct 2026 is a reasonable estimate — not confirmed.

Catalysts: (a) **the lead-flow improvement seen late June / early July 2026 showing up as Q3 organic re-acceleration** — the concrete, dated one; (b) reversion toward the reaffirmed ≥7% organic / ≥30% incremental margin algorithm; (c) continued tuck-in M&A; (d) easier comps into 2027. Absent a catalyst, this needs *time* rather than an event.

### 4. Balance-sheet survival — cleanest leverage in the field
Net debt/EBITDA **1.16x** — lowest of the levered names. FCF **$0.50B**, ROE 37.0%, op margin 18.8%. Recurring contract revenue. Zero distress risk; comfortably funds tuck-ins and the dividend without dilution.

### 5. Margin of safety — WEAKEST of the four; still not cheap
The disqualifying issue. Fwd PE **29.1**, trailing **34.7** — and post-miss reporting still pegs it at a **P/E of 39.67** trailing and **~31x NTM**. Analysts explicitly note that at ~31x NTM **further multiple compression is possible if earnings growth fails to re-accelerate**. Analyst mean target $45.59 implies only **+19.4%** — the lowest implied upside in the group, from the deepest drawdown. That combination is the signature of a stock that fell a long way *and is still expensive*, i.e. it was priced at ~50x+ before. At least one analyst downgraded post-Q2 on the view that the earnings-growth recovery "needs more evidence."

You are not being paid a discount for the risk. You're paying 29x forward for a business that just guided organic growth down to 6%.

### 6. Category position — #1, share intact
Rollins remains the pest-control category leader. The slowdown is demand-side (consumer lead generation), not competitive share loss — commercial and termite grew 7.2% and 8.9% organically, which is not what share loss looks like.

### 7. Value-trap risk — highest in the field
Precisely the falling-knife profile: a beloved defensive compounder de-rating from an unsustainable multiple, where each leg down still leaves it expensive. If residential lead flow doesn't recover, ROL is a ~6% grower trading at 29x — a multiple that could compress toward 20x (**another ~30% downside**) *without anything further going wrong operationally*. The −30% gap to its own 200-day SMA reflects violent, still-unresolved momentum; mean-reversion to the SMA is not a valuation argument. Secondary risk: the AI-search disruption of its residential customer-acquisition funnel proves structural rather than cyclical, permanently raising CAC.

### 8. Rebound score: **5.5/10**
*The best business in this field attached to the worst price — a superb, genuinely AI-proof franchise whose de-rating from ~50x is probably not finished, so the moat is real but the margin of safety isn't.*

## DECK — Deckers Brands
**$99.50 | $14B mkt cap | −19.7% off 52w high | −2.6% vs 200d SMA | −6.3% 12m | fwd PE 11.9 | target $122.81 (+23.4%) | NET CASH $1.13B**

### 1. Why it's down — verdict: TRANSITORY (normal growth digestion, not a fad rollover)
The mildest drawdown here, and the de-rating traces to a specific event: **October 24, 2025**, when the stock **sank 15% on a soft outlook** raising concerns about Hoka and Ugg growth. Since then it has been a multiple story — the market repricing DECK from a hyper-growth footwear name to a mid-single-digit grower.

The most recent data argues *against* permanent impairment. **Q1 FY2027, reported July 23, 2026** (quarter ended June 30, 2026):
- Net sales **+5.7% to $1.02B** — first $1B+ first quarter ever
- **HOKA +7.7% to $703.5M**; **UGG +4.9% to $278M**; other brands **−18.1% to $37.9M**
- **DTC +13% to $352.8M** with comparable DTC **+6.8%**; wholesale +2.2% to $666.7M
- International **+8.4%** vs domestic **+3.2%**
- EPS **$0.94 vs $0.88 consensus** (beat by 6.8%)
- FY27 EPS guidance **raised to $7.35–7.50** (up $0.05); revenue outlook maintained at **$5.86–5.91B**

And the stock still fell **3–6%** on July 23–24, 2026, on deceleration and margin-compression concerns rather than the printed numbers. That's sentiment, not fundamentals.

On the fad question the decisive evidence is the **mix**: DTC +13% with comps +6.8% is *direct consumer pull*, which is not what a brand past its peak produces (fading brands show wholesale channel-stuffing with negative DTC comps). Management attributed the **high-single-digit HOKA June-quarter growth to one-time wholesale timing shifts, not a change in underlying demand**. FY2026 closed at record revenue **$5.47B, +10%**, with HOKA's largest quarter ever at **$671M**, diluted EPS **+11% to a record $7.02**, and operating margins **above 23%**.

The honest deceleration: FY27 EPS growth is guided at roughly **4–6%**, well below the 11% delivered in FY26. Tariffs are the main culprit — DECK paid ~**$120M in gross duties** under IEPA (pursuing refunds, with **no refunds assumed in FY27 guidance**), and tariffs cost **80bps of gross margin y/y**; one estimate puts the hit near **$200M this year**.

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 7/10 (lowest here, but for the right reason)
The moat is **brand**, fully AI-proof in the disruption sense — no LLM or substitute technology commoditizes a physical premium running shoe, and there is no disintermediation vector. Score docked not for AI risk but for **fashion/taste risk**, the genuine substitute threat: consumer preference can rotate to a rival brand in a way it cannot rotate away from pest control or SEC-mandated proxy distribution.

Competitive context is real but nuanced: running footwear grew **8.9% y/y in 2026**, with **Nike losing share** while **Hoka, On, New Balance and Adidas gained** — Hoka is a *share gainer* in a growing category. But On is growing faster: **On Holding cleared CHF 3B in FY25 net sales, +30.0%**, vs **Hoka's $2.233B, +23.6%**. And in consumer preference surveys, **Asics led at 16%, Brooks 15%, Hoka One 9%** — Hoka is a premium challenger, not the default.

### 3. Rebound catalyst — the strongest mechanical catalyst in the field
**NEXT EARNINGS: not found (unconfirmed).** Q1 FY27 was July 23, 2026; DECK historically reports its September quarter in late October, so ~late Oct 2026 is the estimate.

The catalyst is **capital return, and it's already running**: the board **increased the repurchase authorization by $3.5B to ~$5B total** — roughly **36% of the $14B market cap**. In FY2026 DECK repurchased **~10.5M shares for $1.075B at an average of $102.43** — *above today's $99.50*, meaning the company was a willing buyer higher than the current price and is now buying cheaper. At a $14B cap with net cash, this shrinks the share count fast and puts a floor under the stock independent of any narrative shift. Secondary: **tariff refunds under IEPA** (pure upside — explicitly excluded from guidance), HOKA wholesale timing reversing in H2, international momentum (+8.4%), and the multi-year framework to 2030 (high-single-digit revenue CAGR, HOKA low double digits, UGG mid-single digits, **EPS compounding low double digits**).

### 4. Balance-sheet survival — fortress, best in field
**Net cash.** The screen cites $1.13B net cash (net debt/EBITDA **−0.85**); a separate source puts it at **$2.09B cash vs $342.9M debt = $1.74B net cash (~$12.29/share)**, with the company ending the period at **$2.1B cash and zero outstanding borrowings** — discrepancy flagged rather than reconciled; either figure is decisive. FCF **$0.87B**, ROE 42.6%. No scenario of distress or dilution; it can buy back stock through any drawdown, converting a falling price into per-share value.

### 5. Margin of safety — cheapest in the field
Fwd PE **11.9**, trailing 14.2 (other sources: trailing 15.70 / forward 15.27, TTM P/E **12.94**). Against an **apparel-retail industry average nearer 24**, and **EV/EBITDA 9.86, ~27% below median**. Because DECK holds net cash the *enterprise* multiple is cheaper still — ~$12.9B EV for $0.87B FCF plus a 23%-operating-margin brand portfolio. Analyst mean target **$122.81 (+23.4%)**. BofA is the notable skeptic, resetting its target post-earnings and arguing intense running-category competition makes a notable US HOKA reacceleration hard.

Genuinely cheap *because of the dip*, not optically cheap on peak earnings — FY27 EPS is guided to *grow*, not fall.

### 6. Category position — #2 challenger gaining share, not losing it
HOKA is not category #1 (Nike remains larger; Asics/Brooks lead preference surveys), but it is on the **winning side of the share shift**, taking share from Nike alongside On and New Balance. UGG is #1 in its niche with genuine pricing power and seasonal DTC strength. The dip is *not* a share-loss sign — Q1 FY27 grew HOKA +7.7% and UGG +4.9% with DTC comps +6.8%.

### 7. Value-trap risk
HOKA's US business is the swing factor, and BofA's point stands — the running category is crowded (On +30%, New Balance, Adidas, a reinvigorated Nike defending), so HOKA's growth could settle into low single digits domestically while international carries the load. Tariffs (~$200M) are a live margin drag with **no refund assumed**; if refunds are denied while duties persist, the 4–6% FY27 EPS growth guide is the *ceiling*, not the floor. Deepest risk: brands do die — if HOKA's maximalist-cushioning aesthetic rotates out of fashion, 12x forward on a declining brand is not cheap, it's a melting ice cube. Also note "other brands" fell **−18.1%**, showing the portfolio outside HOKA/UGG is not contributing. Mitigant: the net-cash balance sheet and 36%-of-cap buyback mean you're paid to wait.

### 8. Rebound score: **8/10**
*A share-gaining premium brand that just beat and raised, trading at ~12x forward with net cash and a buyback authorization worth a third of its market cap — the cheapest, best-capitalized dip here, carrying fashion risk instead of AI risk.*

**Ranking: BR > DECK > VRSK > ROL.**

**BR is the standout dislocation.** The only name where a company raising guidance (7% recurring revenue, 10–12% adjusted EPS) fell ~40%, and both fears driving it are contradicted by primary evidence — the SEC has affirmed tokenized securities carry identical governance obligations, and BR has gone *live* with on-chain governance rather than being bypassed. At 15x versus a 10-year average of 30.9x, it trades below its own decade-trough multiple. The caveat is timing-critical: **earnings are tomorrow morning**, making today's entry a coin-flip on the FY27 guide.

**DECK is the highest-quality risk-adjusted entry** and the one to favor if avoiding event risk. It beat *and* raised on July 23, holds net cash, and has a buyback worth ~36% of its market cap with management having bought FY26 stock at $102.43 — above today's price. Its risk is categorically different: fashion/taste, not AI. That makes it the best *diversifier* against a field where three names share one correlated thesis.

**VRSK is solid but not urgent** — a chunk of its selling came from NASDAQ-100 and Russell 1000 Growth deletion, which is information-free forced liquidation, and growth already troughed at 3.9% in Q1 and re-accelerated to 5.8%. But at 22x it is fairly priced rather than cheap, and it carries the highest leverage (2.68x).

**ROL is the trap to avoid despite the deepest dip.** Arguably the best and most genuinely AI-proof business here, but the only name that *cut* guidance, and after a 41% fall it still trades at 29x forward with the lowest implied upside (+19.4%). A −30% gap to its 200-day SMA is a momentum fact, not a valuation argument; a further de-rate toward 20x implies ~30% downside with nothing new going wrong. Great business, wrong price — revisit near the low-20s multiple.

**Field-level observation:** three of these four (BR, VRSK, ROL) are being sold on variants of the same "AI commoditizes the toll-booth" trade. If that thesis is wrong — and the regulatory-auditability and compliance-liability evidence suggests it is overstated for BR and VRSK specifically — these names re-rate together, which means they are *not* independent bets. Sizing across BR + VRSK doubles one thesis. DECK is the only name whose drawdown is uncorrelated with that trade.
