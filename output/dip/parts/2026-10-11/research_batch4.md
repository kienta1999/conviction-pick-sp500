## HSY — The Hershey Company (Consumer Staples)
Price ~$162.7 (52w $156.16–$239.48; −30% off high); fwd PE ~16.5; div yield ~3.6%; mkt cap ~$32.4B.

### 1. Why it's down — TEMPORARY (with a real caveat)
- Root cause is the 2024–25 cocoa supply shock: cocoa roughly doubled, and FY2025 adjusted EPS collapsed to $6.31 (down ~a third) (ainvest, Sep 2026).
- 2026 brought the mechanical margin snap-back: Q2 2026 (reported Jul 30, 2026) adjusted EPS $1.90 vs $1.43 consensus; gross margin 41.6% adjusted, +350bp y/y; FY26 adjusted EPS guidance raised to $8.36–8.52; Q2 net sales +6.6% y/y to $2.79B.
- **Cocoa update (Oct 2026): the "rolling over" thesis is now threatened.** NY cocoa is ~$5,500–5,670/tonne (up ~80% from ~$3,000 in April 2026) on a potentially record-strong El Niño threatening West Africa's 2026/27 crop (Goldman Sachs via theagribiz, Oct 2026; Morningstar/Dow Jones Oct 5, 2026). ICE inventories hit a 2.25-year high of ~3.55M bags (Oct 7, 2026), so physical supply isn't scarce *yet* — but Jefferies (Sep 15, 2026) flagged the Super El Niño as a direct HSY risk, and Cargill booked cocoa mark-to-market losses for the quarter ended Aug 31 (stockpil, Oct 2026). **Verdict: cocoa is cyclical, but the cycle is turning AGAINST HSY right now — transitory in the long run, but not a tailwind today.**
- Volume, not just cocoa, is the new worry: total company volume −8% in Q2, NA confectionery volume −10%, and Hershey *lost share* of the US candy/mint/gum categories — growth is price-only (~12–14 pts of price) (ainvest, Sep 2026). The Hershey Trust has been selling shares as the stock slid toward yearly lows (equityswarm, Oct 2, 2026). **Temporary-vs-permanent: TEMPORARY on margins, but watch volume/share as the could-become-permanent signal.**

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 9/10
Moat: #1 US confectionery brand portfolio (Reese's, Hershey's, Kit Kat US rights) — habit-driven, shelf-space dominant, proven pricing power through the shock. AI/substitutes cannot touch physical chocolate consumption; GLP-1 weight-loss drugs are the secular-demand risk, not AI.

### 3. Rebound catalyst
Q3 earnings Oct 29, 2026 (Barchart) with Halloween/holiday seasonal volume — traditionally high-margin periods; volume stabilization would flip the narrative from "price-only growth" to real recovery. Tariff relief and the ~$100M productivity push are margin tailwinds. Path: FY27 EPS ~$9.86 consensus (+16% y/y, Barchart).

### 4. Balance-sheet survival
No distress risk. LT debt $4.68B, cash $791M (Q2 2026); net debt/equity ~104% — elevated but well covered (EBIT interest coverage 11x, Simply Wall St). Q2 FCF $683.7M; dividend payout ~78% (Sep 2026) — tight if cocoa re-spikes, but not a cut-risk today.

### 5. Margin of safety / valuation
- Fwd PE 16.5 vs its own history ~21.0 (tradestie, Oct 11, 2026) — "cheap" vs own history; trailing ~22.2x on depressed earnings.
- Wall St target ~$203.14, +24.8% implied upside (Oct 11, 2026); 24 analysts: 5 Strong Buy, 1 Moderate Buy, 17 Hold, 1 Strong Sell — consensus Hold.
- FY26E EPS $8.49 vs $6.31 in 2025 (+34.6%) — the "E" has recovered; the stock hasn't.

### 6. Category position
Still #1 in US chocolate with real pricing power, but Q2 share losses in candy/mint/gum are the first crack. Salty snacks (Dot's, SkinnyPop) diversify but carry their own execution issues (multipack supply).

### 7. Value-trap risk (bear case)
The knife: cocoa re-inflates on El Niño into 2026/27 and the price lever breaks — volumes already fell 8–10%; a second cocoa leg with no more pricing headroom means margin compression *and* share loss. Add GLP-1 secular demand erosion and the Trust overhang, and the stock re-rates to a no-growth staples multiple (~14x).

### 8. Rebound score: 6/10
Verdict: A proven price-maker mid-margin-recovery, but the dip is half price for a reason — cocoa is re-spiking and volumes are shrinking; buy only if you believe the 16.5x multiple already prices a no-growth chocolate business.

Price ~$2,940 (52w $2,730.65–$4,170; −28% off high); fwd PE ~15.3; mkt cap ~$46.9B.

### 1. Why it's down — TEMPORARY (expectations reset, not franchise break)

- Margin compression from **accounting + tariffs, not demand**: Q1 gross margin −203bp to 51.0% driven by a ~212bp non-cash LIFO charge (ts2.tech, Dec 2025); FY26 full-year gross margin 52.3% (down 30bp) on a 61bp LIFO drag, partly offset by a 48bp tariff-refund benefit (company Q4 release, Sep 22, 2026).
- FY Q4 (16 weeks ended Aug 29, 2026; reported Sep 22, 2026): revenue $6.59B missed $6.70B consensus; domestic same-store +1.6% but **DIY −0.6%** — tariff-related cost pressure on DIY pulled back; commercial (professional installers) +8.6% to $1.9B. EPS $56.05 beat (~$54–55 consensus), but quality was cloudy: $96M tariff refund + favorable LIFO flattered the print.
- Mizuho downgraded to Neutral (PT $3,000) after fiscal Q1, citing "cost of doing business moving structurally higher" and a new investment cycle ($1.6B FY26 capex, ~400 stores/yr) colliding with tariff inflation. **Temporary-vs-permanent: TEMPORARY — a classic premium-multiple reset; underlying comps and commercial growth are intact.**

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 8/10
Moat: #1 US DIY auto-parts scale — 8,031 stores, 137 megahubs (targeting ~300), dense distribution = same-day parts availability competitors can't match. Aging US vehicle fleet (average age ~13 years) is a structural demand tailwind. AI cannot replace physical parts; the secular risk is EVs (fewer wear parts) over 10+ years, not near term. Real competition is O'Reilly (well-executed) and Advance Auto Parts (struggling) — AZO is not losing share to either in commercial, its growth engine.

### 3. Rebound catalyst
FY2027 guide (Sep 22, 2026 call): domestic comps flat to low-single-digit, commercial high-single to low-double-digit, ~400 new stores, capex ~$1.5B; management expects sales acceleration in FY27 as commercial/international carry. Mega-hub rollout (~30 in FY26, ~300 target) lifts per-program commercial sales ~16%. Aging fleet + tariff-refund wind-down normalizing = cleaner comps by mid-2027.

### 4. Balance-sheet survival
Leveraged by design, not distress: total debt ~$9.0B, cash ~$254M; adjusted debt/EBITDAR 2.5x (company, May 2026); stockholders' equity negative (decades of buybacks — the −90% ROE is an accounting artifact, not insolvency). FCF ~$1.8B (simplywall.st, Sep 2026); $2.0B of buybacks in FY26, $1.6B authorization remaining. Easily services debt.

### 5. Margin of safety / valuation
- Fwd PE 15.3 — a multi-year discount to its own ~18–22x compounder multiple (brief screen: trailing ~18.2x).
- MarketBeat (Oct 6, 2026): Moderate Buy on 28 analysts; avg target $3,710.36, +27.4% above ~$2,911; high $4,700, low $3,000. Targets trimmed across the board post-Q4 (JPMorgan $3,850→$3,700, BMO/Raymond James/Baird lower) but ratings stayed constructive.
- FY26 EPS $152.55 (+5.3% y/y); buybacks keep shrinking the float.

### 6. Category position
Still the category leader; commercial is taking share (high-single to double-digit growth). DIY softness is cyclical/tariff-driven, not share loss.

### 7. Value-trap risk (bear case)
The knife: the DIY business structurally stalls (tariff pass-through permanently reprices the DIY consumer), SG&A from the mega-hub buildout outruns comps, and FY27 guidance (flat-to-LSD domestic comps) proves to be the new normal growth rate — then a 15.3x multiple is fair, not cheap, and buyback pace slows as capex stays elevated.

### 8. Rebound score: 7/10
Verdict: A premium compounder de-rated to a fair multiple on tariff/LIFO noise and an investment cycle — commercial momentum, the aging fleet, and relentless buybacks make this the cleanest rebound of the three.

## Appendix — Earnings-Quality Flag: AZO "RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN"
**What the receivables are:** AutoZone extends trade credit to professional/commercial customers (repair shops, garages) under its commercial program — these are accounts receivable from installers buying parts on terms, not consumer financing. Per the 10-Q (May 9, 2026), receivables were $764.9M vs $670.1M a year earlier (+14.1%).
**Benign or warning?** Mostly benign mix shift: domestic commercial sales grew ~11% in FY26 (+8.6% in Q4), and commercial is the credit-heavy channel — receivables growth tracks the fastest-growing segment. CFO/NI of 1.24 shows earnings convert to cash; LIFO is the bigger earnings-quality item, and it's non-cash. Watch item, not red flag: if receivables growth persistently exceeds commercial sales growth (i.e., DSO stretching), that would signal loosening credit to hit comps — currently not evident.
**One-line verdict: Benign — receivables track the booming commercial (credit-based) channel; monitor DSO, not a cash-quality warning today.**
