## TPR — Tapestry, Inc. ($118.12 on 10/02/2026; 52wk $164.80–~$104; YTD ≈ −10%; −28% off Aug high — stockanalysis.com, marketbeat)

### 1. Why it's down
- The single-day trigger: FY2026 Q4 results reported 8/13/2026 — record year (revenue $8.0B +14%, adjusted EPS $7.05 +38%, operating margin 23.4% expanded 340bp) but the stock fell ~16.5% in one session, wiping $5.7B of market cap. The offense: FY2027 revenue guidance of $8.4–8.5B came in $20M light of the $8.47B consensus, and Coach's Q4 growth of 15% fractionally missed a 15.5% FactSet bar — trivial misses, but the guidance embeds a hard deceleration: Coach guided from ~23% FY26 growth to ~8%, while Kate Spade keeps shrinking (ainvest 8/13/2026; beststocks 8/2026; finance.biggo 8/2026).
- The structural anxiety: Kate Spade revenue fell 10% in FY2026 (−7% in Q4); management is rebuilding it — 40% of SKUs cut, unproductive stores closed, promotions pulled back — and guiding another high-single-digit decline in FY2027 with a modest operating loss. FY2025 already saw an $855M impairment on Kate Spade intangibles/goodwill. Coach is now ~86% of revenue and must supply more than 100% of net FY27 growth (beststocks; ainvest 8/2026). Stuart Weitzman was divested August 2025, so there's no third leg.
- Context beyond company control: aspirational luxury is cyclical; tariffs on Chinese/Vietnamese imports add cost/uncertainty; China stimulus-fade risk (marketbeat 9/2026).
- **Verdict: TRANSITORY.** The drawdown is a momentum derate of a record year — $1.86B adjusted FCF, 23.4% margins, Coach growing double-digits globally (China +38% FY26) — punished for one guidance miss and a turnaround-brand drag, not for broken economics.

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 6/10
Moat: Coach's brand equity (Tabby/lead-bag franchises), pricing power (handbag average unit retail up mid-teens, customers paying), DTC mix ~high, 76.6% gross margins, 11M new customers added in FY26 (~35% Gen Z). AI angle: essentially zero substitution threat to a leather handbag brand; AI matters only at the margin (marketing optimization, demand forecasting). The real threat is cultural, not technological — fashion tastes turning. 6/10 (brand moat is strong but entirely sentiment-driven, and Kate Spade shows what brand erosion looks like).

### 3. Rebound catalyst
Q1 FY2027 earnings 11/5/2026 (finance.biggo) — clean Coach comp growth + any Kate Spade stabilization would directly challenge the "growth ceiling" narrative. Longer arc: FY27 guide calls for ~50bp operating-margin expansion, adj FCF approaching $1.7B, and $1.35B more buybacks (~5% of float at $118), which mechanically lifts EPS even on mid-single-digit revenue growth. A tariff de-escalation or China stimulus would be macro tailwinds.

### 4. Balance-sheet survival
Solid. Year-end FY2026: $1.15B cash/short-term investments, $2.38B total borrowings → net debt ~$1.2B; gross debt/adjusted EBITDA 1.1x, a full turn below the 2.5x long-term target (company PR via marketbeat transcripts; finance.biggo). FY26 operating cash flow $1.98B, adjusted FCF $1.86B (+38% y/y) — funding $1.7B in dividends+buybacks with room. Zero distress risk; the question is whether the buyback pace (avg $118/share in FY26) is disciplined.

### 5. Valuation
Consensus: Buy (22 analysts, S&P Global via stockanalysis), mean target $166.50 (+41% from $118; MarketBeat $175.13, +48% per 9–10/2026 snapshots). Forward P/E ~14.2–14.9x on FY27 EPS guide $7.80–7.90 (stockstory 9/28/2026; marketbeat 9/17/2026). For a company printing 23% FCF margins and double-digit EPS growth, that's a mid-teens multiple — clearly below European luxury peers and below TPR's own multiple at the summer peak. **Genuinely cheap because of the dip**, provided FY27 guidance holds. (Ignore the garbled 141.6x forward-P/E quote floating on ainvest — an arithmetic artifact of their model, not reality.)

### 6. Category position
Still the dominant U.S. accessible-luxury house; Coach is demonstrably winning (double-digit growth every quarter of FY26, raising prices, Gen Z acquisition). The dip reflects concentration risk, not share loss — Kate Spade's share is being shed deliberately via SKU/store cuts.

### 7. Value-trap risk
The bear case: Coach is a fashion cycle, and fashion cycles turn. If the Tabby moment fades the way kate spade's moment faded, the "86% of revenue" concentration becomes a liability and the multiple never recovers. Second-order risk: FY27 guidance is back-half loaded on Coach growth that Q4 already showed decelerating (15% vs 31% in Q3).

### 8. Rebound score: 7/10
**Verdict: Record year, one $20M guidance miss, 28% derate — a cash-compounder ($1.86B FCF, 1.1x leverage, $1.35B buyback pipeline) trading at ~14x forward with +41% analyst upside; the bet is that Coach's momentum outlives Kate Spade's rebuild.**

## DECK — Deckers Outdoor Corp. ($79.12 on 10/02/2026; 52wk $77.20–$122.29; YTD −23.7%; −23.2% 1yr — Finnhub/forbestodays)

### 1. Why it's down
- The slide began with Q2 FY2026 results (~9/15–9/16/2026 per ts2.tech, reported "20 days ago" in the 9/16 window): revenue $1.431B (+9.1% y/y), EPS $1.82 (beat), HOKA +11.1%, UGG +10.1%, gross margin 56.2%, international +29% — but management cut full-year guidance to ~$5.35B revenue (below ~$5.45B consensus) and EPS $6.30–6.39, and CEO Stefano Caroti warned of "a more cautious consumer as the full impact of tariffs and price increases will be felt here in the U.S." The stock plunged ~14% that day (investopedia via ts2.tech; CFO quote). New 52wk low ~$77.20 hit 9/30/2026 (transcriptdaily).
- The multi-quarter overhang: Hoka growth decelerating from 24% toward low-teens, DTC softness at UGG (declining UGG DTC flagged by Needham), tariff cost pass-through (~$150+ shoes getting price hikes), and fashion/discounting concerns around Hoka (Barron's 9/30/2026; Needham 10/1/2026 — removed DECK from its Conviction List, kept Buy $115, calling UGG/Hoka a "slower-growth stage"). Stock is ~35% below its 52wk high.
- The cycle was already turning: Q4 FY2026 (May 2026) guidance cut on Hoka; FY27 revenue guide $5.86–5.91B read as decelerating (ainvest 5/2026).
- **Verdict: TRANSITORY but maturing.** Demand is decelerating, not disappearing — both brands still growing double digits; the drawdown prices in a growth-brand re-rating to a slower compounder plus tariff uncertainty, not brand impairment.

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 6/10
Moat: premium brand equity in two categories (UGG cultural icon with personalization in-store experiences; HOKA #2–3 performance-run franchise), selective wholesale (Dick's, REI, Nordstrom) protecting full-price sell-through, 56–58% gross margins, DTC engine. AI cannot replace a shoe — zero substitution threat; the actual threats are fashion shifts and competitor innovation (Nike/Adidas running). 6/10 (strong brands, but discretionary footwear is fashion-exposed and competition is genuine).

### 3. Rebound catalyst
Q3 FY2026 earnings ~1/29/2027 (expected; ts2.tech) — the holiday quarter; Barron's analyst consensus sees HOKA reaccelerating to double digits in H2 on new product introductions, and UGG has a warm winter tailwind debate (warm/wet winter forecast is the headwind). Near-term: $2.2B buyback authorization remaining after $282M repurchased in Q2 alone (~2.6% of market cap quarterly — aggressive). UBS (Jay Sole, PT $161) and Barron's both frame current weakness as a buying opportunity on brand resilience.

### 4. Balance-sheet survival
Fortress-like. ~$1.6B cash, only ~$472M total debt (D/E 20.5%), current ratio 2.75 (forbestodays, Q2 FY26 data). TTM FCF strong; buybacks self-funded. No distress risk whatsoever — this is a net-cash company.

### 5. Valuation
Forward P/E ~10.4x, TTM 10.6–11.2x (Finnhub/forbestodays 10/2026); EV/EBITDA 6.5x; 18% net margins. Analyst consensus: Hold (MarketBeat, avg target $114.42–114.80, +44–45%) though the range is wide ($70 BMO underperform to $161 UBS; forbes consensus $120.41, +52%). Historically DECK commanded 20x+ at hyper-growth; ~10x forward for a mid-to-high single-digit grower with 40%+ ROE is **genuinely cheap — the cheapest of this batch on an earnings basis**. The caveat is the Hold consensus reflects real growth-doubts, not just multiple compression.

### 6. Category position
Still a share winner in premium running (HOKA taking share from Nike for years) and the UGG franchise remains a cultural driver; the dip is margin/growth-rate anxiety, not share loss. International (+29%) is the new engine offsetting a softer U.S.

### 7. Value-trap risk
The honest bear case: Hoka is a fad cycle, and fad footwear (remember Crocs 2022) can decelerate far past mid-teens — if growth goes ex-growth, the premium brand story breaks and the stock is cheap for a reason. Tariff-driven price hikes on $150+ shoes could structurally shrink the TAM in a prolonged cost-of-living squeeze. BMO's $70 underperform target encodes this view.

### 8. Rebound score: 7/10
**Verdict: Net-cash, double-digit brand growth, ~10x forward P/E with $2.2B buyback firepower — the dip is a growth-rate derate plus tariff fog, and holiday-season execution is the reset catalyst; Hoka fad risk is the real bear case.**

- September overhang: on 9/2/2026 Vertiv announced the $1.45B UtilityInnovation Group acquisition (plus up to $1.15B earnout) without disclosing UIG's revenue/backlog/cash-flow — a 13x *projected* 2027 EBITDA multiple — triggering skepticism about overpaying for grid-to-chip expansion (moneycheck 9/2026; trefis 9/23/2026). Also late-Sept: King Environmental Services acquisition; Massachusetts data-center permitting noise; sector rotation on elevated Treasury yields (tickeron; transcriptdaily 9/30/2026).
- Note the frame: even after the 34% drawdown the stock is up ~56% YTD — this is a runaway winner consolidating, not a secular loser.
- **Verdict: TRANSITORY.** Raised FY26 guidance (~$14.0B sales, adj EPS $6.70 mid) on the same call that sold off; the drawdown is execution-timing + acquisition-pricing sentiment on a $95B AI-infrastructure name, not a demand break.

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 9/10
Moat: #1 thermal/power for data centers, NVIDIA co-development (DGX GB300 rack at Naval Postgraduate School as reference architecture), liquid-cooling leadership, $15B+ project backlog, global service footprint with recurring revenue, capital intensity (Padua chiller capacity doubling by end-2026). AI is the *demand driver*, not the disruptor — Vertiv literally sells the picks (power, thermal) for the AI buildout. Disruption risk: a radical shift in rack thermal architecture away from liquid cooling is the only credible threat, and Vertiv leads that shift itself. 9/10.

### 3. Rebound catalyst
Q3 2026 earnings 10/28/2026 (confirmed per tipranks; est ~$1.81–1.83 EPS, +46% y/y) — the decisive test: management guided Q3 sales $3.65–3.85B (+40% y/y); a clean print would prove the Q2 delays were timing, as management claims, and the Trefis back-test notes VRT was higher 12 months after 7 of 9 past ≥20% dips (median +92%). Longer arc: management targets 20–22% annual revenue growth through 2030 with adj op margins >27% (tickeron); hyperscaler 2026 capex ~$650B keeps the demand backdrop extreme.

### 4. Balance-sheet survival
Strong and self-funding. Q2: operating cash flow +241% y/y to $1.1B, FCF +234% to $925M (>150% conversion); $5.6B liquidity at Q2 end, funding the $1.45B UIG deal in cash (fool 9/29/2026). Adjusted operating margin 22.6% (+410bp y/y). No distress risk — the concern is only whether acquisitions dilute the multiple, not solvency.

### 5. Valuation
Forward P/E: FY26 consensus adj EPS $6.73 → ~37x at $252 (MarketBeat); FY27 est $8.93 → ~28x. Trefis: 54.6x trailing, ~2.5x the S&P's 21.4x; PEG ~1.0–1.5 (ainvest 5/2026; Khouw notes PEG <1 on forward est). Analyst consensus: Moderate/Strong Buy, 28 analysts, mean target $357.83 (+42% from $252; range $260–$500) (MarketBeat 9/16/2026; similar $355.42 stockanalysis 8/2026). Verdict: **NOT cheap — derated but still premium.** The dip made it reasonable-for-the-growth (PEG ~1) but this is a priced-for-execution stock; any further revenue softness re-derates it again.

### 6. Category position
Clear leader in data-center power/cooling — $15B backlog (up 109% y/y at Q4'25), grid-to-chip strategy expanding the TAM. The dip is valuation/execution, not share loss.

### 7. Value-trap risk
The real bear case (Trefis 10/2/2026: "Bull $375, base $355, bear $180"): if hyperscaler AI capex is pulled forward and the 2027–28 pipeline normalizes, a 37x-forward multiple on decelerating growth compresses hard. Acquisition indigestion (UIG at 13x projected EBITDA with no disclosed financials) could also destroy capital if the thesis is wrong. If Q3 revenue misses again, the "minor timing" story dies.

### 8. Rebound score: 7/10
**Verdict: AI-infrastructure leader with a $15B backlog and a derated-but-still-premium multiple; Q3's 10/28 print is the make-or-break — a clean beat validates the "minor timing delays" thesis and unlocks +40% analyst upside, but the margin for error at 37x forward is zero.**

**EARNINGS-QUALITY FLAG — INVENTORY_BUILD: plausibly benign, watch Q3.** Per the Q2 2026 PR cash-flow statement (PR Newswire 7/29/2026): inventories were a $663.8M use of cash in Q2'26 vs $8.9M in Q2'25, and $1.048B in H1'26 vs $137.5M in H1'25 — far outpacing the 24% revenue growth. The demand-warning counter: if Q3 revenue again disappoints while inventory keeps climbing, it reads as overstocking into slowing conversion. One-line verdict: consistent with build-ahead-for-backlog plus supply-chain/tariff pre-positioning — flag clears only if Q3 sales land near the $3.65–3.85B guide.

### 1. Why it's down

- CEO Lee Shavel sold 3,535 shares monthly (7/29, 8/3, 9/1, 10/1/2026) — routine, small, but optically unhelpful (insidertrades 10/3/2026).
- **Verdict: MOSTLY TRANSITORY, partially structural.** The derate reflects a genuine growth deceleration (5.8% organic, margin pressure) being repriced; but the business still grows, beats, and compounds — this is a multiple reset from ~40x to ~20x, not a broken franchise.

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 8/10
Moat: near-monopoly data utility — ISO is the licensed statistical agent in all 50 U.S. states, standardizing policy language and rate filings for essentially every P&C insurer; 39B statistical records, 143M properties; 90%+ retention, ~95% recurring revenue (fool 5/30/2026; pricediscoveries). AI threat: the live debate (Fool: "Will AI Commoditize It?") — insurers could build in-house AI analytics; but regulatory submissions require auditable standardized data, which is Verisk's specialty, and complex cat modeling isn't easily replicated. Verisk is embedding AI itself (GenAI underwriting assistant, XactAI computer-vision claims, Synergy Studio cloud platform 2026). Competitors (Moody's RMS, CoreLogic, CCCS) are niche-specific, not franchise-threatening. 8/10 — the regulatory/standardization lock-in is the moat AI can't route around.

### 3. Rebound catalyst
Q3 2026 earnings 11/5/2026 (company PR via globenewswire 10/1/2026) — another beat-and-hold on the FY26 guide ($7.45–7.75 adj EPS) with organic growth re-accelerating would challenge the derate. Capital-return machine: $1.9B in buybacks in H1 2026 alone (ASR at $186.32 avg — above today's $164, ouch) plus $0.50/qtr dividend; buybacks shrinking the float while the multiple is 20x is self-healing. Medium-term: catastrophe-model demand from climate volatility is a structural tailwind.

### 4. Balance-sheet survival
Stretched but manageable. $4.47B total debt at 6/30/2026 (debt-to-equity ~10.4 on MarketBeat's thin-equity basis — a quirk of Verisk's negative-equity-heavy capital structure from buybacks); cash fell to $551M at 6/30 from $2.18B at YE2025 after the $1.9B H1 buyback program (zacks 8/28/2026). Net interest $52.8M/qtr vs $464M adjusted EBITDA — ~4.5% coverage cost, comfortable. Q2 operating cash flow +49.7% y/y to $366M, FCF $298M (+58% y/y). No distress risk, but the buyback-before-leverage discipline deserves monitoring — leverage up, cash down, growth slowing is the wrong combination to persist.

### 5. Valuation
Forward P/E ~20.5x (stockstory 10/2/2026) — a huge compression from Verisk's historical 30–40x premium-compounder multiple. Analyst consensus: Moderate Buy, mean target $226.71 (+38% from $164; 1 Strong Buy, 6 Buys, 8 Holds — MarketBeat 9–10/2026); Barclays cut to $225 (9/8), UBS raised to $235 (7/30). EPS guide FY26 $7.45–7.75 → ~22x the midpoint — **cheaper than its own history, fairly priced for 5–8% growth; cheap only if organic growth re-accelerates to high-single digits**. Not a screaming bargain on absolute terms.

### 6. Category position
Still #1 — the insurance industry's data utility; no evidence of share loss, just slower end-market demand (lower claims activity) and tougher comps. Pricing power intact (annual price increases in forms/rules/loss-cost services per Q2 results).

### 7. Value-trap risk
The bear case: Verisk is a former compounder permanently re-rated to a data-utility multiple — 5% organic growth with 8% EPS growth deserves ~15–18x, not a rebound to $227. The debt-funded buyback at $186 looks value-destructive if growth doesn't re-accelerate, and AI-assisted in-house insurer analytics could slowly erode the data premium over years. This is the one name in the batch where the "never recovers" scenario is most plausible, because the derate has a structural-growth component.

### 8. Rebound score: 5/10
**Verdict: A genuine data-utility moat (8/10) derated from compounder to utility multiple on slowing growth — 20x forward with +38% analyst upside is reasonable, but the 11/5 print must show organic re-acceleration or this grinds sideways as a value trap; weakest rebound conviction of the four.**
