# Ballot B1 — Panelist B (growth/quality compounder) — RUN_DATE 2026-11-07

Lens: revenue durability, margins, returns on capital, and whether the dip has broken the growth story. A great business bought on sale — not the cheapest price, and not a broken business at a low multiple.

## Single-pick ballot

- **Top pick:** ANET
- **Runner-up:** APH
- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** Arista is the cleanest quality compounder in this field, bought after a violent multiple compression rather than a business break. Q3 2026 revenue grew 58.5% YoY — the fastest since the 2014 IPO — with non-GAAP operating margin of 49.1%, and management raised FY2026 revenue guidance for the fourth time and gave a first FY2027 outlook of about \$18.5bn, up 38–40%, with AI networking revenue doubling to about \$7bn. The balance sheet is fortress-grade: \$14.36bn cash, zero debt, and roughly \$5.0–5.2bn of trailing free cash flow. At \$170.80, down 26.6% from its high on a screen forward P/E of 28.3x against 38–40% guided growth, the dip prices a good business as if the AI buildout had already stalled — while its own order book says the opposite.
- **Why the dip is temporary (not permanent):** The single most compelling evidence is that the drawdown followed a blowout print, not a miss: Q3 beat its own \$3.3bn guide by more than \$350m, GAAP net income nearly doubled, and FY2027 guidance came in well above the \$16.54bn consensus — then the stock fell on post-midterm data-center moratorium fears and Nvidia Spectrum-X anxiety, with no law enacted and no hyperscaler capex cut evidenced. Friday's +7.31% reclaim of the 200-day SMA (\$165.79) is the first technical confirmation that the tape, not the business, was the problem.
- **Moat / AI-irreplaceability:** Low risk. EOS — one operating system across cloud/AI fabrics, campus and routing — plus hyperscaler qualification lock-in and a telemetry/software stack on top of Broadcom silicon is the moat, not the chip. AI is the demand driver, not the disruptor: every GPU cluster needs high-speed Ethernet, and Arista's 800G switches are the plumbing. The genuine threats (Nvidia bundling Spectrum-X, Cisco, white-box/SONiC) attack the hardware layer, not the installed software base, and hyperscalers diversifying away from a single vendor actually helps Arista.
- **Rebound catalyst & timing:** The Q4 print in February 2027 against the \$3.9–4.0bn revenue guide and confirmation of the ~\$18.5bn FY2027 outlook is the hard checkpoint; nearer term, stabilisation of the AI tape plus the already-reclaimed 200-day does the work over the next 1–3 months. AI networking doubling to ~\$7bn in 2027 is the compounding leg behind it.
- **12-18 month return scenario:** Base case roughly +40–45% toward the \$240–245 analyst consensus as the FY2027 guide is delivered and the multiple holds near 28–30x forward earnings. Bull case roughly +60–65% toward the \$280 Rosenblatt target if AI networking beats the doubling target and hyperscaler capex holds through 2027.
- **Top risk (value-trap case):** Hyperscaler concentration: a handful of cloud titans drive the order book, so a real data-center permitting freeze or capex pause would decelerate a +58% grower violently, and a ~28–30x forward multiple (39.2x on FinanceCharts' lower EPS base) would compress at the same time earnings estimates fall. Non-GAAP gross margin already slipped to 64.0% from 65.2% a year ago on cloud-titan mix and memory costs — an early sign that mix, not just sentiment, can pressure profitability.
- **Conviction (1-10):** 8

### Top 3 — the single most compelling data point each, and scenarios

**1. ANET.** The temporary-dip evidence and the moat are the same fact: Q3 revenue +58.5% YoY with a raised FY2027 guide to ~\$18.5bn (+38–40%) was sold off on politics, and the EOS software lock-in plus zero debt means there is nothing to fix in the business — only the tape to heal, which Friday's reclaim of the 200-day SMA has started. Base case +40–45% toward \$240–245; bull case +60–65% toward \$280 if AI networking beats its doubling target.

**2. APH.** The single most compelling point is the order book: Q3 orders of \$12.2bn (+99% YoY, 1.19x book-to-bill) behind record sales of \$10.23bn (+65% YoY, +37% organic) and a 29.7% adjusted operating margin, at ~20x forward — the cheapest forward multiple in this batch relative to growth, with a \$4bn buyback and a 20% dividend raise as a floor. **3. Base case +60–85% toward the \$321–339 analyst targets as backlog converts through 2027; bull case higher if political noise decays and Q4 confirms execution keeps pace with the order book.
