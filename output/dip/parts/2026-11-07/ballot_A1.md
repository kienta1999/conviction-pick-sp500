## SINGLE-PICK BALLOT

- **Top pick:** MU
- **Runner-up:** VRT
- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** Micron is the fastest re-rate in this field because its catalyst is dated and near — the FY2027 Q1 print expected around 16 December, guided to a seventh straight record quarter (revenue \$61.5bn ± \$1.5bn, non-GAAP EPS \$37.15–\$39.15) — and the stock still sits 45% below its high at a 3.7x forward P/E after a two-day political rout, not a business break. The dip was driven by midterm/data-center moratorium fear and peak-cycle anxiety, while the operating facts moved the other way: fiscal Q4 revenue \$54.23bn (+379% YoY), 87% gross margin, and customers showing no sign of cancelling chip supply contracts. With over 75% of FY2027 output committed under strategic agreements, including ~\$100bn of minimum-price contracted revenue with price floors, a December beat re-prices a stock the market has priced as if the "E" is already gone.
- **Why the dip is temporary (not permanent):** The single most compelling evidence is that demand is sold out, not shrinking: SK Hynix has sold out its planned HBM and DRAM output through 2029, Samsung and SK Hynix reported customers showing no sign of cancelling contracts or trimming order volumes, and Micron's own customers are prepaying under take-or-pay agreements (\$12.75bn in customer deposits). A permanent impairment would show up as cancelled orders and falling prices; the dossier shows the opposite — contract prices for 16Gb DDR5 rose from \$8.70 in October 2025 to ~\$48 in September 2026, with supply/demand not expected to balance until H2 2028 at the earliest.
- **Moat / AI-irreplaceability:** Low risk. Micron is one of only three HBM makers on earth, HBM is physically co-packaged with every AI accelerator and must be qualified per platform, and AI cannot route around memory — memory is the bottleneck. The deduction is that it is still a commodity-cycle business at heart (DRAM ~73% of revenue) and the \#3 HBM player, losing HBM share to Samsung (18%, down from 21%) with a smaller-than-hoped HBM4 allocation at Nvidia flagged on 6 November — a share-mix warning, not a moat break.
- **Rebound catalyst & timing:** FY2027 Q1 earnings expected ~16 December 2026 (Wall Street Horizon estimate; not yet confirmed by the company) — a beat or reaffirmation of the \$61.5bn revenue guide is the first clean trigger, roughly five weeks out. The second leg is political-noise decay over 1–2 quarters plus HBM4 ramp (already >\$1bn of HBM4 revenue booked, ramping ~2x as fast as HBM3E).
- **12-18 month return scenario:** Base case roughly +25–40% — a partial re-rating toward Seeking Alpha's \$873 base case (+26.5% vs the \$690 close) as the December print confirms the "E" is real and the multiple normalises only modestly from peak-cycle pricing. Bull case roughly +100% or more — toward the MarketBeat consensus of \$1,407.88 (+104%) if HBM pricing holds through 2027, the contracted book converts, and the market stops pricing a cycle peak.
- **Top risk (value-trap case):** The classic memory-cycle trap: the 3.7x forward P/E is the bait. If hyperscaler capex stalls, HBM/DRAM prices fall, the \$100bn of minimum-price contracts get renegotiated or volumes cut, and FY2027 EPS estimates get cut 30%+ — the "E" collapses and 3.7x becomes 15x overnight. Added capacity (CXMT, Samsung HBM4, Micron's own six new plants) eventually creates oversupply, as memory has done every cycle for 40 years, and Seeking Alpha explicitly flags oversupply risk post-2028.
- **Conviction (1-10):** 7
