## 1. SINGLE-PICK BALLOT

- Top pick: KLAC
- Runner-up: AMAT
- Thesis (3-5 sentences): KLA is the purest quality compounder in the 14: a near-monopoly in semiconductor process control (91% of Q1 FY2027 revenue) with a 42.6% operating margin, 91% ROE, and record Q1 FY2027 revenue of $4.14bn (+29% YoY) with non-GAAP EPS beating consensus — yet the stock is −49.5% off its 52-week high after a −13.3% fall on 5 Nov with no company news. The market de-rated the multiple from 48.4× TTM earnings at end-September to 34.4× by 6 Nov while earnings were still rising — the classic transitory-dip signature. Management then guided the December quarter to $4.7bn revenue (+14% sequentially) and raised the CY2026 WFE outlook to ~mid-$150bn, expecting it to exceed $200bn in CY2027, with a $12.6bn backlog (+60% vs FY2025). At a screen forward P/E of ~21.5× (other vendors quote 25.9–27.9× on different EPS bases — do not mix bases) for ~29% revenue growth and a 46% non-GAAP operating margin, the June excess has been removed without the business breaking.
- Why the dip is temporary (not permanent): the single most compelling evidence the drop is about price, not the business: KLA reported a record, beat-and-raise quarter on 28 Oct (revenue +29% YoY, GAAP net income +38% YoY, TTM net income $5.26bn vs $4.24bn a year earlier) and the shares rallied, then gave it all back and more on 5 Nov on midterm / data-center politics and AI-capex fear — with no KLAC disclosure, no lost customer, no missed quarter and no guidance cut. Earnings went up while the price fell by half from the June peak; that is multiple compression, not impairment.
- Moat / AI-irreplaceability: low risk. AI is a demand driver, not a substitute: smaller nodes, 3D packaging and HBM raise defect sensitivity, so inspection intensity per wafer rises, not falls. No AI model can physically inspect a wafer, and KLA's AI sits inside its own tools (defect classification), strengthening the product. Switching costs are extreme — inspection recipes are qualified fab-by-fab and process-by-process, a missed defect at 2nm / in HBM stacks costs far more than the tool — and the installed-base service annuity plus co-development with TSMC/Samsung/Micron-class customers lock the franchise in. Dossier moat score 9/10; the one point off is customer concentration and China at high-20s% of CY2026 revenue under export-control exposure.
- Rebound catalyst & timing: the December-quarter print in late Jan / early Feb 2027 (exact date not confirmed — sources disagree between 28 Jan and 4 Feb) testing the $4.7bn revenue / $1.44 non-GAAP EPS guide; a nearer trigger is NVDA's Q3 FY2027 print on Tue 17 Nov, which either validates or challenges the AI-capex narrative, plus any calming of post-midterm data-center political noise. The $8.81bn buyback authorisation remaining at 30 Sep (Q1 buybacks $936m, second-highest quarterly total on record) is a floor under the stock meanwhile.
- 12-18 month return scenario: rough base +35–45% (multiple stabilises around current forward levels and the guided earnings growth — EPS expected +22.3% next year per MarketBeat — does the work; mean analyst target $219.62 vs $152.32 close, +44%, though targets were largely set before the 5 Nov collapse and may be stale); bull +60–80% if the >$200bn CY2027 WFE call converts, memory mix stays strong, and the multiple re-rates part-way back toward its September level on delivered $4.7bn quarters.
- Top risk (value-trap case): a genuine WFE air-pocket in 2027 — if hyperscaler/memory capex was pulled forward and then pauses, or the >$200bn CY2027 WFE call proves optimistic, KLA's 91%-process-control revenue is the most cyclically exposed of the WFE names, and memory (40% of the December-quarter mix) is the most volatile end-market. The falling-knife version: WFE peaks in H1 2027, orders roll, and the 34× TTM multiple compresses toward a mid-cycle ~20× on falling earnings — multiple and E falling together. China export controls are a live, unquantifiable overhang on ~high-20s% of revenue.
- Conviction (1-10): 8

**2. Rough 12–18mo: base +45–55% (toward the ~$589–605 mean/median targets, +59–63%, discounted for stale targets); bull +75–100% if the >30% WFE growth call converts to the ~$18.56 FY2027 EPS the screen multiple implies.

**3. HWM** — The most compelling data point is a 4-player oligopoly with certification lock-in (single-crystal turbine blades, 60–90-week production, >50% of industrial gas-turbine blades) whose Q3 2026 accelerated anyway — revenue $2.703bn, +29% YoY (strongest quarterly growth on record), adjusted EPS +54%, EBITDA margin 32.5% (+310bp) — while the stock fell ~30% in 90 days on SpaceX/GE-CPP narratives that cannot touch 2026–27 earnings (SpaceX turbine revenue ~4 years away per Jefferies; SpaceX is not a disclosed Howmet customer). AI is a demand tailwind here: gas turbines are the bottleneck for AI data-center power, with GE Vernova's backlog sold out through 2030. Rough 12–18mo: base +25–35% (earnings compounding — FY2027 revenue guided ~$11.5bn, +~12% — with the multiple roughly holding; mean target $296.29, +34%); bull +50–60% if guidance delivery fades the narrative overhang and the multiple re-expands toward its August level. The cap on this pick is valuation: at ~33.2× forward earnings it is above its own 5-year median P/E, so the margin of safety is growth delivery, not price.
