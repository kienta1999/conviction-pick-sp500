## INTU — Intuit Inc.

**Screen snapshot (13 Nov 2026 close):** −48.8% off 52w high, −1.8% vs 200d SMA, fwd PE ~12.7, op margin 28.8%, rev growth +9.4%, analyst upside +19.4%. Price $341.70 (Finnhub, 13 Nov close); 52w range $252.84–$682.27.

### 1. Why it's down — temporary vs permanent
The drawdown is a two-act story, and only the second act has real operating damage.

- **Act 1 — AI de-rating, Jan–Apr 2026 (sentiment, no bad numbers).** Shares fell from ~$662 at end-2025 to $362 on 9 Apr 2026 "without a single disappointing Intuit result — the company beat and reiterated its guidance in November and February" (Dividend Line, Oct 2026). The trigger was agentic AI: Anthropic's Claude Cowork and related tools in early 2026 made investors ask whether AI agents that read a bank statement, categorise a transaction or fill in a tax form would make seat-based/form-filling software redundant (Million Leaf, 9 Nov 2026; Dividend Line).
- **Act 2 — real operating disappointments, May–Aug 2026.** On 20 May 2026 Intuit reported fiscal Q3 revenue +10% to $8.6bn and non-GAAP EPS $12.80 vs $12.57 expected — a beat — but simultaneously cut ~17% of full-time staff (~3,000 roles), cut full-year TurboTax growth guidance from 8% to 7%, and flagged weakness among low-end DIY filers. The stock fell ~20% that day, its worst since 2003 (AInvest, Aug 2026; Dividend Line). A week later Goldman Sachs downgraded, cutting its target to $276 from $519, arguing AI-powered tax services would erode TurboTax. Securities class actions / an investigation into TurboTax DIY pricing during the 2026 season followed.
- **Fundamentals underneath:** FY2026 (ended 31 Jul 2026) revenue +14% to $21.45bn, operating income +20% to $5.88bn, diluted EPS +20% to $16.46 (Million Leaf). But TurboTax filing units fell 39.9m → 39.0m (−2%); online units 35.5m → 34.9m. On 25 Aug 2026 Intuit guided FY2027 revenue growth of only 9–10% (vs 14% in FY2026), TurboTax +2–3%, Mailchimp −1% to flat — and reaffirmed that guide at Investor Day, 17 Sep 2026 (Intuit IR press release). Management admitted in plain words "We lost on price" in TurboTax (empor.top summary of FY2026).
- **Verdict: MIXED — mostly transitory de-rating + one genuinely impaired segment.** The multiple collapse (see §5) is sentiment-driven and looks overdone given FY2026 still grew 14% with 20% EPS growth. The permanent-looking damage is concentrated in low-end DIY TurboTax (price-sensitive filers lost to cheaper/free rivals) and Mailchimp ($12bn acquisition, now guided to shrink). QuickBooks/GBS is not impaired. The dip is not a single one-off miss; it is a growth-reset + AI-narrative repricing with a real, but narrow, operating wound.

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — score 6/10
- **Moat (QuickBooks side, strong):** the ledger is the system of record for millions of US small businesses — invoices, payroll, payments, bank-transaction history and the accountant relationship sit on it. A substitute must replicate the ledger, payments history and accountant workflow — a high bar (empor.top). GBS ex-Mailchimp grew 18% in FY2026; QuickBooks Online accounting revenue +23%; Credit Karma +20% in FY2026. Switching costs and data-network effects (years of categorised transactions) are real.
- **Moat (TurboTax side, weakening):** for a simple W-2 return, an AI agent is a credible substitute — Goldman named Perplexity Tax, Chime Tax and Prime Meridian as emerging competition (AInvest). TurboTax is ~25% of revenue and operating income, per Goldman. The DIY sliver is exactly the part Intuit has spent two years migrating customers out of, toward assisted/done-for-you products where AI is monetised rather than given away. Direct File (free government filing) has been shut down, removing a government-backed competitor.
- **AI posture:** Intuit is embedding AI (Intuit Assist, partnerships with OpenAI and Anthropic putting its tools inside chatbots) rather than being bypassed — but the market has not yet credited it. Score 6: the QuickBooks franchise is hard to route around; the low-end TurboTax franchise is genuinely exposed.

## HWM — Howmet Aerospace Inc.

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — score 9/10
- **Moat: process technology + certification lock-in + capital intensity, arguably the widest in the batch.** Single-crystal vacuum-cast turbine blades/vanes run at 3,000–3,600°F — hotter than their own melting point — take 60–90 weeks to produce, and at industrial scale only four companies worldwide can make them: Howmet, Berkshire's Precision Castparts, CPP, and Doncasters (AInvest/Turbomachinery). Howmet alone holds >50% of turbine blades for industrial gas turbines and a technological edge in proprietary single-crystal casting vs Precision Castparts. A set of 40 blades costs >$600,000. Qualification takes decades; engine OEMs cannot swap a casting supplier without re-certifying flight-critical parts.
- **AI/substitute threat: essentially nil as a substitute; AI is a demand tailwind.** AI data centres need power; gas turbines are the bottleneck (GE Vernova backlog $176bn / 116 GW, sold out through 2030; Siemens Energy 69 GW backlog, 3+ year lead times; Mitsubishi 35 GW). Every heavy-frame turbine ordered in 18 months is sold out. AI increases demand for Howmet's product; it does not replace casting.
- **The SpaceX caveat, kept in proportion:** Jefferies pegs the gas-turbine engine-parts market at ~$10bn (2025) and SpaceX turbine revenue ~4 years away (12 months negotiations + 24 months industrialisation + 24 months ramp/yields). SpaceX is not a disclosed Howmet customer — its foundry would serve its own/xAI data-centre power needs — so the 7.5% drop was "a narrative trade, not a revenue event". GE's CPP deal is about capacity assurance for GE's own engines, priced at a multiple in line with Howmet's own trading multiple. Neither threatens 2026–27 earnings.
- **Score 9:** certification-locked, 4-player oligopoly, 60–90-week production — AI cannot print a single-crystal blade. One point off only for customer concentration/vertical-integration optionality over a 4+ year horizon.

- **Watch item:** if Q2 revenue disappoints while inventory keeps rising, the benign read flips — $17bn of purchase commitments becomes a writedown/obsolescence risk if hyperscaler orders slow. The flag is a monitor, not a veto, at this print.

## TPR — Tapestry, Inc.

**Screen snapshot (13 Nov 2026 close):** −19.3% off 52w high, −3.1% vs 200d SMA, fwd PE ~14.4, op margin 23.7%, rev growth +10.9%, analyst upside +23.5%, net debt/EBITDA 1.65x. Price $132.41 (Finnhub, 13 Nov close); 52w range $99.01–$164.80 (high 10 Aug 2026).

### 1. Why it's down — temporary vs permanent
- **The drawdown is a growth-normalisation repricing after a blow-off top, not a business decline.** TPR hit its 52w high $164.80 on 10 Aug 2026, then fell >14% intraday on 13 Aug 2026 after fiscal Q4 2026 results: revenue +9% to $1.88bn and adjusted EPS +28% to $1.32 (beat $1.28) — but FY2027 guidance (revenue $8.4–8.5bn, EPS $7.80–7.90) came in with a revenue midpoint slightly below the $8.464bn consensus, and management admitted Kate Spade's recovery "is taking longer" / "top-line progress was more gradual than we planned" (CEO Joanne Crevoiserat, via Benzinga/Webull; TickerOn). The stock settled into a $110–120 range through mid-October before recovering to ~$132.
- **The drivers, in order:** (i) **growth deceleration** — FY2026 was a record year (revenue ~$8bn, +17% pro forma constant-currency, adj. EPS $7.05, +38%, operating margin +340bp to >23%) and FY2027 guidance implied mid-single-digit revenue growth — "a sharp deceleration from fiscal 2026's 14% top-line surge" (TickerOn); (ii) **Kate Spade** — Q4 revenue −7% to $235.1m with an adjusted operating loss of $28.9m, and FY2027 guidance of another high-single-digit decline and a modest operating loss; (iii) **tariffs** — FY2027 outlook assumes a mid-20% tariff rate on US imports, with margin expansion dependent on mitigation holding the net tariff impact neutral; (iv) **the blocked $8.5bn Capri merger** (mutually terminated) removed the inorganic-growth story, leaving a single-brand-concentration story.
- **Note the stock has already partially recovered on new news:** Q1 FY2027 (5 Nov 2026) beat and raised guidance (see §3), and the stock rose ~12% premarket that day — the −19.3% off-high snapshot already reflects that bounce. The 1-month return to 13 Nov was +10.6% (Finnhub).
- **Verdict: TRANSITORY (growth-normalisation + one weak brand).** Coach, ~86–87% of revenue, is still growing double-digits. The dip prices FY2026's exceptional year as the new normal being taken away — a sentiment/growth-rate reset, not a broken franchise.

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — score 8/10
- **Moat: brand + pricing power in accessible luxury.** Coach gross margins are ~78–80% (non-GAAP gross margin 78.7% in Q1 FY2027, +220bp YoY); handbag average unit retail (AUR) growing at a low-double-digit rate in the $200–500 price band, with unit growth low-single-digits — i.e. growth from price, not discounting (Fool transcript, 5 Nov 2026). Coach added 2.1m new customers in Q1 FY2027, 40% Gen Z — the brand is recruiting the next generation, not ageing out. TTM gross margin 77.1% vs industry average 41.6%; operating margin 23.7% vs industry ~norm (AInvest).
- **AI/substitute threat: minimal.** AI cannot manufacture brand desire, heritage or the social signal of a Coach bag; there is no AI substitute for a luxury handbag. The disruption risk is fashion cyclicality and consumer discretionary spending, not technology. E-commerce/AI shopping assistants could pressure distribution economics at the margin, but they do not commoditise the product.
- **Score 8:** brand moats in luxury are among the most AI-proof assets there are; one point off for Kate Spade (a brand that has lost its moat — see §6/§7) and for Coach concentration.

### 3. Rebound catalyst
- **Already delivered (5 Nov 2026):** Q1 FY2027 revenue $1.894bn (+11% reported, +12% pro forma), non-GAAP EPS $1.82 (+32%, ~$0.27 above company guidance and ~$0.26 above consensus), non-GAAP gross margin 78.7% (+220bp: +130bp operational, +70bp lower tariffs). Coach +16% constant-currency to $1.66bn; Kate Spade −10% to $233m, in line with guidance, gross margin +190bp. Management **raised FY2027 guidance**: revenue $8.5–8.6bn (from $8.4–8.5bn), EPS $8.25–8.35 (from $7.80–7.90, +17–18% YoY), operating margin >24% (+~100bp) (Fool transcript; Quantisnow; Middle News, 5 Nov 2026).
- **Capital return:** buyback plan raised $350m to $1.7bn for FY2027 ($600m already done in Q1); total FY2027 capital returns ~$2.0bn including dividends; dividend raised 16% to $1.85 annualised.
- **Next checkpoints:** Q2 FY2027 (holiday quarter) expected ~3 Feb 2027 (consensus EPS ~$3.10 on $2.69bn revenue, per Finnhub) — the holiday print is the real test of Coach momentum; Kate Spade stabilisation (any quarter where the decline narrows) removes the conglomerate discount.
- **Timing:** the rebound is already underway (+10.6% in the month to 13 Nov); the next leg needs the holiday print, not a distant cycle turn.

### 4. Balance-sheet survival
Solid, with a buyback-funded-by-cash-flow nuance. Ended Q1 FY2027 with $782m cash/short-term investments and ~$2.56bn total borrowings — net debt ~$1.8bn; gross debt/adjusted EBITDA 1.1x, "more than a full turn below" the long-term target of <2.5x (Fool transcript, 5 Nov 2026). (Screen net debt/EBITDA 1.65x uses a different — likely TTM/net — definition; the company's own 1.1x gross leverage is the cleaner read.) Operating cash flow $197m in Q1 (vs $113m prior year); adjusted FCF $56m in Q1 (vs $103m) — seasonally weak quarter, with full-year adjusted FCF guidance ~$1.7bn unchanged. **No distress risk; leverage is low. Watch item:** Q1 buybacks ($600m) were ~10.7x Q1 adjusted FCF ($56m) — the buyback is funded from full-year FCF and balance-sheet capacity, not Q1 cash, but it leaves little slack if holiday FCF disappoints (Quantisnow, 8 Nov 2026). One data vendor's "total debt/equity 1236%" (AInvest) is an artefact of a small equity base after buybacks — ignore it in favour of the 1.1x EBITDA leverage.

### 5. Margin of safety / valuation
- **Moderately cheap, not a steal.** Fwd PE ~14.4 (screen); trailing PE ~16.2x (Finnhub, 13 Nov). At the raised FY2027 EPS guide ($8.25–8.35 midpoint $8.30), the 13 Nov price $132.41 is ~16.0x forward guide EPS. That is below luxury peers and below its own level at the August peak, but Tapestry has historically traded ~15–20x — it is at the low end of its own band, not far below it.
- **Analysts:** mean target $162.27, +22.3% upside (AInvest, 13 Nov 2026); screen upside +23.5%; consensus skews Buy (UBS upgraded Neutral → Buy; JPMorgan, Barclays, Telsey, TD Cowen Buy), with targets clustering $165–175 on one source (TickerOn) but a wide range overall (below $100 to above $230; Raymond James cut $165 → $140 on growth-deceleration caution). Expected EPS growth ~17.9% for FY2027 supports the multiple.
- **Verdict:** fairly priced for a normalising grower with a 16% dividend hike and a $1.7bn buyback — the margin of safety is the cash return and the 78% gross margin, not a distressed multiple. It is cheap relative to the August euphoria, not cheap in absolute terms.

### 6. Category position
Coach is #1 in its accessible-luxury handbag niche with demonstrated pricing power (AUR +low-double-digits, 2.1m new customers/quarter, Greater China +41% and Europe +27% in Q1 FY2027). But Tapestry is a one-brand company in practice: Coach is ~86–87% of revenue and essentially all of the growth; Kate Spade is a declining, loss-making drag (FY2027: high-single-digit decline, modest operating loss). The dip is not Coach losing share — it is the market discounting a portfolio whose second brand is broken and whose growth rate is normalising.

### 7. Value-trap risk (bear case)
The trap: Coach's momentum is a fashion cycle, not a moat — Gen Z's Tabby-bag enthusiasm fades, AUR growth stalls, and the brand gives back the FY2026 gains; Kate Spade never stabilises and consumes management attention and capital; tariffs rise above the assumed low-to-mid-20% rate or mitigation fails, squeezing the 78% gross margin; China demand (Q1 +41%) reverses; and the $1.7bn buyback at ~16x proves to have been done at the top of the cycle. A consumer-discretionary slowdown into the holiday quarter would hit both revenue and the multiple simultaneously. The August–October range ($110–120) is the downside marker — ~10–17% below the 13 Nov close.

### 8. Rebound score: 7/10
**Verdict:** The rebound is already half-delivered — a beat-and-raise, a raised buyback and a 16% dividend hike — and Coach's brand momentum with Gen Z is real; the score is capped because Kate Spade is still bleeding and the stock is only moderately cheap after its bounce.
