## Single-pick ballot

- **Top pick:** LRCX
- **Runner-up:** HWM
- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** Lam is the etch-and-deposition leader at the physical heart of the AI buildout — every HBM stack, 3D NAND layer, and GAA transistor needs more etch/deposition steps per wafer, not fewer, so AI *increases* demand for Lam's product; there is no software substitute for physically etching a wafer. It just printed a record quarter on every line (revenue $8.22bn, +54% YoY; record 52.4% gross and 40.0% operating margins; record $3.24bn FCF) and fell 54% anyway, to 16.4x forward earnings with an $82% Street-implied upside. It is net cash (~$3.70bn), and management answered the rout with the largest buyback in company history — $25bn, ~10% of market value — plus a 27% dividend hike. This is a record-printing, irreplaceable franchise priced as if the cycle just ended.
- **Why the dip is temporary (not permanent):** the single most compelling evidence the drop is about price, not the business is the Q1 FY2027 print itself: record revenue, record margins, record FCF, EPS $2.20 beating consensus — reported 21 Oct, three weeks before the rout — while the December-quarter guide was capped by *supply* constraints (component lead times, cleanroom space), with CFO Doug Bettinger stating the guide "would have been a bit higher if not for some near-term supply chain constraints" and the shipments slipping to the March 2027 quarter, not lost. A company whose problem is that it cannot ship fast enough has not lost its moat.
- **Moat / AI-irreplaceability:** **Low risk.** AI cannot route around Lam — it is the demand. The moat is process-tech/patents + multi-year fab qualification lock-in + capital intensity: a wrong etch step scraps wafers, so TSMC, Samsung, SK hynix and Micron cannot swap a qualified etch fleet without re-qualifying the process. The credible threat is not AI but a CXMT-driven DRAM glut compressing memory capex — a demand/price-cycle risk, not a product-substitution risk — and CXMT disclosed no yields, qualifications, or volumes, and its product is commodity server DDR5, not HBM. The WFE-share slip belongs to AMAT (20.0% → 17.9%), not Lam; no share loss to Tokyo Electron or AMAT in etch was found in this pass.
- **Rebound catalyst & timing:** the $25bn buyback authorisation is firing now (the +8.2% Friday bounce was attributed to it) and provides a floor; the fundamental re-rate needs the Q2 FY27 print (~27 Jan 2027) showing the deferred December shipments landing in the March quarter, and/or confirmation of Lam's preliminary CY2027 WFE outlook of >$200bn. Buyback support is immediate; the re-rate is a 1–3 month story.
- **12-18 month return scenario:** Base: +50–70% (stock re-rates toward ~$300–340 as the WFE step-up and shipment recovery print; still short of the $364 mean target). Bull: +80–110% (toward $364–$420, i.e. the mean target and the UBS $400 level) if CY2027 WFE >$200bn materialises and DRAM capex holds.
- **Top risk (value-trap case):** the memory supercycle peaks and CXMT's 4F² scales at yield, flooding commodity DDR5 — memory prices fall, Samsung/SK hynix/Micron cut capex, and Lam's DRAM-led 2027 WFE >$200bn evaporates. The 16.4x forward P/E is only cheap if FY27 EPS ~$9.6 holds; a 20% EPS cut at 16x is a ~$155 stock, not a rebound. Data-center moratoriums becoming real law would hit Lam first and hardest (beta ~2.0).
- **Conviction (1-10):** 8
