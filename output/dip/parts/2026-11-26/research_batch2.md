## KEYS — Keysight Technologies ($267.69)

### 1. Why it's down — temporary vs permanent
KEYS is down on sentiment and multiple compression, not on its own numbers. It peaked near $388–$390 in early October 2026, then fell ~23% in 30 days ($341.35 on 21 Oct → $261.51 on 20 Nov 2026) as the AI trade unwound (TickerOn, Nov 2026). Then it reported the strongest quarter in its history on 19 Nov 2026 — Q4 FY26 revenue $1.994bn (+41% YoY), orders $2.339bn (+53%), non-GAAP EPS $3.61 vs $3.37 consensus, FY26 revenue $7.157bn (+33%), EPS $11.72 (+64%) — and still fell 7.07% the next session to $261.51, because the Nasdaq fell 5.28% and the S&P 500 3.70% the same day and analysts trimmed targets on AI-valuation recalibration (Morgan Stanley $425→$400, Wells Fargo $405→$350, Baird $430→$375, all keeping bullish ratings) (Finwire/TTM Financial, Nov 2026). The only company-specific negative in the print was supply: demand exceeds shipment capacity on ~12–24 AI-related SKUs, with $200–300m of backlog above normal levels and backlog duration extended by <2 weeks; shipments already rose from ~$1.4bn exiting FY25 to ~$2.0bn exiting FY26 (Q4 FY26 call, 19 Nov 2026). Earlier in the year there were real margin wobbles — Q1 FY26 (reported early Mar 2026) gross margin fell to 62.2% from 63.2% and operating margin to 15.5% from 16.5% on tariffs, Spirent amortisation and hiring — but Q4 operating margin expanded 950bp to 35.8%, so that pressure has reversed. A CFO transition (Jason Kary succeeding Neil Dougherty effective 18 Dec 2026, announced early Nov) added uncertainty.
**Verdict: TEMPORARY.** A valuation/sentiment unwind plus a capacity constraint on record demand — the classic "too much business" problem, not a demand break. The risk is that the AI-capex narrative, not Keysight's order book, sets the multiple.

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 8/10
Keysight is the largest pure-play electronic test & measurement vendor (Flow Partners) and, per Verified Market Research (Feb 2026), holds ~29.9% share of the electronic-measurement segment vs Rohde & Schwarz ~14.5% and NI (Emerson) ~11.2%. Moat: precision RF/microwave and high-speed digital test IP built on HP/Agilent lineage, deep calibration/certification lock-in (customers must validate to standards — 5G-Advanced, 6G, 1.6T optical — with instruments whose accuracy is itself certified), software workflow lock-in (PathWave), and switching costs (test programs, fixtures and correlation data are instrument-specific). Its Oct 2025 acquisition of Spirent for $1,564m in cash consolidated its closest rival in high-speed Ethernet, network-security and RF channel-emulator testing — so dominant that the US DOJ sued in June 2025 (Case 1:25-cv-01734) to block it on competition grounds; the deal closed with divestitures to VIAVI ($399m). AI does not replace test equipment — AI data centres *increase* the need for it (224G/1.6T optical validation launched Mar 2026; AI bookings ~2.5× prior year, contributing customers nearly doubled, Q4 FY26 call). Threat is cyclical (semi/telecom capex) and Chinese competition at the low end, not substitution.
**Irreplaceability: 8/10.**

### 3. Rebound catalyst
Already in motion: FY27 base-case guidance of ~20% revenue growth ($8.588bn vs $8.187bn consensus), Q1 FY27 revenue guidance $2.04–2.06bn vs $1.98bn consensus, book-to-bill 1.17 in Q4 (1.14 for FY26), record backlog, operating leverage guided at mid-to-upper-40% (above the 40% long-term target), and $275m FY27 capex to expand manufacturing capacity (Q4 FY26 call, 19 Nov 2026). Timing: backlog conversion as capacity comes online over FY27 H1; Q1 FY27 earnings (Feb 2027) are the next proof point. A stabilisation in the AI-hardware multiple would re-rate the stock faster than fundamentals alone.

### 4. Balance-sheet survival
Strong. Cash and equivalents $2.916bn at 31 Oct 2026; FY26 free cash flow $1.75bn (up from $1.28bn), Q4 FCF $465m; $667m of buybacks in FY26 ($150m in Q4 at ~$332/share — above the current price, a mild capital-allocation embarrassment, not distress), with $833m remaining on the authorisation (Q4 FY26 call). Net cash per the screen; no refinancing or dilution risk visible. It can easily endure the drawdown.

### 5. Margin of safety / valuation
Fwd PE 15.5 (screen) against FY26 non-GAAP EPS of $11.72 implies the market is pricing roughly $17+ of forward EPS — i.e. the multiple has collapsed from the ~48–60× trailing P/E quoted in Mar/Sep 2026 coverage (Ainvest, Sep 2026: trailing P/E 59.71) to ~15× forward on rapidly growing earnings. That is a genuine de-rating, not just a price fall on falling earnings — earnings are at records. Mean target $391.08 implies +46% upside. Peer context: test & measurement comps traded at a median 3.4× EV/Revenue in Oct 2026 (Flow Partners) — KEYS EV/revenue not verified in retrieved sources ("not found"). **Genuinely cheap on forward earnings if FY27 guidance holds; the cheapness is a multiple reset, and the multiple could stay reset if AI sentiment stays sour.**

### 6. Category position
#1 player, gaining share: the Spirent deal removed its closest competitor in three niche markets; FY26 orders +49% vs revenue +33% means share of wallet is growing. Communications Solutions Group revenue +51% in Q4. No evidence of share loss — the dip is a multiple event.

### 7. Value-trap risk
The bear case: (a) the AI-infrastructure capex cycle peaks and hyperscaler test budgets get cut — Keysight's 2.5× AI bookings growth reverses; (b) supply constraints persist longer than the guided <2-week backlog extension, pushing FY27 revenue below the 20% guide (management's strongest visibility is ~6 months, per Atlas Peak Research); (c) tariff refunds that flattered FY26 earnings do not recur; (d) the multiple never recovers because the market permanently re-rates AI-adjacent hardware. A falling knife if orders roll over — watch Q1 FY27 book-to-bill.

### 8. Rebound score: 8/10
**Verdict: Record orders, record backlog and 20% guided growth selling at ~15× forward earnings after a sentiment-driven 31% drawdown — the strongest rebound set-up in this batch, gated only by AI-multiple risk.**

#### Earnings-quality flag — RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN (receivables/revenue +0.177, inventory/revenue −0.340, CFO/NI 1.27, accrual −0.030)
**One-line verdict: Benign-leaning — receivables rose with a +41% revenue quarter and a record $2.339bn order book that shipped late in the quarter (supply-constrained AI SKUs), while CFO covered net income 1.27× and the accrual ratio is slightly negative, so cash is not lagging earnings; inventory falling relative to revenue corroborates a shipment backlog, not channel-stuffing — but confirm receivables normalise in Q1 FY27.**

## GS — Goldman Sachs ($801.03)

### 1. Why it's down — temporary vs permanent
A violent, sentiment-led unwind, not a Goldman-specific failure. GS fell 14% in a week to ~21 Nov 2026 and 6.7% on Fri 20 Nov alone (vs S&P 500 −3.7%), its fifth straight daily loss, closing at $790.69, near its 12-month low of $773.70 (20 Nov 2025); −23.3% over three months vs S&P 500 −9.5% (Trefis/Tikr, Nov 2026). Three drivers converged: (i) the broad AI sell-off — the S&P 500's worst losing streak since 2020, VIX above 31, compounded by post-midterm regulatory concerns and Nvidia executive upheaval (FX Leaders, 20 Nov 2026); (ii) Goldman-specific AI-financing headlines — on 20 Nov Goldman and Apollo priced ~$44bn of notes to fund SpaceX's Nvidia Vera Rubin GPU purchases (the largest GPU financing to date, oversubscribed and upsized), the same morning the FT reported SpaceX's lenders had balked at Musk's plans for the chip collateral; investors sold the bank most tied to financing the AI build-out, and asked how much SpaceX paper Goldman kept on its own books (Tikr, Nov 2026); (iii) macro repricing — hot October inflation/jobs data pushed markets to price possible Fed rate hikes by mid-2027, and Goldman Sachs Research itself warned 2027 global GDP growth is "tilted to the downside" with corporate earnings growth decelerating — a direct threat to the dealmaking/IPO pipeline that drove GS's rally (FX Leaders, 20 Nov 2026). Notably, Goldman's own news that week was good: ~$113m in fees each for Goldman and Morgan Stanley from leading Anthropic's IPO, and CEO David Solomon (CNBC, 9 Nov; TBPN podcast, 17 Nov) said AI-infrastructure financing demand remained strong and ~60% of revenues are now "more durable", with $15bn of asset/wealth management fees.
**Verdict: TEMPORARY (sentiment + AI-financing fear), with a real cyclical overlay — if 2027 dealmaking actually cools, earnings estimates, not just the multiple, will fall.**

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 9/10
The premier global M&A and underwriting franchise: #1 adviser on the largest, most complex deals (Anthropic IPO, SpaceX financings), a balance-sheet and structuring capability for $44bn bespoke GPU financings that few rivals can replicate, plus a growing durable-fee base (asset & wealth management). Moat: reputation/network effects in dealmaking (CEOs hire Goldman because counterparties and boards expect it), structuring expertise, capital-markets distribution, and regulatory licences/capital intensity. AI does not commoditise judgement-led advisory or bespoke structured finance; if anything, AI capex *creates* Goldman's financing work (management, Q3 call 13 Oct 2026: demand for computing power far exceeds supply, creating financing-structure openings). Threats are cyclical (deal droughts) and competitive (Evercore/centres, Morgan Stanley in wealth), not technological displacement.
**Irreplaceability: 9/10.**

### 3. Rebound catalyst
Q4 2026 results in mid-January 2027 (next earnings 19 Jan 2027 per screen) — the test of whether IB fees held up through the sell-off; any stabilisation in AI equities (note: GS rose ~4% on Mon 23 Nov as Nvidia rebounded 30%+ after Jensen Huang was reinstated as CEO — Trefis update, 23 Nov 2026) re-rates GS fastest among banks given its AI-beta. Medium term: the durable-revenue mix (60%) and a reopening IPO/M&A window. Timing: weeks (sentiment) to one quarter (earnings proof).

### 4. Balance-sheet survival
A GSIB with no solvency question in this drawdown: GAAP ROE was 21% annualised through September 2026, well above its 14–16% target (Tikr, Nov 2026); record year-to-date revenue and strong Q3 earnings (FX Leaders, 20 Nov 2026). The open question is not survival but retained exposure to AI-chip financings (how much SpaceX paper stayed on book — not disclosed in retrieved sources). CET1 ratio and buyback pace: **not found** in retrieved sources. No distress or dilution risk evident; the risk is earnings cyclicality, not the balance sheet.

### 5. Margin of safety / valuation
Fwd PE 10.8 (screen) — cheap in absolute terms and vs the market, and GS still trades *above* its five-year average multiple of tangible book (Tikr, Nov 2026), so the "cheapness" is partly earnings that the market expects to fade: the stock price still assumes near-20% returns. Mean target $1,030.71 implies +28.7% upside. Historical forward-P/E range: **not found** in retrieved sources. **Cheap on current earnings, fair-to-rich on tangible book — a cyclical value call, not a deep-value one.**

### 6. Category position
Still #1 in its core: lead underwriter on the two biggest AI-era capital events of the month (Anthropic IPO, SpaceX $44bn). Fell further than Morgan Stanley (−13% in the week) precisely because it is *more* tied to the AI build-out — leadership intact, beta higher.

### 7. Value-trap risk
The bear case: (a) the AI-financing boom is the 2026 version of leveraged LBO debt — if GPU economics disappoint or SpaceX paper sours, Goldman faces markdowns/reputational damage on retained exposure; (b) a genuine 2027 deal drought cuts IB fees from record levels, making the 10.8× forward P/E a peak-earnings illusion; (c) Fed hikes into mid-2027 compress capital-markets activity further. A falling knife if Q4 fees disappoint on 19 Jan 2027.

### 8. Rebound score: 7/10
**Verdict: The world's best deal franchise at 10.8× forward earnings after a 30% sentiment unwind — a strong rebound bet if AI financing fears prove overblown, but the multiple is not yet washed out on book value.**

#### Earnings-quality flag — LOW_CASH_CONVERSION (CFO/NI −1.71, accrual ratio 0.030)

## NEM — Newmont ($101.44)

### 1. Why it's down — temporary vs permanent
Almost entirely a gold-price story, not a Newmont story. Spot gold peaked at an intraday record $5,595.46 on 29 Jan 2026, then fell ~37% over ten months — the steepest 10-month slide since 1981 — to $3,502 on 20 Nov 2026, its lowest since Sep 2025, and briefly below $3,500 intraday on 25 Nov (low $3,445.89) before closing at $3,581 (Reuters via FinanceFeeds/Coinotag, Nov 2026). The turn is dated precisely: the day after President Trump nominated Kevin Warsh to chair the Fed (30 Jan 2026), read as hawkish; the slide then accelerated in November on hot October PCE (core +3.3% vs +3.2% forecast), a stronger dollar, and CME FedWatch pricing a ~98–99.8% probability of a December rate hike, plus margin-call selling as AI stocks unwound (Ainvest/Coinpaper, Nov 2026). NEM fell 28% in three months from ~$132 in late Aug to ~$94.68, and ~13% in the trailing 30 days, tracking the metal with leverage — peers Agnico Eagle and Kinross fell too (TickerOn, Nov 2026). Meanwhile Newmont's own Q3 2026 (22 Oct 2026) was a record: adjusted net income $2.20/share (Q3 record), adjusted EBITDA $4.0bn, $1.9bn FCF in the quarter, $7.3bn FCF in nine months (~all of 2025's total), production 1.3m oz gold, AISC $1,454/oz, full-year AISC guidance improved ~10% to ~$1,520/oz (Newmont PR, 22 Oct 2026). The catch, flagged by management: Q4 AISC spikes to ~$1,900/oz and 2027 costs rise (Peñasquito silver output −30%, development capital +~$500m for Red Chris/Lihir, labour inflation above the 3% assumed for 2026).
**Verdict: TEMPORARY if gold stabilises — a commodity-price drawdown with record operating performance underneath; PERMANENT-leaning only if the gold regime has genuinely flipped (hawkish Fed + strong dollar for years), in which case peak-cycle earnings evaporate.**

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 6/10
Newmont is the world's largest gold producer by output and market value (~5.3m oz expected in 2026), with a diversified multi-continent portfolio (US, Canada, Mexico, South America, Australia, Africa) plus copper/silver/zinc/lead by-products, and a lower AISC profile than most peers (TickerOn, Nov 2026). Moat: scale, reserve base and operating diversification, long-life assets (Cadia, Red Chris via the Newcrest acquisition), and capital intensity of mine development — a gold mine cannot be "disrupted" by AI, and gold itself is not substitutable as a reserve/hedge asset. But there is no pricing power: Newmont is a price-taker on a commodity, and its moat does not protect earnings from the gold price. AI relevance is nil either way.
**Irreplaceability: 6/10** — the *assets* are irreplaceable, the *earnings* are entirely hostage to one exogenous price.

### 3. Rebound catalyst
Gold stabilising/recovering (JPMorgan sees $4,500/oz by end-2027, Commerzbank $4,100 — CNBC, 25 Nov 2026) is the whole catalyst; Newmont-specific levers are secondary but real: production growth resuming in 2027 (low- to mid-single-digit, past the 2026 trough), Tanami Expansion 2 commercial production by end-Q2 2027 (ahead of schedule), Red Chris block-cave full-funds decision in early 2027, and ~$200m lower Yanacocha closure spend in 2027 (~$400m from 2028) (Q3 2026 call, 22 Oct 2026). Timing: gold-driven (weeks), production-driven (2027). Next earnings 18 Feb 2027.

### 4. Balance-sheet survival
Very strong. $7.3bn FCF in nine months of 2026; $1.9bn returned to shareholders since the last earnings call (dividends + buybacks), contributing to a ~10% reduction in share count since Feb 2024 (Newmont PR, 22 Oct 2026). Net cash per the screen; Rockflow (Nov 2026) cites debt-to-equity 0.169 and a 2.29 current ratio. No distress or dilution risk — the balance sheet removes solvency from the equation entirely; the risk is purely price.

### 5. Margin of safety / valuation
Fwd PE 10.2 (screen); Rockflow (Nov 2026) cites 9.53× forward PE on $9.75 consensus EPS and a 10.7× trailing multiple, with the stock at just 22.2% of its 52-week range. Mean target $133.80 implies +31.9% upside, but the target range is exceptionally wide ($70–$165, a 136% spread) — the market cannot agree on the gold price. **Cheap on peak-cycle earnings; whether it is genuinely cheap depends entirely on whether ~$3,500–4,300/oz realised gold prices (Q3 realised ~$4,320/oz) persist. At $3,500 gold, forward EPS is not $9.75.**

### 6. Category position
Still #1 gold miner by output, with record margins (55.6% operating margin TTM per screen) and costs improving relative to guidance. No share loss — the dip is the metal, not the miner. The 2027 cost rise and Peñasquito silver decline are portfolio-sequencing facts, not competitive losses.

### 7. Value-trap risk
The classic gold-miner trap: buying a 10× P/E at the top of the commodity cycle. If gold breaks and holds below $3,500 (next supports $3,400/$3,300), earnings collapse faster than the multiple expands — the $70 low target prices exactly that. Cost inflation in 2027 (+$500m development capex, higher AISC) compresses margins from both sides if gold stays soft. Gold has now failed as a hedge in this drawdown (fell 37% while the S&P fell ~10% from peak), which undermines the "insurance" bid that supported the January peak.

### 8. Rebound score: 6/10
**Verdict: A fortress balance sheet and record cash flow at 10× earnings — but this is a gold-price call wearing a stock's clothes, and the metal just posted its worst 10-month slide since 1981.**

## APH — Amphenol ($73.08)

### 1. Why it's down — temporary vs permanent
A valuation reset on a stock whose fundamentals keep improving — the shallowest dip in this batch (−17.5% off high, only −3.3% vs 200d SMA) and the most clearly sentiment-driven. APH hit a 52-week high of $89.93 on 6 Oct 2026, then fell ~13% within a month. It reported record Q3 2026 on 28 Oct 2026 — sales $10.2bn (+65% YoY, +37% organically), adjusted EPS $0.80 vs ~$0.73 consensus (+74%), orders $12.2bn (book-to-bill 1.19×), Q4 guidance $10.3–10.5bn sales and $0.81–0.83 EPS, a 20% dividend increase and a new $4bn buyback — and jumped >5% to $84.19, then gave it all back: −7.10% on 4 Nov 2026 on profit-taking, a Form 144 filing for ~800,000 shares by CEO R. Adam Norwitt, rising Treasury yields (10-year toward 5.33%), and a broad AI-infrastructure risk-off; −4.6% on 30 Oct as Alphabet fell 12.6%, Intel 23% after-hours and Amazon 7% on AI-spending/cash-burn fears; −7.79% on 20 Nov 2026 in the broad rotation out of premium-valued hardware/AI suppliers (TickerOn/Weiss/TradingKey, Oct–Nov 2026). An earlier 12.3% one-month drop in May 2026 (Zacks) had different, company-specific causes — macro/geopolitical worries, debt rising to ~$18.7bn after the CommScope CCS acquisition, integration risk and China tax headwinds — but the CCS deal has since outperformed (see §3). No miss, no guidance cut, no demand break in any of these legs.
**Verdict: TEMPORARY.** Multiple compression on AI-capex durability fears plus profit-taking after a record print; the underlying demand signal (orders, book-to-bill) is the strongest in the batch.

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 8/10
Amphenol is now the world's #1 connector manufacturer: 2025 connector sales $15.74bn (+36.8%), overtaking TE Connectivity ($14.90bn) for the first time in 45 years of Bishop & Associates rankings (TTI MarketEYE, 2026); it also leads fibre-optic connectors with >12.5% share vs Corning 10.8% and TE 9.2% (GMI, 2026). Moat: design-in/qualification lock-in (connectors are specified into customer platforms and certified for harsh-environment/defense use — re-qualification is slow and expensive), breadth across seven end markets, manufacturing scale and an acquisition compounding machine (FCI, Carlisle Interconnect, CommScope CCS). AI is a tailwind, not a threat: Amphenol supplies the high-speed copper backplanes, cabling assemblies and optical interconnects inside AI clusters (IT datacom organic growth +77% in Q3). Threats: commoditisation at the low end (standard formats face price/lead-time competition), optical architectural transitions that could strand copper-heavy designs, and customer concentration in hyperscaler capex.
**Irreplaceability: 8/10.**

### 3. Rebound catalyst
Already guided, not hoped for: Q4 2026 sales $10.3–10.5bn and EPS $0.81–0.83 (28 Oct 2026); CommScope CCS outlook raised to $5.1bn sales and $0.18 EPS accretion (from $4.6bn/$0.15); four further acquisitions signed/closed in Q3 (Empire Electronics $320m sales, AdvancedPCB $270m, Dexgreen $90m, Humanetics Sensors $70m; ~$1.6bn combined); new $4bn three-year buyback plus a 20% dividend rise (Amphenol PR, 28 Oct 2026). Timing: Q4 results (27 Jan 2027) confirm the guide; any hyperscaler-capex reassurance re-rates the multiple quickly given the 1.19× book-to-bill.

### 4. Balance-sheet survival
The one genuine watch-item in this batch, but manageable. Total debt $17.9bn at 30 Sep 2026 (long-term $17.17bn, up from $6.48bn at FY2024 — a 165% rise driven by the ~$10.5bn CommScope deal closed 9 Jan 2026); net debt $12.6bn; net debt/EBITDA 1.15 per the screen. Cash fell to $4.17bn (plus short-term investments, $5.30bn total liquidity) from $11.13bn at end-2025 after funding CCS; Q3 interest expense $212.3m vs $80.7m a year earlier (Amphenol Q3 call/PR, Oct 2026). Offsetting that: Q3 operating cash flow $2.1bn (102% of net income) and FCF $1.6bn in a single quarter, so debt service is comfortably covered and the company returned $553m in the quarter while still investing. No distress or dilution risk at current cash generation; leverage only bites if AI datacom demand stalls.

### 5. Margin of safety / valuation
This is the least cheap name in the batch. Fwd PE 20.2 (screen) — but note contemporaneous coverage quoted higher multiples: ~41× forward earnings at end-October (Weiss, 30 Oct 2026) and mid-30s in early November (TickerOn, 4 Nov 2026); the screen's 20.2× likely reflects a different (possibly FY27/FY28) earnings base after the CCS-accretive guide, so treat the exact figure with care and the direction as the point: the multiple has compressed sharply from ~40× to ~20× in a month while EPS grew 74%. Mean target $106.18 implies +45.3% upside. Historical forward-P/E range and peer median: **not found** in retrieved sources. **Genuinely cheaper than it was — a halved multiple on record earnings — but still the priciest of the four on any earnings base, because the market is paying for 37% organic growth.**

### 6. Category position
#1 and gaining: overtook TE Connectivity for the first time in 45 years, with connector sales growing 36.8% vs TE's 15.6% and the industry's ~14.7% (Bishop via TTI, 2026). Communications Solutions sales +99% YoY ($6.58bn, 33.1% operating margin) in Q3. The dip is not share loss — it is the market questioning how long hyperscaler spending lasts.

### 7. Value-trap risk
The bear case: (a) hyperscaler AI-capex pauses or shifts architecture (optical replacing copper interconnect, inventory digestion) — APH's 77% IT-datacom organic growth is the most cyclically exposed line in this batch; (b) the acquisition-fuelled model (debt 165% higher since FY2024) leaves less room if rates stay high and deals disappoint — CCS integration is the key test; (c) China tax disputes add earnings volatility (MikaMirAI, Oct 2026). At ~20–40× earnings (depending on base), there is less margin of safety than KEYS/GS/NEM if growth normalises.

### 8. Rebound score: 7/10
**Verdict: The best business in the batch — #1 connector maker, record orders, 37% organic growth — but also the least washed-out valuation, so the rebound depends on AI capex staying strong rather than on a cheap multiple.**
