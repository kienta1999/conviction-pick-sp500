## 1. INTUITIVE SURGICAL (ISRG)

### 1.1 Why it's down
The drawdown is almost entirely forward-expectations-driven, crystallized by the Q2 2026 print (released 2026-07-16). The quarter itself was strong: revenue $2.89B (+19% y/y, beat of ~$2.83B consensus), non-GAAP EPS $2.80 (vs $2.51 expected), operating margin 33.6% (up from 30.5%), 468 da Vinci systems placed (vs 395 prior year, incl. 246 da Vinci 5), worldwide procedures +16% (da Vinci +15%, Ion +36%), instruments & accessories revenue +18% to $1.73B. (talkmarkets.com, 2026-07-16; financialcontent/stockstory, 2026-07-16)
Yet the stock fell ~10–11% after hours on four overlapping negatives: (a) full-year 2026 da Vinci procedure-growth guidance held at 13.5–15.5% (midpoint ~14.5%), below ~15.2% consensus — the slowest growth since 2020, per ainvest (2026-07-17); (b) management flagged ~1 percentage point of 2026 gross-margin impact from tariffs (raised 2026 pro-forma gross margin guidance to 68–69% from 67.5–68.5% even so; seekingalpha.com, 2026-07-16); (c) a Class II recall of certain da Vinci components and reported declines in overall surgery volumes; and (d) softer US procedure growth tied to ACA coverage/premium-subsidy dynamics, with HCA Healthcare warning on surgical demand and uninsured-patient mix. The deeper narrative layer, per Motley Fool via webull (2026), is fear of Chinese robotic-surgery competitors and of AI/OpenAI disruption narratives. Earlier in 2026 (January), the stock also slipped on the initial 13–15% procedure guidance plus tariff warning (ts2.tech, 2026-01-25). Leerink's Mike Kratky called the guidance "room for upside," raising his target to $622 (from $600), Outperform (ts2.tech, 2026-01-25).
**Verdict: temporary / transitory.** A fast multiple-compression rerating on a growth slowdown, not an operating collapse. Nothing in the quarter suggests the franchise is impaired.

### 1.2 Moat / AI-irreplaceability
Moat = installed base + procedure economics + training/certification lock-in + process tech. Installed base: 11,710 da Vinci systems (+12% y/y) and 1,096 Ion systems (+21%) as of 2026-06-30; over 20 million patients treated, 3.1M+ procedures in 2025 alone (talkmarkets.com, 2026-07-16). Recurring revenue (instruments, accessories, service) is ~75% of total (Motley Fool). Surgeons are trained on da Vinci; hospitals' workflows and accreditation are built around it. Intuitive is layering software/data on top: My Intuitive Plus with remote collaboration, simulation training, and AI-powered case insights; R&D up 18% to $371M in Q2. (biggo finance, 2026-07)
Disruption threat: Chinese competitors (MicroPort's Toumai and domestic tender pressure — management cited "China tender volumes and competitive intensity" as a 2026 guidance factor, infonasional 2026-01) and Medtronic's Hugo; cost-per-procedure pressure from hospitals. These are real at the low end (Intuitive is responding with XiR, refurbished systems, operating leases, and the Extended Use Program for instruments launching H1 2027 to lower per-procedure cost). AI does not replace the robot; it augments it.
**Irreplaceability: 9/10.** No substitute for robotic surgery in the medium term; threats are pricing/competition at the margin, not franchise displacement.

### 1.3 Rebound catalyst
- Q3 2026 earnings 2026-10-20: any procedure-growth beat vs the 13.5–15.5% guide (held at midpoint) re-rates the stock; management maintained the guide "closer to the midpoint" (seekingalpha.com, 2026-07-16).
- Margin relief already underway: 2026 non-GAAP gross margin guide raised to 68–69% on anticipated tariff relief + cost reductions + da Vinci 5/Ion leverage (ainvest, 2026-08/09).
- Product: da Vinci 5 rollout (246 installs in Q2, new cardiac clearances), >100 da Vinci 5 updates planned, GI robotics expansion, and the H1 2027 Extended Use Program lowering cost per procedure — directly aimed at hospital cost sensitivity (ainvest, 2026-09-04).
- International procedures still growing ~20% (bright spot vs US softness).

### 1.4 Balance-sheet survival
Fortress. $9.03B cash and investments at end of 2025 (ts2.tech, 2026-01-25); ~$8.63B at Q2 2026 with net debt/EBITDA −1.25 (net cash; screen). $380M of share repurchases in Q2 2026 alone (marketbeat, 2026-07-16). R&D and buybacks self-funded from ~34% operating margins. Zero distress risk, zero dilution risk.

### 1.5 Valuation / margin of safety
Forward P/E 32.5 (screen) vs a five-year average of ~54 (Motley Fool, 2026) — a ~40% multiple derating. At ~$353 the stock traded ~33x the 2026 adjusted EPS estimate ($10.79) and ~29x 2027 ($12.01) (biggo, 2026-07). Street: 18 Buy, 4 Hold, 1 Sell; average target $501.24, implying ~42% upside (biggo, 2026-07); MarketBeat mean target $508.68 (2026-09-18). Screen-implied upside +21.1%. Verdict: genuinely cheap vs own history for a 15%-grower with 33%+ op margins; the dip, not the multiple, created the discount.

### 1.6 Category position
Undisputed #1 in robotic-assisted surgery with dominant share and pricing power on instruments/accessories (the 75% recurring stream). Competitors nibble at the low end; no share loss of consequence is evidenced.

### 1.7 Value-trap risk (honest bear case)
(1) The instrument Extended Use Program (H1 2027) could cannibalize recurring revenue faster than procedure volumes compensate — Intuitive itself says financial impacts are "unquantified" and progressive from 2027 (seekingalpha.com, 2026-07-16). (2) US procedure volumes could stay soft if ACA subsidy rollbacks structurally raise uninsured rates (HCA's warning). (3) Chinese competitors win domestic tenders at half the price, permanently capping China growth. (4) The premium multiple never fully returns: if 13–15% procedure growth is the new normal, 30x+ may not be justified. This is a rerating risk, not a distress risk.

### 1.8 Rebound score: **8/10** — Verdict: premium compounder repriced by a one-quarter guide and tariff/surgery-volume sentiment; net cash, raised margin guidance, 75% recurring revenue and a still-dominant installed base make this a buyable dip, with Oct-20 earnings as the near-term proof point.

## 2. ADOBE (ADBE)

### 2.1 Why it's down
Two intertwined causes: the "AI loser" narrative and a leadership vacuum. D.A. Davidson's Gil Luria said on CNBC 2026-09-11 (after FY Q3 2026 earnings): "Adobe has been characterized as an AI loser" — the stock fell on Sept 11 because "merely in-line quarterly results do nothing to dismantle that harmful perception." This is a pattern: Adobe beat estimates in both Q1 and Q2 FY2026 and the stock fell after both ("the market does not believe the earnings; it believes the structural worry," stockmoguls.com, 2026-09-05). FY Q3 2026 (reported 2026-09-10): record revenue $6.76B (+13% y/y, +12% cc), non-GAAP EPS $6.13, ARR $27.50B (+11.2% y/y), FY2026 targets raised (adobe.com press release; marketbeat earnings call transcript). Still the stock opened lower Sept 11.
Leadership: CEO Shantanu Narayen announced his transition on the Q1 FY2026 earnings call (~June 2026), staying as Chair; CFO Dan Durn departed for Marvell at Q2 FY2026 earnings; Steve Day is interim CFO (Morgan Stanley research note excerpt, 2026-07-21). On 2026-09-09/10 the board named insider Anil Chakravarthy CEO effective 2026-12-01, and Creative-Cloud chief David Wadhwani resigned the same day (thepursuitofcompounding, 2026-09-14). Luria argued the internal hire after a "months-long" search implies the board failed to attract outside talent — "clunky transition creates friction" (invezz/tradingview, 2026-09-11).
Sell-side derating is explicit: Morgan Stanley downgraded to Underweight 2026-07-21 with a $240 target, cutting its multiple to 9x FY27 EPS (est. ~$27.23) from 13x — "a discount to Adobe's historical average," citing GenAI disruption and execution risk. BMO downgraded to Market Perform (target $400→$375) back in Jan 2026 on Canva/Alphabet competition (ts2.tech). Stock is down >25% YTD 2026 after a steep 2025.
**Verdict: mixed — predominantly temporary (sentiment + CEO/CFO transition) but with a genuine, unresolved structural question.** The RPO signal is the concrete worry: RPO $22.16B in Q3 was up 8.4% y/y but the first single-digit growth since early FY2023 and down sequentially (thepursuitofcompounding, 2026-09-14); total ARR growth decelerated back to 11.2% after Q2's inorganic (Semrush) boost. The bear case is not fabricated — it shows up in the backlog.

### 2.2 Moat / AI-irreplaceability — the central question
The honest case that generative AI is *not* a permanent impairment:
- Product evidence: AI-first ARR grew >150% y/y in Q3 FY2026 (Adobe press release, 2026-09-10); 1 billion monthly active users (Adobe ecosystem), Acrobat AI Assistant MAU doubled quarter-over-quarter, Acrobat + Express MAU surpassed 900M (+25% y/y) — the freemium funnel is working (marketbeat call transcript, 2026-09-10). Creative & Marketing Professionals subscription revenue +13% y/y to $4.65B with enterprise wins (IKEA, Wells Fargo, Vanguard, BNP Paribas, Marriott — alphastreet transcript). Firefly's commercial-safety/indemnification story keeps it in enterprise content supply chains.
- Moat = workflow standard (PSD/AI file formats, fonts, color pipelines), team/enterprise entrenchment, and the distribution of a billion-user funnel. Microsoft/OpenAI tooling and open models attack the *generation* layer, not the *workflow/integration* layer — and Adobe owns that layer.
The honest case that it *is* impaired:
- Competitors: Canva (prosumer/freelancer, cited by BMO), Figma (product design), and AI-native generation tools pulling work away from Creative Cloud; Morgan Stanley explicitly prices "outsized GenAI disruption debate" into a 9x multiple. RPO deceleration suggests the backlog is not keeping pace with the narrative.
- Adobe is deferring Creative Cloud pricing and pushing freemium — a tacit admission that pricing power is under pressure.
**Irreplaceability: 6/10.** The franchise is durable in professional/enterprise workflows, but the generation layer is being commoditized by foundation models, and the market has reason to demand proof that AI ARR monetization outruns any seat erosion. The 150% AI-first ARR growth is real evidence on the bull side; the RPO deceleration is real evidence on the bear side.

### 2.3 Rebound catalyst
- Proof of AI monetization: continued AI-first ARR acceleration and AI-credit monetization in Q4 FY2026 (earnings 2026-12-09) and FY2027 guide — the single metric that re-rates the stock (Luria: "show revenue acceleration and real AI monetization").
- Leadership clarity: Chakravarthy takes the CEO seat 2026-12-01; a credible AI roadmap + potential restructuring under the new CEO/CFO could flip "story risk" to "execution."
- Freemium conversion: 1B MAUs converting to paid at even small rates is operating leverage the market is not pricing.

### 2.4 Balance-sheet survival
$5.64B cash and short-term investments exiting Q3 FY2026; record Q3 operating cash flow $2.52B (adobe.com, 2026-09-10). Total debt ~$6.76B, debt/equity 0.41, net debt/EBITDA 0.12 (screen) — essentially unlevered. Repurchased ~9.5M shares in Q3; the March 2024 authorization is fully utilized with ~$24.55B remaining under the April 2026 authorization (marketbeat transcript, 2026-09-10). No distress risk; massive buyback capacity is a put under the stock.

### 2.5 Valuation / margin of safety
Forward P/E 9.0 (screen; confirmed tradestie, 2026-09-20) — among the cheapest in Adobe's modern public history; TTM ~14x. Morgan Stanley now applies 9x vs 13x previously and calls that a discount to the historical average. Peers: MSFT ~20.9x fwd, CRM ~14.9x, GOOGL ~23.5x (alcapitaladvisory, Sept 2026). Analyst targets are unusually dispersed: mean $276.40 (39 analysts, range $195–$377.07; indmoney, 2026-09-15), implying ~+11% from ~$249–253 (screen +11.7%); consensus rating Hold (2.06 score). Notable: Bernstein $373, RBC $315, Barclays $295, D.A. Davidson $290, BMO $270, Citi/Piper/Evercore $250, Goldman Sachs $200 (Sept 14 — bearish), Morgan Stanley $240 UW. Verdict: genuinely, historically cheap — but the market is pricing Adobe as a melting ice cube, and the dispersion (72% of share price) shows nobody knows.

### 2.6 Category position
Still #1 in creative software by revenue and in enterprise content workflows, with pricing power under pressure (deferred pricing, freemium). Share is being nibbled at the prosumer edge (Canva) and generation layer, not lost in the professional core.

### 2.7 Value-trap risk (honest bear case)
(1) AI substitution proves structural: enterprises renegotiate Creative Cloud seats down as in-house AI tooling + competitors cover 80% of use cases; ARR decelerates to high-single digits permanently. (2) Freemium cannibalizes paid conversion — the billion MAUs never monetize. (3) The multiple never re-rates: 9x becomes the new normal for "AI-disrupted software," and even 9x compresses to 7x on an ARR miss (Morgan Stanley's bear logic). (4) Leadership transition fumbles the AI roadmap. This is the one name in the batch where the "permanent impairment" thesis has real teeth.

### 2.8 Rebound score: **6/10** — Verdict: the cheapest high-quality software franchise in the market on paper, with genuine AI-monetization evidence (AI-first ARR +150%, 1B MAUs) — but RPO deceleration and a leadership vacuum make it a "show me" name; the rebound requires ARR reacceleration proof at the Dec-9 print, not just another beat.

## 3. BROADRIDGE FINANCIAL SOLUTIONS (BR)

### 3.1 Why it's down
Sentiment, not operations. The decline (52-week high $250.26; −32.7% from high, −24.8% YTD, −31.6% over 52 weeks per Barchart, 2026-09-16) is attributed by holders to two fears: AI-based disintermediation of its technology/business-services operations and tokenization uncertainty around its role as a financial-markets intermediary (Barchart, 2026-09-16; SouthernSun Smid Cap Q2 2026 letter; Jensen Quality Mid Cap Q1 2026 letter). Jensen's letter (Q1 2026) explicitly calls the AI-disruption fear "unfounded with respect to BR" — its services are "legally mandated, mission-critical and subject to constant audits and regulatory reviews, making them very difficult to replicate." Earnings have been fine: Q1 FY2026 revenue $1.59B (+8% y/y), EPS $1.51 (+21.8% beat), recurring-revenue growth outlook raised to the upper end of 5–7%, FY26 adj-EPS growth guided 8–12% (ainvest, 2026-03). The Q2 FY2026 report met guidance ("status quo outlook") and the stock fell 6% anyway — growth investors wanted acceleration (tikr.com). SouthernSun (Q2 2026 letter, July 2026) noted the shares had lost 43.08% over the prior 52 weeks despite "robust fundamentals." A secondary drag: defensive, low-growth profile in a market paying up for AI-exposed growth (barchart/claremarfarms Q2 preview).
**Verdict: temporary / transitory.** An AI-fear derating layered on a low-growth rerating; the operating record (beats in each of the last four quarters per barchart) is intact.

### 3.2 Moat / AI-irreplaceability
Moat = regulatory-embedded infrastructure + scale + switching costs. Broadridge is the plumbing of US investor communications: proxy distribution/tabulation, SEC disclosure composition/filing, trade processing, statement/tax-form delivery for banks, broker-dealers, wealth managers (Jensen letter, Q1 2026). These are legally mandated flows with constant audit/regulatory review — not easily displaced by an AI wrapper. ~75% recurring revenue (financialfreedomisajourney, 2026-02); record closed-sales backlog (DA Davidson, May 2026); client retention is structurally high because ripping out proxy/trade infrastructure is a regulatory risk for clients. CEO Tim Gokey frames tokenization as "a megatrend for the next ten years" and the company is expanding tokenization + agentic AI in its platforms — positioning the supposed threat as an opportunity (ad-hoc-news.de, 2026-09-18; ainvest, 2026-03). AI's real impact here is cost-out (efficiency in document processing), which Broadridge itself is adopting.
**Irreplaceability: 8/10.** AI can automate pieces of the workflow, but the regulated intermediary function and audit trail are not going away; tokenization over a decade is a business-model evolution, not an overnight disintermediation.

### 3.3 Rebound catalyst
- Sentiment reset already in motion: +16.7% over the last three months, trading above the 50-day MA since July (Barchart, 2026-09-16).
- FY2027 guidance issued with Q4 FY2026 results (Aug 2026): recurring revenue growth 6–8%, adjusted EPS growth 8–12%, dividend raised 11.8% to $1.09/quarter (ex-div 2026-09-03, paid 2026-10-05) (ad-hoc-news.de, 2026-09-18).
- Q1 FY2027 earnings 2026-11-03: record closed-sales backlog sets up H2 acceleration narrative (DA Davidson, May 2026).
- Tokenization/agentic-AI initiatives turning the bear thesis into a growth story.

### 3.4 Balance-sheet survival
Net debt ~$3.33B (filing dated 2025-12-31), net debt/EBITDA ~1.54x then (macroaxis); screen 1.72x. FY2026 operating cash flow $1.3B, capex only $67.2M (stocktitan.net) — highly cash-generative. Dividend $3.90 TTM / $4.36 forward (wealthyhood), payout ratio 41.65%. Share count 128M (FY2012) → ~117.7M (Q2 FY2026): steady buybacks. ROE 40.9%, ROA 9.3%. Manageable leverage, no distress, consistent capital returns.

### 3.5 Valuation / margin of safety
Forward P/E 14.1 (screen) vs a history of ~20–21x: at ~$193.50 in Feb 2026, FY2026 est. $9.41 midpoint implied ~20.4x forward adjusted P/E (financialfreedomisajourney, 2026-02-05); wealthyhood shows fwd P/E 16.89 / trailing 17.39 (Sept 2026). EV/Sales ~4.1x, price/cash flow ~12.9x. Consensus: Moderate Buy (4 Buy / 4 Hold, 0 Sell), mean target $216.00, +32.5% upside from ~$163 (MarketBeat, Sept 2026); RBC $226 Outperform (2026-08-31); DA Davidson $214 Buy (May 2026); Morgan Stanley $176 Equal Weight (Aug 2026); UBS $180 Neutral (Aug 2026). Forward dividend yield ~2.6%. Verdict: genuinely cheap vs own history and vs the 8–12% EPS growth guide — a ~14x multiple for a mid-to-high-single-digit compounder with 75% recurring revenue.

### 3.6 Category position
#1 in investor communications/proxy services with pricing power on recurring contracts; trade-processing franchises (Global Technology & Operations) growing double digits (Wealth/Investment Management +22% in Q1 FY26). No evidenced share loss.

### 3.7 Value-trap risk (honest bear case)
(1) Tokenization over 10 years genuinely shrinks the intermediary footprint (settlement/proxy on-chain), eroding the long-term growth rate to low-single digits. (2) AI competitors automate compliance communications and win on price, compressing margins. (3) The multiple never re-rates because the market permanently assigns "ex-growth utility" status — 14x proves fair, not cheap. (4) Capital-markets activity stays muted, capping transaction revenue. The trap version: a well-run company whose growth permanently decelerates while the AI-fear discount persists.

### 3.8 Rebound score: **7/10** — Verdict: a legally-entrenched, cash-compounding compounder derated by AI/tokenization sentiment rather than fundamentals; already bouncing off the lows with FY27 guidance, a record sales backlog, and a raised dividend — the dip looks like mispriced fear, with the Nov-3 print as the next checkpoint.

## 4. VERISK ANALYTICS (VRSK)

### 4.1 Why it's down
A mix of cyclical softness and structural repricing. Documented drivers: (a) moderating organic growth — H1 2026 organic growth guided at 4–5% (insidermonkey) — plus a soft Q3 2025 revenue print and acquisition-related margin pressure; (b) low severe-weather activity cooling demand for property claims solutions (barchart via stoneybrook, ~Feb 2026); (c) the 2026-03-26 −5.2% single-day drop (sharpest in 2+ years) on preliminary Q1 2026 US P&C industry data signaling faster "normalization" — i.e., the end of double-digit premium hikes that had been a tailwind (financialcontent/marketminute, 2026-03-26); (d) elevated debt levels; and (e) an AI-disruption discount: the short thesis (insidermonkey, 2026) argues VRSK's growth depends on pricing/upsell rather than durable data moats, auto underwriting faces insurtech weakness + LexisNexis/Guidewire/Duck Creek competition, and management increasingly concedes premium-linked pricing is "negotiable." Stock: −38.7% over the prior year and −19% YTD at the time of the Barchart piece (~Feb 2026); screen: −29.6% off 52w high.
**Verdict: mixed — majority cyclical/temporary (weather, P&C cycle, tough compares), but the pricing-power softness and AI/internalization risk have a genuine structural flavor.** This is the batch's "quality compounder at a crossroads."

### 4.2 Moat / AI-irreplaceability
Moat = the data and workflow infrastructure of US P&C insurance: ISO statistical data, loss-cost models, catastrophe risk models, claims workflow (Xact), fraud and underwriting analytics embedded in carrier operations, plus regulatory filings carriers depend on. Network effects: pooled industry data improves the models; deep client relationships and "broad connectivity" cited by CEO Lee Shavel on the Q2 2026 call (marketbeat transcript, 2026-07-29). Subscription revenue is 83% of total (+8% in Q2) — sticky.
Threats: (1) clients internalizing analytics with their own AI stacks; (2) competitors LexisNexis, Guidewire, Duck Creek in auto/underwriting; (3) Verisk's AI answer so far is credible but early — XactAI users up nearly 10x since March to ~7,000 licensees, Claude connectors and Premium Audit AI supporting renewals and pricing (thestockobserver/MarketBeat call summary, 2026-07-29); a generative-AI commercial underwriting assistant launched (ainvest, 2026-02). Unlike Adobe, Verisk is not facing a foundation-model substitute for its core — but it faces pricing pressure and "prove the value" renewal negotiations.
**Irreplaceability: 7/10.** The data franchise is genuinely hard to replicate, but the pricing-power erosion (negotiable premium-linked pricing) is the crack in the moat that the market is discounting.

### 4.3 Rebound catalyst
- Q2 2026 (2026-07-29) was a clean beat: organic constant-currency revenue +5.8% (sequential acceleration), adjusted EBITDA +7.4%, EPS $1.98 vs $1.93 est., revenue $806.3M (+4.3%); FY2026 guidance reaffirmed: revenue $3.19–3.24B, adjusted EBITDA $1.79–1.83B at 56.0–56.5% margin, adjusted EPS $7.45–7.75 — guidance midpoint in line with $7.71 consensus (stocktitan.net; marketbeat; ad-hoc-news.de). Management expects continued sequential improvement in H2.
- Q3 earnings 2026-10-28: proof of the H2 acceleration + hurricane-season activity.
- Commercialization of the generative-AI underwriting assistant and XactAI adoption converting AI spend into pricing power.

### 4.4 Balance-sheet survival
Elevated but serviceable leverage: net debt/EBITDA 2.68 (screen). The business prints cash: Q2 2026 operating cash flow $366M (+49.7% y/y), FCF $298M (+57.9%) (stocktitan.net). Capital returns are aggressive: $1.9B in H1 2026 buybacks plus a $200M accelerated repurchase in Q2, $800M authorization remaining; dividend $0.50/quarter ($2.00 annual), next paid 2026-09-30 (stocktitan.net). With ~56% EBITDA margins, debt service is comfortable; the risk is strategic (buybacks at a derated price are fine, but leverage leaves less room if growth stalls), not solvency.

### 4.5 Valuation / margin of safety
Forward P/E 20.2 (screen); the short thesis cites ~23x NTM P/E for mid-teens EPS growth, "a premium to AI-disrupted peers" (insidermonkey) — either way, well below Verisk's historical 30–40x compounder multiple. Operating margin 45.1% (screen) is best-in-class. Screen-implied analyst upside +33.8%. Verdict: derated from compounder to "show me" pricing; cheap vs own history, fair-to-slightly-rich vs the AI-disruption peer set — the multiple is the debate.

### 4.6 Category position
Still #1 in P&C insurance data/analytics with unmatched data assets; pricing power is softening at the margin (negotiable premium-linked pricing) rather than collapsing.

### 4.7 Value-trap risk (honest bear case)
(1) Organic growth permanently resets to 4–5% as P&C premium normalization removes the pricing tailwind and clients negotiate harder. (2) AI competition forces Verisk to spend heavily on GenAI with costs outpacing benefits (ainvest, 2026-02). (3) Quiet catastrophe years persist, keeping transactional revenue (claims) soft. (4) The multiple re-rates permanently from 30x+ to mid-teens as the market decides Verisk is an ex-growth data utility — in which case 20x is not cheap. (5) Elevated leverage + heavy buybacks amplify downside if earnings disappoint.

### 4.8 Rebound score: **6/10** — Verdict: a genuinely high-quality, 56%-margin data franchise derated by cyclical P&C/weather headwinds plus a credible AI/pricing-power discount; the Q2 beat and reaffirmed guide argue the dip is overdone, but leverage and the pricing-power question keep it a show-me name into the Oct-28 print.
