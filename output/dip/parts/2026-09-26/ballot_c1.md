## PART 1 — SINGLE-PICK BALLOT

- **Top pick:** NFLX
- **Runner-up:** VRSK

**Thesis (3-5 sentences):** Netflix at ~43% off its 52-week high is the streaming winner repriced as a loser — a valuation/narrative reset on one analyst's engagement *estimates*, not on cash flows. The business kept compounding through the selloff: Q2 revenue $12.56B (+13.4%), 33.4% operating margins, ~382M paid memberships, ad tier doubling toward $3B in 2026, and a $12.5B FCF guide — all while Ackman's Pershing Square bought a ~$950M position arguing Netflix "effectively won the streaming wars." AI is a tailwind here, not a threat: GenAI is embedded in ~300 titles' production and across the ads lifecycle, and the data/content flywheel (recommendation engine + pricing power + production ecosystem) is the closest thing to an uncopyable moat in media. At ~20x forward vs a 36x five-year average, this is the deepest derating of a pristine franchise in the field.

**Why the dip is temporary (not permanent):** the single most compelling evidence is that the bear case rests on Wells Fargo's *proprietary estimates* (–4% YoY hours/sub/day, Top-100 Originals –21%), while Netflix's own hard numbers kept improving — Q2 beat with margins expanding to 33.4%, FY26 revenue guide held at $51.0–51.4B with the operating margin guide *raised* to 31.5%, and the March 2026 price increase (+8–12.5%) produced no elevated churn. The Nielsen share softness is mature-market, cyclical engagement noise, not competitive displacement.

**Moat / AI-irreplaceability: LOW risk.** Category dominance is absolute — undisputed #1 by scale, margins, and pricing power. AI and substitutes attack creation, but Netflix's moat is distribution, data, and aggregated attention (382M subscribers, 45–60%+ of new sign-ups on the ad tier, 4,000+ advertisers); generative models cannot replicate the subscriber graph or the $19B content flywheel. AI here is an amplifier (production efficiency, ad targeting), owned by the incumbent.

**Rebound catalyst & timing:** Q3 earnings **Oct 20, 2026 (AMC)** — a re-acceleration print with a raised margin guide directly answers the engagement thesis; the definitive data point is the annual engagement report in Jan 2027, which can retire the Wells Fargo bear case entirely. Ad-tier monetization scaling ($1.5B → ~$3B) reprices the growth algorithm through 2027.

**12-18 month return scenario:** Base: ~+34% to the ~$95 consensus mean target as the multiple drifts back toward ~25x on delivery. Bull: engagement data validates, multiple rerates toward ~30x on ~$4.30 2027 EPS with the ad engine compounding → ~$120–130, i.e. ~+70–80%.

**Top risk (value-trap case):** If the engagement decline is real and persistent (thinner slate + Nielsen lows translating into 2027 churn), revenue growth stalls, the fixed-ish $19B content bill compresses margins, and the multiple falls toward Cahall's 15x-2027-EPS case → $57, another ~–20% from here.
