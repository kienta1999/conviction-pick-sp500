## SYK — Stryker (Borda #1, tentative single pick)

### 1. Why-down: Inari supply/backorder + cyber incident + disclosure damage; demand, margins and FY guidance intact, no guidance cut
**CONFIRMED — with one precision correction: guidance was *narrowed with the low end raised*, not left unchanged.**

- Inari / Peripheral Vascular backorder: Stryker's Q2 release (2026-07-30) disclosed
  supply disruptions in Peripheral Vascular producing a meaningful backorder and lost
  sales; US Vascular organic sales fell ~6.7% in Q2. On the Q2 call CEO Kevin Lobo
  declined detail but confirmed a significant backorder specific to one Inari plant,
  with management guidance that backorders should reach a manageable level by end-Q3.
  Shares fell ~6.4% on 2026-07-31. — Hagens Berman investigation PR, 2026-10-01
  (summarising the Q2 release/call); Substack/OpenBookAnalytics Q2 recaps, Oct 2026.
- Disclosure-credibility second leg: at the Wells Fargo Healthcare Conference on
  2026-09-08, CFO Preston Wells said the problem was continuing into Q3 and expected
  to continue a little into Q4, contradicting the July assurance. Shares fell $26.70
  (-8.8%) that day, >$10B of market cap; a shareholder-rights investigation into the
  adequacy of the Inari disclosures followed. — Hagens Berman PR, 2026-10-01;
  Morningstar/PR Newswire republication, 2026-09-17.
- Cyber layer: a cybersecurity incident on 2026-03-11 disrupted Stryker's global
  Microsoft environment, ordering, shipping and manufacturing for weeks; Q1 sales
  ~$6.0B (+2.6%) missed consensus by ~$317M on one broker estimate, and an 8-K/A
  confirmed a material impact on Q1 while stating no expected material impact on
  full-year guidance. Stryker acquired Inari in Feb 2025; reporting attributes the
  Inari plant disruption in part to the post-acquisition cyber disruption of systems
  critical to integration/manufacturing/shipping. — Stryker 8-K/A via StockTitan;
  OpenBookAnalytics, Oct 2026; AInvest Q1 recap, Apr/May 2026.
- Demand / margins intact in Q2: net sales $6.59B, +9.4% YoY, organic +9.0%;
  adjusted EPS $3.69 (+17.9%); adjusted operating margin 27.4% (+170 bps); adjusted
  gross margin ~66.0%. — Stryker Q2 release summaries (Yahoo/MarketBeat/Seeking
  Alpha), 2026-07-30 and after.
- Guidance — **correction to "intact" wording**: at Q2 the FY26 guide was *narrowed*,
  organic growth 8.0–9.5% → **8.3–9.3%** and adjusted EPS $14.90–15.10 →
  **$14.95–15.10** — i.e. the low end was *raised* on both, the top trimmed on
  growth. That is not a guidance cut in substance, and no company cut had been
  issued as of 2026-10-14. Caveat for the panel: September commentary led at least
  one broker to trim forecasts (flagged alongside the 2026-10-06 CEO-succession
  news), so Oct 29 remains a genuine guidance-survival test. — Seeking Alpha Q2
  recap; OpenBookAnalytics, Oct 2026.

### 4. Moat: Mako / surgeon-training installed-base switching costs are the moat's core

## HSY — Hershey (Borda #3)

### 8. Why-down/recovery: cocoa spike reversed ~70% from its ~$12–13k/t peak; Q2 gross margin ~45.3%
**CONTRADICTED IN PART — two corrections, both material to the memo.**

- Peak: broadly right. NY cocoa peaked at **$11,984/t in Dec 2024** (AP, cited
  in source round-ups), with other series putting the 2024 peak at ~$12,000–
  12,900/t. The "~$12–13k" framing is acceptable.
- **"Reversed ~70%" is stale as a description of the run date.** A ~70–75%
  reversal *did* occur at the trough: ~$3,000–3,365/t in early 2026, down ~74%
  from ~$12,900. But cocoa then **rallied roughly 80% off its April 2026 lows
  to ~$5,100–5,670/t in Oct 2026** (TradingEconomics: ~$5,100 on 2026-10-07,
  ~$5,640 on 2026-10-12), on a large 2025/26 surplus offset by El Niño /
  2026/27 West African crop risk — Goldman Sachs was warning in Oct 2026 of a
  renewed squeeze risk. Measured at spot near the run, the drawdown from peak
  is ~53–58%, not ~70%. Any memo line using "~70% reversed" must be dated to
  the early-2026 trough, and the recovery thesis now carries a live
  cocoa-rebound risk that the dossier framing understates. — Walaw/Stonex and
  CropGPT price histories (early 2026); TradingEconomics, 2026-10-07 / 10-12;
  TheAgriBiz Goldman cocoa note, Oct 2026.
- Margin: **45.3% is the *reported* Q2 gross margin, not the adjusted one.**
  Hershey's Q2 release (2026-07-30, period ended 2026-06-28) shows reported
  gross margin **45.3%** (+1,480 bps) but **adjusted gross margin 41.6%**
  (+350 bps). Quoting 45.3% without the "reported" label overstates the
  underlying margin recovery by ~370 bps. — Hershey Q2 2026 release via
  PR Newswire/CRWE and StockTitan FAQ summary.

### 9. Moat/share: ~45% US chocolate share; Q2 volume ~-8% on ~+12% pricing; earnings 2026-10-29
**CONFIRMED — share figure is long-standing boilerplate, not a fresh 2026

## GS — Goldman Sachs (dossier claim underpinning one lens's #1 vote)

### 10. GS reported Q3 on 2026-10-13; second-best quarter ever; vs the Sep 16 Barclays warning of soft FICC and +$500M non-comp expenses
**CONFIRMED — with two precision notes: "second-best" is a *revenue* claim,
and the "Barclays warning" was Solomon speaking *at* the Barclays conference.**

- Primary: Goldman Sachs press release, **2026-10-13** — Q3 net revenues
  **$18.68B**, net earnings **$5.29B**, diluted EPS **$17.01**, annualised ROE
  18.7%; CEO David Solomon characterised the result, in the release, as the
  firm's **second-highest quarterly net revenues** and record year-to-date
  net revenues. It is not a claim of second-highest EPS or profit.
- vs consensus: EPS $17.01 vs ~$13.02–13.09 consensus and revenue $18.68B vs
  ~$16.9B consensus (Reuters/LSEG via Global Brands Magazine and TradingView/
  Seeking Alpha, 2026-10-13). Equities revenue +72% to $6.44B (financing a
  record $3.37B, advisory fees a record $1.65B); **FICC was essentially flat
  at ~$3.47B** — i.e. the soft-FICC warning materialised relatively, and was
  overwhelmed by equities/IB, exactly the tension the dossier claim describes.
- Sep 16 warning: Reuters (2026-09-16) and WSJ reported Solomon, speaking at
  the **Barclays Global Financial Services Conference on 2026-09-16**, saying
  FICC had been a little softer on a relative basis while equities stayed very
  strong, and that **non-compensation expenses would be more than $500M higher
  sequentially** in Q3 on transaction activity and accelerated tech investment.
  GS shares fell ~4% that day. It was Goldman's own guidance delivered at a
  Barclays-hosted event, not a Barclays analyst call. — Reuters, 2026-09-16;
  WSJ live coverage, 2026-09-16; Zacks recap, Sep 2026.
