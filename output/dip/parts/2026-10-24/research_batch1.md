## 5. GNRC — Generac Holdings Inc.

**Screen metrics (of record):** Price $188.02, −36.4% off 52-week high, −13.4% vs SMA200, forward P/E 14.50 (cheapest in batch), analyst upside +52.3%, revenue growth TTM +5.1%, operating margin 17.9%, net debt/EBITDA 1.79, next earnings 2026-10-28, flags NONE (per brief).

### 1. Why it's down — transitory or permanent?
**Verdict: Mostly transitory — a residential-guide trim and a revenue miss measured in 2%, against a C&I/data-center business inflecting violently upward. The impairment question is mix and margin, not demand existence.**

- Down ~35% since 2026-06-30 (Trefis, updated 2026-10-14) from the 52-week high of $296.44; −5.6% in the last month, −0.59% on 2026-10-23 to $186.92 (Finnhub) — drifting, not capitulating, into the print.
- **Q2 2026 (2026-07-29):** adj. EPS $2.91 vs $2.01–2.05 consensus (a ~$0.90 beat), but revenue $1.174B missed ~$1.20B consensus by ~2.3% despite +10.3–11% YoY growth. Residential was the soft side; C&I sales rose +29% YoY to $556M.
- **The guide trim that did the damage:** at the same print Generac cut its FY2026 residential growth outlook to "high single digits" (Trefis) — residential is the legacy core and the market read the trim as the consumer/outage cycle rolling over. The longer backdrop supports caution there: Q4 2025 (reported 2026-02-11, Reuters) revenue fell −12% YoY to $1.09B and missed, on weak outage activity — residential sales −23% to $571.87M — as fewer outages softened home-standby and portable demand. Residential demand is outage- and housing-driven and demonstrably lumpy.
- **Against impairment — the second engine is real and contracted:** data-center backlog reached ~$1.6B by July 2026 (~$1B of it booked in the prior 90 days, excluding a second hyperscaler's committed volumes); Q2 data-center revenue >$100M; FY26 data-center revenue guidance raised to ~$450M; C&I FY26 growth guidance raised to the low-30% range. Then on **2026-09-16** Generac signed a long-term supply agreement with **Amazon**: $2.4B of initial backup-generator deliveries across 2027–2028, with warrant vesting tied to up to $8B of qualifying payments (8-K, 2026-09-16). The stock rose +18.3% on 2026-09-17 — and has since given back the entire deal pop and more, which is the dip.
- **Change vs 2026-10-23:** no new company news in the last day; the Amazon deal and Q2 mix were already in yesterday's research. What is new in today's screen is only the unchanged price against a nearer print (4 days).

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 7/10
Brand + dealer/installer network in home standby (the default name in US residential backup power, with a large installed base pulling service/parts), and — newly — a validated seat in hyperscale backup generation: Amazon does not sign $2.4B initial / up-to-$8B-framework supply deals with commodity assemblers; qualification, megawatt-scale capacity (Generac is spending ~$250M through 2027 and added ~1,000 employees for MW-scale systems) and delivery reliability are the moat there. AI cannot substitute a physical generator; AI demand is the growth driver. Docked because residential standby is a consumer durable with real rivals (Cummins, Kohler, Briggs & Stratton) and no design-in lock-in comparable to semis — the home moat is brand/channel, not physics, and the data-center seat must be re-won against Cummins/Caterpillar-scale competitors each programme.
**Irreplaceability: 7/10** (unchanged).

### 3. Rebound catalyst
- **2026-10-28 Q3 print (4 days out):** the tests are (i) residential holding the trimmed high-single-digit guide (no second cut), (ii) C&I/data-center conversion of the $1.6B backlog, (iii) any quantification of the second hyperscaler agreement (signed 2026-06-24, 2027–28 terms still under negotiation at last disclosure) and of Amazon volumes beyond the $2.4B initial tranche.
- **Contracted 2027–28 ramp:** $2.4B of Amazon deliveries alone is roughly half a year of current total revenue, scheduled, with an $8B framework behind it; management's pre-Amazon target of ~$1B data-center sales by 2028 now looks conservative (BigGo/Zacks summaries, Sep–Oct 2026). Capacity spend (~$250M through 2027) is the execution bridge.
- Analyst response to the deal was upgrades, not cuts: JPMorgan target $265 (Overweight), Stephens $335 (Overweight), Canaccord $375 (Buy) — all September 2026, all still above today's price.
Timing: print in 4 days; the revenue ramp is a 2027 story, so the re-rating path is guide-confirmation now, delivery later.

### 4. Balance-sheet survival
Adequate, the most levered of the semi/AI names here but not stressed: screen net debt/EBITDA 1.79, debt/equity 0.43, current ratio 2.04, quick ratio 1.00 (Finnhub/MarketBeat, Oct 2026). It is self-funding a ~$250M capacity expansion while growing — no distress or dilution risk from the *debt* side. The one dilution item is equity-side and disclosed: Amazon warrants for up to 1,693,745 shares at $200.9266 (307,954 vested immediately) — ~2.6% of fully diluted shares if fully issued, earned only as Amazon payments arrive. Exact TTM FCF figure: **not found** in this pass (not substituted).

### 5. Margin of safety / valuation
Forward P/E 14.50 (screen) — the cheapest forward multiple in the batch by a wide margin, vs a trailing P/E of ~43–46 (Finnhub 46.25) that reflects depressed trailing residential earnings, and vs a ~19.9x historical forward average in one October compilation (Tradestie, 2026-10-23), which also notes the market is pricing in a ~10%/yr earnings *decline* against recent ~92% earnings growth — a pessimistic denominator. Analyst upside +52.3% (screen mean ≈ $286); post-Amazon targets cluster $265–375. **Genuinely cheap because of the dip?** On forward earnings, yes — this is the only batch name priced below its own historical forward multiple *and* below the market, with a contracted 2027–28 revenue ramp behind the estimates. The cheapness is paid for with mix risk: the growth is lower-margin C&I (see §7), so EPS conversion of the $2.4B is the thing to verify, not revenue.

### 6. Category position
#1 in US home standby generation by brand/channel (long-standing; a precise current share figure was **not found** in primary sources in this pass) — but residential is no longer where the growth or the question is. In data-center backup power Generac is the *challenger-insurgent*: $1.6B backlog, two hyperscale agreements, and the Amazon framework are share being *taken* in a market incumbents (Cummins, CAT) historically owned. Per-dollar-of-construction analysis (Rational Investment Management, 2026-10-07) sizes US data-center generator demand at roughly $2.7–7.2B/yr — Generac's entire C&I business sold <$1B in 2019, so even single-digit share is transformational. Pricing power: residential brand pricing is real but currently unexercised into a soft consumer; data-center pricing is contract/qualification-based, not yet proven at margin.

### 7. Value-trap risk — honest bear case
The trap version is coherent: residential — the high-margin annuity — has structurally reset lower (post-pandemic pull-forward, outage-dependent, housing-sensitive; Q4 2025 residential −23% is the template), while the replacement growth is lower-margin, capacity-hungry C&I/data-center revenue won against Cummins and CAT, with Zacks (2026-10-12) already flagging that rising lower-margin C&I mix "challenges profitability." RIM's base case (2026-10-07) has domestic C&I sales more than tripling by 2028 then *giving back ~40%* as the build-out normalises — i.e. even the bulls model this as a spike, not a plateau. Add $250M of capacity capex and ~1,000 heads added ahead of 2027 deliveries (fixed cost if Amazon/hyperscaler timing slips), warrant dilution, and a stock that even after −36% trades at ~43x trailing. If the 2026-10-28 print cuts residential again or pushes data-center revenue right, 14.5x forward becomes 14.5x on falling forwards.

### 8. Rebound score & verdict
**Rebound score: 6/10** (unchanged from 2026-10-23 research)
**Verdict:** The cheapest forward multiple in the batch with a signed $2.4B Amazon ramp behind it — but the rebound requires the soft, high-margin residential core to merely stabilise while lower-margin data-center volume carries growth, so it ranks below the semi/AI names whose dips sit on record prints.
