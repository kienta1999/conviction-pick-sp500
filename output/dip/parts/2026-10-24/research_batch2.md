Management's non-quantitative disclosure style (the lost account surfaced via sell-side notes months before acknowledgment) means trap-confirmation would arrive late.

### 8. Rebound score: 7/10 (unchanged from 2026-10-23)

## 5. TPR — Tapestry, Inc. (NEW to the panel today)

**Screen:** price $113.92, −30.6% off 52-week high ($164.80), −16.9% vs SMA200, fwd P/E 12.82, analyst upside +42.5% (implied mean target ~$162.3; a MarketBeat snapshot on 2026-10-19 put a $171.56 target on the name — targets are being cut, see §5), rev growth TTM +9.3% (screen basis; reported FY2026 revenue +14%, pro-forma +18% ex-Stuart Weitzman), op margin 19.8% (screen basis; FY2026 non-GAAP operating margin 23.4%), net debt/EBITDA 1.39, next earnings 2026-11-05. Finnhub 2026-10-23 close: $110.37 (−3.12%), i.e. the slide continued after the screen.

### 1. Why it's down — TRANSITORY (expectations / guidance reset on record results), with a real concentration question
- **The break was a single print:** FY2026 Q4/full-year results on **2026-08-13** — revenue $8.00B +14% (pro-forma +18%), non-GAAP EPS $7.05 +38%, operating margin 23.4% (+340bp), Coach revenue +24% to $6.91B — and the stock fell **~15–16.5% in one session, wiping ~$5.7B of market cap** (TS2, AInvest, BigGo, Aug 2026). The reported numbers beat (Q4 EPS $1.32 vs $1.28 consensus); what disappointed was the **FY2027 guide**: revenue $8.4–8.5B (midpoint $8.45B vs $8.46B consensus — a ~0.1% "miss") and EPS $7.80–7.90, implying growth decelerating from +14–18% to mid-single digits, with management guiding revenue growth stepping down from high-single digits in H1 to mid-single digits in H2 (TS2 analysis of the print).
- **The two substantive worries inside the guide:** (i) **Coach concentration** — Coach was ~86–87.5% of revenue, and its Q4 growth of +15% came in ~0.5pt below consensus, so the whole equity is now a single-brand momentum trade; (ii) **Kate Spade keeps shrinking** — FY2026 revenue −10% to $1.07B, Q4 −7% to $235.1M, with a Q3 segment operating loss of $20.7M cited in AInvest's post-print analysis. Between FY2019 and FY2026 Kate Spade revenue shrank ~21% with gross margin flat at ~63% while Coach's gross margin rose 70.2% → 78.8% (BusinessModelAnalyst, on the FY2026 10-K).
- **The de-rating since:** Trefis decomposes the 2026-06-30 → 2026-10-22 move ($145.83 → $113.92, −21.9%) as driven by a **−66.3% P/E-multiple change**, partly optical (FY2025 GAAP EPS was depressed to $0.82 by Capri-deal/Stuart charges; FY2026 GAAP EPS is $7.27) — but the direction is real: the market stopped paying a growth multiple for a decelerating guide. Target cuts continued this week: **UBS $232 → $200 (Buy) on 2026-10-20 and Jefferies $144 → $125 (Buy) on 2026-10-21** (Ad-hoc News/MarketScreener, Oct 2026).
- Nothing in the reported P&L is impaired: FY2026 targets set at the September 2025 Investor Day were hit **two years ahead of schedule** (BigGo summary of the release), 11M new customers were added (~35% Gen Z), and inventory *fell* to $826M from $861M.
- **Verdict: transitory — an expectations reset, not a demand break.** The permanent-impairment version requires Coach's brand heat to fade (see §7); it is not in the numbers yet.

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 6/10
- Moat type: **brand + pricing architecture + direct distribution**, not lock-in. Coach occupies the $300–$700 accessible-luxury tier that European houses (LVMH, Kering) vacated by pushing prices into the thousands, and it is converting that gap: handbag average unit retail up mid-teens in Q4 FY2026 *with volume growth*, FY2026 gross margin 76.6% (+120bp despite ~130bp of tariff/duty headwind), full-price selling discipline, and share gains cited across North America (+15% pro-forma), Greater China (+38%) and Europe (+29%) (company FY2026 release via StockTitan/Finviz; AInvest). 1,290 directly operated stores plus ~30% digital mix give it pricing presentation control; the "Tapestry Cloud" analytics platform is an internal margin tool.
- Why only 6/10: a fashion brand is a *rented* moat. There are no switching costs, no certification, no installed base — desirability must be re-earned every season (the Tabby line today), and Kate Spade inside the same house proves the same playbook fails when desirability goes. AI is neither a threat nor a saviour here: an LLM cannot commoditise a handbag, but generative marketing/design tools lower rivals' creative costs symmetrically, and social-media trend cycles — the real disruption vector — now move faster than any brand's planning cycle.
- **Irreplaceability 6/10:** AI/substitutes cannot route around Coach, but *fashion itself* can, and has — to Coach's own sister brand in reverse.

### 3. Rebound catalyst
- **FY2027 Q1 print, 2026-11-05 (12 days out):** the first test of the decelerating guide. A Coach print at/above the guided high-single-digit H1 pace, with Kate Spade's decline narrowing, re-rates a stock that has already paid for the slowdown; the guide's H1/H2 step-down means Q1 is the *easier* half to beat.
- **Buyback as a mechanical bid:** $1.35B of repurchases planned in FY2027 (~5–6% of market cap at the screen price), within ~$1.7B of total returns (also the FY2026 actual: $1.7B returned, including $1.35B of buybacks at an average ~$118/share — i.e. the company was buying above today's price); dividend raised 16% to $1.85/yr (~1.6% yield at screen).
- **Kate Spade optionality:** any credible stabilisation path for a $1.07B brand currently valued as a melting drag is pure upside to the multiple; conversely the reset (promotional pullback, assortment discipline) is already in the guide.
- Timing: binary first gate on 2026-11-05; the FY27 guide cadence (H1 stronger than H2) front-loads the proof.

### 4. Balance-sheet survival
- No survival question. FY2026 (10-K / release, 2026-08-13): cash + short-term investments $1.15B vs total borrowings $2.38B (net debt ~$1.23B, consistent with the screen's 1.39x net debt/EBITDA), gross leverage 1.1x adjusted EBITDA, operating cash flow $1.98B (vs $1.22B), adjusted FCF $1.86B (vs $1.35B), capex/cloud costs $217M. Screen net debt/EBITDA 1.39 includes lease-style obligations on broader definitions — either way, leverage is ~1x on the company's own measure and FCF covers the entire $1.7B return programme with room. Thin book equity ($692M) is a buyback artefact, not distress.

### 5. Margin of safety / valuation
- Screen fwd P/E 12.82; trailing P/E ~15.0–15.1 (Trefis/MarketBeat, Oct 2026) on GAAP EPS $7.27; on the FY2027 EPS guide midpoint ($7.85) the screen price is ~14.5x. Before the Aug 13 print the stock traded at ~44.6x LTM earnings on the distorted FY2025 EPS base (Trefis) and at a premium to peers and its own 5-year median — that premium is gone.
- Vs peers: Ralph Lauren trades at ~23.8x trailing with a $449 target vs a $377.84 price (MarketBeat, Oct 2026) — TPR is the cheap name in its own comp set on current earnings, despite faster FY2026 growth.
- Analyst mean: screen-implied ~$162.3 = +42.5%, but the live tape is cutting (UBS $200, Jefferies $125, both Buy, Oct 20–21) — the mean is a lagging anchor; even the *lowest fresh target found* ($125, Jefferies) sits +9.7% above the screen price, and the dispersion itself ($125–$200+) says the Street has not settled the deceleration question.
- **Genuinely cheap because of the dip?** Yes, on current earnings — 12.8x forward for a business that just grew EPS +38% with 76.6% gross margins and $1.86B of adjusted FCF is a real discount, not a multiple illusion. The discount is the market pre-paying for Coach deceleration and Kate Spade decay; §7 is the deciding section.

### 6. Category position
Coach is the **#1 accessible-luxury handbag brand in its tier and is gaining share** — from European houses that priced out the aspirational buyer, per the FY2026 regional numbers (Greater China +38%, Europe +29% pro-forma) and mid-teens AUR growth *with* volume growth, which is pricing power demonstrated, not asserted. But the group position is now a single-brand position: Coach is ~86% of revenue and >80% of profit, Kate Spade is a shrinking #2 brand in its segment with no credible recovery plan disclosed beyond "intentional choices," and the Stuart Weitzman divestiture (Aug 2025) and dead Capri deal mean there is no third leg. Category position: leader, concentrated.

### 7. Value-trap risk — honest bear case
Fashion is the trap mechanism. Coach's heat is product-cycle-driven (Tabby and core leather goods); if the cycle turns — and Gen Z, ~35% of new customers, is the fastest-moving cohort in consumer — revenue deceleration from high-single to mid-single digits becomes negative growth at exactly the moment the fixed cost of 1,290 stores and a stepped-up marketing budget (~12% of sales guided in earlier FY2026 commentary) deleverages margins. Kate Spade has already shrunk for two years under a deliberate reset; a third year converts "turnaround" into "melting asset" and removes the portfolio story entirely. Tariffs are a recurring ~$160–170M/yr headwind on a Vietnam/Cambodia/Philippines-concentrated supply chain (<10% China for core leather helps, but does not exempt). At 12.8x, the trap is not multiple collapse — it is owning a one-brand fashion cyclical at the top of its brand cycle, where $7.85 of guided EPS proves to be peak EPS.

### 8. Rebound score: 7/10
**Verdict:** A record year (EPS +38%, targets hit two years early) was sold for a decelerating guide and a shrinking second brand — at 12.8x forward with a $1.35B buyback, the dip prices in a Coach fade that has not happened yet, and the 2026-11-05 print is the first verdict on whether it will.
