## IDXX — Idexx Laboratories (~$522; -31.9% off 52w high $769.98; fwd P/E 31.2; next earnings 11/02/2026)

**1. Why it's down — TRANSITORY (valuation reset + cyclical visit softness).**
- The 2026 slide is a premium-multiple derating plus soft US clinic traffic, not a fundamentals break. Company reported Q2 2026 (Aug 4, 2026): revenue +9.7% YoY to $1.22B, EPS $4.27 (+18% as reported), beating consensus ($3.94 est), with CAG Diagnostics recurring revenue +10.3% organic; full-year guidance RAISED (rev $4.7–4.745B, EPS $14.69–$14.94). Yet the stock fell anyway — classic "priced for perfection" behavior.
- Headwinds cited: US same-store clinical visits down ~1.3% in Q2 (guided ~-1.5% in 2H); FX drag (~$15M revenue cut from full-year outlook); a Q4 2025 gross-margin miss (60.3% vs 61% consensus) that bruised confidence; executive turnover chatter (EVP departure, planned CEO transition) earlier in 2026.
- The drawdown predates 2026 too: -13.7% over the prior 12 months (Aug 2026) even while recurring diagnostics/software kept growing double digits — a sentiment/valuation story. **Verdict: temporary.**

**2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability: 9/10.**
- Razor-and-blade: installed instrument base (inVue Dx >9,000 after 1,602 Q2 placements; 5,200+ premium placements in Q2) locks clinics into IDEXX consumables, reference labs, and cloud PIMS software (recurring software revenue +10-12%). High-90s customer retention. Competition (Zoetis diagnostics, Mars/Heska) hasn't dented share. AI is an asset here, not a threat: IDEXX acquired CoVetAI (2026) to add workflow AI into its own ecosystem. Regulatory/clinical certification on tests adds lock-in.

**3. Rebound catalyst.**
- Q3 earnings 2026-11-02: another beat-and-raise into stabilizing US visit trends would force multiple re-rating. Product pipeline: inVue Dx FNA rollout, Cancer Dx multi-cancer panel expansion, Catalyst SDMA CLIPs. International CAG (customer adds + instrument placements) growing double digits — international mix shift is the multi-year compounder. Mgmt reaffirmed long-term framework: >10% organic revenue, >15% EPS growth (Morgan Stanley Healthcare conf).

**4. Balance-sheet survival.**
- Strong. Leverage 0.6x gross / 0.5x net of cash (CFO, Q2 2026 call). H1 2026: op cash flow $613M, FCF $557M, TTM net-income-to-FCF conversion 110%; full-year FCF conversion guided 90–100%. Aggressive buybacks: $332M in Q2, $693M YTD, ~2% YoY share-count reduction. No dilution risk.

**5. Margin of safety / valuation.**
- Cheaper than its own history but not optically cheap: fwd P/E ~31–37 (screen 31.2; Barchart 37.6 using $14.81 FY26E) vs its usual 35–45x premium band; PEG ~2.7–3.0. Analyst mean target $747 (MarketBeat, ~12 analysts, Moderate Buy) → ~+43% upside from ~$522. Simply Wall St narrative fair value $709 (~26% undervalued). The dip removed roughly a third of the premium — quality on sale-ish, not a deep-value steal.

**6. Category position.**
- Still #1 in companion-animal diagnostics with pricing power (~4% global net price realization in CAG recurring). International share gains; US visit softness is industry-wide, not share loss.

**7. Value-trap risk.**
- US vet-visit softness could prove structural (post-COVID adoption unwind + consumer pullback on pet spend), turning 10% growers into 5% growers while the multiple stays in the 30s. Margin sensitivity: any further gross-margin miss gets punished hard at this multiple. Serial guidance "raises" that keep undershooting whisper numbers could grind the multiple down another turn.

**8. Rebound score: 7/10. Verdict:** A dominant compounder derated for perfection — fundamentals intact and beating, but the multiple still demands near-flawless execution, capping rebound torque.

## LVS — Las Vegas Sands (~$37.8; -44.6% off 52w high $70.45; fwd P/E 10.8; next earnings 10/21/2026)

**1. Why it's down — TRANSITORY but China-exposed (macro/regulatory, not moat loss).**
- Q2 2026 (Jul 22, 2026) was a genuine miss: adjusted EPS $0.59 vs $0.77–0.79 consensus (-23%), revenue $3.15B vs $3.37B est (-6.4%, -0.7% YoY); profit $373M vs $519M a year ago. Stock fell ~6% after-hours and kept bleeding to a 52-week low $38.06 (Sep 9, 2026); YTD -41.9%.
- Causes were largely one-off/cyclical: "unusually low hold in rolling play" in Macau cut adjusted property EBITDA by $87M; June Macau gambling revenue softened as bettors diverted spending to the FIFA World Cup; crypto weakness + tighter Chinese capital-flow controls pressured VIP.
- Counter-evidence it's temporary: Q1 2026 was strong (adjusted EPS $0.91 vs $0.78 est; Macau rev +23.7%, Singapore +27.9%); H1 2026 revenue +11.6% to $6.74B, net income $1.01B, EPS +20%. Gaming VOLUMES grew across all Macau segments in Q2 — the miss was yield (hold), not demand. Mizuho/Stifel cut targets ($67→$61; $74→$60) but kept Outperform/Buy. **Verdict: temporary**, with the caveat that Macau recovery pace and Beijing policy are perpetual overhangs.

**2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability: 7/10.**
- Oligopoly: one of six Macau concessionaires (license moat, government-capped supply); Marina Bay Sands is the best asset in Singapore. Integrated-resort economics are capital-intensity + regulatory-barrier moats. AI cannot substitute a casino floor. The threat is regulatory (Beijing anti-corruption/capital controls, concession renewal politics) and demand cyclicality — not disruption by technology.

**3. Rebound catalyst.**
- Hold normalization: rolling-play hold is mean-reverting; a single normal quarter reverses the $87M EBITDA drag. Q3 earnings 2026-10-21: volume growth + normalized hold + Singapore strength (MBS mass gaming +5% in Q2 to $886M) could print a clean beat. Golden Week / Q4 Macau visitation data and any capital-flow easing are sector tailwinds. Massive buyback: $1.542B repurchased in H1, authorization expanded to $6.0B through July 2029 — mechanical support at these prices.

**4. Balance-sheet survival.**
- No distress. Cash $3.38B (6/30/2026); gross margins ~79%; Q2 op income $618M. Leverage is elevated (capital-intensive resorts, ~$19B total liabilities) but the company is buying back billions and generating >$4B annual EBITDA (TTM Dec-2025 EBITDA $4.50B). No dilution risk; dividend maintained historically. FCF figure: not found in sources reviewed.

**5. Margin of safety / valuation.**
- Optically the cheapest of the four: fwd P/E ~10.8 (screen), trailing ~23 (depressed by the weak quarter). Analyst mean target $59.84 (MarketBeat, 10 Buy/9 Hold/1 Sell, Sep 2026) → ~+54–58% upside from ~$38. PEG ~0.53 (InvestingPro via ad-hoc-news). 79% gross margins at a 10–11x forward multiple is deep-value territory *if* earnings are mid-cycle. The catch: Macau earnings are arguably peak-of-fragile-cycle, so "E" quality matters (see flag).

**6. Category position.**
- Still the scale leader in Macau + the dominant Singapore property. Share not being lost — the issue is the size of the Macau pie and hold luck, not competitive erosion. New Cotai supply (Wynn Palace-class expansions) adds competitive intensity but LVS's footprint is the largest.

**7. Value-trap risk.**
- The bear case is Beijing, not Vegas: a renewed anti-gambling campaign, capital-control tightening, or a China slowdown that stalls Macau's recovery at a lower plateau would strand the multiple. Macau has contracted before; if volumes — not just hold — roll over, this is a 10x P/E on a shrinking number. Rolling-play credit exposure means a junket/VIP crackdown hits cash collection, not just the P&L.

**8. Rebound score: 5/10. Verdict:** Deep-value optics on a mean-reverting miss, but the earnings-quality flag and permanent China-policy overhang make this the highest falling-knife risk of the four.

**LVS earnings-quality flag — RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN, one-line verdict:** Watch-item, not fatal — AR rose to $622M at Q2 2026 (MarketBeat: $417M at Q4 2024 → $677M Q1 2026 → $622M Q2 2026), up ~17% YoY against -0.7% revenue, consistent with Macau rolling-play credit markers growing with volumes while low hold reduced cash collected — but receivables outrunning revenue is exactly the value-trap signature, so confirm collection trends on the Q3 call (10/21).

## TPR — Tapestry (~$115; -30.5% off 52w high $164.80; fwd P/E 12.8; next earnings 11/05/2026)

**1. Why it's down — TRANSITORY (expectations reset, not brand impairment).**
- The -30% from the August high came from guidance, not results. FY2026 (reported Aug 13, 2026) was superb: revenue $8.0B (+14% reported; +18% pro forma ex-Stuart Weitzman), adjusted EPS $7.05 (+38% from $5.10), Coach +24% to $6.915B. The stock fell ~16.5–17% anyway because FY2027 revenue guidance ($8.4–8.5B, straddling the $8.47B consensus) implied Coach's extraordinary 24% growth normalizes to high-single-digits — a classic peak-growth derating. Same pattern as spring 2026 (fell 12% on a Q3 beat-and-raise).
- Tariff overhang: ~230bp of tariff/duty drag embedded in FY2026 guidance (~$160M cost), FY2027 guide assumes mid-20s% US import tariffs "neutral net" — a watch item, not a surprise. Kate Spade is the structural drag: -10% in FY2026 to $1.075B, guided to another high-single-digit decline + operating loss in FY2027. **Verdict: temporary** — the market repriced growth expectations while the underlying brand momentum is intact.

**2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability: 8/10.**
- Brand/desirability moat: Coach is the #1 accessible-luxury handbag brand, resonating with Gen Z (35% of ~11M new FY2026 customers Gen Z; Tabby line viral at $295–$725 price points). Pricing power: handbag average unit retail up mid-teens; Coach grew 10% North America, 30% Greater China, 25% Europe in Q4. AI cannot design cultural desirability; fashion brands are effectively disruption-proof to tech substitution. Rival Michael Kors/Capri is the one struggling — confirming Coach is taking share, not losing it.

**3. Rebound catalyst.**
- Execution against the "conservative" FY2027 guide: Coach growing HSD while Kate Spade's decline is contained = beat-and-raise cadence resumes; Q1 FY2027 prints 2026-11-05. Buyback machine: $1.35B repurchased in FY2026 (~11.5M shares @ ~$118 avg), another $1.35B authorized for FY2027, plus a 16%-raised dividend ($1.85 annual). Supply-chain shift (Vietnam/Cambodia/India) de-risks tariffs structurally.

**4. Balance-sheet survival.**
- Healthy: $1.15B cash + short-term investments vs $2.38B total borrowings at FY2026-end; net debt ~$1.2B; gross leverage 1.1x adjusted EBITDA. FY2026 adjusted FCF $1.86B (up from $1.35B), ~23% FCF margin, ROIC 19.7%. Returned $1.7B to shareholders ($1.35B buybacks + $326M dividends) and plans another ~$1.7B in FY2027. No distress, no dilution.

**5. Margin of safety / valuation.**
- Genuinely cheap *because of the dip*: fwd P/E ~12.8–14.7 (screen 12.8) vs FY2027 EPS guide $7.80–$7.90; analyst mean target $175.12 (MarketBeat, 21 analysts, Moderate Buy, Sep 30, 2026) → ~+52% upside from ~$115. JPMorgan target $211 (Overweight, Aug 4). Peer Ralph Lauren trades at richer multiples. The dip took it from growth-multiple to value-multiple while EPS keeps compounding.

**6. Category position.**
- #1 in accessible luxury with clear pricing power and share gains (Coach +24% while Capri lowered its FY2027 outlook). The portfolio's problem is concentration — Coach carries everything — but concentration in a winner is not share loss.

**7. Value-trap risk.**
- Coach's 24% growth was a once-in-a-cycle brand heat moment; if FY2027's HSD guide proves optimistic and growth stalls at 0–3%, the stock deserves a 10x multiple and the "cheap" thesis dies. Kate Spade could become a permanent capital sink if the turnaround never inflects. Tariff escalation beyond the embedded mid-20s% assumption hits the 78% gross margin directly.

**8. Rebound score: 8/10. Verdict:** The cleanest dip thesis of the four — a #1 brand at peak momentum, derated on guidance conservatism, with a cash-machine balance sheet funding aggressive buybacks.

## BKNG — Booking Holdings (~$163 post 25:1 split; -25.6% off 52w high $225; fwd P/E 13.1; next earnings 10/27/2026)

**1. Why it's down — TRANSITORY (narrative + sentiment, not demand).**
- September was the damage: -18.5% in the trailing 30 days (Aug 31 $199 → Sep 29 $162; worst month since June 2022). Three drivers: (a) EU General Court upheld the block on the eTraveli acquisition — a strategic setback for the Connected Trip/flight initiative; (b) AI-agent disruption fears around Meta's Muse (most-downloaded app; Expedia partnered with Muse Sep 2026, and BKNG slid 3.8% on no company news as investors repriced OTA disintermediation risk); (c) soft Q3 guidance (revenue +4–6%) and oil-price/macro jitters.
- The data contradicts the fear: Q2 2026 (Aug 4) beat — room nights +5%, gross bookings +9%, revenue +8%, adjusted EPS +15% to $2.54; FY2025: revenue $26.9B (+13%), FCF $9.1B, room nights +8% (1.235B). Connected Trip growing +30% YoY. The AI-fear is narrative-driven; even analysts calling it overstated (BTIG reaffirmed Buy, $250 target). **Verdict: temporary** — though the AI-disintermediation question is the one structural thread to monitor.

**2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability: 7/10.**
- Two-sided network effects: world's largest OTA by gross bookings; 35% operating margins; unmatched lodging inventory, payments/merchant infrastructure (70% merchant mix), and performance-marketing data flywheel. Survived Google Hotels' entry. The Muse threat is real in theory — an agent that books directly could bypass aggregators — but agents still need inventory, payments, cancellation handling, and customer service: Booking's infrastructure, and OTAs are integrating (Expedia-with-Muse shows the adaptation path). Direct mix is already >mid-50% of room nights. Score reflects a genuine, unresolved disruption debate, not a verdict.

**3. Rebound catalyst.**
- Q3 earnings 2026-10-27: stabilization of room-night growth (guided 3–5%) and any AI-integration metrics (conversion lift, Connected Trip attach) would puncture the narrative. $650M run-rate cost savings by end-2027 supports margin expansion. Buyback regime change: $3.7B repurchased in Q2 alone (~$14B annualized pace), $14.5B authorization remaining at 6/30 — ~10%/yr float shrink at this valuation is the quiet compounder. First-time dividend + CEO pay cut signal management confidence.

**4. Balance-sheet survival.**
- Fortress, asset-light: FY2025 FCF $9.1B; TTM FCF >$8B; negative book equity (N/A D/E) is an artifact of buybacks exceeding retained earnings, not distress. Merchant-model FCF conversion >1.5x net income. One caution from deep-dive work: watch that buybacks stay inside FCF and net debt stays stable — the aggressive pace is funded by cash flow, but verify quarterly.

**5. Margin of safety / valuation.**
- Genuinely cheap *because of the dip*: fwd P/E ~13.1–16.4 (screen 13.1; MarketBeat fwd 16.35) — multiples 26–34% below 5-year medians per independent DCF work; FCF yield ~7%. Analyst mean target $236.70 (MarketBeat, 37 analysts, Moderate Buy) → ~+45% upside from ~$163. Base fair value $205–235 in that work; bear case $125–145 if distribution economics truly weaken. PEG ~1.1. This is the rare quality compounder priced like a no-growth utility.

**6. Category position.**
- Still #1 global OTA by gross bookings with expanding share (airline tickets +37% to 68M, attractions +80% in FY2025). Not losing share — the multiple compression is fear of the future, not evidence of the present.

**7. Value-trap risk.**
- If AI agents structurally reroute booking flow and compress OTA take rates over 3–5 years, today's 13x P/E is the market correctly pricing a melting moat — and the "cheap" multiple is a trap. Regulatory risk (EU DMA, Italian antitrust) could also structurally raise costs. Watch KPIs: room-night growth, marketing spend as % of gross bookings, direct mix, Connected Trip attach.

**8. Rebound score: 7/10. Verdict:** The highest-quality business of the four at the lowest relative multiple — the AI fear is real but unproven by any operating data, and the buyback alone does heavy lifting; downgrade risk is binary on the disintermediation thesis.
