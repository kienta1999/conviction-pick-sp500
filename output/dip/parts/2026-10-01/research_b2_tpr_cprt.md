# 5. TPR — Tapestry (Apparel Retail)

### 1. Why it's down

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability

DTC control, data-driven product/marketing ("logic and magic"), and 11M new customers in FY26 (Gen Z-led, ~9M at Coach) show relevance, not nostalgia. No AI disruption threat to leather goods; AI is a tool (demand prediction), not a substitute. Weakness: fashion brands are rented, not owned — heat cycles turn, and Kate Spade proves Tapestry cannot manufacture heat at will. **Irreplaceability score: 6/10** (Coach alone ~7–8; portfolio ~6).

# 6. CPRT — Copart (Commercial Services)

### 1. Why it's down
A slow, multi-quarter volume de-rating capped by a bad print. FY26 (ended 31 Jul 2026, reported 10 Sep 2026): revenue $4.7B, +0.4% YoY; net income $1.48B, -4.4%; EPS $1.55 vs $1.59. Q4: revenue $1.15B (+2.4%, beat), but EPS $0.35 missed $0.38 consensus (-14.6% YoY), net income -17.4%, gross profit -5.5%, operating margin 32% vs 36.7% a year ago. Drivers: (a) US insurance volumes down — Q4 domestic insurance assignments -7.5%, the 6th consecutive quarter of shrinking US insurance volume; global units sold -2.9% in Q4, -5.5% FY; collision claim frequency -3.4% YoY (fewer accidents reaching insurers); (b) a tough comp — FY25 was inflated by Hurricane Helene/Milton catastrophe volume; (c) cost inflation ahead of volume — facility opex +7.7% in Q4, opex/unit +12.7–13%, G&A +16.6%, on investment in long-haul delivery, Title Express and wholesale facilities, plus lower interest income after $1.63B of buybacks shrank the cash pile; (d) the 10 Sep 2026 announcement of the $1.9B all-cash ACV Auctions acquisition (at $10.50/share, 45% premium) added execution/price doubt, followed by HSBC's downgrade to Hold ($36) on 16–17 Sep. Stock: -30% YTD, -39.5% 1-yr, $27.29 vs 52w high $45.89. **Temporary-vs-permanent verdict: Mostly transitory/cyclical (claim frequency, catastrophe comp, deliberate cost investment, one customer loss) — but with a genuine permanent-impairment question attached: share loss to a reviving IAA/RB Global (see §6–7). Not yet impairment; no longer dismissible as pure macro.**

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability
One of the widest moats in the index: a two-sided network + land + workflow duopoly. Copart/IAA together control ~80% of US salvage auctions; Copart ~58% share per one university equity study (UNC Kenan-Flagler, Dec 2025, citing ~300K+ fragmented buyers, network effects 10/10, switching costs 9/10). Assets: 250+ owned/controlled yards in 11 countries (land ownership vs IAA's leased yards is a structural cost/ permanence edge — zoning/permits for salvage yards are scarce), VB3 online platform since 2003, ~1M members in 185+ countries creating auction liquidity that raises seller recoveries, and deep insurer workflow integration (insurers supplied 81% of vehicles in FY25). AI is an enhancer (imaging, pricing, title processing), not a substitute — someone must physically tow, store, title and auction a wrecked car. Threat is competitive (IAA service recovery), not technological. **Irreplaceability score: 9/10.**

### 3. Rebound catalyst
(i) Lapping: the single lost insurance customer and the Helene/Milton catastrophe comp both annualize during FY27 — ex-that-customer, Q4 domestic insurance assignments would have been +2.3%. (ii) Structural total-loss tailwind still intact: total-loss frequency hit a record Q2 level of 23.3% in FY26, average collision severity >$6,300 (+8.8% YoY), repair costs >50% above 2019, vehicles as "computers on wheels" — more wrecks are uneconomic to repair every year; only claim *frequency* is cyclically soft. (iii) International: Q4 revenue +11.7% to $222.1M, units +10%, now profitable in all markets, German model as European template. (iv) Non-insurance inflected in Q4: dealer units +5.8%, BlueCar (banks/rental/fleet) ~+20%. (v) ACV closes by end-CY2026 (expected breakeven FY27, accretive FY28) adding ~800K units / ~$10B GMV of whole-car volume. (vi) Founder Jay Adair back as Exec Chairman/CEO with an explicit return-to-growth mandate. Timing: evidence should appear at Q1 FY27 on 2026-11-19 and build through H1 FY27 as comps ease.

### Earnings-quality flag — RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN (explicit)
Screen flag: receivables/revenue divergence 3.203; CFO/net income 1.09; accrual ratio -0.014. The underlying data (FY26 statements, 10 Sep 2026 release): net receivables rose $763M → $859M (+12.6%) in FY26 while revenue rose only +0.4%, and the cash-flow statement shows an $80.6M receivables build (vs $34.0M in FY25) — so the divergence is real, not a data error. Benign structural reasons dominate, however: (1) Copart is predominantly an *agent* — service revenue (~83% of total) is fees recognized when the auction completes, while the gross vehicle proceeds flow to the seller; receivables are therefore mainly buyer amounts and insurer service/title fees in transit, and they scale with *gross transaction value and unit mix*, not with net fee revenue — revenue can be flat while receivables rise if mix shifts. (2) Mix did shift exactly that way: principal-basis vehicle sales grew +8.3% in Q4 (gross receivables per unit far larger than fee-only units) and international revenue grew +11.7% (longer collection cycles) while US fee volume fell. (3) Title Express / lien and title-processing timing adds receivables that collect after processing completes. Critically, the value-trap signature is absent where it matters: FY26 CFO $1,604M *exceeds* net income $1,480M (CFO/NI 1.09, as flagged), the accrual ratio is slightly negative (-0.014, i.e. earnings are cash-backed, not accrual-inflated), and the credit-loss allowance rose ($5.6M vs $0.35M) but remains trivial vs $859M receivables. **Flag verdict: BENIGN-LEANING / MONITOR — a mix-and-timing receivables build in an agent model, with cash conversion still >1x; it becomes a trap signal only if receivables keep compounding double-digit while CFO/NI falls below 1.**

### 5. Margin of safety / valuation

GuruFocus (28 Sep 2026) put the 5-yr median P/E at 32.94x — Copart traded at 28–45x for most of the last decade as a presumed perpetual compounder, so today's multiple is roughly half its norm; FCF yield ~4.7% cited by Seeking Alpha (Sep 2026). RB Global trades at a materially higher multiple despite lower margins/ROIC (CPRT screen ROE 27%, op margin 36.2%). Verdict: cheap *because of the dip* relative to its own history and cash generation; the discount is the market pricing in ex-growth — if volume growth merely resumes at low-single-digits, multiple + earnings both recover.

Pricing power intact (ASP gains held revenue +2.4% on -2.9% units), but no longer unchallenged.

### 7. Value-trap risk
Honest bear case: the "one customer" is not idiosyncratic but the first proof that insurers will move volume to a rehabilitated IAA for price/service — six straight quarters of US insurance volume decline is a trend, not noise. Layer on: structurally falling collision frequency (ADAS/driver-assistance reduces accidents even as severity rises — the frequency leg of Copart's model may be in secular decline), capacity built for growth now deleveraging costs (+13% opex/unit), a $1.9B acquisition at a 45% premium into an adjacent whole-car market where Manheim/ACV incumbency is strong, and a stock that at 15.6x forward is only cheap if mid-teens historical growth was not the anomaly. If FY27 shows flat units and another customer loss, this is a former compounder settling at a deserved mid-teens multiple — the classic trap.

### 8. Rebound score and verdict
**Rebound score: 7/10.** Verdict: A fortress-moat, net-cash compounder priced at half its historical multiple for cyclical volume and one contract loss — buyable dip, but size it as a show-me story until 19 Nov proves US insurance assignments have actually inflected.
