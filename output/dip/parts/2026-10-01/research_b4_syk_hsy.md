# 12. SYK — Stryker (Health Care Equipment)

### 1. Why it's down — Temporary-vs-permanent verdict: TRANSITORY (multiple compression + two company-specific shocks, not demand loss)
Three stacked shocks, none is a demand collapse. (a) **Cyber wiper attack, 11 Mar 2026:** Iran-linked Handala / Void Manticore (MOIS, Storm-0842) abused a compromised Microsoft Intune Global Admin credential to factory-reset 80,000+ devices (attacker claimed 200,000) across 79 countries; Stryker's 8-K said no ransomware/malware. Medical devices, Mako, Vocera, LIFEPAK were architecturally isolated and unaffected, but corporate/ordering systems were disrupted for days, Q1 earnings were materially hit, and the stock fell ~8% on disclosure to ~$336-345. Facilities have run uninterrupted since April (CEO Kevin Lobo, Q2 call). (b) **Peripheral vascular (Inari) supply disruption:** the $4.9B Inari acquisition (early 2025) left a manufacturing/supply problem — US vascular organic sales -6.7% in Q2, meaningful backorders. Management in July expected a manageable level by end-Q3; on **8 Sep 2026 CFO Preston Wells said at the Wells Fargo Healthcare Conference the issue persists into Q4** — stock plunged 8.8-8.9% that day to a 52w low ~$275, >$10B of value wiped, Hagens Berman investigation announced, Wells Fargo cut target $418→$348. (c) **Tariffs + premium-multiple fatigue:** guided tariff cost ~$400M in 2026 (vs ~$200M in 2025, incremental $200M in H1; Q2 actually got a net tariff *refund* benefit). Yet operations kept beating: FY2025 sales >$25B (+11% organic), Q2 2026 (30 Jul) sales $6.6B +9.4%, organic +9.0% (all volume), adj. EPS $3.69 +17.9%, adj. operating margin 27.4% (+170bp). Guidance was *narrowed/raised at the low end* to 8.3-9.3% organic and $14.95-15.10 adj. EPS. AInvest (Jun 2026) framed the earlier 16.5% slide exactly as "multiple compression, not broken fundamentals" after four straight beats. Why deepest dip in the quality set despite +11% growth: it started from the richest multiple (~25x forward pre-attack, ~41x trailing in one early-2026 snapshot), so the same growth disappointment (9% vs hoped-for 10%+) de-rates furthest; add a geopolitical cyber event no peer had, plus back-half execution risk concentrated in H2-weighted guidance. **Verdict: transitory** — procedure demand, backlog and margins are intact; the impairments are IT remediation, one plant/supply chain, and tariffs.

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — Irreplaceability score: 9/10
Installed-base + surgeon-training moat. Mako: >3,000 systems installed worldwide, >2.5M procedures, 47 countries; Q2 2026 was a *record* quarter for Mako installations (US + international), utilization climbing. A hospital that buys Mako locks in Stryker implants (Triathlon knees, hips), instruments and service for a decade+; surgeons train on one robotic ecosystem and rarely dual-train. Breadth reinforces it: #1/#2 category-leadership doctrine across Orthopaedics, MedSurg (instruments, endoscopy, beds/stretchers), Neurotechnology and now vascular — cross-selling into the same hospital capital budget. Versus Intuitive Surgical's da Vinci: not head-to-head — ISRG owns soft-tissue (general/gyn/urology), SYK owns orthopaedic bone-cutting; da Vinci 5 expansion validates hospital robotics budgets rather than displacing Mako. Real rivals are Zimmer Biomet's ROSA (new ROSA Shoulder cleared, first cases 2026) and J&J's Ottava/VELYS — credible, but SYK is gaining, not losing, ortho robotics share, and Mako Shoulder + Mako RPS (handheld, full US launch 2026) extend the platform. AI is an *input* (pre-op planning, PROPHECY guidance) that strengthens the installed base, not a substitute for a robot, an implant, or a trained surgeon. No software model replaces a hip.

### 3. Rebound catalyst
Concrete and dated: (i) **Q3 print 29 Oct 2026** — proof point is vascular backorders reaching "manageable" level and backlog conversion; management guided recovery of deferred vascular sales thereafter. (ii) H2-weighted delivery: elevated capital-equipment backlog, zero cancellations at Q2, record Mako installs annualize into implant pull-through in 2027. (iii) Tariff refunds + manufacturing-footprint optimization already flattered Q2 gross margin (66% adj.); guided $400M is known/quantified, so any trade relief is upside. (iv) Buyback resumption: ~$1B remaining authorization, management said opportunistic repurchases resume in Q3 — at $273 the buyback is suddenly accretive. Timeline: sentiment can turn on the 29 Oct call if vascular language changes from "persists" to "normalizing"; full multiple repair likely needs two clean quarters (Q3 + Q4/FY guide in late Jan 2027).

### 4. Balance-sheet survival
No distress risk. Screen net debt/EBITDA 1.59. At 30 Jun 2026: cash & equivalents $3.39B (cash + marketable securities ~$3.5B), long-term debt $14.19B, total debt ~$15.2B, equity $23.99B, total assets $47.93B. H1 operating cash flow $1.84B (vs $1.36B yr ago); Q2 FCF ~$1.1B (+14% YoY). Interest $141M in Q2 — covered ~12x by operating income. Dividend $0.88/qtr (+4.8% YoY), $674M paid H1, well covered. Caveat: goodwill + intangibles ~$25.3B (>50% of assets) after Inari — an impairment would be non-cash but reputationally ugly. Leverage was raised *for* Inari, not by operating losses; deleveraging resumes if M&A pauses.

### 5. Margin of safety / valuation
Screen fwd P/E 16.3; on the company's own FY26 guide ($14.95-15.10 adj. EPS) $272.96 is ~18.2x — either way, versus ~25x forward pre-attack (Mar 2026 research notes) and a long-run habit of mid-20s forward / low-30s trailing (trailing still 28.6x on GAAP EPS depressed by cyber/Inari charges), this is the cheapest SYK has screened in years *because of the dip*, not because estimates collapsed (consensus FY26 EPS still ~$14.95-15.02). Peers: ResMed ~21.9x trailing, medtech sector historically ~30x+ P/E; SYK now trades at a *discount* to slower peers despite 8-9% organic growth vs sector mid-single digits. Analyst targets are being cut from above, which cuts both ways: Wells Fargo $348 (8 Sep, Overweight kept), Stifel $330 (29 Sep, Buy kept), TD Cowen $355→$298 (30 Sep) — yet even the lowest, $298, is +9% and screen-implied mean (~$369, +35.2%) sits well above price. Note the dispersion = the market is pricing execution, not solvency. Margin of safety: moderate-good — you are paid if growth merely stays at the *bottom* of guidance; you are not paid if 2027 growth resets to 5-6%.

### 6. Category position
Still #1/#2 with pricing power in its core. US knees +6.2% in Q2 on Mako pull-through; Orthopaedics organic +8.6%, MedSurg & Neuro +9.2%; SYK has outgrown Zimmer Biomet and J&J in knees/hips over the last 12 months (MD+DI, Q4 2025 review). Guidance embeds modestly *positive* price — hospitals are still accepting price, the cleanest pricing-power test in medtech. The exception is vascular/ischemic, where it is the challenger (Inari) losing momentum to competition and its own supply failure — a sub-segment, not the core.

### 7. Value-trap risk (bear case)
The H2 guide is back-loaded and management has already been wrong once on vascular ("resolved" → "persists into Q4"); a second miss on 29 Oct, or a cut to the 8.3% low end, would confirm a disclosure/credibility problem (hence the securities investigation) and could de-rate it to a mid-teens multiple on lower EPS — i.e. $220-240, another -15-20%. Structural bears: tariffs ratchet above $400M in 2027 with no pricing offset in Europe (EU MDR delays, soft EU capital budgets); Mako saturation in US knees shifts growth to lower-margin international installs; Inari proves a $4.9B overpay (goodwill write-down); another cyber event — the attack vector (single Intune admin credential) was embarrassingly simple. Insider selling (~351k shares, ~$118M last quarter per Wells Fargo-day reporting) is not reassuring.

### 8. Rebound score: 8/10 — Verdict: Deepest dip in the set for the best reason — a de-rating of intact 9% volume growth after a cyber shock and one supply failure — so SYK is the batch's highest-quality rebound, contingent on vascular normalization language on 29 Oct.

# 13. HSY — Hershey (Packaged Foods & Meats)

### 1. Why it's down — Temporary-vs-permanent verdict: MOSTLY TRANSITORY on costs, PARTLY STRUCTURAL on volume — a split verdict, and the batch's hardest call
The drawdown is a two-year cocoa shock working through in reverse. West African disease/weather (black pod, swollen-shoot; Côte d'Ivoire + Ghana ≈70% of world supply) drove ICE cocoa to a record ~$12,931/t in Dec 2024 (~$12,900 peak). Hershey's FY2025 adj. EPS collapsed ~one-third to $6.31; in the worst quarter gross margin printed 37% (-17pp). HSY raised prices repeatedly (+9% price / -3% volume in Q4 2025; ~+12pts price / -8pts volume in Q2 2026; North America Confectionery ~+14 / -10). TTM revenue growth is -1.0% on the screen because 2025's price-led, volume-negative mix plus a weak base rolled through. Then the commodity reversed: cocoa fell ~50% in H1 2026 alone, bottomed ~$2,846/t in Mar 2026 (-78% from peak), rallied to ~$6,478 in July, and was ~$5,400-5,711 in mid-late Sep 2026 — still ~55% below peak but ~2x pre-2023 norms ($1,756-2,943 range, 2017-Feb 2023). StoneX projects surpluses of 287kt (2025/26) and 267kt (2026/27); ICE stocks at 19-month / 2-year highs. So Q2 2026 (30 Jul) looked like recovery: sales $2.79B +6.6%, adj. EPS $1.90 vs $1.43 consensus (+57% YoY), adj. gross margin 41.6% (+350bp). Yet the stock kept falling to a 52w low $161.43 (Sep 2026) and $157.61 on the screen — Trefis attributes the May-Sep -16.2% slide to a -38.7% P/E compression while LTM revenue actually rose 1.4%. Why: investors judge the recovery as *price, not volume* (AInvest, Sep 2026) — units are still falling high-single-digits, Hershey *lost US candy/mint/gum share in Q2*, and Jefferies (15 Sep) flagged a possible record Super El Niño in 2026/27 as a cocoa re-spike risk. **Verdict: the margin/cost leg is transitory and already reversing (management: "good visibility into deflation next year from cocoa" — CFO at Barclays, Sep 2026; 2027 deflation guided on Q1/Q2 calls). The volume/share leg is not proven transitory** — that is the whole debate in §7.

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — Irreplaceability score: 6/10
Brand + distribution moat, real but narrower than it looks. Reese's is America's #1 candy brand; Hershey's, Kit Kat (US license), Twizzlers, plus salty (SkinnyPop, Dot's Pretzels, Pirate's Booty, LesserEvil acquired 2025). >35% share of the US chocolate aisle (Euromonitor via Tickeron), unmatched seasonal (Halloween ≈ a fifth of annual confectionery retail sales; Easter) and checkout/impulse distribution, and Hershey Trust control makes it takeover-proof (a governance moat that also blocks an exit premium). But: chocolate is a *recipe*, not an installed base — Mars (Snickers/M&M's), Mondelez, Nestlé, Lindt (premium, growing with GLP-1 users per its own reporting) and private label can all replicate the product; switching costs are zero. AI does not disrupt chocolate, but GLP-1s are the functional substitute — see §7. Irreplaceability is emotional/habitual, not structural: strong, erodible.

### 3. Rebound catalyst
(i) **Cocoa deflation into 2027:** hedges roll off at lower futures; Q3 2026 laps the highest cocoa cost + full tariff brunt of Q3 2025 (CEO Kirk Tanner on Q2 call: Q3 is the strongest YoY earnings-growth quarter for exactly that reason). FY26 guide already $8.36-8.52 adj. EPS (from $6.31 in 2025) on +4-5% sales; gross-margin expansion guided <400bp for FY26 with more in 2027. (ii) **Halloween/Q3 print 30 Oct 2026:** early-season snack-size chocolate sales +double-digits YoY (Reuters, 19 Aug); a volume inflection (decline narrowing from -8/-10pts toward flat) is the single data point that re-rates the stock. (iii) Innovation pipeline: TD Cowen (1 Sep, Buy, $210) cites a 5-year pipeline worth ~$1.5B of sales; Zero Sugar dollar sales grew ~4.3x 2020-2025; salty snacks give a non-chocolate growth leg (NA Salty +28% in Q4 2025). (iv) Dividend resumed growing Feb 2026 ($1.452/qtr) after a 5-quarter freeze — a management confidence signal. Timeline: margin story pays through 2027 regardless; *multiple* repair needs volume stabilization, earliest evidence 30 Oct, confirmation at Easter 2027 sell-through.

### 4. Balance-sheet survival
Survives comfortably; this is a cash cow with a stretched payout, not a credit story. Screen net debt/EBITDA 1.94. At Q2 2026: total debt ~$5.6-5.9B, cash $791M, current ratio 1.18 (down from 1.53 yr ago), debt/equity ~1.03. Q2 operating cash flow $419M (vs $112M yr ago); H1 ~$888M; TTM FCF ~$1.4B (~15% margin, Mar 2026 deep-dive). The strain is the dividend: $5.81 annualized, ~3.6% yield, payout ~79-82% of earnings — it was frozen for five quarters (early 2024-late 2025) precisely because cocoa ate the coverage. No dilution risk, no maturity wall flagged (not found: detailed maturity ladder in sources reviewed); risk is a second dividend freeze if cocoa re-spikes, not distress.

### 5. Margin of safety / valuation
Screen fwd P/E 16.0; on FY26 guide midpoint ($8.44) $157.61 is ~18.7x, on FY27 consensus (recovery year) it falls to the screen's ~16x. Hershey's own history: it habitually traded 20-25x forward and >30x at the 2023 peak (LTM P/E was 35.8x as recently as May 2026 per Trefis, now 21.9x trailing) — so 16-19x is historically cheap *for Hershey*. Versus peers it is less obviously cheap: Mondelez trades at a similar-to-lower multiple with +13.9% YTD performance, global diversification and a safer dividend (per Sep 2026 comparisons), and staples peers generally offer mid-teens multiples *with* volume growth. Analyst consensus: Hold (22 brokers), mean target $204.78 (MarketBeat, 19 Sep) = screen's +29.9% upside; TD Cowen Buy $210. Morningstar called shares discounted after Q2. Cheap *because of the dip* on earnings power if EPS reaches $9-10 in 2027 (cocoa deflation annualizing); still expensive if 2027 EPS stalls at ~$8.50 because volumes keep falling — at 16x a no-growth HSY is fairly priced, not a bargain. The 3.6% yield pays you ~half the equity risk premium to wait.

### 6. Category position
Still #1 in US chocolate (>35% share, Reese's #1 brand) with demonstrated pricing power — it *took* 12-14pts of price and kept revenue growing; few food companies could. But position is deteriorating at the margin: Hershey conceded US candy/mint/gum share loss in Q2 2026; Mars and Mondelez are taking shelf space; Lindt is winning premium; private label wins the value tier HSY's price hikes created. Salty snacks is the offset — Dot's/SkinnyPop/LesserEvil make HSY a broader snacker, and NA Salty was its fastest-growing segment — but salty margins were hit by Dot's capacity/supply problems (segment margin 9.8% in Q1, -530bp; automation capacity only online in 2027). Net: leader, pricing power intact, share momentum negative.

### 7. Value-trap risk (bear case) — the panel's stress case, taken seriously
The trap: you buy "cocoa normalization" and get *permanent volume loss*. Evidence for structural, not cyclical, decline: (a) **Elasticity regime change** — two years of double-digit cumulative price (+12-14pts in single quarters) may have reset habits: consumers traded to Mars, private label, gummies/sour (non-chocolate candy is outgrowing chocolate industry-wide), or simply fewer bars; volume -8 to -10pts is far worse than Hershey's historical ~-1pt per price point. (b) **GLP-1 overhang is real but sized "mild" so far:** ~11% of US adults now on GLP-1s (Gallup Sep 2026, up from 3% in 2024; 137M eligible); Cornell/Numerator: GLP-1 households cut grocery spend ~6% (~9% high-income), snack categories -6.7 to -11.1%; Worldpanel UK: chocolate spend cut sharply, 75% of users eat less chocolate; Brand Finance (Sep 2026) puts Hershey's and Reese's among the 10 most GLP-1-exposed food brands globally, with confectionery/savory = 53% of the $73B brand value at risk. Hershey's own line — prior CEO called impact "mild," current execs say chocolate is "more insulated" and GLP-1 users still treat — is plausible (small treats survive appetite suppression better than meals) but it is the *company's* framing, and US category volume data have not yet proven resilience. (c) **Cocoa is not "solved":** ~$5,500/t is still 2x pre-crisis; Ghana swollen-shoot affects ~a third of its cocoa land; a Super El Niño (Jefferies) could re-spike prices into Hershey's unhedged 2027 book, repeating 2025 with a weaker consumer. (d) SNAP cuts, fuel costs and value-seeking consumers were all cited by management at Barclays (9 Sep) as live headwinds. If volumes fall another 5%+ in 2027 while price anniversaries go to zero, revenue declines outright, the 16x becomes 16x on falling E, and the dividend (79-82% payout) gets cut — the classic staples value trap (cf. Kraft Heinz). That path is a minority case, not the base case — but its probability is materially above zero, unlike SYK's.

### 8. Rebound score: 6/10 — Verdict: A real cocoa-deflation earnings rebound (+35% EPS guided for 2026) priced at a historically cheap multiple, but the rebound is price-bought while volumes and share fall — buyable only as a sized, catalyst-gated position that must see volume declines narrow on 30 Oct, because if they don't, this is the batch's one genuine value trap.
