# 9. EFX — Equifax (Professional Services)

### 1. Why it's down
Not an operating collapse — a guidance, rates and policy repricing. Q1 2026 (Apr 21): revenue $1.649B, +14%, adj. EPS $1.86 beat, U.S. mortgage revenue +38%; stock still fell. Q2 2026 (Jul 21, company PR/Reuters): revenue $1.700B, +11%, adj. EPS $2.25 (+13%, top of guide), USIS +17% with mortgage +40%, Workforce Solutions (WFS) +7% to $705.4M — yet shares fell ~11% pre-market because FY26 revenue guidance was only $6.71–6.78B (below the $6.76B LSEG mean and far below some Street models) and Q3 EPS was guided $2.15–2.25 vs ~$2.27 consensus. Arithmetic: after a +14%/+11% H1 flattered by mortgage pull-forward ahead of rate rises, guidance mechanically implies a decelerating H2. The rate backdrop then worsened: mortgage rates have risen >1pp since the late-Feb 2026 Iran strikes pushed oil up (Reuters, Sep 23); the Fed *hiked* 25bp on Sep 16 to 3.75–4.00%; MBA 30y hit 7.12% (week of Sep 18) then 7.30% (week of Sep 25, highest since Nov 2023), with total applications −6% w/w, purchase −14% y/y, refi −56% y/y. Two structural policy hits compound the cycle: (a) FICO's Mortgage Direct License Program (launched Oct 1, 2025) lets tri-merge resellers buy scores directly, bypassing bureau mark-ups — EFX fell ~11% that day; (b) FHFA Director Pulte has attacked the bureaus as overcharging/"cartel-like" and is "seriously considering" bi-merge or single-bureau pulls, which would cut report volume per loan by a third to two-thirds. Smaller drags: Employer Services guided to decline in 2026 because Work Opportunity Tax Credit legislation was not extended; a $100M FCRA settlement accrual in Q2 ($40M net of insurance) for a previously disclosed coding issue. Target cuts cascaded Jul 22 (RBC $222→$194, JPMorgan $230→$210, Morgan Stanley $243→$225, Barclays $215→$200, Baird $245→$232) and Sep 30 (BMO $179→$160; Goldman neutral $171); new 52w lows Sep 24 ($150.38) and Sep 30 ($138.15). **Temporary-vs-permanent verdict: predominantly transitory/cyclical (rates, pull-forward, sentiment) with a real but bounded structural overhang — bi-merge and FICO direct licensing would permanently shrink mortgage-score economics, not the core data franchise. No evidence of lost data moat or demand destruction outside volume cyclicality.**

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability
Triopoly credit data (EFX/Experian/TransUnion) embedded in FCRA-regulated underwriting, plus the differentiated asset peers lack: The Work Number — ~217M active records / ~108M unique SSNs (Simply Wall St community narrative, Sep 2026) — powering WFS verification at a 44.9% Q2 operating margin. Switching is regulatory and workflow-deep (lender, employer, government integrations); WFS Government signed ~$300M of state contract value in H1 2026. AI is a tailwind, not a substitute: EFX sells the proprietary data AI models need, and is harvesting it internally — AI cost-savings target doubled Jul 21 to $150M for 2026–2028; new-product Vitality Index 16% vs a 10% long-term goal. Concrete threats: score commoditisation (EFX itself prices VantageScore 4.0 at $1 through 2027; Experian offered it free in Apr 2026 pricing), FICO direct licensing, and bi-merge. **Irreplaceability score: 8/10** — the underlying income/employment and credit files cannot be replicated by a model or a start-up; the *score-distribution toll* on top of them can.

### 3. Rebound catalyst
The trough-ender is specific and observable: the 30y mortgage rate. It needs oil/inflation cooling enough for the 10y Treasury to fall and the 30y to retake ~6.5% — that unlocks the refi wave EFX's H1 pull-forward proved is pent up (mortgage revenue +38–40% on mere rate dips) and lifts purchase apps off the slowest pace since 2025 (MBA, Sep 25 week). Near-term checkpoints: Q3 print on 2026-10-22 (guide $1.68–1.70B revenue, EPS $2.15–2.25) — any guide hold/raise against a 7.3% rate tape would signal the trough is in the numbers; VantageScore adoption — transactions tripled q/q to 2.2M in Q2 with ~1,200 lenders testing after FHFA opened it to *all* lenders on Sep 3–9, 2026 (following Jul 8, 2025 GSE acceptance and the Apr 22, 2026 FHFA/HUD FHA announcement; FHA implementation expected Jan 2027); the ~$300M of government contracts principally benefit 2027; Círculo de Crédito ($750M, Mexico's fastest-growing bureau) closes Q4 2026, guided accretive in year one; buyback pace ($300M in Q2 alone) supports EPS. Timeline: sentiment can turn on the first sub-6.75% rate weeks; earnings torque is a 2027 story.

### 4. Balance-sheet survival
Levered but self-funding — no distress or dilution case. At Jun 30, 2026 (10-Q, via StockTitan/Zacks summaries): total debt ~$7.5B, cash only $170M, short-term debt/current maturities $1.41B (commercial paper $1.40B, up from $762M at YE25), revolver availability $0.6B, ~74% of debt fixed-rate. Screen net debt/EBITDA 3.08x; revolver covenant max leverage 3.75x (electable to 4.25x for material deals); in compliance at Jun 30. Management targets leverage <3x and guided >$1B FCF in 2026 at ≥100% cash conversion (TTM FCF ~$1.1B), with ~$1.5B of 2026 capacity for M&A plus returns. It returned $366M in Q2 ($300M buybacks + $66M dividends) and ~$1.6B over the last year. Caveats: thin cash and heavier CP reliance make it dependent on market access, and Círculo adds $750M of deal value to fund in Q4. Survives a prolonged trough; it does not have ISRG-style shock absorbers.

### 5. Margin of safety / valuation
Cheap *because of the dip* versus its own history, roughly fair versus peers. Screen fwd P/E 13.8 (note: Zacks showed ~20x fwd in early Sep on a different EPS basis — definitional gap; trailing P/E was 23.95 on Sep 30, FullRatio). Ten-year average trailing P/E is 41.0 (median 40.5; 2025: 40.5), so the multiple has more than halved. EV/EBITDA ~12.9x vs TransUnion ~12.4x and TRU trailing P/E ~19.1x (AInvest benchmarking, Sep 2026) — the historic EFX premium is essentially gone. Analyst mean target $210.30–$213.95 (MarketBeat, Sep 30; 15 Buy / 6 Hold, "Moderate Buy"), matching the screen's +53.4% implied upside — but targets are still being cut (BMO $160 is the low recent print), so treat the mean as lagging, not leading. Margin of safety comes from trough-multiple-on-mid-cycle-earnings, not from asset value; if H2 guidance is cut again on Oct 22, the "cheap" multiple de-rates with the E.

### 6. Category position
Co-leader, still with pricing power in its best business. One of three bureaus with an irreplaceable file; clear #1 in income/employment verification (TWN), where WFS holds ~45–52% EBITDA margins and is winning government share. USIS mortgage revenue +40% in Q2 shows share/volume leverage, not share loss. The contested ground is narrow but real: mortgage *score* pricing (FICO direct, $1 VantageScore, Experian free) and the tri-merge requirement itself (Pulte's bi-merge review). Core credit-report and verification pricing has not been shown to be slipping.

### 7. Value-trap risk
The falling-knife case: FHFA actually adopts bi-merge/single-pull — a permanent 33–66% cut to mortgage report pulls that no rate recovery restores; rates stay ≥7% through 2027 because the Fed is hiking into an oil shock, so the "trough" is the run-rate; hiring stays frozen, WOTC lapses permanently and WFS verification volumes stall; VantageScore wins share at $1/free and permanently resets score economics while FICO direct strips the remaining mark-up; leverage at ~3.1x plus $1.4B of CP turns any FCF miss into a ratings/covenant story; another data/FCRA failure (the $40M net Q2 charge is a reminder the 2017 breach franchise risk never fully dies). If two of these land, $140 is not the bottom — it is a mid-teens multiple on structurally lower EPS.

### 8. Rebound score and verdict
**Rebound score: 7.5/10.** Verdict: The deepest dip in the quality set is mostly a rate-and-policy repricing of a still-growing (+11% revenue, +13% adj. EPS in Q2) triopoly franchise — buyable torque to any mortgage-rate reversal, sized for the genuine bi-merge tail risk and 3.1x leverage.

# 10. ISRG — Intuitive Surgical (Health Care Equipment)

### 1. Why it's down
Multiple compression on decelerating — not negative — growth, layered with tariffs and the first credible competition narrative in two decades. Shares are −29% YTD (Finnhub, Oct 1), off a 52w high of $603.88. Start of the slide: Q4 2025 results (Jan 2026) beat (revenue +19% to $2.87B) but 2026 da Vinci procedure growth was guided to 13–15%, down from 18% in 2025, with non-GAAP gross margin guided 67–68% including a ~1.2%-of-revenue tariff hit (>80% of instruments/accessories are made in Mexicali, Mexico). Q2 2026 (Jul 16, company PR): revenue $2.89B, +19%; non-GAAP EPS $2.80, +28% (incl. a $28M net / $0.08 IEEPA tariff-refund benefit); worldwide procedures +16% (da Vinci +15%, Ion +36%); 468 da Vinci placements incl. 246 da Vinci 5; installed base 11,710 (+12%). The stock still fell ~10–11% after hours because full-year procedure guidance was *held* at 13.5–15.5% (midpoint expected) rather than raised, and U.S. da Vinci growth slowed to 12% vs +20% outside the U.S. Management attributes U.S. softness to deferrable-case pressure after enhanced ACA premium subsidies expired, plus GLP-1 pressure — U.S. bariatric procedures fell high-single-digits. China stayed weak: lower tender activity, domestic competition, policy pricing pressure. Competition headlines did the narrative damage: Medtronic Hugo FDA-cleared for urology in Dec 2025 (first U.S. case, Cleveland Clinic, Feb 17, 2026); J&J Ottava filed De Novo in Jan 2026 and was FDA-authorised in Jul 2026 for upper-abdominal general surgery; CMR Versius Plus cleared and launching in the U.S. Add management's own deliberate price give-back — the Extended Use Program extending EndoWrist instrument life from H1 2027 — and a new Q2 10-Q risk factor on third-party remanufactured instruments, and the market stopped paying a monopoly multiple (fwd P/E ~55–60x at the start of 2026 → ~30–35x now). **Temporary-vs-permanent verdict: transitory/cyclical plus valuation reset — procedure growth of 16%, a +12% installed base and 85% recurring revenue are not impairment; the permanent element is only that the *uncontested-monopoly multiple* is gone, and some instrument pricing power is being voluntarily traded for volume.**

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability
Installed-base ecosystem moat, among the widest in medtech: 11,710 da Vinci + 1,096 Ion systems (Jun 30, 2026), 20M+ cumulative procedures, surgeon training and muscle memory (>15,000 surgeons on da Vinci 5 alone), hospital workflow integration, service contracts, and a proprietary instrument attach on nearly every case — recurring revenue was $2.47B, 85% of Q2 sales. Certification/regulatory lock-in is real (each platform and indication needs FDA clearance; rivals are cleared for narrow indication sets only). AI strengthens rather than substitutes it: da Vinci 5 captures higher-fidelity case data and My Intuitive+ adds AI case insights, simulation and remote collaboration — a data flywheel a challenger cannot shortcut. The disruption threat is physical, not digital: Hugo/Ottava/CMR and Chinese domestic robots, plus remanufactured instruments attacking the aftermarket. None yet matches the full stack of platform + instruments + training + evidence. **Irreplaceability score: 9/10.**

### 3. Rebound catalyst
Concrete and near: (i) Q3 earnings on 2026-10-20 — consensus ~$2.64 EPS / ~$2.91B revenue (MarketBeat, Sep 29); a procedure-guide raise or U.S. re-acceleration from the 12% Q2 print is the single biggest re-rating trigger, and deferred ACA/GLP-1 cases are postponements against an unchanged disease burden, i.e. catch-up volume exists. (ii) The da Vinci 5 upgrade cycle still has runway: only ~1,710 DV5 installed and just ~136 of them outside the U.S.; Q2 placements rose to 468 from 395 a year ago, 54% on operating leases, which lowers hospital capex friction. (iii) Portfolio breadth: SP procedures +61% in Q2, Ion +36% to 48k procedures (>400k cumulative), Jan 2026 FDA cardiac clearances on DV5 opening a new category. (iv) Margin guide already raised at Q2 to 68–69% non-GAAP gross margin (from 67.5–68.5%) despite the ~1.0% tariff drag, with a Malaysia plant (Penang, ~$500M over 5 years, operations from 2028) as the structural tariff hedge. (v) Capital return: a $5.0B buyback authorisation with ~$4.7B remaining. Timing: sentiment can turn on the Oct 20 print; the fundamental compounding (installed base +12%/yr at +3% utilisation) does the rest over 4–6 quarters.

### 4. Balance-sheet survival
Fortress — verified from the Q2 2026 10-Q as summarised by StockTitan and independent 10-Q reviews: cash, equivalents and investments **$8.63B at Jun 30, 2026**, **no debt** (fixed obligations are ~$170.9M of operating leases; purchase commitments are largely cancellable orders). H1 2026 operating cash flow $1,972.9M less $215.9M capex = ~$1,757M FCF; TTM FCF ~$3.22B. It *earns* net interest ($82.7M interest/other income in Q2) and still grew the cash pile while repurchasing 0.9M shares for $380M in Q2. No distress, dilution or refinancing scenario is plausible; the balance sheet is itself a competitive weapon against rivals trying to compete on price.

### 5. Margin of safety / valuation
Less expensive — and cheap versus its own history — but not cheap in absolute terms, which is exactly why analyst upside is modest despite a −31% drawdown. Screen fwd P/E 33.6; Zacks (Sep 9) put fwd P/E at 30.26x, the lowest in 10 years vs a 10-year median of 58.18x (high 93.22x), though still above the Medical-Instruments industry at 25.96x; trailing P/E ~46x. Analyst mean target $508.68 (MarketBeat, Sep 29; 31 analysts, consensus "Moderate Buy") implies ~+25% from $406.63 — the panel screen's +17.1% implies a lower mean (~$476) in its dataset; either way it is far below EFX's +53% because targets have been cut hard to the new multiple: Stifel $670→$550, Wells Fargo $654→$487, Leerink $573→$454, JPMorgan $550→$450 (Jul 17), RBC $650→$600. Analysts are anchoring on ~30x forward earnings for mid-teens growth, not on the old $600 price. Margin of safety: real on a 3–5-year compounding view (a 10-year-low multiple on a business still growing EPS +28%), thin on a 12-month view — at ~33x forward, another guide hold can still de-rate the stock.

### 6. Category position
Still the undisputed #1 with pricing power it is choosing to spend selectively. Dominant share of global soft-tissue robotic procedures (cited ~83% penetration of U.S. urologic robotic procedures — Hugo's only initial beachhead), the only platform with da Vinci + SP + Ion breadth, and ASPs still rising through the DV5 mix. The give-backs — Extended Use instruments (H1 2027), XiR/refurbished systems, lease mix — are offensive moves to widen the funnel and pre-empt Hugo/Ottava on cost, made from strength ($8.63B cash), but they do cap near-term per-procedure economics. No evidence of share loss in reported placements or procedures yet; China is the one geography where share/pricing is genuinely contested today.

### 7. Value-trap risk
The bear case: U.S. procedure growth keeps sliding (12% → single digits) as ACA-subsidy lapse, GLP-1 bariatric erosion and hospital capex caution compound; the Extended Use Program cannibalises the highest-margin recurring line faster than volumes offset it — a self-inflicted margin reset; Hugo (hernia/gyn indications pending) and Ottava (commercial from 2027–28 per analyst estimates) take *new* placements even if the installed base never switches, slowing the flywheel; China imposes national robotic-surgery pricing and domestic substitution, closing a growth market; tariffs broaden beyond the guided ~1% of revenue before Malaysia (2028) helps; remanufactured instruments win legal/regulatory ground. At ~33x forward, none of this needs to be catastrophic to hurt: a drift to 25x on intact earnings is another ~−25%.

### 8. Rebound score and verdict
**Rebound score: 8/10.** Verdict: A fortress-balance-sheet compounder (procedures +16%, EPS +28%, $8.63B net cash) de-rated to a 10-year-low multiple for guidance caution, tariffs and new rivals — the prior run's pick is a better buy here, provided the position is sized for a still-premium ~33x forward multiple rather than a deep-value one.
