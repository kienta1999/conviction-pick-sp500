# 11. SPGI — S&P Global (Financial Exchanges & Data)

Screen (2026-10-01 close): $388.16, -8.3% vs 200-day SMA, -24.4% off 52-week high, fwd P/E 19.2, revenue growth TTM +8.3%, net debt/EBITDA 0.93, analyst upside +34.0%, next earnings 2026-10-29. Market cap ~$115bn (Finnhub, 2026-10-01).

## 1. Why it's down

Three stacked hits, not one business break:

- **10 Feb 2026 — guidance miss + AI rout (the big leg).** Reuters (10 Feb 2026): SPGI forecast FY2026 adjusted EPS of $19.40–$19.65 vs $19.94 consensus (LSEG) and reported Q4 adj. EPS $4.30 vs $4.33 expected on revenue +9% to $3.92bn; shares fell ~18% pre-market in a single session. Reuters explicitly framed it as "amid growing fears of AI-fueled disruptions" in a broader software/services sell-off. Management signalled Ratings normalisation after two strong issuance years.
- **AI-disruption narrative through H1 2026.** Morningstar (2026, post-review) notes the sell-off intensified after Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.6 financial-research release raised fears LLMs would disintermediate capital-markets information services. The whole complex de-rated in sympathy — Moody's fell ~11% around the February print (FinancialContent, 12 Feb 2026).
- **Rates + portfolio confusion in summer.** Motley Fool (6 Aug 2026): the 28–29 July FOMC held rates with three dissents favouring a *hike* on persistent inflation — bad for debt issuance, SPGI's Ratings engine. Q2 (28 July) then fell 3.5% despite a beat, partly on headline confusion between GAAP EPS $4.12 and pro-forma ex-Mobility adj. EPS $4.83, and on the recast FY guidance of $17.50–$17.75 that mechanically reflects the 1 July 2026 Mobility spin-off (completed 1 July), not a like-for-like cut from $19.40–$19.65. Stock drifted from >$431 in late August to ~$405 mid-September to $388.16 on 1 Oct (Tickeron, Sep 2026; Finnhub).

Fundamentals did *not* follow the price: Q1 2026 revenue $4.171bn (+10% y/y); Q2 GAAP revenue $4.146bn (+10%), pro-forma revenue $3.678bn (+11%), adj. EPS $4.83 (+23%) — see §3.

**Temporary-vs-permanent verdict: Transitory / multiple-driven, not permanent impairment.** The drawdown is a guidance + AI-narrative + rate-fear de-rating of a stock priced for perfection, compounded by spin-off accounting confusion. No lost franchise, no revenue decline, no share loss is in evidence through Q2 2026 — Ratings and Indices set records. Permanent impairment would require proof in §2/§7, which has not appeared.

## 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability

Moat is regulatory + benchmark lock-in, stacked:

- **Ratings oligopoly:** S&P and Moody's together control >80% of ratings, Fitch most of the rest (Motley Fool, 28 Sep 2026). The SEC's NRSRO designation (informal 1975, codified 2007) means institutional buyers require NRSRO ratings and most issues need two; a new entrant lacks the century of default data and investor trust — "even armed with the latest AI tools, can't aggregate that much data."
- **Index licensing:** An ETF issuer cannot casually drop the S&P 500; Indices is a toll on AUM and on SPX derivatives. Benchmarks (Ratings + Indices + Platts energy) are ~65% of revenue and >80% of operating profit (Tickeron Q2 recap, 2026).
- **Data lock-in / switching costs:** Market Intelligence / Capital IQ is embedded in bank, buy-side and corporate workflows; Morningstar specifically cites switching costs there even while conceding it is the most AI-exposed segment.

**AI threat, specifically:** Real but narrow. LLMs threaten the *presentation/query layer* of Market Intelligence — summarising filings, drafting research — not the licensed, audited, proprietary dataset underneath, nor a regulated rating, nor the S&P 500 trademark. Analyst commentary (AInvest, Aug 2026) estimates <7% of revenue is directly exposed and Capital IQ Pro desktop <6% of consolidated revenue — treat as secondary-source estimates, not company disclosure. SPGI is also monetising the other side: >500 customers on LLM-ready APIs in Q2 2026, Kensho-powered natural-language agents, a late-2025 Google Cloud / Gemini Enterprise partnership, and reported AI-product upcharges. Morningstar reaffirmed a **Wide Moat** and $570 fair value after its industry AI review, calling SPGI oversold.

**Irreplaceability score: 9/10** — only the commodity desktop/data-display slice is substitutable; ratings, indices and Platts benchmarks are not.

## 3. Rebound catalyst

- **Q3 print, 29 Oct 2026 (near-term proof point):** Any repeat of Q2 kills the "structural slowdown" story. Q2 (28 July 2026) was: Ratings revenue $1.34bn, **+17% y/y, a record quarter**; Indices $534m, **+20%, 13th consecutive record quarter**; Market Intelligence $1.29bn (+6%); adj. operating margin 54.3% (+200 bps) (Zacks/Tickeron/AInvest Q2 recaps).
- **Debt-issuance tailwind, 2026–27:** Hyperscaler AI-capex bond supply is already buoying ratings demand (Reuters, 10 Feb 2026), and a large corporate maturity wall must be refinanced into 2027–30 regardless of the exact rate path — refinancing is non-discretionary Ratings volume. Private-markets/private-credit ratings and benchmarks are the fastest-growing extension (company commentary, Q2).
- **Buyback as a floor:** At Q2, management **raised the 2026 repurchase target to >$7bn** — >5% of the current market cap — funded in part by ~$2bn received on the Mobility spin (Tickeron/TIKR summary, Sep 2026). In Q2 alone it returned $500m buybacks + $358m dividends (MarketBeat financials summary).
- **Multiple normalisation:** The catalyst needs no heroics — if AI fears merely stop worsening, a 19–20x forward multiple on a wide-moat compounder has room to re-rate toward the $520 consensus (see §5). Unconfirmed press reports of a strategic review / possible Capital IQ Pro separation would, if real, remove the AI-drag segment — flagged as rumour, not a base case.
- **Timing:** Binary check on 29 Oct; fuller re-rating over 2–4 quarters if issuance holds and Market Intelligence growth stabilises.

## 4. Balance-sheet survival

No survival question. Screen net debt/EBITDA **0.93x** (2026-10-01). FY2025: revenue $15.34bn, free cash flow ~$5.1bn (vs ~$5.3bn in 2024), operating cash flow $5.651bn, ~$5bn of buybacks (company/secondary summaries of FY2025 results). Q1 2026 cash $1.8bn vs $10.6bn long-term debt (StockTitan summary of filings); Q2 ended with $4.13bn cash and ~$15.2bn total debt after $1.86bn of issuance around the Mobility separation (MarketBeat) — gross debt rose for the spin, net leverage did not become distressed. Dividend $0.97/quarter in Q1 2026, yield ~0.9–1.0% at $388. Asset-light, ~50%+ adjusted operating margins, recurring subscription/index revenue covering the cyclical Ratings piece. It can endure an issuance drought with no dilution — indeed it is retiring >5% of the share count in 2026.

## 5. Margin of safety / valuation

- **Vs own history: genuinely cheap for SPGI.** Screen fwd P/E **19.2** (other feeds 19.9–20.4 in Sep). FinanceCharts (crawled 1 Oct 2026): SPGI trailing P/E 24.0 vs 3-yr average 35.2 and 5-yr average 35.4. A stock that lived in the low-30s/40s now trades at a market-like multiple.
- **Vs peers (1 Oct 2026, Finnhub/FinanceCharts/ValueInvesting):** Moody's (MCO) $447.36, trailing P/E ~28.3–28.8, forward ~28.6–29.4; MSCI $545.39, trailing ~28.7–29.5, forward ~28.8; FactSet (FDS) $277.61, trailing ~16.7–17.8, forward ~17.8 — FDS is cheaper but is a $9.5bn, slower, desktop-exposed business, i.e. it *should* be. SPGI now trades at a clear discount to its two true benchmark peers (MCO/MSCI) despite comparable-to-better Q2 growth.
- **Analysts:** Mean target ~$520–$523.59 (Tickeron Sep 2026: ~$520; MarketBeat 9 Sep 2026: $523.59, range $485–$560, 20 analysts) = **+34.0% implied upside** on the screen at $388.16. Morningstar fair value $570. Even the low target ($485) sits ~25% above the price.

**Verdict: cheap *because of the dip*, not a structurally cheap stock.** 19x forward for ~8–11% revenue growth, 50%+ margins and $5bn+ FCF is a de-rating discount, not value-stock pricing — the margin of safety is the gap to peers/history plus the buyback, not a low absolute multiple.

## 6. Category position

Still **#1**. SPGI remains the largest ratings agency in the S&P/Moody's duopoly, owner of the world's default equity benchmark (S&P 500 / Dow Jones Indices), the Platts benchmark franchise in energy, and a top-two capital-markets data platform. Pricing power is evidenced, not asserted: record Ratings and Indices revenue in Q2 2026 at a 54.3% adjusted margin, 13 straight record Indices quarters, and continued annual price escalators in subscription data. No evidence of share loss to Fitch, MSCI/FTSE Russell, Bloomberg or an AI entrant in any 2026 print found. The Mobility exit makes it a purer benchmark/data company — ~2/3 of revenue, >80% of profit from benchmarks.

## 7. Value-trap risk

Honest bear case:

- **Issuance drought:** Ratings is ~40% of adjusted operating income. If the Fed hikes (three July dissents), spreads widen, or corporates deleverage, Ratings can go flat/negative and the earnings base the 19x sits on shrinks. H2 2026 faces tough y/y issuance comps.
- **Private credit bypass:** A growing share of borrowing is private and often unrated — a slow structural leak from the rated-bond pool SPGI tolls.
- **AI commoditisation of Market Intelligence:** This is the one segment where an LLM front-end plus cheaper data could compress seats/pricing and lengthen enterprise sales cycles (already cited in Sep 2026 sentiment). If that spreads from desktop to the core data licences, the wide-moat thesis cracks.
- **Benchmark economics:** Index fees face perpetual fee pressure, and the CBOE SPX-options licence is a recurring renegotiation cost/concentration risk.
- **Political/antitrust:** Ratings agencies carry permanent post-2008 political scrutiny; any NRSRO reform, issuer-pay regulation or EU/SEC action would hit the moat itself, not just earnings.
- **Multiple trap:** "Cheap vs history" fails if history was the bubble — if the market permanently caps data names at ~20x, upside is earnings growth only (~high-single digits), not re-rating.

## 8. Rebound score and verdict

**Rebound score: 8/10**

**One-sentence verdict:** A wide-moat benchmark monopoly de-rated ~24% on a small guidance miss, spin-off confusion and an AI narrative that its record Q2 Ratings (+17%) and Indices (+20%) prints contradict — with a >$7bn buyback and a 29 Oct catalyst, this is a transitory dip in the category leader, not a value trap, provided Market Intelligence does not deteriorate.
