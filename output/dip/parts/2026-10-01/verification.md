## SYK — Stryker (ranked #2, entry $272.96)

### 1. Why-down cause — **CONTRADICTED** as worded in the claim (attack label, disclosure date, guidance direction, and revenue-shift figure are all wrong); the underlying two-shock story (cyber + Inari vascular) and the Q2 recovery numbers are CONFIRMED except "worldwide sales +11.1%"
What the claim gets wrong:
- **"Ransomware" label — contradicted by the company's own 8-K.** The attack was a *wiper* executed through Microsoft Intune on **11 Mar 2026**, and Stryker's SEC Form 8-K stated the company found "**no indication of ransomware or malware**" (8-K of 11 Mar 2026, as reproduced in multiple security-press analyses of the filing). The attackers (Iran-linked Handala / Void Manticore) abused a compromised Intune Global Admin credential to factory-reset devices (BleepingComputer-sourced reporting: ~80,000 devices; attacker claimed 200,000) across 79 countries. Calling it a "ransomware attack" misstates the primary disclosure.
- **"Disclosure (Apr 30)" — wrong disclosure date.** The incident was disclosed 11 Mar 2026 via 8-K (Item 1.05). 30 Apr 2026 was the *Q1 earnings report* that quantified the impact — a different event.
- **"EPS guidance cut" at Q1 — contradicted.** At Q1 (30 Apr 2026) Stryker **reiterated/maintained** full-year guidance: StockStory (30 Apr 2026) — "Management reiterated its full-year Adjusted EPS guidance of $15 at the midpoint"; Barchart — "maintained its full-year adjusted EPS guidance of $14.90–$15.10"; AInvest (1 May 2026) — guidance maintained at $14.90–$15.10 and organic growth 8.0%–9.5% "despite" the cyber impact. There was no Q1 guidance cut.
- **"Revenue shift (~$160M)" — UNVERIFIED, and contradicted by the only quantification found.** No source located by this verifier puts the Q1 revenue shift at ~$160M. AInvest's post-Q1 analysis (Jun 2026) puts "about **$375 million of deferred or lost sales**" from a three-week production shutdown; the reported Q1 revenue miss itself was ~$320M vs consensus ($6.02B actual vs ~$6.34B expected, StockStory/Zacks, 30 Apr–1 May 2026). The $160M figure should not be used.
What the claim gets right:
- Inari vascular supply failure: CONFIRMED in primary management language. Q2 release/call (30 Jul 2026), per Hagens Berman's PR Newswire release (17 Sep 2026) quoting management: the Peripheral Vascular disruption caused "a meaningful backorder situation with lost sales in the quarter"; CEO Kevin Lobo: "it did cause a significant backorder specific to one plant in the Inari business… the backorder is pretty elevated right now." The primary Q2 release sales table shows U.S. Vascular sales −6.7% in Q2. On 8 Sep 2026 at the Wells Fargo Healthcare Conference, CFO Preston Wells said the issue "is continuing into the third quarter and will certainly continue… a little bit into the fourth quarter as well" — shares fell $26.70 (−8.8%) that day, wiping out >$10B of market value (PR Newswire, 17 Sep 2026). Inari was acquired Feb 2025 for ~$4.9B.
- Not demand loss — Q2 2026 recovery: CONFIRMED from the company's Q2 release (GlobeNewswire, 30 Jul 2026): reported net sales **+9.4% to $6,589M**, organic net sales **+9.0%**, adjusted EPS **+17.9% to $3.69**, adjusted operating margin +170 bps to 27.4%; full-year guidance **narrowed to 8.3%–9.3% organic** (low end raised from 8.0%) and adjusted EPS $14.95–$15.10.
- **"Worldwide sales +11.1%" — CONTRADICTED.** Total Q2 2026 net sales grew **+9.4%** as reported (company release). The +11.1% figure belongs to Q2 *2025* (Motley Fool, 2 Aug 2025: "11.1% year-over-year GAAP revenue growth… net sales reached $6.0 billion" — the prior-year quarter). In Q2 2026, the ~11% figure is *international* sales growth (+11.0% reported), not worldwide. Correct figure: **worldwide +9.4%**.
