## BRO — Brown & Brown, Inc. (~$61.70; 52-wk high $96.55 / low $53.81; ~36% off high)

### 1. Why it's down — verdict: TRANSITORY (cyclical + integration noise), with one idiosyncratic overhang
- Jan 26, 2026: Q4 2025 report showed organic revenue **−2.8%**, blamed on a drop in flood-claims processing revenue; the call disclosed **275 former employees defected to a competitor, taking ~$23M of annual revenue** (ainvest, Jan 27, 2026). Stock fell 6.91% to $74.12 on Jan 27, 2026.
- Feb 17, 2026: Pomerantz LLP announced a securities-fraud investigation (alleged misconduct tied to the revenue shortfall/attrition) — legal overhang, no formal charges (ainvest).
- Jul 27, 2026: Q2 2026 adjusted EPS $1.07 (missed consensus $1.08 by $0.01); revenue $1.67B (+30.4% y/y, driven by the Accession acquisition) while **organic revenue −0.7%**; adjusted EBITDAC margin −100bps to 35.7% (Zacks via ainvest, Aug 2026).
- Sector backdrop: CAT property rates down ~15–35% in recent quarters, compressing the premium base; peers also weak (AON −5.5%, AJG −31.3%, WTW −12.4% over the past year per ainvest, Jun 2026).
- Verdict: the drawdown is a soft insurance-pricing cycle + post-mega-deal digestion + an earnings-expectations reset, not a lost franchise. The talent defection and legal probe are real but bounded risks, not secular impairment.

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 7/10
- Scale moat: post-Accession, a top-5 U.S. insurance broker (Accession was #9; $1.7B pro forma revenue, 5,000 professionals, 190 add-ons since 2014); decentralized agency model with teammate equity ownership aids retention. Sticky carrier/client relationships and $15B+ of premium placements give pricing power in specialty lines.
- AI angle: management is running an "AI-first transformation" with partnerships announced with **Anthropic, McKinsey, and Accenture** (Seeking Alpha, Jul 31, 2026) on a 10-year data-standardization base. Brokerage is relationship- and placement-driven; AI improves productivity/margins rather than disintermediating the broker.
- Threat: producer poaching (the 275-person defection) is the human-capital version of moat erosion — watchable, not structural.

### 3. Rebound catalyst
- **Accession integration**: $150M of synergies targeted by 2026; Specialty Distribution segment posts ~44% EBITDAC margins (everyticker analysis) — margin mix improves as it scales. De-levering from ~3.9x debt/EBITDA at close toward 0–3x target over 12–18 months via natural cash generation.
- **Capital return**: $350M of buybacks in Q1 2026 (~5M shares retired), dividend raised to $0.165/q (Aug 19, 2026 payment); 32-year Dividend Aristocrat streak.
- **Timing**: organic growth re-acceleration as customer churn from departures runs its course + CAT rate cycle trough; Q3 earnings (late Oct 2026) and 2027 guidance reset are the clearing events.

### 4. Balance-sheet survival
- MarketBeat (updated 9/22/2026): debt-to-equity **0.58**, current ratio **2.11**; net income $1.05B, net margin 17.75%. Free cash flow ~$1.45B on a ~$21B market cap (~16x P/FCF — not screaming cheap, per ainvest Aug 2026).
- The Accession deal was financed with a $9.4B bridge loan plus a ~$4B equity raise (14% dilution) — leverage is elevated but serviceable from the cash engine; no distress or dilution risk visible beyond what's already absorbed.

### 5. Margin of safety / valuation
- Forward P/E **12.9x (Zacks) / 14.4x (MarketBeat)** vs. brokerage industry 14.9x; tradestie: 12.8x next-year vs. 10-yr historical 14.2x — modestly below its own history.
- Analyst mean target **$76.60** (+18.6% implied), high $90 / low $60; consensus **Hold** (19–22 analysts; only 3–4 buys). Seeking Alpha (Sep 20, 2026): $90 fair value, +26% upside at 15.2x forward P/E.
- Genuinely cheap-ish because of the dip, but the Hold consensus and estimate cuts (−0.6%/−1.8% on 2026/27 EPS in 60 days, Zacks) say the market wants proof of organic re-acceleration first.

### 6. Category position
- Still a consolidator with pricing power in specialty/wholesale (top-5 post-Accession), but **losing the organic-growth race** to peers right now (−0.7% vs. peers' positive organic) — execution, not share, is the question.

### 7. Value-trap risk (bear case)
Producer defections persist or widen (management flags "tens of millions" of revenue at risk); CAT property rates keep falling; Accession integration under-delivers on the $150M synergies; the Pomerantz probe turns into a real suit. The 14% dilution from the Accession equity raise permanently raised the EPS bar.

### 8. Rebound score: 6.5/10
**Verdict:** A cyclical, integration-masked compounder trading at a below-history multiple — a reasonable dip bet, but the rebound needs organic growth to turn positive, so it's a show-me story into 2027.

## EFX — Equifax Inc. (~$155.32; 52-wk high $262.30 / low $150.75; ~41% off high)

### 1. Why it's down — verdict: mostly TRANSITORY (policy noise + rate cycle), one genuine STRUCTURAL risk
- **VantageScore 4.0**: FHFA approved VantageScore 4.0 for agency mortgages (limited rollout announced at the FHFA/HUD joint press conference; broad approval posted by Director Bill Pulte on X "effective immediately," ~Sep 3, 2026). EFX fell **−8.6% on Sep 4, 2026** (QuiverQuant). Note: VantageScore is a JV **owned by the three bureaus themselves**; on the Q2 call (Jul 21, 2026) CEO Mark Begor said ~1,200 lenders pulled 2.2M free VantageScores alongside paid FICO, and ~100 lenders moved exclusively to VantageScore at the **$1 price point** (held through end-2027). FICO mortgage-score revenue is ~50% of USIS mortgage revenue and **~7% of total EFX revenue at zero margin** — losing it is revenue-negative but margin-positive.
- **Bi-merge threat**: Pulte then floated moving from tri-merge to **bi-merge** (two bureaus instead of three) — EFX fell −5.62% on the Friday of the "cartel-like" criticism (Longbridge, ~Sep 18, 2026). This is the real structural risk: it would cut mortgage report volumes by ~one-third industry-wide.
- **Fundamentals were fine**: Q2 2026 (Jul 21, 2026) adjusted EPS $2.25 (+12.5% y/y, beat by 1.8%), revenue $1.7B (+10.6%); USIS mortgage revenue +40%; Workforce Solutions +7%. Q1 (Apr 21, 2026) was a 10.1% EPS beat that still sold off −3% ("buy the rumor, sell the news," ainvest). Guidance assumes the U.S. mortgage market down low-single-digits in 2026; Iran-conflict rate spike hurt Q1 activity.
- Verdict: the selloff prices in a structural impairment that, for EFX specifically, is largely a pass-through-revenue scare (zero-margin FICO) — except bi-merge, which would genuinely dent volumes.

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 8/10
- Tri-bureau oligopoly on credit files plus the **unique The Work Number (TWN) payroll database** powering Workforce Solutions (verification services +7%, high-double-digit Talent Solutions growth in Q2). 400 pending/granted AI patents; doubled AI savings target to **$150M**; New Product Vitality Index 16–17% (record).
- AI disruption threat is low: scoring models (FICO 10T vs. VantageScore 4.0) compete on analytics, but the underlying data oligopoly is the moat and EFX co-owns the challenger. The genuine threat is **regulatory** (FHFA), not technological.

### 3. Rebound catalyst
- **Policy clarity**: bi-merge either dies (FHA already affirmed tri-merge for FICO 10T/VantageScore 4.0 rollouts — Scotsman Guide, ~May 2026) or gets priced in; either way the overhang compresses.
- **Operating**: $150M AI savings, cloud transformation completing (capex rolling off), mortgage origination recovery on any rate relief; VantageScore $1 pricing drives volume through 2027; **Círculo de Crédito** ($750M EV, Mexico's fastest-growing bureau) expected to close Q4 2026, accretive in year one.
- **Timing**: Q3 earnings (Oct 2026) with FCF >$1B reiteration; 2027 as cloud capex ends and cash conversion (>100%) inflects.

### 4. Balance-sheet survival
- Debt-to-equity **0.92** (MarketBeat, 9/23/2026) / ~1.25 (stocks-expert); $7.5B total debt vs. ~$170M cash; current ratio **0.60** (thin short-term liquidity); $1.41B short-term debt / $1.40B commercial paper as of Jun 30, 2026 (Zacks, Sep 2, 2026).
- Offset: expected **2026 FCF >$1B at >100% cash conversion**; returned $366M to shareholders in Q2 ($300M buybacks + $66M dividends). Leveraged but cash-generative — no distress, dilution unlikely.

### 5. Margin of safety / valuation
- Forward P/E **15.3x (stocks-expert) / 18.1x (MarketBeat)**; trailing 27x is inflated by acquisition amortization. PEG **0.90**; P/FCF 16.5x.
- Analyst mean target **$214.53** (**+38.3%** implied), high $266 / low $179; consensus **Moderate Buy** (21 analysts; 8 Strong Buy, 13 Buy, 9 Hold, 0 sells per stocks-expert). Simply Wall St narrative fair value $214.95 (~20% undervalued).
- Genuinely cheap vs. its own history (historically ~20–25x forward) — the dip is real, driven by policy fear rather than earnings collapse.

### 6. Category position
- Still a co-equal member of the bureau triopoly and **#1 in employment/income verification** (TWN is unmatched). Not losing share — USIS mortgage +40% in Q2 with share gains in pre-approval soft pulls.

### 7. Value-trap risk (bear case)
FHFA actually implements bi-merge (loses ~1/3 of GSE mortgage pulls — the one truly structural hit); mortgage volumes stay depressed through 2027; FICO 10T undercuts VantageScore; $100M FCRA settlement accrual (Q2 2026, net $40M after insurance) hints at recurring compliance costs; leverage + thin current ratio leave little room for error if cash flow slips.

### 8. Rebound score: 7.5/10
**Verdict:** The market is pricing a regulatory worst case onto a zero-margin revenue stream while the real franchise (TWN, verification, data oligopoly) keeps compounding — the best risk/reward of the three if bi-merge stays a threat rather than a rule.

## SYK — Stryker Corporation (~$273.40; 52-wk high $392.55 / low $267.00; ~30% off high)

### 1. Why it's down — verdict: TRANSITORY operating disruption, but compounded by a self-inflicted disclosure problem
- **Mar 11, 2026 cyberattack**: attackers abused Microsoft Intune to wipe workstations/phones (an Iran-linked group claimed credit); connected devices (robots, defibrillators) were architecturally unaffected, but **electronic ordering went offline for ~3 weeks**, manufacturing/shipping disrupted globally, patient-specific implant cases rescheduled. Stryker was fully operational again by April 2026 (SEC filing). Q1 2026 (May 7, 2026): revenue $6.02B vs. ~$6.29B expected; adjusted EPS $2.60 vs. $2.98 consensus; ~200bps gross-margin compression; organic growth only 2.4% — but management held full-year guidance.
- **Jul 30, 2026 (Q2)**: disclosed a "meaningful backorder situation" with lost sales in **Peripheral Vascular** (specific to one plant in the Inari business, per CEO Kevin Lobo), assuring it was "addressed" and would normalize by end of Q3. Stock fell −6.4% to $325.70 on Jul 31.
- **Sep 8, 2026 (Wells Fargo Healthcare Conference)**: CFO Preston Wells admitted the manufacturing issue was "continuing into the third quarter and certainly… will continue" into Q4 — contradicting the July assurance. Stock fell **−8.8% (−$26.70) to $276.43**, erasing >$10B of market cap (Business Wire/Hagens Berman summaries).
- **Sep 8–10, 2026**: securities-fraud investigations launched by Hagens Berman, Howard G. Smith, Glancy Prongay, and Block & Leviton over the disclosure discrepancy.
- Verdict: the cyberattack was a one-off; the backorder is operational and fixable; the stock's real problem now is **trust** — the July-vs-September guidance mismatch. Underlying demand (procedures, Mako installs) was never impaired.

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 9/10
- #1 in orthopedics with the **Mako robotic surgery platform** (record Q1 2026 installations, rising utilization; Mako 4, Mako Shoulder launching H2 2026, Mako RPS handheld). The moat is the installed base + 3D planning software + workflow integration + surgeon training — classic razor/razorblade with recurring implant demand. The **Inari Medical acquisition** (~$4.9B, closed Feb 2025) enlarged the vascular intervention platform.
- AI/robotics is a tailwind, not a threat: Stryker is the automation beneficiary. No credible substitute disrupts joint replacement or surgical robotics. Gross margins 63–64% confirm pricing power.

### 3. Rebound catalyst
- **Backorder resolution**: CFO guided the issue extends "a little bit into" Q4 2026 — deferred shipments convert to recognized revenue in Q4/H1 2027 (revenue is recognized on shipment, so the backlog is pent-up, not lost).
- **Q3 earnings (late Oct 2026)**: the first clean disclosure checkpoint; any confirmation of normalization + legal overhang fading (investigations rarely become material suits absent fraud evidence) re-rates the stock.
- **Product cycle**: Mako Shoulder launch H2 2026, continued Mako placements, Inari integration.

### 4. Balance-sheet survival
- $3.48B cash vs. $14.72B debt; **net-debt/EBITDA 1.5x** (StockStory); debt-to-equity **0.59** (MarketBeat, 9/24/2026); $783M annual interest easily covered by $7.28B LTM EBITDA; current ratio 2.16. Management paid off $1.0B of maturing notes from operating cash flow in H1 2026 and **doubled operating cash flow** y/y (Milvern, Jul 2026). Fortress-grade — zero survival concern.

### 5. Margin of safety / valuation
- Forward P/E **~18.2x (MarketBeat) / 17.1x NTM (StockStory)** vs. a historical premium multiple (~22–25x forward for this compounder). Trailing 28.4x.
- Analyst mean target **$374.42** (**+36.9%** implied), high $465 / low $298; consensus **Moderate Buy** (23 analysts). StockStory: "for those confident in the business and management, this is a good time to invest."
- Genuinely cheap vs. history — a premium franchise trading at a market multiple because of a trust discount, not an earnings impairment.

### 6. Category position
- Still the **#1 player in orthopedic robotics and joint replacement** with pricing power intact; elevated capital-equipment order book entering H2 2026; no share loss evidenced — the disruption was supply-side, not demand-side.

### 7. Value-trap risk (bear case)
The securities investigations coalesce into a class action with real damages or an SEC inquiry (governance discount persists); the peripheral vascular backorder drags into 2027, suggesting deeper manufacturing/quality issues; tariff pressure and the open German tax audit add earnings noise; Inari integration stumbles. Management credibility is the swing factor — one more guidance walk-back would re-rate it from "dip" to "avoid."

### 8. Rebound score: 7.5/10
**Verdict:** The highest-quality franchise of the three at its cheapest relative multiple in years — the bet is that a fixable supply-chain embarrassment, not a broken moat, is what's on sale; the 9/16 investigation wave is noise unless it becomes a filed suit.

They are substantially offset by premiums payable on the liability side, and cash conversion remains strong (~$1.45B FCF). Not a collections problem.
