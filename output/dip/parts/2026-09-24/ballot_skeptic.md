## Single-pick ballot

- **Top pick:** IDXX
- **Runner-up:** NFLX
- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** IDEXX is the cleanest "price broke, business didn't" name on the shortlist: it beat and raised in both 2026 quarters (Q2 EPS $4.27, +18% YoY, +8.1% vs consensus; CAG recurring revenue +11% organic; VetLab consumables +14.7% organic), raised FY26 EPS guidance — and then the stock *fell* 7.8% over the next month on no company news, in a healthcare-sector rotation. That is multiple compression on a razor/razorblade compounder (66% ROE, 63% gross margins, $1B+ annual FCF), not impairment. Net debt/EBITDA is 0.55x and the earnings-quality screen shows zero flags, so there is no survival or accounting question to answer — the upside is a plain re-rating as the beat-and-raise cadence continues into the Nov 2 earnings print.
- **Why the dip is temporary (not permanent):** the single most compelling evidence is direction: earnings guidance went *up* in August while the price went *down* — the exact inverse of a falling knife, where price falls because the earnings trajectory broke. There is no documented demand damage: installed base grew 11%, consumables grew double-digits, and the new canine lymphoma test adds to the pipeline.
- **Moat / AI-irreplaceability:** low risk. AI assists IDEXX (diagnostic-utilization algorithms) rather than replacing it — no AI substitute performs in-clinic blood work. The moat is the proprietary instrument installed base plus consumables pull-through and the reference-lab network, with pricing power visible in both price and volume.
- **Rebound catalyst & timing:** Q3 report ~Nov 2, 2026 — another beat-and-raise is the clearing event; continued double-digit CAG recurring-revenue growth and installed-base expansion thereafter. Timing: 1–2 quarters.
- **12-18 month return scenario:** base ~+30–35% (re-rating toward ~$680–700, still below the ~$699–747 analyst mean targets, ~+34–44% implied upside); bull ~+45–50% if the multiple moves back toward the middle of its 35–75x five-year forward-P/E band on sustained double-digit recurring growth.
- **Top risk (value-trap case):** PEG 2.31 — this is a growth-scare discount, not deep value, so the "cheapness" is relative and offers no floor if growth slows. If a consumer pullback cuts vet visits, the recurring-consumables engine (the core of the thesis) stalls, and the multiple stays compressed; also note the thin cash cushion (current ratio 1.17) with heavy buybacks (~$347M/quarter ≈ operating cash flow) — management is spending the FCF, not hoarding it.
- **Conviction (1-10):** 8 — the impairment case is essentially absent (the dip is provable sentiment), but forward P/E ~31x is not absolute-cheap, so this is a quality re-rating bet, not a steal.
