3. **Rate sensitivity:** The drawdown was partly a duration event (10Y at 5.35%). If yields stay at 2002-era highs, CAT's customers finance machines at punitive rates and its own multiple stays capped; Cat Financial's funding costs rise too.
4. **Valuation trap shape:** This is the classic "great company, peak earnings, peak multiple" setup. Trefis (2026-10-16) notes that after some historical >20% CAT dips (e.g. Aug 2011), shares were *below* the dip-day price a year later; buying requires believing mid-cycle EPS supports >$700.
5. **Regulatory overhang:** FTC/USDA ag-equipment inquiry (2026-10-07) is peripheral to CAT but unresolved; not found to have produced any CAT-specific finding as of this writing.

Trap probability: **moderate** — not because the business is impaired, but because $699 still capitalizes a lot of backlog perfection. The trap, if it springs, is multiple compression on peak earnings, not business failure.
