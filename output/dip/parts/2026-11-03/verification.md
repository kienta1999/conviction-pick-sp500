## 1. KLAC (KLA Corporation) — tentative single pick

### (a) Q1 FY2027 revenue $4.14bn, up ~29% year-on-year, a record quarter — CONFIRMED

- KLA reported fiscal Q1 2027 (quarter ended 2026-09-30) on 2026-10-28, after market close. Revenue was $4.14 billion ($4.136bn in one summary), up 28.9% year-on-year from $3.21bn and up 13% sequentially, above the $4.04bn consensus and above the midpoint of guidance ($4.0bn ± $200m).
- The company and the call transcript describe it as a record quarter — "Revenue was another record at $4.14 billion" (CEO Rick Wallace, earnings call 2026-10-28) — with a record 46.1% non-GAAP operating margin and non-GAAP EPS of $1.22 vs ~$1.18 consensus.
- Sources: KLA Q1 FY2027 earnings call transcript, 2026-10-28 (AlphaStreet / Motley Fool); MarketBeat earnings report, 2026-10-28; SiliconReport, 2026-10-28/29 (noting the 10-for-1 split effective 2026-06-11 in the per-share figures).

### (b) December-quarter guide ~$4.7bn (raised guide) and raised 2027 WFE outlook — CONFIRMED

- December-quarter (fiscal Q2 2027) guidance given on the 2026-10-28 call: revenue $4.7 billion ± $200 million ($4.5–$4.9bn), non-GAAP EPS $1.44 ± $0.11 — above the ~$4.5bn consensus at the time.
- Wafer-fab-equipment (WFE) outlook: management raised the calendar-2026 WFE market estimate (including advanced packaging) to the mid-$150bn range, and said it currently expects the market to exceed $200bn in calendar 2027. It also said fuller 2027 market/segment expectations would come on the late-January call, so the 2027 figure is a preliminary outlook, not a full formal guide — but the direction and the >$200bn number are as claimed.
- Sources: Motley Fool Q1 FY2027 call transcript takeaways, 2026-10-29; MarketBeat earnings takeaways; Quantli summary, 2026-10-29. Note on wording: "raised guide" is accurate for the WFE outlook and for guidance vs consensus; the $4.7bn itself is the new December-quarter guide, not a revision of a prior December-quarter guide.

### (c) Moat: dominant (~50%+ / near-monopoly) share of process-control metrology/inspection for advanced nodes — CONFIRMED

- This is the strongest-documented moat claim in the three names, because KLA's own investor-day material, sourced to Gartner, puts a number on it: KLA's March 12, 2026 Investor Day deck shows 2025 semiconductor process-control market share of 58% (up 360 bps from 2021; 2024 share shown as 56% in the same deck series), sourced to Gartner (April 2026 / April 2025). The next competitors are shown at a fraction of that share (one summary: ~6.5× the nearest competitor).
- Independent commentary corroborates the same range: ~50%+ / high-50s to low-60s depending on market definition, and "effectively a near-monopoly at the most advanced nodes (N5/N3/N2/sub-2nm) and at advanced packaging" (MacroPlane thesis summary, Nov 2026).
- Caveat, stated plainly: the 50%+ figure is for the *semiconductor process-control* market as Gartner/KLA define it. A much broader "metrology and inspection" definition that sweeps in non-competing vendors (e.g. Thermo Fisher, Zeiss) yields a far lower share. The claim as briefed — process-control metrology/inspection, advanced nodes — uses the narrow definition, so it stands; it must not be quoted as a share of all metrology.
- Sources: KLA 2026 Investor Day deck (CEO Rick Wallace, 2026-03-12, via KLA IR PDF); KLA Q3 FY2026 earnings slides summarising the same Gartner series; MacroPlane process-control thesis, Nov 2026.

## 2. FIX (Comfort Systems USA)

### (a) Record backlog ~$17.81bn, up ~90% year-on-year — CONFIRMED

- Company press release (BusinessWire, 2026-10-22) for the quarter ended 2026-09-30: "Backlog as of September 30, 2026 was $17.81 billion as compared to $14.06 billion as of June 30, 2026 and $9.38 billion as of September 30, 2025." Same-store backlog was $17.39bn. $17.81bn / $9.38bn − 1 = 89.9%, i.e. ~90%.
- Zacks' 10-Q-based summary confirms the same three numbers and the +90.0% year-on-year / +26.7% sequential changes, and describes the backlog as a record. Management noted backlog duration has extended to ~18–24 months (from ~9 months historically) and that backlog covers contracted installation/replacement work, usually over the next 6–12 months for recognition — it is not all of future revenue.
- Sources: Comfort Systems USA Q3 2026 results press release, BusinessWire, 2026-10-22; Zacks, 2026-10-23 and 2026-10-26.

### (b) Q3 2026 beat: revenue ~$3,766.3m (+54%) and EPS ~$13.91 vs ~$12.85 consensus — CONFIRMED (consensus varies by provider; beat holds on every basis)

- Reported (press release 2026-10-22; 10-Q filed 2026-10-22): revenue $3,766.3m vs $2,451.0m a year earlier = +53.7% (≈54% rounded); net income $489.0m; diluted EPS $13.91 vs $8.25 a year earlier (+68.6%).
- Consensus: the briefed ~$12.85 is one legitimate provider figure (MarketBeat / StockStory: $12.85; Tickeron: $12.81), but Zacks' consensus was $12.71. Revenue consensus was ~$3.35–$3.40bn (Zacks $3.35bn; StockStory $3.40bn). On every published basis the quarter was a clear beat: EPS beat by $1.06–$1.20 and revenue beat by ~11–12%. The briefed numbers are therefore accurate; the panel should cite the provider when quoting consensus, because $12.71 vs $12.85 is a real $0.14 spread, not a rounding difference.
- Sources: BusinessWire Q3 2026 press release, 2026-10-22; Zacks earnings report, 2026-10-22 ($12.71 consensus); MarketBeat earnings report, 2026-10-22 ($12.85 consensus); StockStory earnings-call summary, 2026-10-24.

### (c) Founder CFO Bill George retiring after the 2026 10-K, succeeded by Julie Shaeff — CONFIRMED

- Company press release "Comfort Systems Announces 2027 Leadership Transition" (BusinessWire, 2026-10-22, also filed as Exhibit 99.1 to an 8-K dated 2026-10-22): "Bill George, Executive Vice President and Chief Financial Officer, will retire following the filing of the Company's Annual Report on Form 10-K for the year ending December 31, 2026, completing a remarkable 30-year career"; "Julie Shaeff, currently the Company's Senior Vice President and Chief Accounting Officer, is being promoted to Executive Vice President and Chief Financial Officer, effective upon Mr. George vacating that role."
- Precision on "founder": George "helped found the Company in 1997 and has served as Chief Financial Officer since May 2005" (same release). "Founder CFO" is fair shorthand, not a literal "sole founder" claim. Shaeff has been Chief Accounting Officer since May 2005 — an internal succession, not an external hire.
- The selloff context is also supported: multiple reports note shares fell ~6% after hours on 2026-10-22 (and ~9% on 2026-10-23) despite the beat, attributing the move to the absence of formal forward guidance plus the CFO transition and AI-data-centre capex worries — i.e. the transition headline cited in the selloff is real, though it was not the only cited factor.
- Sources: BusinessWire leadership-transition release, 2026-10-22; FIX 8-K Exhibit 99.1, 2026-10-22; Tickeron Q3 recap and 30-day decline analysis, Oct 2026.

## 3. HWM (Howmet Aerospace)

### (a) Recent beat-and-raise quarter — CONFIRMED: Q3 2026, reported 2026-10-29

- Quarter: Q3 2026, reported 2026-10-29 (Pittsburgh press release via PR Newswire / Morningstar).
- Numbers: revenue $2,703m, up 29% year-on-year (25% organic — "the strongest quarterly growth rate on record for Howmet"); GAAP EPS $1.42, adjusted EPS $1.46, up 54% from $0.95; adjusted EBITDA $879m, up 43%, margin 32.5% (+310 bps). Revenue, EBITDA margin and EPS all exceeded the high end of management's own guidance for the quarter.
- Beat vs Street: adjusted EPS $1.46 beat the $1.37 consensus (+6.6%); revenue $2.70bn beat the ~$2.59bn consensus (+4.2%).
- Raise: full-year 2026 guidance raised to revenue $10.29–$10.31bn (from ~$10.0–$10.1bn), adjusted EBITDA $3.325–$3.335bn, adjusted EPS $5.49–$5.51 (from $5.23–$5.31), free cash flow $1.95–$2.05bn (from ~$1.9bn). Q4 2026 guide: revenue $2.74–$2.76bn, adjusted EPS $1.49–$1.51. Management also introduced initial 2027 revenue guidance of ~$11.5bn (~+12%). The board authorised a further $5bn of buybacks on 2026-10-28 (total authorisation $5.397bn).
- Sources: Howmet Q3 2026 press release, 2026-10-29; Zacks, 2026-10-30; Motley Fool, 2026-10-29; Finimize, 2026-10-29.

### (b) Moat: certification lock-in / dominant position in aerospace forgings and engineered fasteners that new entrants cannot readily replicate — CONTRADICTED as worded (certification barrier confirmed; dominance not established)

- What IS confirmed — the certification / switching-cost mechanism: Howmet operates in an "aerospace and defense oligopoly characterized by steep barriers to entry, immense capital requirements, and rigorous regulatory certification processes"; suppliers must clear customer audits and approvals such as AS9100, FAA and EASA before shipping safety-critical parts, and for castings/forgings/engine components qualification "can take years, not months, because one process change can trigger re-testing and re-approval," so the threat of new entrants stays low. OEM programmes run 20+ years and buyers prefer suppliers with long track records. Howmet's proprietary metallurgy, patents and OEM relationships are real, and its April 2026 ~$1.8bn acquisition of Consolidated Aerospace Manufacturing (CAM) was explicitly aimed at higher-margin, certification-heavy fastener content.
- What is NOT established — "dominant position" in forgings and engineered fasteners: no primary source found in this check gives Howmet a dominant (let alone majority) share of either market, and the third-party segment estimates located point the other way: Engine Products ~15% share vs a ~25% leader (GE Aviation), and Fastening Systems ~18% vs a ~22% leader (LISI Aerospace) (FernFortune BCG analysis, citing 2023 10-K-era data). Formidable named competitors — Berkshire Hathaway's Precision Castparts, ATI, VSMPO, LISI Aerospace, TriMas — are consistently listed alongside Howmet. "Global leader" / "leading supplier" language is common; a quantified dominant share is not.
- Verdict reasoning: a moat claim whose central factual assertion is dominance cannot be marked CONFIRMED on the strength of its mechanism alone when the share evidence contradicts dominance. The panel may keep the certification-lock-in / high-switching-cost moat, but it must drop "dominant position in forgings and engineered fasteners" or replace it with the sourced, weaker claim: a leading, entrenched oligopoly supplier whose qualification barriers make displacement slow and costly, facing large named rivals (notably Precision Castparts in castings/forgings and LISI in fasteners).
- Sources for the barrier: AlphaStreet Howmet analysis (oligopoly/certification); DCF Analyst HWM Porter's Five Forces preview (AS9100/FAA/EASA, years-long qualification); AlphaStreet Q1 2026 franchise analysis (CAM acquisition, switching costs). Source contradicting dominance: FernFortune Howmet BCG analysis (segment share estimates ~15% / ~18%, leaders named). Note: the FernFortune figures are third-party estimates, not a company filing — but they are the only quantified share evidence located, and no contrary quantified evidence was found, so the dominance wording cannot stand as verified.
