## 1. SNDK — SanDisk Corporation (NAND flash / enterprise SSDs)

### 1. Why it's down — temporary vs permanent
The drawdown is a violent round-trip after a parabolic run, not a fundamental collapse — but it is a *cyclical* drawdown, which is a different kind of risk from a one-off miss.

- **The run first:** SNDK rose ~4,000% from its 52-week low near $36 (Tickeron, Oct 2026, citing the move to the $2,354.39 high set 2026-06-22) and was still up 352.9% year-to-date at the 2026-11-02 close (MetaCap/Finnhub data). A stock with a beta of ~3.3–5.2 (sources vary) that has run that far is exposed to profit-taking on any excuse.
- **The trigger sequence in October 2026:** (i) mid-October, China's CXMT announced progress on "4F²" DRAM technology with a product planned before end-2026, rattling memory stocks — SNDK fell ~15% in the week to 2026-10-19 (FX Leaders, 2026-10-19); Goldman Sachs and KeyBanc argued the CXMT supply fear was overstated. (ii) 2026-10-22, an 8.5% single-day drop as AI-spending concerns swept semiconductors after Tesla trimmed its 2026 capex plan and Lam Research issued a cautious outlook (Tickeron, Oct 2026). (iii) 2026-10-29, SNDK beat its own guidance (see below) yet fell 3.8% after hours and then 13.47% on 2026-10-30 to $1,150.89 — financial media tied the 10-30 drop to Kioxia, its fab partner, guiding below estimates and warning the AI-driven flash-price surge is fading (Gotrade/TIKR, Nov 2026). The Q2 FY2027 guide ($12.2–12.6B) is still +10.6–14.2% sequentially, but slower than the +23% sequential jump just printed — "a profit this large can still meet a seller when the increment stops widening" (The Traders Spread, Oct 2026).
- **Verdict: TEMPORARY / CYCLICAL, with peak-cycle risk attached.** The cause is sentiment + sector rotation + peak-pricing fear after a historic re-rating, not lost demand, a lost customer, or a failed product. That said, NAND is famously cyclical: today's 85% gross margin is itself the thing that invites new supply. Temporary does not mean safe — the "temporary" here is a cycle turn, and cycles can last quarters.

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 6/10
- **Moat:** process technology plus the Kioxia joint venture (Flash Ventures) — SNDK's net PP&E was only $705M against a >$44B revenue run-rate because manufacturing sits in the JV (Analysis.org, Oct 2026); BiCS 8 products and enterprise-SSD qualification with three U.S. hyperscalers (Q1 FY2027 call); and the new-business-model (NBM) long-term agreements with floor prices covering ~50% of FY2027 bits and ~two-thirds of FY2028 bits, with total contract liabilities of $1.53B (Analysis.org). Qualification cycles and hyperscaler relationships are real switching costs.
- **Disruption threat:** NAND is a commodity at the margin — CXMT (China), Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron and Kioxia itself can all add bits, and AI does not make NAND irreplaceable; it *increases* demand for fast storage for inference, but any supplier's bits are largely interchangeable once qualified. AI is a demand tailwind, not a moat.
- **Score 6/10:** AI cannot route around storage, but a rival's wafer can. The moat is qualification + JV scale, not uniqueness.

### 3. Rebound catalyst
- **Already delivered, not yet rewarded:** fiscal Q1 2027 (quarter ended 2026-10-02, reported 2026-10-29): revenue $11.031B, +23% sequentially and +378% YoY, above $10.3–10.8B guidance; non-GAAP gross margin 85.1% (vs 84.6% prior quarter, 29.9% a year earlier); non-GAAP EPS $49.51 (vs $39.25 prior quarter, $1.22 a year earlier); data-centre revenue $4.8B, +61% sequentially; adjusted FCF $5.7B, a 52% margin (Sandisk Exhibit 99.1 / earnings call, 2026-10-29).
- **Next steps:** Q2 FY2027 guidance of $12.2–12.6B revenue, 84.5–85.5% non-GAAP gross margin, $55–57 pre-split non-GAAP EPS; Q2 earnings expected ~2027-01-27 (InTickers estimate, not company-confirmed); the 8-for-1 split (Nov 19–20) is cosmetic but often improves retail liquidity; and any Kioxia/NAND pricing print that shows the price surge is *not* fading would directly rebut the 10-30 selloff thesis. Timing: weeks for the split, one quarter for the pricing proof.
- The contracted-bit coverage (50% FY2027, ~67% FY2028) is the structural de-cyclicalisation story: if NBM holds, the next NAND downturn is shallower than past ones.

### 4. Balance-sheet survival
- **Exceptionally strong:** $5.1B cash and cash equivalents and **no debt** at quarter-end (Q1 FY2027 call); operating cash flow $6.4B in the quarter; capex only $378M (3.4% of revenue); $5.0B returned via buybacks (3.2M shares) in the quarter, with a $14B authorisation approved 2026-08-05 (MarketBeat). It can endure a deep NAND downturn without distress or dilution — the risk is earnings collapse, not solvency.

### 5. Margin of safety / valuation
- **Optically absurdly cheap — which is itself the warning:** trailing P/E ~8.6x, forward P/E ~4.4x (StockAnalysis, Nov 2026); MarketBeat P/E 8.62x. That is far below a ~30x peer median (MetaCap) and far below any plausible through-cycle multiple — because the market is pricing peak-cycle earnings that may not repeat.
- **Analyst targets:** MarketBeat consensus $2,056.23 (Moderate Buy; 1 Strong Buy, 22 Buy, 5 Hold, 0 Sell), implying ~+78.7% vs the $1,150.89 screen price; StockAnalysis mean $2,023.63 (+75.8%). Post-earnings trims cut both ways: RBC cut $1,600 → $1,400 (Sector Perform, 2026-10-31), while Rosenblatt reiterated Buy at $2,400 and Goldman held Buy at $2,200 (MarketBeat roundup, Nov 2026).
- **Judgement: genuinely cheap on current earnings, not cheap on through-cycle earnings.** If 85% gross margins normalise toward even 50%, the forward P/E roughly doubles and the "margin of safety" evaporates. The safety here is the balance sheet and contracted volumes, not the multiple.

### 6. Category position
- Still a top-tier NAND player — one of a handful of scaled suppliers (Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron, Kioxia, SNDK) — and the data-centre mix shift (44% of revenue, from 33% in one quarter; consumer now only 3.6%, $397M, −29% sequentially) shows it is winning where AI demand is, not losing share. No evidence in this window of share loss to a named rival; the Kioxia warning is about *price*, not SNDK share.

### 7. Value-trap risk
- **The honest bear case:** this is a classic memory peak. If NAND prices roll over — CXMT adds supply, hyperscalers digest inventory, or the NBM floor prices prove renegotiable — revenue and that 85% margin can fall faster than any multiple expansion compensates. The stock already fell 51% from its high *while printing record results*; a genuine pricing downturn could take it far lower even from here, and the 352.9% YTD gain means early holders still have huge profits to take. A falling knife here looks like: Q2 guide missed, gross margin guides down from 84.5–85.5%, or contract-liability growth stalls.

### 8. Rebound score: 7/10
**Verdict:** A debt-free, record-printing NAND leader sold off on peak-cycle fear rather than broken fundamentals — a real rebound candidate, but sized as a cyclical trade, not a compounder, because the margin itself is the risk.

- **Verdict: MOSTLY TEMPORARY / TRANSITIONAL.** Crypto weakness is cyclical and now only 8.4% of revenue (vs ~21% a year ago), so it can no longer sink the whole story; the durable question is whether 12% growth with 57% EBITDA margins deserves a premium multiple. ### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 5/10

- **Disruption threat:** brokerage is structurally low-switching-cost and commission-free everywhere; AI agents / AI-first advice could commoditise the front end and route orders to the cheapest venue, and incumbents (Schwab, Fidelity, IBKR) plus Coinbase on crypto can match features. Prediction markets — the current growth engine — are legally contestable state by state.
- **Score 5/10:** a real retail franchise with network-ish deposit momentum, but no certification lock-in or proprietary technology that AI cannot replicate. The moat is distribution and habit, not irreplaceability.

### 3. Rebound catalyst
- **No single near-term trigger; a grind, not a gap:** next earnings are estimated 2027-02-16 (TipRanks, not company-confirmed) — a long wait. Nearer catalysts: (i) October deposit momentum annualising (>$13B in October alone, though $6B is a one-off Trump Accounts contribution — strip that out before extrapolating); (ii) crypto-volume recovery lifting the smallest segment; (iii) event-contract revenue holding after the World Cup and despite state halts; (iv) buybacks — only $25M in Q3 (0.2M shares at ~$105, covering just 31% of $80M SBC in the quarter, per GeminiQ analysis of the 10-Q) against a ~$1.35B remaining authorisation (Substack analysis, Oct 2026) — acceleration here would be a direct EPS support; (v) Dubai expansion authorisation and in-principle Singapore approval, plus the 1% (potentially up to 10%) stakes in Crypto.com/OG.com received without cash investment (MarketBeat, Oct 2026).

### 4. Balance-sheet survival
- **Strong, with a brokerage caveat:** corporate cash and cash equivalents $5.8B at 2026-09-30 (Zacks), up from $4.3B a year earlier; TipRanks total cash $5.83B vs total debt $2.66B; a further ~$6B of corporate cash, investments and stablecoin plus $4.9B undrawn credit lines cited in earnings coverage (Substack, Oct 2026); $2.2B of June-2026 convertible notes at 0% interest. No distress risk.
- **Caveat:** the consolidated balance sheet ($58.3B total assets) is dominated by customer-related items — receivables from users $23.6B, mainly margin loans (SEC-filing analysis) — so "total assets/liabilities" must not be read as corporate leverage. Corporate survival is not in question; customer-credit quality is the watch item (see flags below).

### 5. Margin of safety / valuation
- **Cheaper than its own history, still expensive vs the sector:** trailing P/E ~38.6x (Finnhub/TipRanks, Nov 2026; Stocknear shows 53.35x on a different EPS base — use the 38.6x consensus feed); forward P/E ~28.2–28.5x (Stocknear/Tradestie, Nov 2026) vs a 36.6x historical average (Tradestie) — i.e. compressed ~22% vs its own history. But that is still +134% above the Financial Services sector median P/E of 16.5x (CleaRank, Nov 2026), and P/S 14.86x is +291% above the sector median.
- **Analyst targets:** MarketBeat consensus $126.45 (Moderate Buy, 36 analysts; range $80–$165), +50.8% vs the $83.84 Nov-2 price; TipRanks average $133.82 (+57.5%); CleaRank median $135.00 (+61.0%, 26 of 32 Buy). The target-implied upside is large, but targets have not yet been cut to the new growth rate — they are a lagging indicator here.
- **Judgement: a partial margin of safety on its own history, none on sector comparables.** The dip has de-rated HOOD from hyper-growth to fast-fintech pricing; it is not "cheap" in any absolute sense, and a further growth deceleration would compress it again.

### 6. Category position
- Still the leading retail-first brokerage by funded-customer growth and deposit momentum, and management claims double-digit market-share gains YoY in each of equities, options, crypto and margin (Q3 call via Trefis). It is not losing share — it is losing *growth rate* as it laps huge 2025 comps and crypto cools. Event contracts ($173M/quarter) make it the scaled leader in a new category, subject to the legal risk above. Pricing power is limited (commission-free is table stakes); monetisation power comes from Gold, margin, and payment-for-order-flow-adjacent volumes, not price hikes.

### 7. Value-trap risk
- **The honest bear case:** growth keeps decelerating (75% → 21% → 12%) while opex (+16%) outruns revenue; crypto never re-accelerates; event contracts get halted in more states or shut down; the Trump Accounts deposit sugar-rush flatters a quarter and then reverses; and the multiple keeps compressing from 28x forward toward a mature-broker ~15–20x, which would swamp earnings growth. Insider selling ($1.9B sold vs $57.3M bought over 18 months, per CleaRank) is a soft negative, not proof. A falling knife here looks like: Q4 deposits ex-Trump Accounts stall, net interest revenue falls again on securities-lending weakness (already −$19M vs +$87M a year ago), and credit provisions (already $71M in Q3, $61M on credit cards) keep rising.

### 8. Rebound score: 6/10
**Verdict:** A record quarter punished for decelerating growth and a crypto hole that is now too small to matter — a credible rebound on deposit momentum and buyback capacity, but the next earnings print is months away and the multiple still assumes a growth rate it no longer prints.

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 9/10
- **Moat:** dominant process-control franchise — inspection, metrology and yield management that become *more* valuable as devices get more complex (advanced logic, advanced packaging, AI chips); deep patent/process-tech base, installed-base data and service annuities, and qualification lock-in: a fab cannot swap an inspection recipe mid-ramp without re-qualifying yield. Semiconductor Process Control was $10.9B of $12.2B fiscal-2025 revenue (Trefis). Operating margin 41.7% (TTM) and ROIC ~38.4% (ClearThesis) are the financial signature of that moat.
- **AI angle:** AI does not substitute for KLAC — it *drives* KLAC, because AI accelerators are the most defect-sensitive, most complex devices ever built, and every additional process step adds inspection intensity. No AI model inspects a wafer.
- **Score 9/10:** among the least replaceable names in the entire S&P 500 equipment chain. The only deductions are cyclicality of customer capex and China exposure, not substitutability.

### 3. Rebound catalyst

The Investor Day gross-margin target range is 63–64% (vs 62.7% printed, 62.5% ± 1pp guided for December).
- **Timing:** the next print (fiscal Q2, estimated late January / 2027-02-04 per FMP via StockExpertAI, not company-confirmed) is the first proof point; the December-quarter guide itself, if hit, mechanically re-rates a stock sold on "peak" fears. ### 4. Balance-sheet survival

Operating cash flow $1.531B and FCF $1.336B in the quarter (32.3% of revenue); LTM OCF $4.512B, FCF $4.038B. Capital returns of $1.241B in the quarter ($936M buybacks + $305M dividends) were 93% of FCF — aggressive but fully covered. No distress or dilution risk in any plausible drawdown.

### 5. Margin of safety / valuation

Trailing P/E 39.2–45.2x depending on feed (Finnhub 39.19x; Stockstobuynow 45.2x) — feeds differ on the EPS base after the split; use the forward multiple for comparability.

Post-earnings target *cuts* (UBS $180, Needham $200) sit below the mean — the Street is trimming into strength, which is a caution, not a capitulation.
- **Judgement: no margin of safety on its own history; a margin of safety only if you believe the 2027 WFE >$200B guide.** The dip has made KLAC cheaper than it was, not cheap. The safety is earnings visibility, not the multiple.

### 6. Category position

The share-loss narrative belongs to AMAT's WFE share debate (see below), not KLAC. Memory mix (~40% of December-quarter process-control revenue guided) is a mix headwind to watch, not a share loss.

### 7. Value-trap risk
- **The honest bear case:** semicap is cyclical and KLAC's own history proves the drawdown risk — it fell 69% in 2008–09 and 39% in 2022, deeper than the index both times, with a median 2.3-month recovery but 26.6 months after the financial crisis (Trefis). If AI capex pauses, if China restrictions widen, or if the 2027 WFE >$200B call proves optimistic, a 28x-forward stock with 63–64% margin targets has a long way to fall, and the post-earnings target cuts show analysts are already hedging. Elevated opex for next-gen tools (December-quarter opex guided ~$710M) caps near-term margin expansion even if revenue hits.

### 8. Rebound score: 8/10
**Verdict:** The strongest rebound case in this batch — a record quarter, a raised guide, and a raised 2027 market outlook, sold off purely on sector sentiment — docked only because the multiple still demands flawless execution.

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 8/10
- **Moat:** breadth of materials-engineering leadership — the company describes itself as the leader in materials engineering for virtually every chip made; management claims #1 process-equipment positions in leading-edge foundry-logic, DRAM and advanced packaging, which should drive ~75% of WFE growth 2025–2030 (investor-day materials, Oct 2026); the EPIC Center (Silicon Valley, unveiled Oct 2026) as the world's largest collaborative process/R&D facility; a services business growing >20% in calendar 2026 toward >$12B by 2030; and capital intensity + process IP that no AI model replaces — AI *needs* the deposition, etch and inspection tools AMAT builds.
- **Threats:** direct, scaled rivals (Lam Research in etch, Tokyo Electron, ASML in lithography, KLA in process control) contest every segment; the eBeam/optical inspection push ($2B revenue expected in calendar 2026, $9B addressable market cited at the investor day) puts AMAT into KLA's backyard, where it is the challenger, not the incumbent.
- **Score 8/10:** AI cannot route around materials engineering, and AMAT's breadth is unmatched — docked for genuine segment-by-segment competition and the China access loss.

### 3. Rebound catalyst — with a dated binary event
- **The binary event is 10 days away: fiscal Q4 2026 earnings on 2026-11-12** (per the task brief and TIKR). Street expectations ~$10.30B revenue and ~$4.06 adjusted EPS, inside the $9.75–10.75B / $3.82–4.22 guidance range; the line to watch is gross margin against the ~50.4% guide — meeting it is the first datapoint on the road to the 2030 54% target (TIKR). Any calendar-2027 WFE framework — the thing withheld on Oct 13 — would directly remove the overhang that caused the selloff.
- **The 2030 bridge (investor day, 2026-10-13):** non-GAAP gross margin ~54% (from ~50% now, ~+100bps/year, not in a straight line), non-GAAP operating margin ~40% (from ~33% EBIT margin, via +4pts gross margin + 200–300bps opex leverage, part of it from AI automation in corporate functions, per CFO Brice Hill), >2pts of WFE share gain from 2025, service revenue >$12B, and 80–100% of FCF returned to shareholders. Also: a planned increase of the Besi stake to 20%, strengthening hybrid-bonding exposure for AI-chip complexity (MarketBeat/TIKR coverage, Oct–Nov 2026).
- **Timing:** Nov 12 is the catalyst; the 2030 targets are a multi-year grind, not a near-term re-rating, and a Q4 miss or another refusal to frame 2027 would extend the drawdown.

### 4. Balance-sheet survival
- **Strong:** at 2026-07-26 (Q3 balance sheet): cash and equivalents $7.037B plus short-term investments $2.196B = $9.233B liquidity vs total debt ~$6.5B (debt-to-equity ~0.3x, current ratio ~2.4x, per Motley Fool analysis of the Q3 release); TipRanks fiscal-year table shows FY2025 cash + short-term investments $8.57B vs total debt $7.05B (net cash −$191M on that definition) and FY2025 FCF $5.70B. Q3 alone generated record $3.04B operating cash flow and returned $860M ($440M buybacks + $420M dividends).
- **Watch item, not a distress signal:** nine-month FY2026 FCF fell ~2% to $3.58B despite net income +44% to $7.37B, because capex surged 35% to $1.99B and working capital absorbed $1.88B (receivables $7.69B at Jul-26, +$2.51B from FY-end; inventories $6.56B, +$649M) — though Q3 FCF alone was $2.33B, ~92% of net income, so the nine-month drag was H1-weighted (Business-News-Today analysis of the Q3 filing). No distress or dilution risk.

### 5. Margin of safety / valuation
- **Materially de-rated, still not cheap in absolute terms:** trailing P/E ~32–34x (MarketBeat 32.29–33.63x, Oct–Nov 2026); forward P/E ~21–24x depending on feed and date (Apex News: 21.14x; TIKR: 24.41x after the investor day, down from 33.48x on 2026-01-25 while NTM EPS estimates rose 83% to $17.61 — i.e. the de-rating is multiple compression on rising estimates, the healthiest kind). PEG ~1.09–1.16x.
- **Analyst targets:** MarketBeat consensus $632.70 (Moderate Buy; 30 Buy / 9 Hold), implying +62.2% vs the $390.09 screen price; TIKR Street mean $632.28; Cantor Fitzgerald reiterated $850 on Oct 13 (the high outlier); Evercore $590 and B. Riley $700 (cut from $790) bracket the post-selloff view. Note the targets pre-date much of the October fall and may be cut further — treat the +62% as an upper bound, not a forecast.
- **Judgement: a genuine margin of safety has opened up vs AMAT's own January multiple and vs the target-implied price, but not vs a through-cycle equipment multiple (~15–20x).** The safety is the earnings trajectory (record quarter, Q4 guide up) plus the balance sheet, not a bargain multiple.

### 6. Category position
- Still the largest WFE supplier by revenue with #1 positions in its core segments (per management, Oct 13) — but this is the one name in the batch where share loss is a live, sourced question rather than a straw man: the reported 20.0% → 17.9% WFE share decline (2024 → 2025) is cited as unverified in the source itself, and management's answer is that the 2030 >2pt share-gain target *already assumes* restricted-China growth under current trade rules, so ex-restriction share is rising. The Nov-12 print and any 2027 framework are the adjudicators. Pricing power: 50%+ gross margins and a 54% target say yes in served markets; in China, pricing power is moot where it cannot sell.

### 7. Value-trap risk
- **The honest bear case:** the investor day told the market AMAT will not frame 2027 — if Q4 on Nov 12 also withholds a 2027 framework, or guides Q1 below expectations, the "no visibility" discount persists and the stock grinds lower despite good quarters. China could tighten further from the ~70% addressable level; clean-room capacity could cap the 2027 ramp; and the WFE share question, if confirmed, would reframe AMAT from share-gainer to share-defender. A falling knife here looks like: Nov-12 gross margin below ~50.4%, no 2027 WFE number again, and a Q1 guide that implies the Q4 record was a peak.

### 8. Rebound score: 7/10
**Verdict:** A record-printing equipment leader de-rated 24% in a month on a missing forecast rather than a missed quarter — a strong rebound setup into a dated Nov-12 catalyst, docked for China and for the share question that only that print can settle.

## Earnings-quality flags (deterministic screen — explained, not re-litigated)

### HOOD — LOW_CASH_CONVERSION
- **What it likely captures:** headline operating cash flow at a brokerage is dominated by customer money, not corporate earnings. Nine-month 2026 operating cash flow of $3.983B includes a $6.45B rise in payables to users and a $7.85B rise in securities loaned (SEC-filing analysis), while net income after non-cash adjustments added only $274M in the same walk (GeminiQ analysis of the 10-Q). Q3 operating cash flow of $1.225B likewise "moves mainly with customer funds" (Drillr). In other words, OCF can look huge (or negative — FY2024 OCF was −$157M) for reasons that have nothing to do with HOOD's own profitability.
- **One-line verdict: LARGELY A BENIGN BUSINESS-MODEL ARTIFACT — but it means you must judge HOOD on adjusted EBITDA ($808M, 57% margin in Q3) and corporate cash ($5.8B), not on headline OCF, and the flag correctly warns against treating brokerage OCF as earnings quality.**

### HOOD — RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN
- **What it captures:** receivables from users of $23.6B at 2026-09-30, mainly margin loans (SEC-filing analysis); the margin book grew +59% YoY to $22.1B, with margin interest +59.5% to $244M — far faster than total revenue (+11.8%). Receivables are outrunning revenue because customers are borrowing more, not because HOOD is stuffing channels or ageing trade debtors.
- **One-line verdict: BENIGN EXPLANATION (margin-lending growth) WITH A REAL CREDIT-CYCLE RISK ATTACHED — provisions already rose to $71M in Q3 ($61M on credit cards), so the risk to watch is a market drawdown forcing margin calls and credit losses, not revenue-recognition games.**

### KLAC — RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN
- **What it captures:** at 2026-09-30, accounts receivable were $3.072B and inventories rose 12% sequentially ($3.649B → $4.090B) into a record $4.136B quarter (KLA release/call summaries). Working capital is building alongside — and slightly ahead of — shipments, consistent with (i) long lead-time components (12 months, 18–24 months on some products, per the July call) being procured for the guided $4.7B December quarter, and (ii) process-control revenue-recognition timing: tools ship, install and are accepted before revenue is fully recognised, so receivables can lead reported revenue in an accelerating quarter.
- **One-line verdict: MOSTLY TIMING / SUPPLY-CHAIN ARTIFACT, NOT EARNINGS RUNNING AHEAD OF CASH — proven by $1.531B operating cash flow and $1.336B free cash flow (32% of revenue) in the same quarter; watch only if receivables/inventory keep rising while the December guide is missed.**

### SNDK and AMAT — no flags
- **Screen result: no flags — but two supplementary watch items the panel should note, since they are not contradictions of the screen (which uses its own window/method):** (i) SNDK accounts receivable rose $1.877B in fiscal Q1 2027 to $6.585B while revenue rose $2.066B sequentially — 91 cents of each incremental sales dollar still owed at 2026-10-02, with days sales outstanding moving ~48 → ~54 days (Analysis.org, Oct 2026) — mitigated by hyperscaler counterparty quality and by $6.4B operating cash flow / $5.7B adjusted FCF in the same quarter. (ii) AMAT accounts receivable reached $7.691B at 2026-07-26, +$2.51B from fiscal year-end, and nine-month FCF was only 48.6% of net income vs 71.7% a year earlier (Business-News-Today) — mitigated by Q3 FCF at ~92% of net income and by contract liabilities rising to $3.271B (customer cash ahead of revenue).
- **One-line verdict for both: NO SCREEN FLAG, AND NO HIDDEN CASH-CONVERSION PROBLEM FOUND — working-capital build in both cases tracks a genuine acceleration in shipments, with cash flow in the latest quarter validating the earnings.**
