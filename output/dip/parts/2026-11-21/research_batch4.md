## 1. BKNG — Booking Holdings

### 1.1 Why it's down — temporary vs permanent
The drawdown is a two-layer story: (a) a **cyclical travel softening** and (b) an **AI-disintermediation re-rating** — both layered on top of a still-growing business.

- **Feb 19, 2026: the AI panic day.** BKNG reported a blockbuster Q4 2025 (revenue \$6.35B, +16% YoY; adjusted EPS \$48.80 pre-split, beating \$48.23; gross bookings \$41.9B, all-time high) yet fell ~8.5% the next day. The trigger was Q1 2026 revenue guidance of 7–9% (deceleration from 11%) plus a \$700M "strategic reinvestment plan" to build AI infrastructure. The market read it as agentic AI (ChatGPT/Gemini agents booking trips directly) threatening the OTA "search arbitrage" model (financialcontent/MarketMinute, 2026-02-19). Sector-wide: by Feb 13, 2026, BKNG was -22% YTD, EXPE -16%, Airbnb -11%, while hotel operators (Marriott +14%, Hilton +12%) rose — investors were repricing who controls distribution (eMarketer, 2026-02-13). Deloitte: US travelers using genAI for trip planning tripled from 8% (2023) to 24% (2025); genAI-driven traffic to US travel sites jumped 3,500% YoY in July 2025 (Adobe).
- **Q3 2026 (Nov 3, 2026): the quarter was actually good; the guide was not.** Adjusted revenue \$9.79B (+9–10% cc, beat vs \$9.55B consensus), adjusted EPS \$4.66 (+17% YoY, beat vs \$4.49), room nights +6.7–7%, gross bookings \$54.5B (+10%, beat vs \$52.63B), adjusted EBITDA \$4.61B (+9%, 51.8% margin). But GAAP revenue fell 1% to \$8.89B after a **\$1.0B FTC charge** (\$900M revenue reduction for customer refunds + \$100M accrued loss; Priceline negotiating with the FTC over disclosures/fees/billing; the filing warns the FTC may seek more). Q4 guide was near-flat: room nights +1–3%, adjusted revenue 0–2%, adjusted EBITDA ~flat — ~4 points below Street at the midpoint — citing softer international travel late in Q3, renewed Middle East disruption in October, unrest in some European cities, and a planned investment step-up (Aardvark Labs recap; IBD, Nov 2026). Shares fell ~3.5% on the print and kept sliding: -10.1% over the 5 sessions to Nov 20, YTD -32.35% (Finnhub, Nov 20–21, 2026).
- **Verdict: mostly TRANSITORY, with a real but unresolved structural question.** The travel softening (Middle East, Europe unrest, US midterm-election uncertainty) is cyclical and explicitly cited by both BKNG and EXPE. The AI threat is genuine and unresolved — LLM referrals are accelerating but still <1% of room nights (Aardvark, Nov 2026) — so the market is pricing a *future* disruption, not a present one. The FTC charge is a one-off, though the final amount is not yet capped.

### 1.2 Moat / AI-irreplaceability — score 6/10
Moat: **supply scale + merchant-platform control + payments/compliance complexity**. Booking.com has ~8.6M alternative-accommodation listings; ~73% of gross bookings now run on its own merchant platform (up 4 pts YoY); Connected Trip transactions are a low-double-digit share of total and growing at a low-double-digit rate, >2x overall transaction growth. The CFO's counter-argument: fulfilling a booking means 100+ payment methods, 50+ currencies, regulation across 200+ countries, customer service and cancellations — an LLM asked to cancel a Dubai flight during the Middle East conflict simply couldn't action it (eToro, Nov 2026). BKNG is also building its own agentic tools (Priceline's Penny assistant now completes end-to-end bookings; CEO Glenn Fogel cited a "noticeable uplift" in conversion; ~\$450–550M annual run-rate transformation savings targeted, ~\$150M from AI-driven customer service; customer-service cost per booking falling at a double-digit rate). The bear case: if an agent becomes the front door, the OTA's search-arbitrage margin compresses and it becomes a commoditized fulfillment pipe. Score 6: the fulfillment/complexity moat is real and underappreciated, but the discovery layer — the most valuable part — is genuinely contestable by AI.

### 1.3 Rebound catalyst
- **Q4 2026 print (Feb 2027; FundSpec lists Feb 17, 2027 after close, EPS est. \$2.14 on \$6.5B revenue):** a room-night re-acceleration toward 3–5% and any easing of Middle East/Europe disruption would reset the guide narrative.
- **Buyback compounding:** \$9.5B repurchased in 9M 2026 (\$2.1B in Q3 alone), \$12.4B authorization remaining at Sep 30, 2026; diluted share count -7.5% YoY in Q3 and -35% from 2016 to 2025. At the depressed price each buyback dollar buys ~25% more stock than in January.
- **AI proof points:** Penny conversion data and LLM-referral share rising above 1% of room nights would directly attack the bear thesis.
- **FTC resolution:** a capped settlement removes the overhang (the filing warns more may be sought — the other direction is also possible).

### 1.4 Balance-sheet survival
Very strong. Cash and investments \$16.2B at Sep 30, 2026 (cash \$15.81B); long-term debt \$18.07B; TTM FCF \$9.2B (+11% YoY; \$9.09B in 2025, analysts expect \$11.7B by 2028 per TIKR). Net debt/EBITDA 0.56x per screen. Caveat: buybacks are running *ahead* of FCF — \$10.5B returned in 9M 2026 vs ~\$9.32B expected full-year FCF — funded by drawing down cash (\$17.7B → \$16.2B in Q3). Sustainable at a moderated pace, not at the 9M pace indefinitely. No distress or dilution risk.

### 1.5 Margin of safety / valuation
Genuinely cheap *because of the dip* — this is a multiple compression, not an earnings collapse. Aardvark downgraded in August 2026 at ~20x FY2026 earnings and upgraded to Outperform after the Q3 print at 16.4x FY2026E / 14.6x FY2027E, fair value \$188–212. Screen fwd P/E 11.8x vs that history; Bloomberg consensus (via eToro, ~Sep 2026): 32 buys, 9 holds, 0 sells, avg target implying ~40% upside. Median target \$213 = +44.6% above the Nov 19 close of \$147.32 (FundSpec, Nov 20, 2026). Screen upside +49%. Trailing P/E ~16x at a 36% discount to the sector median; net margin ~26% (AL Capital, Nov 19, 2026). The stock has beaten EPS estimates in 8 of its last 8 quarters.

### 1.6 Category position
Still the **#1 OTA globally** — largest of Booking/Airbnb/Expedia, most diversified (hotels, flights +19.4% in Q3, attractions +24%, alternative accommodation +7%, OpenTable, rental cars). No evidence of share loss: room nights re-accelerated to 6.7% in Q3 from 5.3% in Q2, gross bookings beat. The dip is a sentiment/multiple event, not a share event — though hotel brands gaining direct share is the slow-burn threat to watch.

### 1.7 Value-trap risk
The falling-knife scenario: agentic AI actually disintermediates — ChatGPT/Gemini become the booking front door, take rates compress, and BKNG's 87% gross margin / ~52% EBITDA margin prove to have been search-arbitrage rents, not durable economics. Second risk: the FTC matter escalates beyond \$1B. Third: international travel softness persists into 2027 and the Q4 guide proves optimistic, not conservative. If room-night growth stalls below 3% for several quarters while AI referral share climbs, the "cheap" multiple is a trap.

### 1.8 Rebound score: 7/10
**Verdict:** A world-class, cash-gushing #1 OTA repriced on a real-but-unproven AI fear while still growing room nights, bookings and EPS double-digit — the dip is a multiple reset, not a broken business, but the AI question must be answered by evidence, not hope.

## 2. TPR — Tapestry

### 2.1 Why it's down — temporary vs permanent
The drawdown is a **growth-rate derating after a record year**, not a business decline — plus a genuine weak brand (Kate Spade) inside a strong one (Coach).

- **Aug 13, 2026: the -16.5% day.** FY2026 was a record: revenue \$8.0B (+14% reported, +17% cc), adjusted EPS \$7.05 (+38%), adjusted FCF \$1.86B (+66%), gross margin 83.3%, Investor Day targets hit two years early. Q4 FY26 itself beat (revenue \$1.876B, adjusted EPS \$1.32 vs \$1.28). But FY2027 guidance — revenue \$8.4–8.5B (mid-single-digit growth, decelerating from +14–17%) and EPS \$7.80–7.90 — landed ~\$20M below consensus on revenue, essentially in line on EPS. The stock fell 16.49% to \$128.39 from \$153.74; the forward multiple compressed from ~19.6x to ~16.4x FY27 EPS in one day — the selloff was a *multiple reset*, not an earnings cut (BestStocks, Aug 13–14, 2026). About \$5B of market cap was sold in three days; shares fell ~20% from the >\$164 52-week high (early Aug) to ~\$128, and ~27–30% from the high by mid-October.
- **The soft spot is real:** Kate Spade revenue fell 10% in FY26 and 7% in Q4 (Q4 adjusted operating loss \$28.9M), with FY27 guided to a high-single-digit decline and a modest operating loss — a second straight year of shrinkage. Coach Q4 growth (+15%) also came in a touch under expectations. Guidance assumes a mid-20% tariff rate on US inventory receipts (management calls the YoY margin impact net-neutral after pricing/cost actions).
- **Q1 FY2027 (Nov 5, 2026) — the rebuttal, partially:** revenue \$1.89B (+11.1% YoY, beat vs \$1.85B), adjusted EPS \$1.82 (+32%, beat vs \$1.56), operating margin +230bp to >23%. Coach +16% cc (\$1.66B; Greater China +41% in the quarter per TickerReport, +39% cc per the CIIE interview; North America +7%). Kate Spade -10% to \$233M. Guidance *raised*: FY27 revenue \$8.5–8.6B, EPS \$8.25–8.35 (from \$7.80–7.90). Shares jumped ~7% on Nov 5, then faded again to \$120.04 by Nov 20 — the market still doesn't trust the multiple.
- **Verdict: TRANSITORY (multiple reset on decelerating growth), with a contained permanent drag.** The FY26→FY27 growth step-down is a normalization after an exceptional year, and Q1 FY27 shows Coach still compounding. The permanent-ish element is Kate Spade: a structurally shrinking brand (~12–13% of sales) that management admits is recovering "more gradually than planned."

### 2.2 Moat / AI-irreplaceability — score 8/10
Moat: **brand + accessible-luxury price positioning + Gen Z customer acquisition engine**. Coach is a 1941 heritage brand that has successfully repositioned into the \$200–500 handbag "sweet spot" — below true luxury (Chanel ~\$3,000+), above fast fashion — in a segment where the mid-range (3,500–14,000 RMB) fell 26% in China while Coach's entry price band grew. Coach was the top-selling international handbag brand on Tmall as of late 2025; Greater China has grown double-digit for six straight quarters (+39% cc in Q1 FY27 to \$385M); penetration in China is <1% with 80–100 new stores planned over 3–5 years and 5M people turning 18 each year. Customer engine: 2.6M new customers in Q1 FY27 (~35% Gen Z), ~11M in FY26; both AUR and units growing (handbag AUR low-double-digit). AI cannot substitute a physical status/leather-goods product; the threat is fashion-cycle and domestic Chinese brands (Songmont took the Tmall Double 11 top spot in 2025, selling at 1,000–3,000 RMB, directly overlapping Coach's entry price). Score 8: leather goods are AI-proof, but fashion taste is cyclical and Chinese domestic competition is a real, rising share threat.

### 2.3 Rebound catalyst
- **Q2 FY2027 print (Feb 4, 2027):** the holiday quarter; management guides revenue +5–6% cc, adjusted EPS ~\$3.05 (low-teens growth). A Coach beat with unit growth (not just price) would settle the "peak vs compounder" debate.
- **Guidance momentum:** the Nov 5 raise (+5.7% to the EPS midpoint) shows the Aug guide was conservative; a second raise in Feb would force a re-rating.
- **Capital return:** ~\$2B to be returned in FY27 (\$1.7B buybacks + \$1.85/share dividend); \$600M repurchased in Q1 alone; \$1.35B bought back in FY26. At ~13x forward earnings, buybacks are accretive.
- **Kate Spade stabilization:** any quarter where Kate Spade's decline narrows toward flat removes the main overhang; a divestiture/impairment decision would also clear the air (note: Kate Spade carries \$1.08B of intangibles with only ~30% impairment headroom — Deepval, Oct 16, 2026).

### 2.4 Balance-sheet survival
Comfortable. Q1 FY27 ended with \$782M cash/short-term investments and \$2.56B borrowings (net debt/EBITDA 1.65x per screen — the highest of the three, but modest for a brand company). FY26 operating cash flow \$1.98B, adjusted FCF \$1.86B. Q1 FY27 adjusted FCF was only \$56M (down from \$103M) despite operating cash flow rising to \$197M — a cash-conversion watch item, not a distress signal. No dilution risk; the buyback + dividend are funded from FCF.

### 2.5 Margin of safety / valuation
Cheap on a trough multiple, fair on a peak multiple — the whole debate. Screen fwd P/E 13.0x; at the Nov 5 close of \$134.15 it was ~16.2x the new \$8.30 FY27 EPS midpoint. Consensus target ~\$167–174 (16 of 20 analysts buy) vs \$120–127 — screen upside +36%; MarketBeat noted analysts seeing up to 54% upside in Sep 2026. Deepval (Oct 16, 2026, at \$119.75): ~15x FY27 EPS, ~11x EBITDA, ~7% FCF yield fully returned; 10% DCF ~\$118, fair value ~\$125 — "fair, not a bargain" on the base case, 40%+ upside to ~\$170 if Coach is a durable compounder. Gross margin 83.3% is best-in-class for apparel/accessories. The key uncertainty: Q4 FY26 handbag *units* were flat — growth came from mid-teens price increases — so the multiple hinges on whether unit growth resumes.

### 2.6 Category position
**#1 in accessible luxury handbags by the evidence that matters:** top-selling international handbag brand on Tmall, Coach = 87.7% of group revenue at a ~36% segment operating margin, gaining share in China/Europe/North America simultaneously. But global share is <1% and low-single-digits in North America — huge runway *or* a sign the category is fragmented and share is contestable. The dip is not share loss at Coach; it is Kate Spade shrinkage + growth deceleration + tariff anxiety.

### 2.7 Value-trap risk
The falling-knife scenario: Coach is at a **fashion-cycle peak** — FY26's 24% Coach growth was price-led (flat units, mid-teens AUR increases) and Gen Z's attention moves on; China luxury mall sales fell 12% in July 2026 and LVMH leather goods fell 5% in Q3, so the China engine can stall; tariffs become a real H2 headwind; Kate Spade's intangibles get impaired; and management keeps large M&A on the table (the \$8.5B Capri deal was blocked in 2024) — a big acquisition at a peak multiple would destroy the buyback story.

### 2.8 Rebound score: 6/10
**Verdict:** Coach is a genuine compounder at a derated multiple with a \$2B capital-return kicker, but the stock is a bet that unit growth resumes and Kate Spade stops bleeding — fairly priced if Coach is peaking, 40% upside if it isn't.

## 3. EXPE — Expedia Group

### 3.1 Why it's down — temporary vs permanent
The drawdown is a **Q4 guidance air-pocket after a strong Q3**, compounded by the same AI/OTA fear hitting BKNG — not a demand collapse.

- **52-week high \$342 on Aug 26, 2026; shares had climbed 49% in the year to Nov 4, 2026** (vs S&P +14%) on stronger growth and profits. Then Q3 2026 (Nov 4, 2026): revenue \$4.94B (+11.9% YoY, beat vs \$4.74B), adjusted EPS \$9.39 (+24%, beat vs \$8.89), adjusted EBITDA \$1.69B (+17%, 34.2% margin), gross bookings \$34.0B (+11%), B2B bookings +19% / revenue +23%, booked room nights +7% to 115.7M. Full-year guidance was *raised* for the second time (bookings \$131.4–131.9B, revenue \$16.37–16.43B, +11–12%).
- **The selloff trigger was Q4:** bookings growth guided to only +3.3–5.2%, revenue \$3.69–3.75B (+4.0–5.7%, midpoint below the \$3.74B consensus), adjusted EBITDA \$830–880M (22.5–23.5% margin, below consensus). CFO Derek Andersen cited the US midterm elections, Middle East conflict, unrest in Europe hurting long-haul routes, lapping prior-year B2B promotions, and a fall sale pulled forward into Q3. Shares fell ~9% after hours Nov 4 and ~13% from the Nov 4 close to \$275.05 intraday Nov 5; by Nov 20 the stock was \$234.96, -17.7% in a month and -30% off the high (Finnhub). Note the pattern: the same Q4 guide that sank BKNG's print sank EXPE's — a sector-wide demand wobble, not an EXPE-specific problem.
- **Verdict: TRANSITORY.** A one-quarter deceleration driven by geopolitics, election uncertainty and tough comparisons, with the full year raised twice. The structural AI question is shared with BKNG (see below) but EXPE's B2B engine (supplying inventory to banks, airlines, other travel sites) is arguably *less* exposed to consumer-facing AI agents than BKNG's B2C funnel.

### 3.2 Moat / AI-irreplaceability — score 7/10
Moat: **B2B distribution scale + Vrbo supply + One Key loyalty**. Expedia Partner Solutions is the largest B2B travel business; B2B gross bookings grew 19–22% across Q1–Q3 2026, ~3x the B2C rate, on wallet-share gains and new partners — B2B partners (banks, airlines) embed Expedia inventory in *their* products, which is sticky, contracted distribution that an AI agent cannot easily replicate. Consumer brands (Expedia, Hotels.com, Vrbo) plus the trivago advertising network add breadth. Management says it has early AI partnerships with Google and Meta, but agentic traffic is still small (Quantisnow, Nov 2026). Score 7: higher than BKNG because the B2B pipe is a genuine network/contract moat and Vrbo's alternative-accommodation supply is differentiated; lower than a pure physical product because the B2C half faces the same agent-disintermediation risk as BKNG.

### 3.3 Rebound catalyst
- **Q4 2026 print (Feb 11, 2027):** the guided air-pocket is the bar — beating \$3.69–3.75B revenue / \$830–880M EBITDA, or even confirming the low end with a clean 2027 outlook, removes the overhang. Any sign the Q4 softness was election/geopolitics timing (not demand) is the re-rating trigger.
- **B2B momentum:** B2B revenue +23% for three straight quarters; continued wallet-share gains with bank/airline partners.
- **Buyback + dividend:** \$576M repurchased in Q3, \$1.5B in 9M 2026, \$5.1B authorization remaining; \$0.48/quarter dividend. At ~9.4x forward earnings the buyback is highly accretive.
- **2027 guide:** management's first full-year 2027 outlook (with the Q4 print) is the real catalyst — a return to double-digit bookings growth would close much of the 30% gap to the high.

### 3.4 Balance-sheet survival
Fortress-level. Net cash \$107M at Sep 30, 2026 (essentially unlevered; net debt/EBITDA 0.04x per screen); cash \$5.04B + short-term investments \$527M; debt \$5.46B (down 12.2% YoY); \$2.5B revolver to 2031 undrawn; \$1B of 5.500% notes due 2036 issued in 2026. TTM FCF \$3.7B (+25% YoY) despite a Q3 free-cash *outflow* of \$1.40B (vs \$686M outflow a year earlier — seasonal; Q3 operating cash flow was -\$1.16B). 2026 acquisitions \$676M. No distress or dilution risk whatsoever.

### 3.5 Margin of safety / valuation
The cheapest of the three on the screen: fwd P/E 9.4x. At \$275.05 (Nov 5) it was 16.1x TTM diluted EPS of \$17.13; at the current ~\$235 it is ~13.7x TTM EPS. Screen analyst upside +42%. Sell-side expects revenue to grow 11.3% over the next 12 months for the peer group context (BKNG note). The cheapness is real *if* Q4 is a one-quarter dip: the business just raised full-year guidance twice and grew revenue 12% with expanding margins. If Q4's deceleration extends into 2027, 9.4x forward is a fair price for a low-growth OTA, not a bargain.

### 3.6 Category position
**#2 OTA globally, #1 in B2B travel distribution.** B2C is growing slower (bookings +7% in Q3 vs B2B +19%) and B2C adjusted EBITDA margin is 43.5% — healthy, not collapsing. No evidence of share loss: B2B is *gaining* wallet share; B2C deceleration mirrors the sector (BKNG guided the same way). Vrbo remains a top alternative-accommodation platform. The dip is a guidance event, not a share event.

### 3.7 Value-trap risk
The falling-knife scenario: Q4's slowdown is not a blip but the start of a demand downshift (US consumer weakening, long-haul travel structurally impaired by geopolitics), B2B growth decelerates from ~20% toward B2C's ~7% (it has already slipped 3 points since Q1), and the AI agent wave hits the B2C funnel while B2B partners eventually build direct supply. High beta (1.43) means it falls hardest in a risk-off tape. The Q3 cash outflow doubling is a reminder that the merchant model consumes working capital when growth wobbles.

### 3.8 Earnings-quality flag — RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN (recv_rev_divergence +0.11)
Accounts receivable \$5,513M at Sep 30, 2026 vs \$4,482M a year earlier (+23.0% YoY) and \$4,166M at Dec 31, 2025 — while Q3 revenue grew 11.9%. The 10-Q cash-flow statement shows a \$1,269M accounts-receivable drag in 9M 2026 (vs \$1,289M in 9M 2025 — essentially the same dollar drag, not worsening). **Benign business-model explanation, mostly:** the receivables build is driven by the fast-growing B2B segment (revenue +23%), where Expedia bills corporate/bank/airline partners on credit terms rather than collecting cash up front from consumers as in B2C merchant bookings — so mix shift toward B2B mechanically lifts receivables faster than revenue. Partially offset by deferred merchant bookings rising to \$12,579M from \$10,428M (cash collected ahead of travel). TTM FCF still grew 25% to \$3.7B, so cash is *not* failing to follow earnings over the full year. **One-line verdict:** largely a B2B mix-shift artifact with a seasonal Q3 cash outflow, not a channel-stuffing value-trap signature — but watch whether receivables growth stays above revenue growth into Q4; if it does while B2B decelerates, the benign story weakens.

### 3.9 Rebound score: 7/10
**Verdict:** The cheapest name in the batch — near-zero net debt, \$3.7B TTM FCF, B2B growing 3x B2C, and a Q4 guide that looks like a one-quarter pothole — but the rebound needs Q4 to prove the pothole, not the road.
