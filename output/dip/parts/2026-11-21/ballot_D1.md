## SINGLE-PICK BALLOT

- **Top pick:** FIX
- **Runner-up:** CAT
- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** FIX fell about 49% from its May peak despite a blowout Q3 — revenue up 53.7% year over year, EPS up 68.6%, and backlog up 90% year over year to \$17.81bn, its eighth straight sequential gain. The work is physical, skilled, mission-critical mechanical and electrical contracting, including direct-to-chip liquid cooling at 100–140 kW per rack, which AI cannot do and which AI demand actually accelerates. The balance sheet is fortress-like: about \$2.11bn net cash, with Q3 operating cash flow up 82.6% and free cash flow of \$805.6m. The sell-off was priced on margin anxiety, backlog duration stretching to 18–24 months, a CFO transition, and no forward guidance — not on a broken order book.
- **Why the dip is temporary (not permanent):** The single most compelling evidence is the backlog itself: \$17.81bn, equal to about 1.8x trailing-twelve-month revenue, with 65–75% of performance obligations expected to be recognised in the next 12 months, while Q3 free cash flow exceeded net income by a wide margin. A permanently impaired business does not keep winning record orders and converting them to cash at that rate.
- **Moat / AI-irreplaceability:** Low risk — AI is a demand accelerant, not a substitute. Skilled-labour, certification and execution lock-in in mission-critical contracting cannot be replaced by a model or a chatbot; direct-to-chip cooling is hands-on physical work. The residual risk is concentration, with 59% technology revenue, not disruption.
- **Rebound catalyst & timing:** Margin normalisation in H1 2027 as jobs mature, as the CFO expects, plus the Q4 print around February 2027 and any hyperscaler capex reaffirmation. Backlog conversion over the next 12 months is the mechanical trigger, not a sentiment turn.
- **12-18 month return scenario:** Base case roughly +30–40%, toward analyst upside in the dossier of +70% only partly realised as margins normalise and the backlog converts; bull case roughly +60–70%, in line with the dossier's +70% analyst upside, if margins recover and hyperscaler capex is reaffirmed. Earnings estimates in the dossier are rising, at \$50.18 for 2026 and \$65.30 for 2027.
- **Top risk (value-trap case):** Hyperscaler capex slows in 2027–28, the backlog converts slower and at lower margins, labour-cost inflation bites, and fixed-price risk on early-stage large projects turns a record backlog into a low-margin burden. That is a duration and margin trap, not a solvency trap, given the net-cash position.
