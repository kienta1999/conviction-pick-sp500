# Panelist B — Growth / quality compounder — ballot, run 2026-11-20

Lens: revenue durability, margins, returns on capital, and whether the dip has broken the growth story. A great business bought on sale, not the cheapest multiple or the fastest snap-back.

## Single-pick ballot

- **Top pick:** LRCX
- **Runner-up:** KLAC
- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** Lam is the number-one etch supplier, with roughly 45-50 percent of dry etch, and it reported its best quarter on record on 21 October 2026, then fell about 16 percent in the week ended 6 November including 16.4 percent on 5 November alone, a pure sector de-rating rather than a company miss. Revenue growth TTM is 28.6 percent, operating margin is 37.0 percent, ROE is 70.1 percent per the screen, and consensus normalised EPS is expected to rise from USD 5.82 in FY2026 to USD 9.65 in FY2027, up 66 percent, supported by management raising calendar-2026 WFE to the mid-USD 150bn range and giving a preliminary 2027 view above USD 200bn. The stock is net cash, produced record Q1 FY27 free cash flow of USD 3.24bn, and the board announced a USD 25bn buyback authorisation on the evening of 5 November, about 10 percent of market capitalisation at the time. At a screen forward P/E of 16.6, down from its own five-year average per TIKR, this is a dominant, high-return franchise repriced as if its orders had broken, when the constraint cited by management was Lam's own component supply, not weak demand.
- **Why the dip is temporary (not permanent):** The single most compelling evidence is that Lam fell after reporting its best quarter on record, with memory systems revenue at a record and up 120 percent year over year, and the dossier records explicitly that Lam did not announce a company-specific operational setback; the fall was the 5 November SOX drop of 12.25 percent, post-midterm data-centre politics, and a December rate-hike scare, followed by Nvidia's 17 November beat-and-raise (revenue USD 115.8bn, up 103 percent year over year, Q4 guide USD 130bn) confirming AI demand rather than a collapse.
- **Moat / AI-irreplaceability:** Low risk. Etch intensity rises with every node transition, GAA at 2nm, 3D NAND beyond 300 layers, and TSV for HBM, and Lam's cryogenic etch is described as the only proven high-volume path for HBM4 TSVs. AI cannot substitute for etch: every chip pattern must be physically etched, and qualification lock-in, more than 8,000 patents, and a services stream of USD 2.49bn in Q1 FY27, up 40 percent year over year, entrench the installed base. The residual threat is share loss at the margin to TEL, AMEC, or Naura, not obsolescence.
- **Rebound catalyst & timing:** Q2 FY27 earnings on 27 January 2027, guided at revenue of about USD 8.6bn plus or minus USD 400m and EPS of USD 2.33, is the hard proof point, with the WFE step-up toward more than USD 200bn in 2027 (Bernstein forecasts USD 217bn in 2027, up 39 percent, and USD 279bn in 2028) accruing through 2027. The USD 25bn buyback provides a floor in the meantime, and the stock already rose 7.7 percent the day after that announcement.
- **12-18 month return scenario:** Base case roughly plus 40-60 percent, toward the screen analyst upside of plus 77.8 percent, as the FY2027 EPS step-up to USD 9.65 is delivered and the multiple normalises from 16.6x forward toward its own history. Bull case roughly plus 78 percent, the full screen analyst upside, if WFE reaches Bernstein's USD 217bn in 2027, supply constraints ease, and the market re-rates Lam back toward a growth-equipment multiple on that EPS step-up.
- **Top risk (value-trap case):** WFE is cyclical and customer-concentrated, and memory is about 46 percent of systems revenue, so if the 2027 WFE step-up above USD 200bn does not materialise because of data-centre delays, hyperscaler capex cuts, or China export controls (China supplied about 75 percent of Q1's sequential growth per Aardvark), Lam's operating leverage cuts both ways and the forward EPS step-up evaporates.
- **Conviction (1-10):** 8
