# Ballot C — Moat & AI-irreplaceability — stock-pick-dip, run 2026-11-20

Lens: category dominance, balance-sheet durability, and above all irreplaceability. A name is rejected if AI or a substitute technology can realistically disrupt or commoditise it, or if the dip reveals an eroding moat. I want a moat nobody — and no model — can copy.

## Single-pick ballot

- **Top pick:** LRCX
- **Runner-up:** KLAC
- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** Lam is the number-one etch supplier, with roughly 45–50% of dry etch, and its cryogenic etch is described as the only proven high-volume path for HBM4 TSVs — every node transition and every 3D NAND layer adds etch content, so the AI build-out raises Lam's content per wafer rather than threatening it. It fell 52.9% from its 52-week high and 28.1% below its 200-day moving average after reporting its best quarter on record, with no company-specific setback — a pure sector de-rating on AI-capex politics. Consensus normalised EPS is expected to rise from USD 5.82 in FY2026 to USD 9.65 in FY2027, a roughly 66% step-up that management's own WFE guide (above USD 200bn in 2027) supports, and a USD 25bn buyback authorisation announced on 5 Nov puts a floor under the stock. With a net-cash balance sheet and record quarterly free cash flow of USD 3.24bn, Lam can fund both the buyback and growth while the market reprices a supply-constrained, not demand-constrained, business.
- **Why the dip is temporary (not permanent):** The single most compelling evidence is that Lam reported its best quarter on record on 21 Oct 2026 and then fell 16.4% on 5 Nov alone, with the dossier noting explicitly that "Lam did not announce a company-specific operational setback." Management said supply-chain constraints, not weak orders, held the Q1 FY27 guide down, and memory systems revenue hit a record, up 120% year over year. The drop is about the price of AI-capex fear, not the business.
- **Moat / AI-irreplaceability:** Low risk. AI cannot substitute for etch — every chip pattern must be physically etched — and there is no software or alternative-material shortcut around it. The moat is process know-how plus more than 8,000 patents, qualification lock-in (a tool qualified for a step on a node tends to stay the tool of record), and a large installed base with a growing services stream (USD 2.49bn in Q1 FY27, up 40% year over year). Dossier irreplaceability score: 9/10. The only real threat is share loss to TEL/AMEC/Naura at the margin, not obsolescence.
- **Rebound catalyst & timing:** Q2 FY27 earnings on 27 Jan 2027 is the proving print, with Q1 guidance of revenue around USD 8.6bn and EPS of USD 2.33; the WFE step-up to above USD 200bn in 2027 (Bernstein forecasts USD 217bn in 2027 and USD 279bn in 2028) is the demand backdrop. The USD 25bn buyback authorisation is already in place, and Nvidia's 17 Nov beat-and-raise (Q4 guide USD 130bn) confirms the demand side. Near-term trigger in weeks to a few months; the earnings print is the confirmation.
- **12-18 month return scenario:** Base case roughly +50% to +60% — a partial re-rating toward the screen's +77.8% analyst upside as the WFE step-up and the EPS jump to USD 9.65 in FY2027 materialise. Bull case roughly +80% to +100% — full recovery toward the analyst target if WFE reaches the USD 217bn Bernstein forecast and the buyback is executed aggressively at depressed prices.
- **Top risk (value-trap case):** WFE is cyclical and customer-concentrated; if the 2027 WFE step-up above USD 200bn does not materialise (data-centre delays, hyperscaler capex cuts, China export controls — China supplied roughly 75% of Q1's sequential growth), Lam's operating leverage cuts both ways. Memory exposure (roughly 46% of systems revenue) ties it to the same NAND/DRAM cycle as MU.

### Top 3 — the single most compelling temporary-dip + intact-moat data point

**1. LRCX.** The dip is pure sentiment: Lam fell 16.4% on 5 Nov, the day after no company event at all, having just reported its best quarter on record with memory systems revenue up 120% year over year and guidance held back only by its own component supply. The moat is the strongest kind this lens rewards — a physical process no model can replace, with cryogenic etch as the only proven high-volume path for HBM4 TSVs and qualification lock-in on every node. Base case roughly +50% to +60% over 12–18 months as the FY2027 EPS step-up to USD 9.65 lands; bull case roughly +80% to +100% if WFE hits USD 217bn in 2027 and the USD 25bn buyback is executed.

**2. KLAC.** The dossier scores KLA's irreplaceability 10/10 — the widest moat in the field: roughly 50–55% of semiconductor process control, more than 60% of brightfield and more than 50% of darkfield wafer inspection, more than 80% of mask inspection, an installed base above 20,000 systems, and 52 consecutive quarters of services growth. The dip came as profit grew (TTM net income USD 5.26bn versus USD 4.24bn a year earlier; revenue growth accelerating to +28.9% year over year in fiscal Q1 2027), so the de-rating is about multiples, not the franchise. Base case roughly +35% to +45% over 12–18 months as WFE above USD 200bn in 2027 and the margin recovery to 63–64% in mid-to-late 2027 get priced in; bull case roughly +55% to +70%, in line with the screen's +55.2% analyst upside, if advanced-packaging process-control revenue reaches USD 1.1bn in calendar 2026 as guided. The honest caveat: KLAC is the least cheap name in the field (screen forward P/E 19.9, the lowest analyst upside in the equipment batch), so this is a buy-the-moat pick, not a bargain pick, and the rebound is a grind rather than a snap-back.

**3. AMAT.** Applied reported a record Q4 FY2026 after the worst of the sell-off — revenue USD 10.41bn, up 53% year over year, non-GAAP EPS USD 4.16, up 92% — and still fell, with at least 10 firms cutting price targets while keeping Buy-equivalent ratings: a textbook pure de-rating, with the multiple falling to 19.5x FY27 EPS from 26.8x in August while the FY27 EPS estimate rose 5.6% to USD 19.00. Its moat is breadth: deposition, etch, CMP, ion implant and metrology, number-two overall equipment vendor behind ASML, with AI raising its content per wafer (record DRAM revenue including HBM packaging, up 52% year over year). Base case roughly +45% to +55% over 12–18 months as Q1 FY27 guidance (USD 11.1bn revenue, USD 4.35 EPS) proves the record quarter was not a one-off; bull case roughly +65% to +85%, toward the screen's +65.8% analyst upside and Bernstein's USD 600 target, if 2027 is another record WFE year.

### Deliberately left out of the top 10

Left out: MU, FIX, INTU, HOOD, ADBE. MU is a commodity oligopoly where Micron is number three in DRAM (24% share) and number three in HBM (18%, down from 21%), with a genuine cycle-peak warning — peak 86% gross margins, a forecast NAND glut in 2027 and a class action — so the "3.3x forward P/E" is peak-earnings optics, a classic value-trap shape. FIX has an execution-scale moat only (irreplaceability 6/10), with 59% of revenue from technology customers and a backlog that is lumpy and delayable if the data-centre build-out pauses. INTU is the first company-admitted AI casualty in this field — Credit Karma's guidance was cut because consumers now use LLMs to find financial products — and TurboTax is exactly the bounded, rules-based document an agent plus bank data can assemble (irreplaceability 6/10). HOOD is a low-switching-cost, procyclical brokerage (irreplaceability 5/10) whose rebound needs a crypto-volume turn, with a margin book up 59% year over year amplifying any downturn. ADBE is the clearest value-trap suspect in the field: the dossier verdict is that the drawdown is mostly a structural repricing, not a cyclical one — generative tools collapse the skill barrier that locked users into Photoshop/Illustrator, AI-first ARR is only about 2.4% of total ARR, and three downgrades in five weeks plus a CEO transition away from the creative business mean the 8.1x forward P/E may be paying for a melting franchise, not a durable one.
