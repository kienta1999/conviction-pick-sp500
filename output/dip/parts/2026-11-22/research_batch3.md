Power-generation sales +30% to $3.4bn in Q3, sales to end users +75%.
- **Verdict: TEMPORARY.** A guidance-expectations gap plus tariff costs and the AI-power sentiment unwind, against record backlog and record sales. No lost customers, no substitute product, no secular demand decline is in evidence. ## Earnings-quality flag — GS LOW_CASH_CONVERSION

**One-line verdict: benign business-model artifact, not a value-trap signature — for a bank/broker-dealer, operating cash flow is not comparable to net income.**

Why: a bank's operating cash flow is dominated by balance-sheet flows that have nothing to do with earnings quality — changes in trading inventory, reverse repos, deposits, loans held for sale, and collateral move operating cash flow by tens of billions in a quarter, in either direction, regardless of profitability. GS grew its loan book 6% QoQ to $276bn and its balance sheet to $2.18tn in Q3 2026; lending growth alone consumes "operating" cash while creating the interest income that *is* the earnings. The screen's cash-conversion ratio (OCF/net income) is designed for industrial companies, where OCF should track earnings; applied to GS it flags the plumbing, not the profits. The right earnings-quality checks for GS are the ones that passed: 8 straight EPS beats averaging a ~21.6% surprise (investwithattention, 17 Nov 2026), ROE 18.7–20.7%, CET1 stable, and cash actually returned ($4.49bn in Q3). No evidence earnings are running ahead of cash in the value-trap sense.

### 5. Margin of safety / valuation
- Forward P/E ~7.9x (screen) / 8.9x FY2026 consensus (Arincen) / 9.4x NTM (Tikr, Nov 2026) vs 5-yr average ~23x and 10-yr average ~30x (peak 51–52x in 2020–21); 5-yr low 7.5x, hit June 2026. TTM P/E ~12.1x vs 5-yr median 33.7x. PEG ~0.5–0.91. This is the cheapest Adobe has been in a decade on every multiple — *if* earnings hold.
- Analyst mean target: ~$261–267 (Arincen $260.61, +19.6%; RockFlow $266.91, +22.6%, 39 analysts, range $140–$373) — but the distribution is the story: 4–9 Buy, 17–23 Hold, 6–7 Sell; the five October/November targets run $200 (Goldman Sell) to $267 (HSBC Hold). Citi's $207 target requires either the multiple staying at its 10-yr low (7.5x FY2027 EPS) or FY2027 EPS estimates falling ~17% to ~$22.85 — below FY2026's $24.48 (Tikr). The market is pricing ~$8 of FY2027 EPS at a 30x multiple, i.e. assuming two-thirds of expected earnings evaporate.
- Genuinely cheap *because of the dip* — the cheapest in a decade — but the cheapness is the market's verdict on the disruption question, not a misprint. The margin of safety exists only if FY2027 EPS stays near $27.

### 6. Category position
- Still #1 in creative software by revenue, users, and enterprise footprint — no competitor has taken the professional core. But the dip *is* a sign of share pressure at the margin: SMB softness, web-based and AI-native tools taking the casual/prosumer tier, and Adobe itself shifting to MAU growth (lower ASP products) to defend share. Pricing power — historically the moat — is explicitly being traded away for users. That is the most important sentence in this dossier.

### 7. Value-trap risk
- The trap: generative AI is a genuine substitute for a growing share of creative work, Adobe's response (hosting rivals' models, opening its apps to third-party agents) accelerates the commoditisation of its own interface, inference costs compress its ~89% gross margins, and the FY2027 guide on Dec 9 confirms estimates must fall 15–20%. Then 8x forward earnings becomes 8x *declining* earnings, the buyback (88% of OCF) burns cash defending a melting multiple, and the stock does what it has done for three straight years — falls another 20%+. The three consecutive >20% annual declines are the market's way of saying the burden of proof is now on Adobe.

### 8. Rebound score: 6/10
**Verdict:** The cheapest high-quality software franchise in a decade on every multiple, with revenue still growing 13% — but the rebound is a bet that the Dec 9 FY2027 guide proves the AI threat is pricing-power erosion, not subscription extinction.
