# Ballot B1 — Growth/Quality Compounder lens — run date 2026-11-22

## 1. Single-pick ballot

- **Top pick:** FIX
- **Runner-up:** GOOGL
- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** FIX is the rare name in this batch where the dip is a pure de-rating on improving fundamentals: Q3 revenue rose 53.7% YoY to $3.77B with EPS $13.91 beating consensus, backlog hit a record $17.81B (+90% YoY, ~18–24 months of work), and FY2027 consensus EPS estimates were revised *up* (to $65.30 from $58.85) while the stock fell 49% — forward P/E is now ~15.5–17x versus a 5-year median of ~23.6x. It is a net-cash business (net cash ~$1.78B) with ROE of 58.6% and Q3 operating cash flow of $1.01B against $489M net income, so cash runs ahead of earnings. The work is physical, labour-constrained installation for data centers and mission-critical buildings — demand exceeds FIX's ability to staff it, the opposite of a demand problem. As the backlog converts through 2027, margin normalisation plus a multiple back toward its own history is the explosive re-rating path.
- **Why the dip is temporary (not permanent):** The single most compelling evidence is that FIX sold off ~9% the day after a blowout Q3 — record revenue, record backlog, 8th straight EPS beat — because management issued no forward guidance and flagged margin mix, not because any customer cancelled or any project was delayed (management confirmed no FIX project has been delayed by a data-center moratorium). The backlog nearly doubled in a year while technology customers rose to ~59–61% of revenue; demand is demonstrably intact, only the multiple compressed.
- **Moat / AI-irreplaceability:** Low risk. AI cannot pour concrete, bend pipe, or commission a cooling system — data centers need physical HVAC/mechanical installation more than ever as rack densities rise. FIX's moat is scale (211 US locations, 22,700 employees), reputation and certification in mission-critical builds, and a labour force competitors cannot replicate quickly. The risk is demand cyclicality, not substitution.
- **Rebound catalyst & timing:** H1 2027 margin normalisation as early-stage jobs mature (management's own expectation), confirmed by the Q4 2026 earnings print (expected late Jan/Feb 2027) showing gross margin stabilising near ~25% and bookings continuing. JPMorgan (Overweight, $1,700, Nov 10) and Morgan Stanley (Overweight, $1,410, Nov 17) both initiated coverage into the selloff — the Street is buying the backlog story at lower prices.
- **12-18 month return scenario:** Base case ~+35% to ~$1,410 (Morgan Stanley's target, the lowest on the Street) as margins stabilise and the multiple partially normalises on FY2027 EPS of ~$65. Bull case ~+63% to ~$1,700 (JPMorgan's target) if backlog conversion accelerates and the multiple returns toward its 5-year median. The stale consensus mean (~$1,772–1,947, +70–87%) is not relied on.
- **Top risk (value-trap case):** The AI-capex cycle turns: hyperscaler data-center spending slows or financing tightens in 2027–28, the $17.81B backlog converts slower, new bookings dry up, and a labour-heavy contractor sees margins compress fast. If 26.3% gross margin (Q1) was the cycle peak and 24.8% is the new normal or worse, FY2027 EPS estimates are too high and the "cheap" 16x forward P/E is an illusion. Backlog is a promise that can be delayed, not revenue.
- **Conviction (1-10):** 8

### Top 3 detail

**1. FIX:** The single most compelling data point is the combination of a record $17.81B backlog (+90% YoY, with $3.8B of new bookings in one quarter) against a forward P/E of ~15.5–17x — below its 5-year median of ~23.6x — while FY2027 EPS estimates were revised up to $65.30 from $58.85 into the selloff; that is a de-rate on improving fundamentals, not a broken growth story, and the moat is the most AI-irreplaceable in the batch (physical trades AI cannot do). Base case ~+35% to ~$1,410 over 12–18 months as H1 2027 margins normalise; bull case ~+63% to ~$1,700 if backlog conversion accelerates and the multiple returns toward history.

**2. GOOGL:** The single most compelling data point is Google Cloud growing +103% YoY to $30.7B with a $561bn backlog, while the stock trades at the very bottom of its 5-year P/E range (trailing ~13.6–14.7x vs a ~23.7–23.9x median) with ~$130bn+ net cash — the drawdown is a capex-financing shock, not a franchise break, and search still holds ~90% share with revenue +25% in Q3. Base case ~+30–50% over 12–18 months toward the ~$403 mean target as Cloud backlog converts and FCF inflects; bull case higher if the 2027 capex plan comes with a credible FCF trough date and buybacks resume. (Note: the FY2026 forward P/E is flattered by one-off investment gains; the cleaner 2027 EPS implies ~18.8x.)

**3. APP:** The single most compelling data point is Q3 2026 — revenue +55% YoY to $2.185B, net income +72% with a 66% net margin, adjusted EBITDA margin of 84%, driven by the next-gen AXON model lifting net revenue per install +55% — the strongest operating momentum in the batch, with AI as AppLovin's weapon rather than its killer, plus e-commerce ad budgets gaining share fastest of any surveyed platform. Base case ~+30% over 12–18 months toward the ~$454–456 mean target as AXON rollout and e-commerce scale; bull case ~+60–80% if the SEC probe closes or the class action is dismissed and the regulatory discount lifts. The open SEC probe and Sep 2026 class action are why it is #3, not #1, for this lens.

**Left out of my top 10:** HOOD — revenue growth slowed to 12% with crypto revenue −55% YoY and weak switching costs, a cyclical brokerage not a compounder; CSCO — ~17x forward earnings is above its 5-year average of 15.7x with FY28 growth already modelled at ~9%, de-rated but not cheap; DECK — HOKA growth has decelerated 55% → 7% with share loss below $120 and a receivables build (+23% YoY vs +3.5% sales) that is a genuine yellow flag, a brand-cycle question not a temporary dip; NEM — a gold-price call, not a compounding franchise: it is a price-taker whose "cheap" 9.5x P/E depends entirely on gold holding near $4,000, and at $3,500 gold the earnings underpinning the multiple shrink materially. Of these, ADBE-adjacent risk aside, the clearest value-trap suspects are NEM (peak-cycle commodity earnings) and DECK (brand-cycle deceleration masked by a low multiple).
