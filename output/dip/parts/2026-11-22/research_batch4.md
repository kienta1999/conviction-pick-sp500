## BKNG — Booking Holdings

**Snapshot (Nov 20 close):** $144.91; 52-week range $144.10–$220.81 (Finnhub); −34% off the $220.81 high (TIKR cites a $233.58 high, max drawdown −33.75% on May 15, 2026). Market cap ~$108–115B. Forward P/E ~12.0x (MarketBeat, Nov 21) / 13.0x (RockFlow); brief screen figure ~11.8x. Trailing P/E ~15.6x. YTD −25% to −32% depending on source date.

### 1. Why it's down — temporary vs permanent
- **Apr 28, 2026 (Q1):** beat everything — revenue $5.53B +16% YoY, adj. EPS $1.14 vs $1.08 consensus — then **cut FY26 revenue growth guidance from low-double-digits to high-single-digits** and guided Q2 room-night growth of only 2–4%. Stock fell 5.57% on May 11 after a further outlook revision and a €1.9B senior-notes issue. JPMorgan cut its target $224 → $208 (kept Overweight).
- **Cause #1 — Middle East conflict (transitory, but open-ended):** CFO Ewout Steenbergen (Q1 call) said the conflict clipped room-night growth by ~2pp in Q1 (underlying ~8%, above the high end of guidance) and ~3pp in Q2; ~7% of 2025 global room nights touch the Middle East as destination or transit hub. Full-year plan assumed impact through June, then H2 recovery — which did not fully materialise.
- **Cause #2 — Q3 (Nov 3, 2026) one-off charges + soft Q4 guide:** reported revenue $8.89B, −1.3% YoY, but that includes a **$900m revenue reduction for an FTC matter at Priceline** plus a $100m accrued loss in G&A (~$1.0B pre-tax total). Adjusted revenue was $9.8B, +9–10%; room nights 345m, +6.7% (accelerating from +5.3% in Q2); gross bookings $54.5B, +9.6%; adj. EPS $4.66, +17% YoY, beating $4.49 consensus. But Q4 guidance was soft: room nights +1–3%, adjusted revenue +0–2%, adjusted EBITDA ~flat, citing Middle East disruption, tough comps and higher airfares. FY26 guide held at high-single-digit growth, adj. EPS low-to-mid-teens.
- **Cause #3 — AI disintermediation fear (the structural question):** Sep 22, 2026 Meta announced its Muse AI assistant partnered with **Expedia** to let users search/plan/book inside the AI platform; BKNG fell 5.1% on Sep 23 to $155.90. Fear: AI agents capture the customer at the point of intent and OTAs become commoditised backends, eroding commissions. Compounded Sep 9, 2026: EU General Court upheld the veto of Booking's €1.63B eTraveli acquisition, closing a flight-ecosystem hedge against exactly this disintermediation.
- **Cause #4 — regulation:** Italy's AGCM opened a formal investigation Apr 22, 2026 into whether Booking.com's Preferred Partner programmes prioritise hotels paying higher commissions.
- **Verdict: MOSTLY TEMPORARY, with one genuine structural watch-item.** The demand shock is geopolitical + cyclical (travel demand softening, airfares) and the Q3 revenue decline is an FTC one-off, not an operating collapse — room nights and bookings are still growing mid-to-high single digits, US room nights grew low-teens in Q1 for a fourth straight quarter of acceleration. The AI-disintermediation threat is real but unproven: no evidence yet of share loss to AI booking; it is a multiple-compression story, not a broken business — yet.

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 7/10
Moat: **two-sided network effects + scale in supply** — Booking.com is the largest OTA globally with the deepest lodging inventory (alternative accommodations ~37% of room nights, +7% in Q3), a direct-booking mix and brand that give it the lowest customer-acquisition cost in travel, plus 47% adjusted-EBITDA margins (Q3) that fund a $9.5B 9M buyback. Switching costs for hotels are real (inventory, reviews, ranking, Genius loyalty). AI threat: an LLM can surface a hotel, but fulfilment — inventory depth, cancellation terms, payments, customer service, fraud — still runs through the OTA or the hotel; Booking is itself embedding AI (Connected Trip, AI distribution) and CEO Glenn Fogel argued at the JPMorgan conference (May 20, 2026) that AI is a competitive advantage, not a threat. The Meta–Expedia deal shows the risk is that the *front door* moves, not that the inventory disappears. Score 7: durable franchise, but the discovery layer is genuinely contestable.

### 3. Rebound catalyst
- **Geopolitical de-escalation** in the Middle East restores the ~2–3pp of room-night growth management says it is costing; Q3 already showed acceleration (+6.7% room nights) despite it.
- **Q4 2026 earnings (~Feb 16, 2027 est.)** — a return to positive reported revenue growth (ex-FTC) and room nights holding ≥5% would re-rate; the $650m annual run-rate transformation savings land in 2027.
- **Buyback engine:** $9.5B repurchased in 9M 2026 ($2.1B in Q3 alone), $12.4B authorisation remaining at Sep 30 — at this price the buyback retires ~8–9% of the float a year. Timing: sentiment reset over 1–2 quarters; full re-rate needs a clean quarter without a new guidance cut.

### 4. Balance-sheet survival
Strong but **leveraged by buyback choice, not distress**: cash and investments $16.2B at Sep 30, 2026 (down from $17.7B at Jun 30); debt ~$20.1B after €1.9B May 2026 notes (long-term debt $18.07B vs $16.86B at end-2025); net debt ~$3.9B. TTM free cash flow $9.24B; Q3 FCF only $1.07B (−22% YoY) on a seasonal $3.6B unwind of deferred merchant bookings — and buybacks ($2.1B in Q3) ran at ~2x quarterly FCF, drawing cash down $1.5B in one quarter. TTM cash returned $13.3B vs $9.24B FCF — the buyback pace exceeds FCF and is funded from the balance sheet. No distress risk at these cash levels, but the pace is unsustainable if FCF stays depressed; no dilution risk.

### 5. Margin of safety / valuation
Genuinely cheap *for Booking*: forward P/E ~12.0x (MarketBeat) vs 15.8x NTM P/E cited in May 2026 as already a multi-year low, and vs its own history of ~20x+; 12.08x NTM EV/EBITDA was its lowest in at least two years (TIKR, May). FCF yield ~5.3% (RockFlow). Peers: Expedia forward P/E ~10.9x — Booking's premium has nearly collapsed despite structurally higher margins (25.5% net margin vs Expedia's 13.0%) and a stronger direct mix. Analyst consensus: 36 analysts, ~30 Buy / 7 Hold / **0 Sell**; mean target $215.06 (RockFlow) to $221.90 (MarketBeat), implying **+48% to +53%** upside; range $150–$267 (low target ≈ current price). Caveat: the Nov 4 tape was a wall of *reaffirmations*, not fresh upgrades, and Bernstein cut its target to $172 on Nov 20 — targets look stale vs the demand shock; consensus 2027 EPS $19.92 could be cut if Q4 disappoints. Verdict: cheap on history and vs peers, but the "cheap" rests on estimates that have already been cut twice.

### 6. Category position
Still **#1 OTA globally** with pricing power intact — it takes commission and raises take-rate via merchant model shift; no evidence of share loss to Expedia (Expedia is down similarly, −27% 1-yr) or to hotels' direct channels. The dip is a demand + sentiment story, not share loss.

### 7. Value-trap risk
The bear case: (a) Middle East conflict extends through H2 2026 into 2027 → another guidance cut, and the "temporary" label expires; (b) AI agents (Meta/Expedia, ChatGPT, Google) actually capture booking intent and compress OTA take rates structurally — then 12x earnings is not cheap, it's a melting moat; (c) EU/Italian regulatory action caps commission or ranking practices; (d) buybacks funded above FCF erode the balance-sheet cushion if travel demand turns down cyclically. If Q4 revenue declines again and 2027 EPS estimates start falling, this is a falling knife.

### 8. Rebound score: 7/10
**Verdict:** The best risk/reward of the three — a still-growing #1 franchise priced as if the geopolitical shock were permanent, with a buyback that pays you to wait; the AI threat is the one thing that could make it a trap, so it needs a clean Q4, not blind faith.

## CSCO — Cisco Systems

**Snapshot (Nov 20 close):** $90.64; 52-week range $72.80–$130.37 (intraday high early Jun 2026); −30% off the high; market cap ~$357–363B. Forward P/E ~16.6–17.2x (TIKR/Trefis; brief screen figure ~15.0x). YTD +17.7% (the drawdown is from a June blow-off top, not a down year). Next earnings Feb 10, 2027.

### 1. Why it's down — temporary vs permanent
- **The run-up, then the de-rate:** Cisco broke above its dot-com-era record in May–Jun 2026, peaking ~$130.37 on Jun 4, on a $9B hyperscaler AI-infrastructure order ramp, the Silicon One G300 custom ASIC, and the $28B Splunk acquisition repositioning it as a top cybersecurity vendor. FY26 revenue rose 12% to $63.3B; Q4 FY26 grew 18% with product orders +35%.
- **Margin compression (the core driver):** non-GAAP gross margin fell 2.5pp YoY to 65.6% in Q1 FY27 (product gross margin −3.5pp to 63.7%) on higher memory-component costs and a shift toward lower-margin AI networking hardware. Full-year guide implies H2 non-GAAP operating margin ~35% vs 37.8% in Q1.
- **Morgan Stanley downgrade (Nov 5, 2026)** to Equal Weight, target $135 → $120, warning early ordering ahead of price increases could weaken later demand; Cisco estimates price increases add ~5pp to FY27 revenue growth — i.e. part of the growth is price, not volume.
- **Nov 12, 2026 (Q1 FY27): beat-and-raise, stock fell anyway:** record revenue $18.5B, +24% YoY; non-GAAP EPS $1.40, +40% YoY, vs $1.32–1.33 consensus; GAAP EPS $1.14, +58%. FY27 guidance raised: revenue $74.8–75.8B (from $72.2–73.4B), non-GAAP EPS $5.47–5.53 (from $5.05–5.11); hyperscaler AI revenue raised to $9B (from $7.5B), >2x FY26's ~$4B. Stock fell 6.24% on Nov 13 to $95.64 (−$25B market value vs ~$1.7B of implied annual profit from the raise) and kept sliding to $90.64 by Nov 20. Why: Q2 guidance ($18.0–18.2B revenue, $1.34–1.36 EPS) is sequentially *down* from Q1; to hit the FY midpoint, H2 must average ~$19.3B/quarter; and the whole tape was hit by the AI-unwind (Nvidia crisis, Nov 20 correction week). Total product orders +21% YoY.
- **Verdict: MOSTLY TEMPORARY — a valuation de-rate of a cyclical price/mix story, not a broken franchise.** But the "temporary" label has a real expiry: if FY27 growth is mostly price increases + a one-off campus refresh + memory-inflated hardware, FY28 growth collapses to the ~9% analysts already model (EPS $6.01 in FY28 vs $5.50 FY27) and the stock is a 17x multiple on a 4–5% grower — fairly priced, not cheap.

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 6/10
Moat: **installed-base switching costs + certification lock-in** — Cisco is the largest enterprise networking vendor; campus/branch networks, IOS software, security (Splunk) and services create a sticky annuity (recurring revenue ~49–54% of revenue, though the share fell as AI hardware surged). Silicon One ASICs + Acacia optics give it a real position in AI scale-across networking. **But the AI data-centre core is where its moat is weakest:** Nvidia Spectrum-X and Arista lead AI data-centre Ethernet; Broadcom merchant silicon powers white-box/SONiC alternatives that let hyperscalers bypass Cisco's integrated model; HPE–Juniper is a strengthened rival. Hyperscaler AI orders are concentrated in a handful of buyers who dual-source aggressively and design for white-box economics. AI does not *replace* networking — it increases demand for it — but it shifts value toward merchant silicon and optics, where Cisco is a participant, not the gatekeeper. Score 6: irreplaceable in the enterprise campus, contestable in the AI fabric that is driving the growth.

### 3. Rebound catalyst
- **Q2 FY27 print (Feb 10, 2027):** the first test of whether orders hold once price increases annualise; product orders +21% and AI orders need to stay strong. A Q2 beat with H2 visibility would re-rate.
- **Memory-cost normalisation** would restore gross margin toward 68% and is the single biggest margin lever; timing uncertain (memory cycle).
- **AI infrastructure revenue converting:** $9B hyperscaler AI revenue in FY27 (12% of sales, from 6% in FY26) plus campus refresh cycle (Wi-Fi 7, Silicon One design wins). If AI capex proves durable rather than a 2026 surge, the growth story extends into FY28.
- **Capital return:** ~$6.1B FY26 buybacks, $23B over 3 years, plus dividend (~1.4–2% yield); but ~$3.8B/yr stock comp means buybacks barely move the diluted share count (−0.3% in FY26).

### 4. Balance-sheet survival
Comfortable, not fortress: debt ~$29.8B (largely Splunk-deal debt), cash ~$15.8B, **net debt ~$14B** (up from $12.0B at FY25 year-end as dividends/buybacks were sustained rather than deleveraging). LTM operating cash flow ~$15B, FCF ~$14B, FCF/revenue ~21% — ample to service debt and fund the dividend; no distress or dilution risk. Undrawn $5B credit line. The issue is capital allocation (buybacks offset by stock comp), not survival.

### 5. Margin of safety / valuation
**Not genuinely cheap — de-rated, not cheap.** Forward P/E 17.2x is down from 27.7x at the June peak but still ~10% above its 5-year average of 15.7x (TIKR); Trefis shows FY27 P/E 16.6x vs FY23 15.2x. Consensus mean target ~$126–133 (MarketBeat $126.17, +39% upside; Sparkasse avg $132.82 as of Nov 16; Tickeron avg ~$137), range ~$110–$170; Rosenblatt reiterated Buy $140 on Nov 20, Citi cut $139 → $120 on Nov 13, DZ Bank upgraded Hold → Buy $125 on Nov 17. Independent value work is much cooler: Deepval (Nov 11) fair value $86, DCF ~$65, bull ~$95 — i.e. at $90.64 the stock is roughly fairly priced, with base-case returns ~5%/yr. EPS growth is modelled to slow from ~27% in FY27 to ~9% in FY28. Verdict: the dip removed the bubble premium, but you are paying a full price for a business whose growth may normalise to high-single-digits — the margin of safety is thin.

### 6. Category position
Still **#1 in enterprise switching/routing** and the largest networking vendor overall, with pricing power demonstrated by the ~5pp price contribution to FY27 growth. But in the *growth* segment — AI data-centre Ethernet — Arista and Nvidia lead; Cisco is #2/#3. The dip partly reflects that share reality, not just sentiment. Security (Splunk) revenue was flat YoY in Q3 FY26 — the cross-sell thesis is unproven.

### 7. Value-trap risk
The bear case: (a) FY27 is the peak of a price + refresh + memory cycle — hyperscaler digestion in FY28, pricing reversal, and purchase-commitment charges (Deepval bear case ~$40); (b) webscale buyers standardise on merchant silicon/white-box and Cisco's AI hardware margin compresses further; (c) the Splunk $28B acquisition never produces ARR acceleration; (d) ~$3.8B/yr stock comp means shareholders capture far less of the FCF than the headline suggests. If revenue growth prints below trigger for two consecutive quarters, the growth re-rating thesis fails and the stock grinds toward a 13–14x multiple (~$70s).

### 8. Rebound score: 5/10
**Verdict:** A great quarter punished for being priced for perfection — the business is genuinely strong, but at ~17x forward earnings with FY28 growth already modelled at ~9% and the AI-fabric moat unproven, the dip is a fair price, not a bargain; rebound needs margin repair and proof the AI orders are durable.

## DECK — Deckers Outdoor (Deckers Brands)

**Snapshot (Nov 20 close):** $77.94; 52-week range $77.20–$122.29 (high Feb 20, 2026); −36% off the high; market cap ~$10.5–11.2B. Forward P/E ~10.6x (RockFlow); brief screen figure ~9.5x. Trailing P/E ~10.5x. YTD −24.8%. Next earnings Q3 FY27 ~Jan 27, 2027 (the UGG peak quarter).

### 1. Why it's down — temporary vs permanent
- **The long deceleration:** HOKA growth fell from 55% (FY22) → 28% (FY23/FY24) → 24% (FY25) → 15.9% (FY26, $2.59B) → 7.3% in H1 FY27. The Q4 FY25 print (HOKA wholesale +10%) caused the worst single-day drop since 2018 (−19%); Deckers declined to guide FY26, citing uncertainty.
- **Jul–Sep 2026 slide:** DECK lost ~30% between late July and mid-September, setting a 52-week low of $77.20 on Sep 30.
- **Oct 22, 2026 (Q2 FY27): the guidance cut.** Revenue $1.481B, +3.5% YoY, a record but below the $1.51B consensus and below management's own ~5% guide; EPS $2.31 beat ($2.03 ex-tariff-refund, +11.5% YoY). Full-year sales cut to $5.700–5.725B from $5.86–5.91B; HOKA growth cut to high-single-digits (from low-double-digits), UGG to low-single-digits (from mid-single-digits). Stock fell 5% on Oct 23 to $81.01. EPS guidance was *raised* to $7.80–7.95 from $7.35–7.50 — but ~$0.52 of that is IEEPA tariff refunds (Supreme Court struck down IEEPA tariffs Feb 2026; Deckers had paid ~$120m); ex-refund EPS is ~$7.28–7.43, slightly *below* the prior outlook.
- **Demand, not just tariffs:** comparable DTC sales fell 6.4% in Q2 (wholesale +5.5%, DTC −1.8%); HOKA DTC growth slowed to ~4% from 17% in Q1; UGG DTC fell ~8%. Management blamed softer US traffic/conversion, shoppers waiting for promotions, and heavier American discounting. Several calendar-2026 HOKA franchise updates (Transport, Kawana, Solimar) failed to resonate, and HOKA is **losing share below the $120 price point**. UGG's biggest weeks run late Nov–Dec, so the December quarter is the test.
- **Competitive context:** On Running is pulling away — On grew 43% in Q1 2026 (727m CHF) and raised FY guidance to +28%, vs HOKA's +10% in Q4 FY25 and +6.8% in Q2 FY27. Nike is weak (revenue still falling through FY28 per its Nov 17 Investor Day) which should help HOKA/On take shelf space, but Dick's noted the athletic-footwear market is "increasingly promotional."
- **Verdict: MIXED — cyclical deceleration with a genuine brand-cycle question.** The tariff noise and promotional US consumer are transitory; but HOKA's growth curve flattening from 55% → 7% and losing sub-$120 share to On/Nike is a *brand-cycle* risk that can last 2–3 years. This is the least clearly "temporary" of the three.

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 8/10
Moat: **brand + product design**, not technology. HOKA's maximalist-cushion identity and UGG's sheepskin fashion staple are brand franchises with pricing power (FY25 gross margin 57.9%, Q2 FY27 gross margin ~60.5% — up from 56.2%); DTC is ~41% of sales with 203–223 owned stores. AI cannot design a better-feeling shoe or replicate brand heat; there is no AI substitution threat to footwear. The threat is **fashion/brand rotation** — the oldest risk in footwear — and competition from On, Nike's running turnaround, and niche labels (Norda, NNormal). Score 8 for AI-irreplaceability; the moat is brand durability, which is real but faddish.

### 3. Rebound catalyst
- **Q3 FY27 (Dec quarter, ~Jan 27, 2027):** management wants it to be UGG's largest quarter ever; guides EPS $3.52–3.60 (incl. ~$0.24 refunds). A strong holiday sell-through with clean inventory would reset the demand narrative fast.
- **2027 HOKA pipeline:** Bondi 10 (Jan 2027), Mach 8, $115 FlyPace (attacking the sub-$120 segment where share is being lost), $325 Bondi Ultra X; strong Mach Pro bookings; external review of marketing deployment commissioned.
- **International:** FY26 international +26.8% (41.7% of sales); Q2 FY27 international +9.0% vs US −0.4% — the growth engine is now ex-US.
- **Buyback:** $637.5m repurchased in H1 FY27 (6.5m shares at $98.77 avg — above today's price, a sunk-cost note), $4.4B authorisation remaining at Sep 30 — ~40% of market cap, a powerful lever if the board uses it at these levels. Long-term framework (May 2026): high-single-digit sales growth, low-20%+ operating margin, low-double-digit EPS growth with buybacks.

### 4. Balance-sheet survival
**Fortress — the strongest of the three:** zero debt (no notes payable, no long-term debt at any recent quarter-end), cash $1.21B at Sep 30, 2026 (down from $1.91B at Mar 31, 2026 after $637.5m of buybacks and seasonal inventory build to $861m). FY26 free cash flow >$1B (company definition). No distress or dilution risk whatsoever; the balance sheet can fund the buyback and survive a multi-year brand trough.

### 5. Margin of safety / valuation
Genuinely cheap *for Deckers*: forward P/E ~10.6x (RockFlow) vs its history as a 20x+ growth compounder; EV/EBITDA 9.28x; trailing P/E 10.5x. Gross margin actually *expanded* (60.46% vs 56.18% a year ago). Analyst consensus: ~29 analysts, mean target ~$95–97, implying **+22–24%** upside; range $70–$137; split 13 Buy / 12 Hold / 4 Sell (recommendation mean 2.57 = Hold) — genuinely undecided, unlike BKNG's 0 sells. Caveat: consensus forward EPS $10.49 and revenue $6.51B embed a recovery that Q2 guidance just contradicted (FY27 sales $5.71B); if estimates fall again, the "cheap" multiple is on inflated earnings. Verdict: cheap on history and vs peers, but the earnings stream underpinning the multiple is not yet proven durable — the cheapest of the three, and the one where the multiple most depends on a demand re-acceleration that hasn't arrived.

### 6. Category position
**HOKA is #2 challenger, not #1** — Nike remains the scale leader (though shrinking), On is now the faster-growing challenger and is widening the gap. HOKA is losing share below $120; UGG remains the category-defining sheepskin brand. The dip is partly share/brand-cycle, not pure sentiment — the honest read is that HOKA's hyper-growth phase is over and the market is repricing it from growth to value.

### 7. Value-trap risk
The bear case: (a) HOKA has hit its growth ceiling — the 55% → 7% curve is a mature-brand plateau, and the 2027 pipeline (Bondi 10, FlyPace) fails to re-accelerate; (b) UGG is a fashion cycle that can turn (it has before); (c) promotional US market forces discounting that erodes the 60% gross margin; (d) wholesale channel-stuffing (see receivables flag below) masks weakening sell-through, and the Dec quarter disappoints — then the stock re-rates to a mid-single-digit multiple on lower earnings and the "cheap" thesis evaporates. Two-brand concentration (HOKA 47% + UGG 50% of sales) means one brand stalling halves the growth story.

### 8. Rebound score: 6/10
**Verdict:** The cheapest and the best balance sheet, with a real holiday-quarter catalyst in ~2 months — but the dip is partly a genuine brand-cycle deceleration, not just sentiment, so the rebound needs proof (Q3 sell-through + FlyPace traction), not just a low multiple.

## Earnings-Quality Flag — DECK: RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN

**Facts (10-Q for quarter ended Sep 30, 2026, filed Oct 29, 2026; Zacks balance sheet):**
- Trade accounts receivable: **$675.2m at Sep 30, 2026 vs $547m at Sep 30, 2025 (+23% YoY) and $319.0m at Mar 31, 2026** — while Q2 FY27 sales grew only +3.5% YoY and H1 sales ~+4%. Receivables are growing ~6–7x faster than sales.
- Allowance for doubtful accounts nearly doubled: **$61.9m vs $38.2m at Mar 31, 2026**.
- Inventories also jumped: $860.8m vs $487.0m at Mar 31, 2026.
- Offsets: trade payables also rose sharply ($647.9m vs $384.5m at Mar 31), so suppliers are partly funding the build; and Q2 operating cash flow was weak — RockFlow notes quarterly FCF of −$79.1m.

**Benign explanation (plausible, not proven):** Deckers' wholesale shipments are seasonal — Q2 is the pre-ship quarter ahead of the Dec holiday peak (UGG's biggest weeks), so receivables and inventory *should* spike at Sep 30 and convert in Q3. Wholesale grew +5.5% in Q2 while DTC fell, consistent with a wholesale-heavy pre-ship. This is the normal working-capital cycle for a footwear company heading into its peak quarter.

**Why it is still a yellow flag:** the *magnitude* is off-trend — +23% YoY receivables vs +3.5% sales, with the allowance nearly doubling, is not explained by seasonality alone (Sep 2025 was also a pre-holiday quarter). It is consistent with looser wholesale terms / channel fill into a soft US market where DTC comps fell 6.4% and management cited heavy discounting. If Q3 sell-through is weak, receivables become markdowns or returns.

**One-line verdict:** Mostly seasonal wholesale pre-ship ahead of the UGG peak quarter, but receivables growing ~7x sales with a near-doubled bad-debt allowance is a genuine yellow flag — the Q3 FY27 print (~Jan 27, 2027) is the test: clean conversion clears it, a further build confirms the value-trap signature.
