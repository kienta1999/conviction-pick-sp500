### Top 3 — temporary-dip + intact-moat evidence and scenarios

**1. FIX — Comfort Systems USA.** The single most compelling data point is the combination of a record quarter and a net-cash balance sheet: revenue \$3.77B (+54%), EPS \$13.91 (+69%), backlog \$17.81B (+90%), with cash \$2.16B against debt of only \$49.6M — a business that cannot be killed by the sell-off that hit it, since the sell-off was a multiple event (from ~42x at the peak to 16.3x) on a sector rotation. The moat is physical craft labour and modular fabrication that AI cannot replicate, and the backlog is contracted demand converting at 65–75% within 12 months. Base case roughly +30–45% over 12–18 months on multiple normalization; bull case roughly +60% if hyperscaler capex holds and guidance is raised.

**2. CAT — Caterpillar.** The single most compelling data point is the record \$78.1B backlog alongside Q3 sales of \$20.68B (+17.3%) and adjusted EPS of \$7.20 (+45.5%) — the stock fell ~12.7% in two days not on a miss but because management *held* guidance instead of raising it, the largest earnings-day decline in 31 quarters, which is an expectations reset rather than a broken business. The moat is a century-deep dealer network, installed-base services, and engine/turbine depth, and AI data centres are a customer for CAT power generation, not a substitute for it. Base case roughly +25–40% over 12–18 months as the January 2027 outlook resets expectations upward; bull case roughly +50% (toward the \$807 mean target) if power-gen capacity ramps and guidance is raised — with the caveat that buybacks plus dividends (\$10.9B TTM) exceeded FCF, so capital return is not fully self-funded.

**3. APH — Amphenol.** The single most compelling data point is Q3 orders of \$12.17B (+99%, book-to-bill 1.19) with management *raising* guidance — revenue \$10.23B (+65%, +37% organic) — yet the stock fell on analyst target cuts after a vertical run, which is profit-taking and multiple compression, not a demand break. The moat is custom-engineering qualification lock-in in high-speed interconnect, and AI is the demand driver (IT Datacom +117% YoY). But as the skeptic I must flag the disputed valuation: the screen's 20.2x forward P/E is contested by contemporaneous coverage at ~35–41x on a different forward-EPS base, so the "cheap for Amphenol" claim is directionally right but the exact margin of safety is unverified — and ~75% of IT Datacom (48% of sales) is AI, so this is a concentration bet. Base case roughly +20–35% over 12–18 months if the growth rate persists; bull case roughly +45% if IT Datacom keeps compounding at triple digits and the CommScope debt is digested without margin damage.
