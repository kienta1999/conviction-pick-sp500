## FIX — Comfort Systems USA (price \$1,099.58, 2026-11-25 close)

### Claim 1 — Q3 2026 record quarter sold off ~17% on rotation, not a miss or guidance cut

**Verdict: CONFIRMED** (with a material caveat on the cause — see below).

- Revenue \$3.77B (+53.7% YoY), diluted EPS \$13.91 (+68.6% vs \$8.25), backlog \$17.81B (+90% YoY): confirmed verbatim in the company Q3 2026 release, 22 Oct 2026 (Business Wire / SEC Exhibit 99.1) and the Motley Fool call transcript of 23 Oct 2026.
- It was a beat, not a miss: EPS beat the Zacks consensus of \$12.71 by 9.4% and revenue beat the \$3.35B consensus by 12.5% (Zacks, 23 Oct 2026).
- Price move: close 21 Oct \$1,401.27 → 22 Oct \$1,279.37 (−8.7%) → 23 Oct \$1,160.86 (−9.26%); two-session fall −17.2% (Finnhub daily candles; corroborated by stockstobuynow.ai "price window −17.2%"). The ~17% figure checks out.
- No guidance cut: FIX issues no formal numeric guidance — the Q3 release contains no revenue or earnings guidance — so there was nothing to cut. That part of the claim holds, but it is trivially true.

Caveat — the cause is not sector rotation alone. Contemporaneous coverage (Motley Fool / Nasdaq, 23 Oct 2026; StockStory, 23 Oct 2026) attributes the selloff to a mix of (a) the announced retirement of co-founder and longtime CFO Bill George at year-end, (b) sell-the-news / profit-taking after a huge run, (c) no forward guidance and flat gross margin (24.8%, unchanged YoY), and (d) broader AI-trade nervousness. The dossier omits the CFO retirement entirely. "Sector rotation" is one ingredient, not the whole story; the CFO-retirement headline is a company-specific negative the panel should carry.

### Claim 2 — 65–75% of the \$17.81B backlog converts within 12 months; Q4 2026 report expected 17–18 Feb 2027

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**

- The 65–75% figure is the company's own disclosure: the Q3 2026 10-Q states it expects to recognise revenue on approximately 65–75% of remaining performance obligations over the next 12 months (quoted in Zacks, 23 Oct 2026; Investomine Q3 analysis; AInvest). That is \$11.6–13.4B of the \$17.81B backlog.
- Q4 2026 report: Trefis lists the next FIX earnings release as 18 Feb 2027 (EPS \$14.02E, revenue \$3.0B); the screen's next-earnings date is 2027-02-18. The dossier's "17–18 Feb 2027" range is consistent with that estimate. Note it is a vendor estimate, not yet a company-confirmed date.

### Claim 3 — Forward P/E ~16.3x vs ~42x at the May 2026 peak (52-week high \$2,073.99 on 14 May 2026)

**Verdict: CONFIRMED** (with a base-mixing caveat — see below).

- 52-week high \$2,073.99 on 14 May 2026: confirmed by Finnhub (intraday high; the 52-week *closing* high was \$2,066.51 on 22 Jun 2026 per Trefis — both are true, different measures).
- Current ~16.3x: Trefis shows FY2027 P/E 16.5x at a \$1,111.32 price with FY2027 consensus EPS \$67.08; at the dossier price \$1,099.58 that is 16.4x. HoldingsHub shows FY2027 forward P/E 16.6x. The screen's 16.3x is the FY2027E-based figure — confirmed.
- Peak ~42x: arithmetic checks out on a forward basis. Peak price \$2,073.99 divided by FY2026 consensus EPS of ~\$49 gives ~42x (Zacks Oct 2026 consensus for 2026 was \$47.65, implying ~43.5x at the peak). Runchey Research (25 Apr 2026) independently put FIX's forward P/E at 32–35x on FY2026E with trailing P/E ~44x around that period.

Caveat — the two figures use different forward years (16.3x is on FY2027E, ~42x is on FY2026E). On a like-for-like basis the compression is smaller: FY2026E P/E is ~21.6x now (\$1,099.58 / \$50.99 Trefis FY2026 EPS) vs ~42–43x at the peak; FY2027E P/E would have been ~35x at the peak (\$2,073.99 / \$59.31). The direction (a halved multiple) is real, but the 16.3x-vs-42x pairing overstates it by mixing years. Zacks' forward-12-month P/E is higher than the screen figure (17.9x on 13 Nov 2026; 17.17x on 18 Nov 2026).

## APH — Amphenol (price \$73.08)

### Claim 5 — Q3 2026 record, guidance raised, yet the stock fell ~\$3.90 that Friday on analyst target cuts / profit-taking after a vertical run

**Verdict: CONTRADICTED** (the price and earnings facts check out; the attributed cause does not).

What checks out:

- Record Q3: sales \$10.23B (+65% YoY, +37% organic), orders \$12.17B (+99%, book-to-bill 1.19:1) — confirmed in the company release, 28 Oct 2026 (investors.amphenol.com) and the call transcript.
- Guidance raised: confirmed — the company raised its 2026 outlook to sales of \$36.9–37.1B and adjusted EPS of \$2.82–2.84 (MostlyMarkets, 29 Oct 2026; MarketBeat guidance alert, 30 Oct 2026).
- The Friday fall: APH closed Thu 29 Oct at \$82.08, traded down to an intraday low of \$78.18 on Fri 30 Oct — exactly −\$3.90 intraday (MarketBeat, 30 Oct 2026) — and closed at \$79.30, −\$2.78 (−3.39%). The "\~\$3.90" figure is the intraday drop, not the close-to-close move.

What contradicts the claim:

- The Redburn "cut" (\$197 → \$107, 30 Oct) is mostly a stock-split restatement, not a downgrade. APH did a 2-for-1 split on 3 Sep 2026; split-adjusted, the old target was \$98.50, so the new \$107 target is actually *higher* than the old one (The Inference, 31 Oct 2026; Perplexity APH price page). Presenting it as a sharp cut is wrong.
- The Baird "cut" (\$190 → \$105) was published 6 Oct 2026 — three weeks *before* earnings, also on a pre-split scale (split-adjusted \$95 → \$52.50) — and cannot explain a post-earnings Friday move. (MarketBeat / American Banking News.)
- The Friday move tracked a broad AI-infrastructure selloff, not APH-specific news: Nasdaq −2.9%, S\&P 500 −1.8%, Philadelphia Semiconductor Index down ~7–9%, Intel −29% the same day (Perplexity APH page, Oct 30 entry). Other analysts *raised* targets around earnings (JPMorgan \$115, Citi \$115, UBS \$111, Evercore ISI \$100 from \$90).
- Timing: APH actually *rose* 3.5% on earnings day (Wed 28 Oct, close \$80.96) and a further 1.4% on Thu 29 Oct (\$82.08) before Friday's selloff. There was no post-earnings analyst-driven drop; the drop was a market-wide AI de-rating three sessions later.

The dossier's "profit-taking after a vertical run" is a fairer description than "analyst target cuts", but as written the claim's causal mechanism is contradicted.

### Claim 6 — Forward P/E ~20.2x per the screen vs ~35–41x in contemporaneous coverage on a different forward-EPS base

**Verdict: UNVERIFIED** (discrepancy resolved as a base mismatch; the 35–41x figure is not supported as a forward P/E).

- The screen's 20.2x is supported on a FY2027E base: Seeking Alpha shows 2027 estimated P/E 20.16x; Trefis shows FY2027 P/E 19.4–20.0x (FY2027 consensus EPS \$3.64); MarketBeat shows forward P/E 20.09. At \$73.08 / 20.2x the implied forward EPS is ~\$3.62, matching FY2027 consensus.
- The 35–41x figure is *not* supported as a forward P/E on any current consensus base: FY2026E P/E is 24.9–25.8x (Trefis 25.7x; Seeking Alpha 25.78x); trailing P/E is ~30.6x (Finnhub) / 29.35x (MarketBeat).
- The closest matches for 35–41x are trailing/2025-earnings bases: Seeking Alpha 2025 actual P/E 43.76x; at the 6 Oct 2026 52-week high of \$89.93, trailing P/E was ~39x. The Inference's "41 times forward earnings" (31 Oct 2026) appears to use one of these trailing/2025 bases, not forward consensus EPS.
- Conclusion: use 20.2x (FY2027E) or ~25x (FY2026E) as the forward multiple. The 35–41x number should not be quoted as a forward P/E; it is a trailing/2025-earnings multiple, and the dossier's own caveat that the figures use "a different forward-EPS base" understates the gap — they are not the same metric at all.

## CAT — Caterpillar (price \$543.69)

### Claim 7 — Q3 2026 beat, yet the stock fell ~12.7% over two days because management held full-year guidance instead of raising it

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**

- Q3 numbers: sales \$20.68B (+17.3% YoY), adjusted EPS \$7.20 (+45.5% YoY), record backlog \$78.1B — confirmed across TradingView/Benzinga, Nasdaq/Zacks, Morningstar (Dow Jones), and IBD, all 29 Oct 2026, and the company release.
- Price move: close 28 Oct \$713.38 → 29 Oct \$647.39 (−9.25%) → 30 Oct \$622.75 (−3.81%); two-day fall −12.7% (Finnhub; TipRanks; stockanalysis.com). Confirmed exactly.
- Guidance held: the company maintained full-year 2026 guidance of mid-to-high-teens sales growth, with adjusted operating margin near the bottom of its target range (TradingView/Benzinga; Nasdaq, "CAT Shares Fall Despite Q3 Earnings Beat"). Nasdaq's analysis explicitly attributes the selloff to the decision to maintain rather than raise guidance after a strong quarter.

Caveats, not contradicting the verdict:

- "Beat" is vendor-dependent on revenue: sales of \$20.682B beat the Zacks consensus (\$20.58B) but slightly missed the FactSet consensus (\$20.76B) (IBD, 29 Oct 2026; Morningstar). EPS beat everywhere (\$7.20 vs \$7.06–7.12).
- About \$0.21 of the \$7.20 adjusted EPS was a gain from refunds for tariffs invalidated via a Supreme Court ruling (IBD; Medium analysis) — the underlying beat was thinner than the headline.
- 30 Oct was a broad down day (SPY ~−1.8%), so not all of the second day's −3.81% was CAT-specific.

### Claim 8 — First 2027 guidance comes in January 2027; Q4 2026 print expected ~2 Feb 2027

**Verdict: CONTRADICTED** (both dates are wrong).

- Q4 2026 print: TipRanks, Zacks, and MarketBeat all estimate **Thu 4 Feb 2027** (before market open; consensus EPS \$6.47–6.62), based on past reporting schedules — not ~2 Feb. The dossier's screen date of 2027-02-02 is two days early versus every vendor estimate found.
- January 2027 guidance: no source supports a January guidance event. Caterpillar issues its full-year outlook *with* the Q4 results — i.e. with the 4 Feb 2027 print, not in January. The dossier's "first 2027 guidance comes in January 2027" appears to be an assumption, not a dated company event. The make-or-break catalyst is therefore the 4 Feb 2027 release (results + 2027 outlook together), and the panel should correct the date in its memo.
