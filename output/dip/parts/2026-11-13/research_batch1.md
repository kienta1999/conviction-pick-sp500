### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 6/10
- FIX is the leading US mechanical/electrical (MEP) contractor for data centers and advanced manufacturing: 47 operating units across 178 locations / 136 cities (2025 10-K era; ~190 locations per later filings), decentralized roll-up model, modular/offsite construction that delivers projects ~40% faster than traditional methods, and the scale to take the largest, most complex jobs (backlog 18–24 months vs a historical ~9). The moat is execution + skilled-labor scarcity + scale/selectivity ("hiring, not demand, will limit growth" — management via Middle Market Value), not technology: HVAC/electrical installation is a fragmented $550B+ US market (per FIX's 2024 10-K) where even the largest players hold single-digit share (Quanta ~6–8%). AI cannot substitute the work — data centers physically need cooling, power distribution and piping installed by licensed trades — but it cannot be monopolized either: competitors (EMCOR, MasTec, Quanta, APi, Legence) and PE-backed roll-ups compete for the same labor and projects, and MasTec's $1.65B acquisition of The Superior Group (Q3 2026) shows rivals are scaling into the same niche. Switching costs are project-level (relationships, safety record, delivery speed), not structural. The lowest irreplaceability score in the batch because the advantage is operational, not proprietary.

### 6. Category position
- Not a monopolist — a leading consolidator in a fragmented trade. The relevant "share" signal is selectivity: a $17.8B backlog lets FIX choose high-margin, complex work smaller competitors cannot execute, and technology work tripled from $808M to $2.7B over two years (per a Jan 2026 profile; now 59–61% of revenue). ### 7. Value-trap risk (bear case)
- Concentration is the trap: 59–61% of revenue from tech/data-center customers; if hyperscaler capex is delayed by moratoriums or financing stress, backlog growth stalls and the market will re-rate FIX from "AI infrastructure" back to "cyclical contractor" — a much lower multiple. Gross-margin erosion (152bp in two quarters, electrical segment −190bp YoY) could be the leading indicator of price competition or labor-cost pressure rather than mix noise; if gross margin breaks below 24% the thesis weakens materially (RockFlow downgrade trigger). Labor is the binding constraint (hiring, not demand, limits growth) — wage inflation + project execution risk on a $17.8B backlog is real. CFO transition adds execution uncertainty at the top. The stock's prior fast fall (Mar 2025, −40.4% in 30 days) shows this name can de-rate violently on sentiment alone.

### 8. Rebound score: 7/10
