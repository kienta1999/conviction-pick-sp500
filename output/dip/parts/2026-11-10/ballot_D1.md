## Single-pick ballot

- **Top pick:** LRCX
- **Runner-up:** CAT
- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** Lam is the least-trapped name in a field where almost every dip is a repricing of the same AI-capex fear. Fiscal Q1 2027 (reported 21 Oct 2026) was a record on every leg that matters — revenue $8.219bn (+54% YoY), non-GAAP operating margin 40.2%, record FCF $3.24bn — and the stock halved anyway, first on a supply-capped guide and then in the 5 Nov rout (-16.36% that day, no company news). The dip is a demand-expectation repricing, not a share loss: gross margin was a record 52.4% non-GAAP and preliminary CY2027 WFE spending is >$200bn versus the mid-$150bn range for CY2026. A $25bn buyback authorised 5 Nov 2026 (~9% of shares at the time) puts a real floor under the name while the market waits for the Mar 2027 quarter to prove the slipped shipments arrive.
- **Why the dip is temporary (not permanent):** the single most compelling evidence is that the Dec-quarter guide was capped by supply, not demand — CFO Doug Bettinger said on the 21 Oct call that affected shipments slip to the Mar 2027 quarter, not that they are lost — and demand signals point the other way (preliminary CY2027 WFE >$200bn, led by DRAM). A permanent impairment would show up as lost share or collapsing margins; Lam shows the opposite, with record margins and a Customer Support Business Group (~$2.50bn in Q1 FY27, ~30% of revenue) compounding recurring revenue.
- **Moat / AI-irreplaceability:** low risk. Lam is one of the big-three wafer-fab equipment oligopoly in plasma etch and deposition; tools are qualified tool-by-tool into fabs and switching mid-node is prohibitively expensive. AI does not route around Lam — it is the demand driver, with record memory revenue +120% YoY in Q1 FY27 and more etch/deposition steps per wafer as devices get more complex. The real long-run threat is Chinese domestic substitution (CXMT) in China, which was 35.1% of Sep-quarter revenue, not product obsolescence.
- **Rebound catalyst & timing:** Dec-quarter print and the Q2 FY27 report ~27 Jan 2027 (guided $8.6bn revenue, $2.33 EPS), when slipped shipments should show up in the Mar quarter; then CY2027 WFE >$200bn delivery through 2027. The $25bn buyback, authorised 5 Nov 2026, is already live as a floor.
- **12–18mo return scenario:** Bear ~$150 (-33% vs the $222.43 screen price) by mid-2027 if CY2027 WFE rolls over, hyperscaler capex is cut, or China controls/substitution bite — the forward multiple de-rates violently on lower earnings. Base ~$310 (+39%) by ~Q2–Q3 2027 if WFE >$200bn materialises, slipped shipments land in the Mar quarter, and the multiple re-rates partway toward the $364.03 analyst mean target (+63.7% vs screen). Bull ~$380 (+71%) by late 2027 if the WFE boom arrives on schedule with margins held and the buyback retiring ~9% of shares — near Berenberg's $420 target (raised 9 Sep 2026). All figures are research scenarios, not guarantees.
- **Top risk (value-trap case):** a genuine WFE down-cycle — if AI capex is cut, data-centre moratoria become law rather than rhetoric, or an AI-demand air pocket opens, the >$200bn CY2027 WFE figure evaporates and 18.2x forward PE becomes 30x+ on lower earnings very fast. Memory, the fastest-growing customer segment (+120% YoY), is also the most cyclical and cuts both ways, and component/cleanroom bottlenecks could persist beyond one quarter.
- **Conviction (1-10):** 7

No — the CFO's own words on 21 Oct 2026 are that shipments slip to the Mar quarter, and the WFE guide is up, not down. (2) Is the halving a share loss?
