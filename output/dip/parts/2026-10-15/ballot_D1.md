- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** This is the one dip in the 14 where I can verify the cause is outside the business and already reversing, rather than having to believe a forecast. Cocoa nearly tripled into 2024 records and crushed earnings; cocoa futures have since fallen ~70% to the low-$3,000s, and because Hershey contracts 6–8 months ahead that relief is only now reaching the P&L — Q2 2026 adjusted EPS $1.90, +57% YoY and ~33% above consensus, with adjusted gross margin +350bp to 41.6%. The franchise never broke: ~45% U.S. chocolate share was retained, seasonal share gained in Q1 2026, and there is no sourced evidence of share loss to Mars, Ferrero or private label. Survival is not a variable — $2.1bn of TTM free cash flow was generated *through* the worst input-cost year on record, against net debt of roughly $4.5–4.8bn (~2.3–2.5x trough-to-recovering EBITDA) that falls fast as guided EPS recovers +32.5–35% to $8.36–$8.52 in FY2026. The explosive leg is mechanical: depressed earnings re-rate toward Hershey's pre-shock low-to-mid-20s forward range as margins rebuild toward the historical mid-40s.
- **Why the dip is temporary (not permanent):** The single most compelling evidence is that the shock is an input price, not demand: cocoa futures are down ~70% from the 2024 record and the lagged benefit is already printing (+350bp gross margin, +57% EPS in Q2), while the volume decline is category-wide price elasticity on ~12 points of pricing — not share transfer, with Hershey still the U.S. #1 and gaining seasonal share.
- **Moat / AI-irreplaceability:** Low AI risk — software cannot substitute a physical treat, and AI is simply irrelevant as a displacer here. The moat is brand plus scale/distribution: Reese's is the #1 U.S. candy franchise behind ~45% U.S. chocolate share, ~23% through-cycle ROIC and a 96-year unbroken dividend. Honest caveat: this is a consumer-brand moat with no switching costs — the real disruption vector is GLP-1 adoption (which management calls "mild" so far), not AI.
- **Rebound catalyst & timing:** Already in motion — the cocoa cost unwind annualises through H2 2026 into 2027 via the 6–8-month contracting lag, landing first in Hershey's biggest selling season. Proof points: the Q3 2026 print (expected late Oct 2026) on Halloween sell-through, then any FY2027 guide converting the FY2026 recovery year into a second year of margin rebuild toward the mid-40s gross-margin range.
- **12-18 month return scenario:** Base ~+24% (toward the $201.62 mean target, on the guided EPS recovery holding); bull ~+45% (back toward the $239.48 52-week high area if gross margin normalises to the mid-40s and the multiple re-rates to its pre-shock range), with a ~3.5% dividend yield paid while waiting.
- **Top risk (value-trap case):** That the recovery is price-led into a shrinking pie — Q2 growth was ~+12 points price / ~-8 points volume, so if two years of double-digit price rises plus compounding GLP-1 drag leave a structurally lower volume base, EPS stalls after the one-off cocoa deflation and 16.6x forward becomes 16.6x peak-recovery earnings. A cocoa re-spike on one West African harvest is the second leg. I am watching the volume line, not cocoa, as the falsifier.
