## 1) TPR — Tapestry (Coach, Kate Spade) — ~$113.58 (10/6/26)

**Why it's down.** Not a business miss — a multiple reset. FY2026 (reported 8/13/26) was strong: revenue $8.004B (+14%), adj. EPS $7.05 (+38%), Q4 EPS $1.32 (+28% YoY), FCF +66% YoY to ~$1.8B (ainvest 8/13–8/15/26). But FY2027 guidance midpoint ($8.45B revenue, $7.85 EPS) was ~$20M light on revenue vs consensus, and — more importantly — the growth cadence collapses: revenue growth from +14% → ~+6%, EPS growth +38% → ~+11%, margin expansion 340bp → ~50bp (BestStocks, 8/13/26). Stock fell 16.5% on 8/13 and is down ~30–31% since the 52wk high of $164.80 (finnhub: now ~$113.58; high 164.80). Second driver: Kate Spade — full-year FY26 revenue fell 10% to $1.075B, guided to a high-single-digit decline in FY27 with a continued operating loss; the company took an $855M Kate Spade impairment last year (ainvest, 8/13/26). Cramer (9/9/26): "very rare that you see this stock this down with the fundamentals this up."
**Verdict: TEMPORARY.** This is growth-deceleration derating, not impairment — Coach is still +15% in Q4, +23% constant-currency in FY26.

**Moat / AI-irreplaceability: 7/10.** Moat = desire-driven brand equity (Coach's Tabby handbag, rising AUR, Gen Z/millenial pull) + DTC distribution. AI cannot commoditize taste, status signaling, or craftsmanship aspiration; generative-AI impacts copywriting/marketing costs at the margin only. No credible AI substitute route.

**Rebound catalyst.** Holiday Q1 FY27 (reported ~Feb 2027) showing Coach high-single-digit growth holding + any Kate Spade stabilization; continued buybacks; 2.9%+ dividend. Path: multiple re-rating from ~14.5x back toward 17–18x on one clean holiday print.

**Balance sheet.** FCF $1.8B FY26 (ainvest, 8/13/26); operating margins 23%+; cash flow per share $8.29, price/cash flow ~14x (MarketBeat 10/6/26). Debt-to-equity 3.44 looks high but is largely accounting (years of buybacks shrunk book equity, book value only $3.43/sh); current ratio 1.75, quick 1.25 — no liquidity stress, no dilution risk.

**Valuation.** Forward P/E ~14.7 (MarketBeat, 10/6/26) vs $7.85 FY27 guided EPS at $113.58 ≈ 14.5x. Own history: traded 18–20x+ through 2024–early 2025 at peak growth; ~11x at the 2022 lows. Now at the low end of its 5-yr range despite record earnings. Analyst mean target $175.13, 21 analysts, Moderate Buy → ~+54% implied upside (MarketBeat, 10/6/26). Genuinely cheap for the quality.

**Category position.** Coach is #1 in accessible luxury with rising AUR and double-digit growth — pricing power intact. Kate Spade is losing relevance (2nd straight shrinking year), but it's ~13% of revenue; the dip is NOT Coach share loss.

**Value-trap risk.** Coach's 20%+ growth was partly Tabby-cycle; if accessible-luxury demand rolls over in a consumer downturn, FY27's guided deceleration becomes an actual decline. Kate Spade turnaround may never come (impairment already signals this). TPR is effectively a one-brand company — concentration risk.

**Rebound score: 8/10.** Verdict: The cleanest classic buy-the-dip here — a beat-and-raise business derated on guide cadence, not fundamentals; Coach's engine intact, valuation at cycle-low multiples.

## 2) VRT — Vertiv Holdings (data-center power + cooling) — ~$243.74 (10/7/26)

**Why it's down.** Two legs. (1) Q2 FY26 (7/29/26): revenue $3.27B (+24% YoY) missed consensus $3.38B ("minor timing shifts" — temporary supply-chain congestion, multi-phased project execution) while adj. EPS $1.52 beat $1.43 and full-year guidance was RAISED (sales $13.8–14.2B, adj. EPS $6.65–6.75); stock sank 17.2% to $223 in one day — "good isn't good enough" at a 55x trailing multiple (Barron's, 7/29/26). (2) Sentiment: elevated Treasury yields + debate over AI data-center project timing; September saw a second leg −15% from the 9/8 high (MarketBeat news summary, 10/2/26). Hagens Berman securities investigation adds a litigation overhang (announced post-Q2). Note: stock is +50% YTD even after the −36% drawdown from $379.94 (May 2026 high) — this is a froth-compression, not a broken thesis.
**Verdict: TEMPORARY.** Demand indicators (backlog, pipelines, raised guidance) all point up; the drawdown is multiple + timing noise.

**Moat / AI-irreplaceability: 8/10.** Moat = mission-critical infrastructure: power (UPS, switchgear, busbar), thermal/liquid cooling, plus a global service footprint and certification lock-in (data centers can't swap vendors mid-build). AI is a TAILWIND, not a threat — every AI rack needs more power and more cooling; Vertiv is the pick-and-shovel beneficiary. September 2026 acquisitions (UtilityInnovation Group, King Environmental Services) extend the "grid-to-chip" stack. No AI-substitution route exists for electrons and heat.

**Rebound catalyst.** Q3 FY26 report (late Oct 2026; guide: sales $3.65–3.85B, adj. EPS $1.77–1.83), continued hyperscaler capex prints, and investor-day 20–22% revenue CAGR-through-2030 framing (May 2026 investor day). Each clean quarter re-rates it.

**Balance sheet.** Essentially unlevered: total debt $2.94B vs cash $3.11B → net debt ≈ −$171M (net cash), D/E 0.62, $5.6B liquidity (ainvest statistics; Q2 8-K via stocktitan, 7/29/26). Adj. FCF guide $2.4–2.6B for 2026 (+32% at midpoint; Q2 alone $925M, +200% YoY). Zero distress risk.

**Valuation.** Trailing P/E ~55 (finnhub), forward P/E ~27 on raised 2026 guide (Motley Fool, 9/29/26); 5-yr PEG <1. Own history: 30–50x+ through the 2024–25 run — now derated to the bottom of its AI-era range. Peers: Eaton ~30x, Schneider ~25x — VRT at 27x forward with 60% adj. EPS growth guided is no longer obviously expensive. Mean target $357.83, Moderate Buy (MarketBeat, 9/23/26) → ~+47% implied upside.

**Category position.** #1/#2 globally in data-center thermal and power alongside Schneider/Eaton; leading in both AC and DC architectures with 800V DC validation in 2027 (Q2 call). Share intact — the dip is valuation, not share loss.

**Value-trap risk.** Multiple still embeds 20%+ multi-year growth; any hyperscaler capex air-pocket before 2028 craters orders. Execution: Q2's "timing shifts" must not become a pattern. The securities investigation is noise unless it finds something. Beta 2.1 — it will keep swinging.

**Earnings flag — INVENTORY_BUILD (inv_rev_divergence 0.544; accrual_ratio −0.123; cfo_ni 1.937):** inventory growing faster than revenue ahead of 30–32% organic growth guidance — BENIGN business-model reason (building stock for a record backlog and multi-phase AI projects); accruals are negative and CFO runs 1.94x net income, i.e., earnings are cash-conservative, not inflated. Watch: write-down risk if project timing slips further. One-line verdict: pre-build for growth with strong cash conversion — benign, monitor.

**Rebound score: 7/10.** Verdict: The AI-infrastructure thesis is intact and the balance sheet is fortress-grade, but you pay a growth multiple and ride 2x beta — a momentum-rebound, not a value, bet.

## 3) CPRT — Copart (salvage/insurance auto auctions) — ~$26.62 (10/7/26)

**Why it's down.** Fundamental deceleration, not a one-off. Q4 FY26 (9/10/26): revenue $1.15B (+2.4%, beat), but EPS $0.35 missed $0.38–0.39, −14.6% YoY; net income −17.4% to $327.4M; gross margin 41.8% (from 45.1%); op margin 32% (from 36.7%); per-vehicle opex +12.7% YoY; worldwide units sold −2.9% in Q4, −5.5% for FY26 ex-catastrophe; US insurance-sourced volume soft on reduced claims frequency (moneycheck/AlphaStreet, 9/10–11/26). Earlier: Q2 FY26 (2/19/26) revenue −3.6% YoY, both lines missed, stock −3.1% (barchart via cfgag). Stock: 52wk high $45.89 → $26.62, −42%; −32% YTD; below its 200-day MA (~$44) since June 2025 (finnhub; barchart). This is a full-year growth stall: FY26 EPS expected ~$1.58 vs $1.59 FY25 (flat-to-down).
**Verdict: TEMPORARY but slower-burning.** Cyclical volume trough (fewer claims/total losses) + cost pressure; no structural impairment to the auction model.

**Moat / AI-irreplaceability: 8/10.** Moat = two-sided network effects (insurers list, global buyers bid on the VB3 platform) + capital intensity (250 locations, land bank, vehicle storage) + switching costs for insurers. AI cannot replace physical salvage yards and tow/storage logistics; if anything, AI helps pricing. Disruption vectors are mundane: fewer accidents (ADAS), lower used-car prices, EV repair complexity — volume drivers, not moat threats.

**Rebound catalyst.** Salvage-volume normalization as claims frequency reverts; per-vehicle opex discipline (management flagged intent to reduce it); the $1.9B all-cash acquisition of ACV Auctions (announced 9/10/26, $10.50/sh, 45% premium) expanding into dealer wholesale — integration upside and a growth leg; any sign of unit-volume stabilization in Q1 FY27 (Nov/Dec 2026 report).

**Balance sheet.** Fortress: ~$6.4B liquidity, "no debt on its balance sheet" (barchart, 2/2026); net debt ≈ −$2.3B (net cash) per macroaxis; current ratio 7.61. FCF margin 24.2% in Q4 (down from 31.2%, still elite). 282M shares remain on the buyback authorization (medium/Shcorp, ~6/2026). Zero distress risk; can self-fund the ACV deal in cash.

**Valuation.** Trailing P/E ~17 (finnhub), forward ~20 (MarketBeat, 9/11/26) — vs own history of 30–40x+; this is the cheapest CPRT has been in a decade. Mean target $38.83 (8 analysts, Moderate Buy, 9/11/26) → ~+46% implied upside from $26.62. But note: EPS is flat-to-down — the "E" is not growing, so the cheap multiple partly reflects a growth stall.

**Category position.** Still the #1 global salvage auctioneer with record US insurance average selling prices (Q2 call) — pricing power intact. The dip is volume/cost, not share loss to IAA/RBAC.

**Value-trap risk.** The honest bear case: what if this isn't cyclical? Structurally fewer total losses (safer cars, ADAS), declining salvage values, and higher opex could mean the 35%-margin era is over and 20x is a fair multiple for a no-growth compounder. The ACV deal at a 45% premium could destroy value if dealer wholesale doesn't fit Copart's model. Claims frequency has been soft for 4+ quarters — troughs can last.

**Earnings flag — RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN (recv_rev_divergence 3.203; accrual_ratio −0.014; cfo_ni 1.085):** receivables growing ~3.2x faster than revenue is the eye-catching one, but accrual ratio is ~0 and CFO covers net income 1.09x — earnings are NOT running ahead of cash. Most consistent with timing (seller-advance/receivable mix around quarter-end and the insurance billing cycle) rather than aggressive revenue recognition. One-line verdict: flag reflects balance-sheet timing, not cash-quality deterioration — monitor next quarter, not a value-trap signature today.

**Rebound score: 6/10.** Verdict: Highest-quality balance sheet of the four at a decade-low multiple, but the "E" has stalled and the rebound needs a volume cycle turn — a patient compounder's dip, not a snap-back.

## 4) ADBE — Adobe (Creative Cloud / Document Cloud / Experience Cloud) — ~$232.61 (10/7/26)

**Why it's down — the AI-disruption question, head-on.** The market's fear: generative AI commoditizes creative work and pulls the next generation of creators toward free/cheap AI-native tools (Midjourney, Runway, Canva), eroding Creative Cloud pricing power. Evidence FOR erosion: net new ARR down ~38% YoY in Q3 FY26; cRPO growth slowed to 9%; Q4 FY26 revenue guide came in below consensus; the stock fell after BOTH Q2 and Q3 FY26 beat-and-raises (tickeron, 9/28/26; iocharts, 9/29/26). Morgan Stanley downgraded to Underweight ($240 target, summer 2026) on AI-substitution risk. Stock: March 2025 peak $453 → ~$233, −49%; 52wk range $190.12–$363.70 (finnhub 10/7/26).
**Evidence AGAINST erosion (the dated counter-data):** Q3 FY26 (reported 9/10/26): record revenue $6.76B (+13% YoY), adj. EPS $6.13 (beat $6.09), FY26 targets RAISED (revenue $26.576–26.626B, non-GAAP EPS $24.45–24.50); total ARR $27.50B (+11.2% YoY); AI-first ARR >$650M, +150%+ YoY; AI-influenced ARR is over one-third of the entire book; Firefly did 18B+ generations (quantabundance, ~5/2026; ad-hoc-news, 10/6/26). Freemium: 100M+ free users with paid conversion still unproven — that's the real near-term sacrifice. Also: CEO transition (Shantanu Narayen out; new CEO effective 12/1/26 per stockmoguls 9/5/26; beatingthebull 8/2026 notes the CEO search as an overhang).
**Verdict: MOSTLY TEMPORARY (sentiment overshoot), with a genuine watch-item.** No moat breach is visible in the numbers — revenue, ARR, and margins keep growing; the "disruption" is in the multiple, not the P&L. But net-new-ARR deceleration and freemium conversion are real metrics to police.

**Moat / AI-irreplaceability: 6/10.** Moat = professional workflow lock-in (PSD/AI file formats, plugin ecosystems, enterprise seats), 97% subscription revenue, and Firefly trained on licensed Adobe Stock = the only commercially-safe gen-AI model for enterprises. Adobe is monetizing the disruption (AI-influenced ARR >1/3 of book). The threat is real but bounded: AI-native tools attack the low end (casual creators, stock photos — management admitted Firefly is cannibalizing its own stock-photo library "faster than expected," quantabundance), pressuring pricing power at the margin. Professionals aren't leaving en masse — that's the 6, not a 3.

**Rebound catalyst.** (1) CEO transition resolved 12/1/26 with a credible AI roadmap; (2) Q4 FY26 print (~mid-Dec 2026) showing freemium conversion and ARR reacceleration; (3) DA Davidson raised target to $290 Buy (9/2026); sector rotation back into software already lifted shares off the $190 low (+25%).

**Balance sheet.** Cash + short-term investments $5.64B vs ~$6.4B debt → net debt only ~$1.1B (fool.com 10/4/26; indmoney 9/2026) — trivial vs $10.6B FCF (40.8% FCF margin, ROIC 56%, gross margin 89%). Buyback machine: $6.82B repurchased in 9M FY26 (26.1M shares), $24.55B authorization remaining (~25% of market cap); share count −6.2% YoY. Zero survival risk.

**Valuation.** Forward P/E ~8.9–9.6x (forbestodays 10/6/26; iocharts 9/29/26) vs own 5-yr history of 25–40x — a decade low. Peers: MSFT 23x, Intuit 16x, Workday 39x (ainvest 7/2026). PEG ~1.0. Analysts split: mean target ~$275–283 (consensus Hold, 33 analysts; MarketBeat via ad-hoc-news 10/6/26) → ~+18–21% implied upside; range $195 (KeyCorp Underweight) to $373; HSBC $308 bull, BofA $190 bear. Genuinely cheap IF the franchise isn't impaired — the market is pricing structural decline.

**Category position.** Still #1 in professional creative software by a wide margin; no share-loss evidence in revenue/ARR. The dip is narrative (AI fear + leadership), not competitive displacement — yet.

**Value-trap risk.** The Kodak scenario: freemium trains 100M users to expect free AI creation and they never convert; net new ARR keeps decelerating; pricing power erodes 2–3% a year for a decade and the 9x multiple was fair. Insider selling (CEO included) and a 5-month CEO search don't inspire. If AI-native workflows become the default for the next generation, today's cheap is tomorrow's trap.

**Rebound score: 7/10.** Verdict: The highest-conviction contrarian bet of the four — a 9x-forward-P/E compounder with 89% gross margins and a $24.5B buyback cannon, priced as if already disrupted while the P&L says otherwise; the risk is narrative, and narratives need a catalyst (Dec CEO + Q4 print) to break.
