### 1. Why it's down

Hershey then took ~12–14 points of price in 2026 to recover costs, which crushed volumes: in Q2 2026, ~12 points of price vs. ~−8 points volume (−10 points in North America confectionery) — the classic "price without brand recovery" pattern. The stock hit a 52-week low of $161.40 in late Sept 2026 (financier.news). Additional weight: dividend payout ratio ~78–82% of earnings while margins were squeezed (Fool, 9/23/2026), and a Jefferies note flagging a potential record-strong 2026–27 Super El Niño as a renewed cocoa-supply risk (GuruFocus, 9/15/2026).
- **Verdict: TRANSITORY (cyclical commodity), with one real brand warning.** Cocoa is now in *surplus* — benchmark ~$4,000–4,400/tonne by Feb 2026, down >60% from peak; StoneX forecasts a 287,000-ton 2025/26 global surplus vs. the record 494,000-ton deficit in 2023/24 (AInvest, 2/5/2026). CFO Dave Hulays said at the Sept 2026 Barclays conference the company has "good visibility into deflation next year from cocoa." The caveat that keeps this from a pure 10: Hershey **lost share of US candy, mint, and gum categories** even as margins snapped back — the recovery is price-driven, not brand-driven (AInvest, 9/12/2026).

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — score 9/10

Disruption vectors are demand-side (GLP-1/health trends) rather than technology. Supply-chain/economies-of-scale moat in manufacturing and distribution. Threats are substitutes (health/wellness demand shifts, private label in a squeeze) — slow-moving, not existential.

Beyond cocoa: salty snacks (Dot's, SkinnyPop) grew volumes ~18–28% in late 2025 — a genuine diversifying engine. ### 6. Category position
