## TPR — Tapestry, Inc. (Coach, Kate Spade)

**Price snapshot (Finnhub, ~Oct 3, 2026):** $118.12; 52-week $93.00–$164.80 → **−28% off high**; YTD −7.6%.

### 1. Why it's down — Verdict: TRANSITORY (growth-derating after a guide miss)
- On Aug 13, 2026 Tapestry reported FY26 Q4: revenue $1.876B (+9%, beat), adj. EPS $1.32 (beat), FY26 revenue $8.0B +14%, Coach revenue +24%. But FY2027 revenue guide of **$8.4–$8.5B vs $8.47B consensus** (miss by a rounding error) and Coach FY27 guided only at high-single-digit growth → stock fell **16.5% to $128.39 in one session**, worst S&P 500 performer that day (Barron's). It has since slid to ~$118 (−30% in ~7 weeks since early-August record).
- The repricing is about the *growth trajectory*, not solvency: market punishing the deceleration off a +38% EPS year and Coach concentration. Kate Spade revenue fell 7% in Q4 to $235M and is guided to another **high-single-digit decline in FY27** — the second straight year of shrinkage after the Stuart Weitzman divestiture. Jim Cramer (Mad Money, Sep 9, 2026): "It's very rare that you see this stock this down with the fundamentals this up."
- Additional overhangs: tariff exposure on Chinese/Vietnamese imports, China luxury softness risk, and broader consumer-discretionary rotation (beta 1.47).

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — **7/10**
- Moat = **brand + heritage pricing power in "accessible luxury"**: Coach is the dominant US affordable-luxury handbag brand; gross margin ~78–83% and 23.4% operating margin signal genuine pricing power, not commodity retail. Fashion brands are largely AI-proof (no software substitute for a Coach bag; dupes are a margin risk, not disruption). Switching costs live at the carrier/retailer level via DTC customer relationships. Kate Spade's turnaround failure shows brand moats are perishable, but Coach's is currently intact (Q4: +15% revenue).

### 3. Rebound catalyst
- Q1 FY27 guide (EPS ~$1.55 vs $1.48 consensus) and FY27 plan: mid-single-digit revenue growth, +50bps operating-margin expansion, adj. EPS $7.80–$7.90 (low-double-digit EPS growth). Management raised the dividend **16% to $1.85 annualized** and plans **$1.7B capital return in FY27 (~$1.35B buybacks)** — a mechanical buyer under a thin-float-cap stock. Stabilization path: Coach sustain high-single-digit growth + Kate Spade bottoming + any tariff relief. Timing: next earnings (Q1 FY27, ~Nov 2026) is the next test.

### 4. Balance sheet
- FY26 year-end: $1.15B cash/short-term investments, $2.38B total borrowings → **net debt ~$1.2B; gross leverage 1.1x adj. EBITDA** — modest. **Adjusted FCF $1.86B** (23% margin); FY27 guide ~$1.7B FCF. Watch: equity is thin (debt/equity 3.5x after the buyback binge) — buybacks + dividends ($1.88B in FY26) slightly exceeded FCF, so the cushion comes from earnings, not balance-sheet depth.

### 5. Valuation
- At $118.12 vs FY27 adj. EPS guide midpoint $7.85 → **forward P/E ~15.0x**; vs FY26 EPS $7.05 → 16.8x. Historically TPR has traded 14–17x — this is the low end of its own range and a discount to Ralph Lauren (~24x) and Capri (~20x) (beststocks.com, Aug 2026). FCF yield: ~$1.7B guide vs ~$23.7B market cap ≈ **7.2%**. Analyst mean target **$175.13** (21 analysts, Moderate Buy; MarketBeat, Oct 3, 2026) → **~+48% upside**; post-selloff trims: Morgan Stanley $159 (Overweight), Wells Fargo $160, UBS $232, TD Cowen $175. Genuinely cheap *because of the dip* — but the multiple assumes Coach's growth story resumes.

### 6. Category position
- Coach is the #1 player in US accessible luxury with pricing power (gross margin expanding +180bps in Q4). Dip reflects concentration risk, not share loss: the entire selloff happened while Coach was *gaining*. Kate Spade is the share-loser, and it's ~12% of revenue.

### 7. Value-trap risk
- If Coach's FY26 +24% was a one-off (post-Covid affordable-luxury cycle peak) and FY27's high-single-digit guide marks the start of a multi-year deceleration, the 15x multiple is not cheap — it's a consumer-discretionary cyclical peak in disguise. China reversal + tariffs + recession would compress earnings while the buyback binge keeps leverage fragile.

### 8. Rebound score: 6.5/10 — Verdict: Transitory growth-derating on a still-strong Coach franchise; the 15x P/E + 7% FCF yield + $1.7B return program is a clean dip setup, but Kate Spade and China are live risks.

## VRT — Vertiv Holdings Co. (data-center power & thermal)

**Price snapshot (MarketBeat, Oct 2, 2026):** $252.38; 52-week $147.82–$379.93 → **−34% off high**; YTD +~42%.

### 1. Why it's down — Verdict: TRANSITORY (execution/timing miss + valuation + macro)
- Peak ~$380 in mid-May 2026. Q2 2026 (Jul 29, 2026): revenue $3.27B (+24% YoY) but **~$110M below consensus** → sharp single-day selloff; analysts trimmed targets. Company attributed it to timing shifts in large multiphase projects, supply-chain congestion, and tariff/logistics volatility (Zacks, Sep 1, 2026). Adj. EPS $1.52 beat $1.43 consensus and FY26 EPS guide was *raised* to $6.65–$6.75 — fundamentals kept improving while the multiple compressed.
- Sentiment shocks: Jan 2026 — Jensen Huang said "no water chillers are necessary for data centres" at CES; Barclays warned not to shrug it off → AI-cooling names sold (ts2.tech). Sep 2026 — **$1.45B acquisition of UtilityInnovation Group (UIG)** announced without UIG's revenue/backlog/cash-flow disclosure (13x *2027E* EBITDA incl. earnouts up to ~$2.6B) → investors repriced M&A discipline (cryptocompass.com, Sep 2026). Add: Massachusetts data-center permitting regulations, Hagens Berman securities-law investigation, and rate-sensitivity (beta 2.05).

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — **7/10**
- Moat = **process-tech + certification lock-in + switching costs in critical infrastructure**: power systems, thermal/liquid cooling, UPS, switchgear co-designed with hyperscalers and chipmakers; mission-critical downtime risk makes customers sticky. 2026 targets: 20–22% annual revenue growth through 2030, adj. operating margins >27%. Risk is not AI replacing it — it's AI *rearranging* the stack: more efficient chips or Nvidia-direct liquid-cooling designs could shrink per-rack thermal spend; hyperscalers can also vertically integrate. So far order growth (+24% sales, Q3 guide $3.65–$3.85B) says the supercycle is intact.

### 3. Rebound catalyst
- **Q3 2026 earnings ~Oct 28, 2026** (Barchart): consensus $1.81 EPS (+46% YoY); FY26 guide $6.70 adj. EPS midpoint, 37% revenue growth, **FCF $2.4–$2.6B (+32% YoY)**. Investor event / investor day expected; King Environmental Services closes Q4 2026 (deepens liquid-cooling services). Any order/backlog acceleration or margin print >22.6% would unwind the "lumpy projects" narrative.

### 4. Balance sheet
- **Net leverage 0.2x** (Q1 2026: net debt <$700M, fell 50% in a quarter); cash ~$3.1B vs total debt ~$2.9B (ainvest, Sep 2026). FCF $2.92B TTM. UIG deal ($1.45B + earnouts) is easily fundable — distress risk is nil.

### 5. Valuation
- At $252.38 vs FY26 adj. EPS guide ~$6.70 → **forward P/E ~37.7x**; trailing P/E 57x (MarketBeat, Oct 2026); Motley Fool (Sep 29, 2026) cites ~27x on 2027E with **5-yr PEG <1** — growth is priced in but not extreme for the growth rate. vs own history: premium has compressed materially from the May peak (PEG 0.99). Analyst mean target **$357.08** (MarketBeat, Oct 2026, Moderate Buy) → **~+41% upside**; Vertical Research initiated Buy (Oct 2026). Cheap *because of the dip* only relative to 40%+ EPS growth — it is not an absolute value name.

### 6. Category position
- Vertiv is a top-tier (arguably #1) pure-play in data-center critical power & thermal, diversified across product lines with Americas +53% growth (68% of sales). The Q2 miss was timing, not share loss; no competitor is visibly taking share.

### 7. Value-trap risk
- If the May peak was "peak AI-capex," order books go lumpier, hyperscalers push back build schedules or in-source thermal design, and the 38x forward P/E grinds down for years. The UIG deal's opacity and the Hagens Berman probe are governance hairs that justify a permanent discount.

### 8. Rebound score: 7.0/10 — Verdict: A timing-miss derating inside an intact AI-infrastructure supercycle; fortress balance sheet (0.2x leverage) and Q3 earnings on Oct 28 as the near-term trigger, but at 38x forward earnings the rebound demands continued 40%+ EPS delivery.

**INVENTORY_BUILD screen flag — verdict: BENIGN (business-model build, not earnings-ahead-of-cash).** Inventory rose from $1.456B (Dec 31, 2025) to $1.835B (Mar 31, 2026) and ~$2.52B by Q2 (+33–79% YoY); receivables rose similarly. But inventory days went 65→82, *below* the 89 peak in 2024 (MarketChacha); receivables grew 32% vs revenue 28% — in line with growth. Crucially, earnings are MORE than cash-backed: 5-yr cumulative CFO/PAT ≈ **1.8x**, Q1 2026 operating cash flow was $766.8M vs net income $390.1M, and deferred revenue (customer advances) grew $1.81B→$2.46B — the build is pre-funded by the record AI order backlog, not channel-stuffing. Watch receivables conversion as backlog ships.

## VRSK — Verisk Analytics, Inc. (insurance data & analytics)

**Price snapshot (Finnhub, ~Oct 3, 2026):** $163.88; 52-week $155.94–$253.35 → **−35% off high**; YTD −26.7%; 1-year −34.2%.

### 1. Why it's down — Verdict: MOSTLY TRANSITORY (cyclical insurance softness + derating), with a structural AI question to watch
- The slow burn started 2025: moderating organic growth, soft Q3 2025 revenue, acquisition-related margin pressure, and "elevated debt" concerns (barchart, early 2026). On **Mar 26, 2026**, preliminary Q1 2026 US P&C data showed the market "normalizing" faster than expected → **−5.2% single-day** ($2.1B wiped) as investors feared the end of the double-digit premium-hike tailwind that fed Verisk's premium-linked pricing (financialcontent, Mar 2026).
- Aug 2026: a court **revived the $2.35B AccuLynx deal** (~10% of market cap) → −5.6% Aug 10 close; financing questions (Verisk repaid $1.5B acquisition debt in Jan 2026, so the revived close needs cash/new borrowing). Q2 2026 (Jul 29, 2026) was actually fine: revenue $806M (+4.3%), organic constant-currency +5.8%, adj. EPS $1.98 (+5.3%), FCF $298M (+57.9%), EBITDA margin 57.5%. The stock falls anyway — a multiple derating on slowing growth, not a broken business. AI-disruption chatter (Pulse, May 2026) adds a permanent-ish overhang.

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — **6/10**
- Moat = **data-network effects + workflow embedding**: Verisk (ISO loss costs, Xactimate/Property Estimating Solutions, catastrophe models) is the near-standard data infrastructure for US P&C underwriting/claims — highly recurring subscription revenue, ~57% EBITDA margins. BUT the threat is real and two-sided: insurtech/analytics rivals (Guidewire, LexisNexis, Duck Creek) expanding capabilities + carriers **internalizing** data functions, and advanced AI models potentially replacing traditional actuarial tools (pulse.bot, May 2026). Verisk is responding (XactAI licenses up 10x to 7,000; Anthropic MCP pilot; cat-modeling via MIS acquisition). Score reflects a strong-but-contested moat.

### 3. Rebound catalyst
- FY2026 OCC growth guide **5.8–6.3% reaffirmed** at Q2; margin-accretive AccuLynx close (expected EPS accretion was targeted for late 2026 pre-dispute); continued buybacks ($200M in Q2; $1.9B repurchased in H1 2026 at avg $186.32 — above today's price, signaling management's view of value). Insurance cycle: any hard-market re-acceleration or severe weather season lifts transactional revenue. Next earnings ~late Oct 2026.

### 4. Balance sheet
- **Total debt $4.47B** at Jun 30, 2026 (LT $4.22B, up from $3.23B at YE2025); net interest $52.8M/quarter (+49% YoY); leverage at high end of 2–3x target range, intended delever toward middle by YE2026 (Q2 2025 call). Negative equity −$1.2B from buybacks. Offset: **FCF ~$298M/quarter**, 41.7% FCF margin, Q2 operating cash flow +49.7%. Distress: no; but elevated leverage constrains flexibility if growth stalls.

### 5. Valuation
- At $163.88 → **~21.7x forward 12-month earnings** (Zacks, Aug 2026), vs own **5-yr median 35.6x** (range 19.6–43.5) — trading near the bottom of its historical range, yet still above the Zacks sub-industry avg 18.8x. Analyst mean target **$226.71** (15 analysts, Moderate Buy; MarketBeat, Sep 2026) → **~+38% upside**; Wells Fargo $260 (Overweight), Barclays $275→$225 (Overweight). Simply Wall St DCF (Sep 2026): ~$291 fair value on cash generation. The multiple is derated *because of the dip* — reasonable if 5–6% OCC growth holds, expensive if AI/internalization compresses pricing power.

### 6. Category position
- Still the #1 data franchise for P&C underwriting/claims with unmatched statistical depth; the dip is a growth-slowdown rerating, not share loss. Threats are substitution (AI/insurtech), not a lost customer base.

### 7. Value-trap risk
- The bear case is real: premium-linked pricing model decays as P&C premiums normalize; carriers internalize analytics with their own AI; $4.5B debt + negative equity makes any growth stall a leverage trap; 21.7x is still a growth multiple for a company growing 5–6% organically with flat net income (−9.8% GAAP net income in Q2 2026). If AI compresses the data moat, the stock never regains the 35x multiple.

### 8. Rebound score: 6.0/10 — Verdict: Defensive subscription compounder trading near its 10-year valuation floor with real cash flow; the 5–6% growth question is cyclical and likely transitory, but elevated debt and the AI-moot overhang cap the upside multiple.

## ADBE — Adobe Inc. (Creative/Document/Experience Cloud)

**Price snapshot (Finnhub, ~Oct 3, 2026):** $237.69; 52-week $190.12–$363.70 → **−35% off high**; YTD −32.1%.

### 1. Why it's down — Verdict: TRANSITORY SENTIMENT DERATING, with a structural AI question to disprove
- The core issue is the **"AI loser" narrative** vs. the numbers. Fiscal Q3 2026 (Sep 10, 2026): record revenue $6.76B (+13%), non-GAAP EPS $6.13 — both beats — and FY26 guide raised to $26.58–$26.63B revenue / $24.45–$24.50 adj. EPS (~17% growth). Yet the stock fell ~2–19% across the month because: **net new ARR down ~38% YoY** and **cRPO growth slowed to 9%** (RPO $22.16B +8.4% — first single-digit since early FY23, noted by Griffin Securities on the call); investors fear the **freemium AI push** (100M+ creative-freemium MAU, >1B total MAU) is trading near-term monetization for uncertain conversion (tickeron/fool, Sep 2026).
- **CEO transition**: Shantanu Narayen stepped down mid-2026; Anil Chakravarthy (internal president) named CEO effective **Dec 1, 2026** — D.A. Davidson's Gil Luria (CNBC, Sep 11, 2026) called the internal hire after a months-long search a signal the board couldn't attract outside talent. The stock is "down more than 25% YTD amidst AI disruption fears" and pays no dividend to reward holders for waiting.
- Genuine AI threat: management estimates the **traditional stock-imagery business (~$450M) is declining faster than expected**, directly cannibalized by generative AI (Tipranks, Apr 2026) — the first visible AI bite.

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — **6/10**
- Moat = **entrenched creative workflows + file-format lock-in (PSD/PDF) + cross-app integration + enterprise contracts + Firefly's commercially-safe IP**; 97% subscription revenue, 88.7% gross margins, ROIC ~53%. AI-first ARR already >$650M (+150% YoY) — Adobe is monetizing AI, not just threatened by it. But this is the most AI-exposed name of the four: text-to-image/video models (OpenAI, Midjourney) and Canva attack the *economic control point* of creative work over 5–10 years (millionleaf.com, Jun 2026). Irreplaceability 6 — strong today, debatable tomorrow.

### 3. Rebound catalyst
- CEO transition resolves **Dec 1, 2026** (Narayen → Executive Chair; CFO still interim). Q4 FY26 earnings (~Dec 2026) is the first full test of freemium→paid conversion; FY2027 guidance sets the tone. **Topaz Labs acquisition** (closing Q4 FY26) adds AI imaging/video tech. Buyback does the heavy lifting: **9.5M shares repurchased in Q3** (share count −7% YoY), **$24.55B remaining** under the April 2026 $25B authorization — at single-digit P/E, that's a ~10%+/year share-count shrinker.

### 4. Balance sheet
- ~$4.4–5.6B cash vs ~$6.4–7.1B debt → **essentially net-debt-neutral** (net debt/EBITDA ~0.07; 35x interest coverage). **FCF ~$10.6B TTM** (40%+ margin); Q3 operating cash flow a record $2.52B (+15% YoY) funded buybacks AND $250M debt repayment. Altman Z 8.35. Fortress — zero distress/dilution risk.

### 5. Valuation
- At $237.69 vs FY26 guide adj. EPS $24.45–24.50 → **forward P/E ~9.7x**; vs FY27 estimates → **~8.6–9x** (techi.com, Oct 2026). vs own **5-yr average ~35x** (alcapital, Oct 2026) — the cheapest Adobe has been in a decade+. Peers: Salesforce ~14x, ServiceNow ~33x, Autodesk ~19x (same-period fwd). Analyst mean target **~$275–283** (tradestie $275.06, MarketBeat $282.68; D.A. Davidson Buy $250) → **+16–19% upside**. Genuinely cheap *because of the dip* — the market is pricing zero AI survival; even modest RPO/ARR stabilization forces a re-rate.

### 6. Category position
- Still the #1 creative suite with real pricing power (non-GAAP op margin ~47% in Q1 FY26); AI-first ARR +150% is share *defense* working. Dip is sentiment, not share loss — but the single-digit RPO print is the first objective crack in the contract base.

### 7. Value-trap risk
- The falling-knife case: freemium converts poorly and enterprise ARR decelerates to low-single-digits, Firefly fails to command a price premium, AI-native tools hollow out the professional tier, and the sub-10x multiple is *fair* because earnings growth is over. A CEO who is "more of the same" accelerates nothing. Cheap stocks in disrupted software can stay cheap for a decade.

### 8. Rebound score: 7.5/10 — Verdict: The cleanest risk-reward of the four — fortress cash flow, decade-low ~9x forward P/E, and an aggressive buyback eating 7%+ of the float, all at the price of having to disprove the AI-loser narrative; CEO resolution on Dec 1 and Q4 ARR conversion are the catalysts to watch.
