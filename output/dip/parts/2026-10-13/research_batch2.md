## HWM — Howmet Aerospace

**1. Why it's down — TEMPORARY (sentiment/vertical-integration scare), with a real long-tail watch item.**
Two discrete September shocks, not a demand break: (i) ~1 week before Sep 8, 2026 the stock fell 7.5% in a day after Elon Musk said SpaceX would cast turbine blades in-house at a new Texas foundry for AI-data-center gas turbines; (ii) on Sep 8, 2026 GE Aerospace agreed to pay $11.75bn for Consolidated Precision Products (CPP), a rival turbine-blade caster — HWM fell 10.7% that day, ~$11bn of market cap (Substack deep dive, ~Oct 7, 2026; corroborated by Bernstein Oct 7, 2026 note citing "a potential SpaceX entry and GE's planned acquisition of CPP"). Bernstein also noted GE continues to use suppliers including Howmet under long-term contracts, limiting immediate read-through. Meanwhile the business accelerated: Q2 2026 (reported Aug 6) revenue $2.55bn +24% YoY (+21% organic), EPS $1.33 +46%, EBITDA margin 32.1% (+340bp), guidance *raised* to FY26 revenue ~$10.05bn, EPS $5.27, FCF ~$1.9bn. Stock is −14.1% in 30 days / −18.6% in 90 days but still +~8% YTD (Simply Wall St, ~Oct 8). Wells Fargo cut its target $315→$255 (Sep 30) on valuation, not on lost orders. **Verdict: transitory** — investors priced a future insourcing threat into a name whose customers are publicly asking for *more* 2027–28 output than it can make; no contract loss has been reported.

**2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 8/10.**
Market leader in single-crystal / directionally-solidified superalloy airfoil castings, forgings and fasteners; parts are flight-qualified over years, sole-/dual-sourced on LEAP, GEnx and GTF engines, and capacity is physically scarce — the CPP price (reported ~26x earnings) and SpaceX choosing to *build* a foundry rather than wait in Howmet's queue are market evidence of that scarcity. Forged Wheels adds ~80% North America / ~90% Europe share in aluminium truck wheels. AI cannot route around this: it is atoms, metallurgy and FAA/OEM qualification, not software. Concrete threat is not AI but *customer vertical integration* (GE-CPP, SpaceX foundry) eroding merchant share over 3–7 years, plus PCC (Berkshire) as the other scaled caster.

**3. Rebound catalyst.**
Q3 earnings Oct 29, 2026, with first 2027 revenue guidance promised on the November call ("2027 revenue expected to increase" — Q2 call, Aug 6); capacity ramps in engine products / industrial gas turbines backed by customer agreements through 2026–27; IGT demand from AI-data-center power is a direct volume tailwind (the same force behind the SpaceX scare). A clean Q3 + 2027 guide-up and any statement that GE LTAs are intact likely re-rates it toward the Bernstein $298 (cut from $328, Oct 7) / consensus ~$315 zone first.

**4. Balance-sheet survival — strong.**
Q2 2026: cash $564m, undrawn $1bn revolver (+$1bn CP programme, $450m drawn for the CAM acquisition), FCF $479m in Q2 alone (~$840m H1), net debt / trailing EBITDA 1.4x post-CAM with management guiding back to ~1.0x by year-end; $186m yen term loan repaid in Q2. No survival question.

**5. Margin of safety / valuation — thin on multiple, real on growth.**
Brief fwd P/E 34.0 is still a large premium to aerospace peers (RTX ~35x *trailing*, peer median forward well below HWM in most compilations; a late-2025 analysis put HWM trailing P/E ~54 vs its own 5-yr average ~44.8 and industry ~36.5). Note source dispersion: Finnhub trailing P/E 48.0 at $220.81 (Oct 13). So the dip has taken the *froth* off, not made it cheap — safety comes from 20%+ organic growth and 46% EPS growth, not from a low multiple. Target $332.25 = +50.5%.

**6. Category position.**
Still #1 in engine airfoils with pricing power intact — Q2 incremental EBITDA flow-through 46%, spares demand strong on legacy and next-gen engines, customers (GE ~14% of H1 sales, RTX ~10%) requesting more capacity. No evidence of share loss *yet*; CPP under GE is a 2027+ share question, not a 2026 fact.

**7. Value-trap risk (bear case).**
If GE progressively insources castings to CPP and SpaceX/other IGT buyers replicate the foundry model, Howmet's highest-margin growth engine de-rates permanently while the stock still carries a growth multiple — a double hit. Customer concentration (two customers ~24% of sales), commercial-aero cycle, and a $255 Wells Fargo target show the Street is not unanimous. At 34x forward, one guidance miss hurts badly.

**8. Rebound score: 7/10 — Best fundamentals in the batch, worst entry multiple: a sentiment scare on a scarce, still-accelerating franchise, but you are paying up for the rebound.**

## CPRT — Copart

**1. Why it's down — MOSTLY TEMPORARY volume/margin trough, with one secular undertow.**
Growth stalled: Q2 FY26 revenue −3.6% YoY on a 9.3% drop in global insurance unit volumes (softer insurance volumes, consumer underinsurance); FY26 (ended Jul 31, reported Sep 10, 2026) revenue $4.67bn, only +0.4% YoY, net income $1.48bn −4.4%. Q4 FY26: revenue $1.15bn +2.4% but EPS $0.35 vs $0.38 consensus (−7.9% miss), net margin 28.5% vs 35.1% a year earlier — facility/G&A cost growth outran pricing (US segment revenue +0.4%, operating income down; International +11.6% carried growth). HSBC downgraded to Hold, target $46→$36 (Sep 17, 2026). CEO share sales (July 2026) added sentiment drag. Offsetting: US insurance ASPs +9% ex-catastrophe in Q2 — pricing power is not the problem, *volume* is. **Verdict: temporary/cyclical on volumes and costs, permanent-watch on the secular side** — ADAS / crash-avoidance and higher repair costs vs total-loss frequency determine the long-run unit pool; the market is now pricing that in.

**2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 9/10.**
Scarce, zoned salvage-yard land near metros that cannot be replicated with capital alone; single-network insurer contracts (insurers route all write-offs to one network); bidder-liquidity network effect — more volume → more bidders → higher ASPs → next contract (Insider Monkey moat analysis, Oct 11, 2026: 32.0% operating margin, ROA >10%, almost no borrowing). AI helps Copart (imaging, pricing, Title Express) rather than replacing a physical yard + licence + liquidity network. Concrete threat: IAA/RB Global competition on fees/service, and a structurally shrinking accident/total-loss pool if ADAS and autonomy cut crash frequency.

**3. Rebound catalyst.**
Next print Nov 19, 2026 (Q1 FY27); any normalisation in insurance unit volumes or a catastrophe season lifts the highest-margin units; cost leverage after the FY26 investment year (25 dedicated wholesale facilities, Title Express, long-haul delivery); closing of the announced ACV Auctions acquisition ($10.50/share, expected by end of calendar 2026) expands dealer/wholesale TAM; $1.63bn of buybacks already executed in FY26 at falling prices mechanically lifts EPS. No dated volume-recovery guidance was found — "not found" for a management volume guide.

**4. Balance-sheet survival — fortress.**
Jul 31, 2026: cash & equivalents $1.91bn + held-to-maturity securities $2.58bn = ~$4.5bn; CFO on the Q4 call: ~$5.7bn total liquidity including a $1.25bn undrawn revolver, *no debt outstanding*; total liabilities only $0.91bn vs equity $9.10bn. FY26 operating cash flow $1.60bn, capex $0.34bn → FCF ~$1.27bn. Survival is not a variable here.

**5. Margin of safety / valuation — the deepest de-rating in the batch.**
Fwd P/E 15.1 (brief) / trailing 17.2 (Finnhub, Oct 13) vs a 5-yr average forward P/E of 31.09 (Dec 2025 analysis, when it was already down to 23.2x) and ~40x trailing at the 2025 peak — the multiple has more than halved. Target $39.00 = +47.3%; note targets are being cut toward the price (HSBC $36, Sep 17), and one recent mean-target print was $47.22 (older) — use $39 as the conservative brief anchor.

**6. Category position.**
Still the clear #1 salvage network with pricing power (+9% US insurance ASPs ex-cat) — this is a volume drought in its category, not observed share loss to IAA; US service revenue was essentially flat (−0.9% in Q4 service rev) while it invests in adjacent wholesale/dealer volume via ACV.

**7. Value-trap risk (bear case).**
The classic trap setup is present: a former 30–40x compounder whose growth algorithm (total-loss frequency rising with repair costs) may have peaked. If insurers repair more / total less, if ADAS cuts accidents, and if FY26 margin compression (net margin −6.6pp in Q4) is cost-structure reset rather than investment, then 15x is fair, not cheap, and EPS can flatline for years. ACV integration and insider selling add noise.

**8. Earnings-quality addendum — RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN flag.**
Primary filing (FY26 release, Sep 10, 2026): accounts receivable, net $809.2m at Jul 31, 2026 vs $762.8m a year earlier = **+6.1% while revenue grew +0.4%** — receivables did outrun revenue, so the flag is factually triggered, and the allowance for credit losses rose $12.9m→$18.6m (+43%) with FY credit-loss expense $5.6m vs $0.35m in FY25. Benign/business-model explanation is substantial, however: (a) mix shift toward *vehicle sales* (where Copart takes title and sells as principal — Q4 vehicle sales $182.9m vs $168.9m, International +11.6%) and toward international buyers, who settle slower than US insurers; (b) revenue is recognised on auction/service completion while insurer and member settlement lags period-end volume timing; (c) crucially, cash did *not* lag earnings for the year — FY26 operating cash flow $1.604bn *exceeded* net income $1.480bn (108% conversion), and the FY cash-flow statement shows only an $80.6m AR drag. AR days rose only ~60→~63 days. **One-line verdict: Flag real but benign-leaning — a ~3-day DSO / mix effect with rising allowance to watch, NOT the earnings-ahead-of-cash value-trap signature, because OCF still covers net income.**

**Rebound score: 6/10 — Cheapest and safest balance sheet in the batch, but the catalyst is 'volumes come back' with no date on it; a quality franchise in a volume drought, not yet a proven trap.**
