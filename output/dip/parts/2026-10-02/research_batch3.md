## 2) Equifax (EFX) — credit-bureau oligopolist under political fire

The same scrutiny already cut the stock −6.27% in a day in Aug/early-Sep 2026. (b) **Mortgage-origination drought**: elevated rates since 2022 depressed EFX's mortgage unit volume for years; revenue now only recovering. Current ~$139.73 vs 52w high $258.40 (Finnhub, Oct 2026); −45% over 1 year, −35.6% YTD. **Verdict: partly transitory (mortgage cycle), partly structural risk (political margin compression)** — the regulatory leg could be permanent if bi-merge becomes policy.

AI cannot "route around" a credit bureau the way it can a services company — the threat is the state, not substitutes. **Irreplaceability score: 8/10.**

**3. ## ADDITIONAL: BRO receivables flag (RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN) — explained

**One-line verdict: benign business-model artifact, not a value-trap signature — but a watch item while organic growth is negative.**

The mechanics: as an insurance intermediary, BRO collects premiums from insureds and remits them (net of commission) to carriers, so "premiums receivable from insureds" sit on the asset side with an almost-matching "premiums payable to insurance companies" on the liability side (fiduciary assets/liabilities; since Jan 1 2025 reported separately per the Q1 2025 10-Q). Receivables therefore grow with *premium volume flowing through*, not with revenue recognized — and the $9.8B Accession book added a large acquired receivables balance at close (Aug 2025). In the Q2 2026 10-Q cash-flow statement, "Commissions, fees and other receivables" grew $143M (a use of cash) vs $139M a year earlier — stable, not accelerating — and net fiduciary cash flows are presented in financing, matched by the carrier payables. So the flag is mostly timing + the acquired book, matched by liabilities. **The residual caution:** with organic revenue declining while total receivables inflate via acquisitions, the flag could mask genuine collection deterioration — check Q3 10-Q for any bad-debt provision movement before upgrading this to clean.
