## FIX — Comfort Systems USA

### FIX-1. Backlog: record $14.06bn (+73.1% YoY, +12.9% sequentially) — **CONFIRMED** (rounding note)

- Note: the dossier's +73.1% vs the release recap's +73.2% is a 0.1pp rounding difference on $14.06bn / $8.12bn (= +73.15%). Immaterial; does not affect the thesis.

### FIX-3. Valuation: forward P/E ~23.9x (screen) — **CONFIRMED as one provider's figure, but MISLEADING as a single number; the skeptics' 24–30x range is also corroborated, and the "low-teens history" framing is CONTRADICTED**

- The 23.9x print itself exists live: TickerNerd (market figures as at 17 Oct 2026) — "Trailing P/E: 35.4×; forward P/E: 23.9×"; StockAlert (Oct 2026) — Forward P/E 23.91. At the 16 Oct close of $1,439.07 that implies ~$60 of forward EPS — i.e. a next-fiscal-year (FY27) denominator (DefenseWorld, 15 Oct: analysts expect $48.15 EPS for the current fiscal year and $59.31 for the next; $1,439.07 / $59.31 ≈ 24.3x).
- On nearer-term / 12-month-forward denominators the live multiple is materially higher, exactly as the skeptics say: MarketBeat (updated 16 Oct 2026) — Forward P/E 29.84–29.87, trailing 35.4–36.4; Zacks (Oct 2026 valuation piece) — "forward 12-month P/E ratio of 30.57"; Yahoo Finance via ad-hoc-news (12 Oct 2026) — forward P/E 30.23.
- History — the dossier's "traded at low-teens multiples for most of its history" does **not** hold: Yahoo (12 Oct 2026) puts the five-year median forward P/E at 23.45; an earlier Zacks valuation piece put the five-year median at 21.34 with a historical range of 13.32x–35.29x; Wisesheets puts the 10-year mean TTM P/E at 21.78 (median 20.96, quarterly low 9.27 in Mar 2022). Low-teens was the *bottom* of the range, not the norm; the median is low-20s. A separate analysis notes top-tier contractors historically trade at 15–20x forward.
- Implication: FIX is **not** cheap versus its own history on any denominator. At 23.9x (FY27) it is roughly *at* its 5-year median; at ~30x (12-month forward) it is at a clear premium to that median. The dossier itself concedes this ("margin of safety comes from backlog coverage… not from a historically cheap multiple"), so the investment case survives — but any ballot language implying a historically cheap multiple should be struck. **Flagged: weakens the margin-of-safety leg for FIX, does not break the backlog/balance-sheet case (see Overall Verdict).**

### FIX-4. Moat / balance sheet: cash $1.85bn vs total debt $54.1m — **CONFIRMED**

- Zacks (Q2 recap): "With $1.85 billion of cash and just $54.1 million of debt, FIX has substantial financial flexibility"; also "Second-quarter 2026 operating cash flow reached $1.14 billion, while free cash flow was nearly $1 billion" (FCF $999.3m per Quartr).

Long-term debt declined to $53.8 million from $139.1 million at Dec. 31, 2025." The $54.1m total = $53.8m long-term + ~$0.3m current. Net cash >$1.8bn stands.

**What failed / was qualified (2 sub-claims):**
- **FIX-3 — QUALIFIED / PARTIALLY CONTRADICTED:** the 23.9x screen figure is real on an FY27 denominator (TickerNerd, 17 Oct), but 12-month-forward providers put FIX at 29.8–30.6x (MarketBeat 16 Oct; Zacks; Yahoo), and its 5-year median is ~21–23x, not "low-teens for most of its history." FIX is at-to-above its historical multiple, not below it.
- **SCHW-2 — PARTIALLY CONTRADICTED:** the 13.4x normalized / 18.1x five-year-average comparison is CONFIRMED verbatim (TIKR, 16 Oct), but the 11.5x screen figure could not be verified against any live source (live forward: ~13.4–14.2x) and overstates the discount.

**Would any failure change the ranking (FIX #1, IDXX #2, SCHW #3 by Borda)? — No. No ranking-changing failure found.**

- FIX stays #1 on the facts that actually carry its ballot case — a verified record $14.06bn backlog, a verified net-cash balance sheet ($1.85bn cash vs $54.1m debt), and the nearest verified catalyst (22 Oct, after close). FIX-3 only removes a *supporting* cheapness argument the dossier itself had already disclaimed; it does not touch backlog, cash, or catalyst. **Marked explicitly: FIX-3 is the closest call — if a ballot's #1 vote for FIX rested primarily on "23.9x = historically cheap," that ballot's stated rationale (not its rank on the verified facts) is undermined, because on a 12-month-forward basis FIX is ~30x, a premium to its own median.**
- IDXX at #2 is untouched — all three of its claims verified against primary releases, including the exact visit-softness cause for the drawdown.
- SCHW at #3 is, if anything, *strengthened* on quality (record print verified; receivables flag verified benign) and only modestly weakened on valuation (13.4x vs 18.1x average is still a real ~26% discount — just not the ~36% the 11.5x screen implied). That is not enough, on verified numbers alone, to move SCHW above IDXX or FIX, nor to drop it below #3: the corrected multiple still supports its ballot position.

**Net: the dossier's top-3 ordering (FIX #1, IDXX #2, SCHW #3) survives independent verification. The two valuation qualifications (FIX-3, SCHW-2 screen leg) should be corrected in the final memo's wording, but neither alters the outcome.**
