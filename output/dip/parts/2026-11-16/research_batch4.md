## ODFL — Old Dominion Freight Line (LTL trucking)

1. **Why it's down — TRANSITORY (freight-cycle trough, pricing intact).**
   Q3 2026 (Oct 28, 2026, company release): revenue $1.59B +13.1% YoY,
   diluted EPS $1.65 +28.9% beating $1.61 consensus; LTL revenue per
   hundredweight +13.3% (ex-fuel +5.1% to $30.25) offsetting a 0.1% dip in
   LTL tons/day (shipments/day -2.0%, weight/shipment +1.9%); operating
   ratio improved to 71.5% from 74.3%; operating cash flow $469.5M in Q3 /
   $1.1B in 9M 2026. Demand improved during the quarter — tons/day turned
   positive YoY in September. Shares pulled back on the print anyway; stock
   well below the ~$230 level of late July as investors weigh pricing
   strength against uneven freight demand and cost inflation (Tickeron).
   Q4 guide: operating ratio ~73.5%; capex ~$380M for 2026; general rate
   increase took effect in October. Verdict: **temporary** — a cyclical
   trough with pricing power intact and volumes inflecting in September;
   the classic LTL-cycle dip.
2. **Moat / AI-irreplaceability: 8/10.** Best-in-class LTL network: 99%
   on-time service, 0.1% claims ratio, union-free single integrated
   network, disciplined yield management, 45–50% incremental margins on
   revenue growth. AI/automation is an efficiency tool for trucking, not a
   substitute for the physical network. No earnings-quality flags.
3. **Rebound catalyst:** Q4 print Feb 3, 2027 (screen); freight-cycle turn
   (tonnage inflecting); pricing (Oct general rate increase) flowing through;
   operating leverage as volumes return.
4. **Balance sheet:** net debt/EBITDA -0.24x (net cash, screen); op margin
   26.2%; ROE 26.0%; strong cash generation.
5. **Valuation:** fwd PE 25.8 vs trailing 31.3 — the most expensive
   non-HWM name in the field relative to growth (rev +4.5% TTM); +22.3%
   analyst upside. The multiple assumes the cycle turns; it is not cheap
   in absolute terms.
6. **Category position:** #1 premium LTL carrier; share not being lost —
   volumes are a market-wide freight issue, not an ODFL issue.
7. **Value-trap risk:** freight recession deepens/lengthens; pricing
   discipline breaks under competition; healthcare/fringe-benefit cost
   inflation (flagged by management) compresses the operating ratio.
8. **Rebound score: 6/10.** Verdict: highest-quality cyclical in the field
   with volumes inflecting, but fwd PE 25.8 on 4.5% revenue growth limits
   the margin of safety — the rebound is a slow cycle turn, not a snap-back.

## DECK — Deckers Brands (HOKA / UGG)

1. **Why it's down — MIXED: real growth deceleration, priced as if it ends.**
   Fiscal Q2 2027 (reported Oct 22, 2026): revenue $1.481B +3.5% YoY
   (missed $1.51B consensus), GAAP EPS $2.31 (+27%, beat $1.80 by 28.7%);
   HOKA +6.8%, UGG +3.5%, comparable DTC sales -6.4%. Full-year FY2027
   revenue guidance cut to $5.700–5.725B (from $5.86–5.91B, -2.9%
   midpoint); HOKA growth cut to high single digits (from low double
   digits); sales growth now ~4% vs 7.5% promised in July. EPS guidance
   raised to $7.80–7.95, but that includes ~$0.52/share of IEEPA tariff
   refunds + interest — ex-refund underlying EPS ~$7.28–7.43, slightly
   below the prior forecast. Shares fell ~5.4% on Oct 23 to ~$80.72 and
   are down 62% from the $217 peak of Jan 2025; trailing P/E compressed
   from 38x at the peak to ~11.8x (Credit Investing, Oct 2026). Drivers:
   slowing HOKA growth (24% FY25 → 16% FY26 → ~7% in the June quarter),
   promotional US footwear market, European shipping delays.
   Verdict: **mixed** — the deceleration is real and management-cut, but
   the multiple (11x) now prices in no growth at all while the business
   still grows mid-single digits with ~24% EBITDA margins.
2. **Moat / AI-irreplaceability: 6/10.** Two strong brands riding long-term
   shifts (running boom: US running footwear +13% in H1 2026 vs +1% for
   footwear overall; UGG as year-round casual). Brand + distribution moat;
   AI largely irrelevant to footwear. Risk: fashion cycles — HOKA's
   hypergrowth phase is over; franchise updates (Bondi 10, Mach Pro,
   Clifton Pro) got mixed feedback. Earnings-quality flag:
   RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN — not found explained in research; wholesale
   channel mix is a plausible cause, unverified.
3. **Rebound catalyst:** Q3 FY2027 print Jan 28, 2027 (screen) — the
   December quarter is UGG's biggest; international (APAC/EMEA) growth;
   new franchise launches; $1B buyback planned this year (~9% of market
   value); buyback authorization worth ~42% of market value per Credit
   Investing.
4. **Balance sheet:** net debt/EBITDA -0.48x (screen); ~$1.6B net cash,
   no borrowings (Credit Investing); op margin 23.6%; ROE 44.3%; converts
   ~90% of earnings to cash.
5. **Valuation:** fwd PE 10.6 vs trailing 11.2 — joint-cheapest in the field
   with ADBE; +11.0% analyst upside (lowest in the field — the street is
   skeptical). Credit Investing base case $123 vs ~$83 (their model);
   downside case $88, still above the then-price.
6. **Category position:** HOKA and UGG are still growing, just slower;
   share not demonstrably lost — the category (running) is growing.
7. **Value-trap risk:** HOKA growth keeps fading toward zero/negative;
   US promotional environment deepens; fashion turns away from maximal
   cushion; tariffs.
8. **Rebound score: 6/10.** Verdict: the cheapest consumer name on a
   cash-rich balance sheet, but the growth deceleration is management-
   confirmed and the street's upside is the lowest in the field — a
   value case, not a momentum rebound.
