- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** Howmet makes forged engine components,
  fasteners and turbine parts that no model and no substitute can replace:
  certification lock-in, process technology and sole-source positions on
  engines mean an AI cannot make a turbine blade and a new entrant cannot
  certify one quickly. The business is compounding — Q3 revenue +29%,
  EPS +54%, a record engine-spare backlog, gas turbines +45% — and the
  dip is only -24.5%, a give-back of a run rather than a verdict on the
  franchise. The multiple is rich (33.2x forward), but this lens buys the
  moat first: the moat here is physical, certified and irreplaceable in a
  way no software name in the field can claim.
- **Why the dip is temporary (not permanent):** Nothing about the business
  deteriorated — Q3 was a beat-and-raise with 2027 revenue guided to ~$11.5B
  (+12%). The drawdown is sector rotation and profit-taking, not a moat
  breach; there is no moat breach to find.
- **Moat / AI-irreplaceability:** Low disruption risk — the lowest in the
  field. Forged aerospace components are protected by certification, process
  tech and sole-source positions; AI is irrelevant to the product and a
  tailwind to demand (aircraft build rates).
- **Rebound catalyst & timing:** Q4 print Feb 18, 2027 (guide EPS $1.49–1.51
  vs $1.42 consensus); Boeing 737 MAX recovery and Airbus A320/A350 build
  rates rising through 2027; F-35 spares demand.
- **12-18 month return scenario:** Base ~$270–300 (+22–36% from $220.82)
  on 2027 guidance delivery and modest multiple compression offset by
  earnings growth; bull ~$330+ (+50%) if build rates accelerate and the
  market pays up for the moat.
- **Top risk (value-trap case):** This is not a value trap — it is a
  valuation trap: at 33.2x forward earnings the market already pays for the
  moat, so an aerospace cycle turn or a Boeing production stall would hurt
  the multiple as well as earnings.
