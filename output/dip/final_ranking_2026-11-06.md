## Per-name thesis

**1. ANET — Arista Networks.** The dip is a sentiment story, not an operating one: a 3 Nov print of +58.5% revenue (fastest since IPO), a raised Q4 guide and a first-ever FY2027 outlook of about \$18.5bn, followed by a 10.8% fall two days later on politics — with \$14.36bn cash, zero debt and a 9/10 moat (EOS + hyperscaler qualification lock-in) untouched. The catalyst is the Feb 2027 Q4 print confirming the FY2027 guide; the value-trap case is a real hyperscaler capex pause or Nvidia Spectrum-X capping Arista's share in AI fabrics. The only name in every lens's top 3, and the single pick (see final_pick_2026-11-06.md).

**2. APH — Amphenol.** The steadiest AI-levered compounder here: record sales \$10.23bn (+65% YoY, +37% organic), record orders \$12.2bn (book-to-bill 1.19x), adjusted operating margin 29.7%, a doubled \$4bn buyback and a 20% dividend raise, at about 20x forward (PEG about 0.94–1.00) — the cheapest forward multiple in the field relative to growth. The moat is physical: copper/optical/power interconnects designed into server architectures years ahead, which AI cannot route around, only demand more of. Catalyst: the Q4 print (guide \$10.3–10.5bn) confirming the order book converts. The trap case is the order book stalling if hyperscaler builds pause — but the book, not hope, de-risks the next two quarters.

**3. VRT — Vertiv.** The strongest fundamentals in the field — organic orders more than doubled YoY, guidance raised about \$650m after a Q3 print of +61% revenue, deferred revenue +127% to \$4.12bn (customers prepaying to secure capacity), net cash — trading at a 53% discount to its 52-week high because the market fears its customers' financing, not its products. Power and cooling cannot be routed around by AI; the NVIDIA reference-design lock-in is the moat (8/10). The trap door is financing: if project finance stays expensive, the 12–18-month backlog converts slower, and at about 18x forward a single growth disappointment re-rates it. The INVENTORY_BUILD flag is mostly benign (build ahead of an order-doubling backlog) but worth monitoring into Q4. Note from verification: "net cash" is true of the 30 Sep balance sheet, but the \$1.45bn UtilityInnovation acquisition closed 27 Oct 2026, so the current net-cash position is materially smaller pending the December balance sheet.

**4. HWM — Howmet Aerospace.** Record Q3 (revenue +29%, adjusted EPS +54%, EBITDA margin 32.5%, record quarterly FCF \$562m), raised FY2026 guidance and a new \$5bn buyback — sold off on a narrowly scoped scare (SpaceX casting its own industrial gas-turbine blades; GE's acquisition of Consolidated Precision Products) that concerns future in-sourcing of one product line, not the FAA/EASA-certified aero-engine aftermarket where single-source approvals make switching near-impossible. Moat 9/10, the joint-strongest in the field. But at about 30x forward the rebound is a multiple-stabilisation story, not a value re-rating, and the in-sourcing overhang caps it until volumes disprove it.

**5. FIX — Comfort Systems USA.** A record backlog (\$17.81bn, +90% YoY), \$2.1bn net cash, and a forward multiple cut by a third, sold off on sentiment and a principled refusal to chase weak AI-lab credit on loose terms. The rebound needs margin stabilisation (gross margin faded to 24.8%) and AI-capex sentiment, not just a cheap multiple. Concentration is the watch item: technology customers are 59–60.8% of revenue. The RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN flag is benign (billed AR +49% tracks +54% revenue growth; Q3 operating cash flow \$1.01bn).

## Risk lens & leverage-safety note

Sector concentration is the list's defining risk: the top 3 (ANET, APH, VRT) plus FIX and MU are all AI-capex proxies — a single hyperscaler capex pause hits the whole top of the ranking at once, which is exactly the bear case in every top-3 scenario table. The steadier compounders (SCHW, CAT, BKNG) sit at the bottom of the ranking for that reason: they diversify the list but offer the slowest re-rates. The deepest drawdowns (VRT -53%, FIX -53%, APP -51%, MU -46%) are also the names most likely to keep falling if the dip deepens — VRT and FIX carry the financing/concentration version of that risk, MU the cycle version. A beaten-down name can keep falling: catching a knife with margin is brutal, and any position in this list should be sized for the possibility the dip deepens before it turns. This is risk education, not a leverage recommendation and not a specific position size.
