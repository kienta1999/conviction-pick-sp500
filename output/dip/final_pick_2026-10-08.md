## One-paragraph thesis

S&P Global is the cleanest "the price broke, the business didn't" name on this 15-name shortlist. The stock is down 22.8% from its 52-week high on a February guidance reset, the July Mobility spin-off recast, and AI-terminal sentiment — while the franchise printed record Ratings and Indices revenue with 23% adjusted-EPS growth in Q2. The near-duopoly in credit ratings (~80% combined with Moody's, NRSRO-regulated) plus the S&P 500 benchmark (~$6.35T ETF AUM) is regulation- and contract-embedded: no AI model can issue a rating that moves a bond price or replace a benchmark the ETF industry is contractually locked to. With a >$7B 2026 buyback (>5% of the float), a raised Indices organic guide (12–14%), and an $11T corporate maturity wall ahead, the dip is a sentiment derate on a tollbooth whose tolls are still rising.

Meanwhile the operating numbers never broke: Q2 2026 delivered **record Ratings and Indices revenue and 23% adjusted-EPS growth**. A permanently impaired business does not print record volumes at ~48% operating margins. Every batch researcher and the value-trap skeptic (Agent D) independently cleared it as price, not impairment.
