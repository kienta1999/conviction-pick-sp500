# Dip conviction pick — 2026-10-14

## THE PICK
**SYK — Stryker Corporation**, Health Care / Health Care Equipment. Market cap ~\$106.4B. Price at pick: **\$277.33** (2026-10-13 screen).

## One-paragraph thesis
Stryker is a category-dominant medtech franchise — Mako robotic systems, implants and surgical equipment embedded in operating rooms and surgeon muscle memory — trading at a decade-low forward multiple because one acquired product line (Inari) had a supply failure, not because its business weakened. Verified Q2 evidence: net sales \$6.59B (+9.4%), organic +9.0%, adjusted EPS \$3.69 (+17.9%), adjusted operating margin 27.4% (+170bp) — and the FY26 guide was narrowed with both low ends raised, not cut. Three of four lenses put SYK first or second, the falling-knife skeptic ranked it #2, and it won the Borda count by the widest margin of this run (38 of a possible 40). Oct 29 is a near, binary-ish catalyst on the exact problem that caused the fall.

## Why the dip is temporary, not permanent
The verified cause chain is operational: the Mar 11, 2026 cyber incident disrupted systems critical to Inari integration/manufacturing/shipping; Q2 disclosed a significant Peripheral Vascular backorder at one Inari plant (US Vascular organic sales -6.7% in Q2); then the Sep 8 Wells Fargo conference admission that backorders run into Q4 contradicted July's assurance and cost the stock -8.8% in a day plus a shareholder investigation. None of that is demand: Q2 demand, margins and guidance all held (see verification). A supply failure at one plant of an acquired line, layered on a disclosure-credibility hit, is a price event on an intact franchise — the textbook temporary dip.

## Why it's the category leader / why the moat is intact
Mako is a closed platform: it runs only Stryker implants (Triathlon/hip families), so every capital placement converts into a recurring consumable-implant stream — a record Q2 for Mako installations, utilisation rising, >2.5M procedures globally, systems in 47 countries (all verified directionally; no source quantifies switching costs in dollars, so that figure stays an inference). Surgeon training, simulation and proctored-case requirements are a real learning-curve cost a hospital must re-incur to switch. **AI cannot disrupt it** — AI assists surgery; it does not substitute for the implant, the robot, or the rep in the room. There is no software layer here for a model to commoditize. Irreplaceability 8/10.
