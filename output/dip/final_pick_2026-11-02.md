## One-paragraph thesis

KLAC is the cleanest great-business-on-sale name in this panel: a process-control near-monopoly for leading-edge fabs, down 48.6% from its 52-week high not on any lost contract, product failure, or guidance cut, but on a sentiment-driven multiple compression after a strong run — while the business itself printed a record quarter into the selloff. Q1 FY2027 revenue of \$4.14bn (+29% YoY) beat consensus, the December-quarter guide was raised to \$4.7bn, and management raised its calendar-2026 wafer-fab equipment outlook — the opposite of a demand break. At a verified premium multiple (27–31x forward, not the screen's unreproduced 22.6x), this is not a deep-value price; it is a compounder repriced by fear, with the re-rating driven by guide delivery rather than multiple hope alone.

## Why the dip is temporary, not permanent

The single most compelling evidence: the drawdown preceded the strong print. KLAC had already fallen about 21% over the month before its October 28 earnings (Motley Fool, 2026-10-29 transcript coverage), then reported record revenue, a 46.1% record operating margin, and a raised guide — and the selloff is described across sources as a broad AI-spending scare / sector-wide multiple compression (Tickeron, late Oct 2026; Perplexity KLAC timeline), with analyst trims explicitly reflecting "tempered multiples rather than deteriorating fundamentals." No demand break, lost customer, or cancelled fab programme was found in any source checked. Finnhub at the Oct 30 close: YTD +29.96%, 1-year +30.64%, 1-month -23.67% — a give-back of a strong run, not a breakdown.

## Why it's the category leader / why the moat is intact

KLA holds roughly 50–58% of the process-control market (Market Supply Chain research, 2026-07-01; Macroplane thesis), with the nearest broad competitor (Applied Materials' metrology unit) at an estimated 8–10% — "effectively a near-monopoly at the most advanced nodes (N5 / N3 / N2 / sub-2nm)." AI cannot substitute for metrology/inspection physics; if anything, more complex chips increase demand for inspection. AI-disruption risk: LOW. Caveats carried honestly: genuine niche competitors exist (Onto Innovation, Camtek in advanced packaging, Lasertec in EUV mask inspection), and no source retrieved quantifies patent coverage or switching costs, so that specific mechanism is unverified rather than proven. The "growing backlog" element of the catalyst is likewise unverified (no backlog figure independently confirmed), though record shipments plus a raised guide point the same way.
