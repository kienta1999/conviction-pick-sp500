## THE PICK

**KLAC — KLA Corporation** (Information Technology / Semiconductor Materials & Equipment), market cap ~\$165.3bn, price \$145.85 (Mon Nov 23, 2026 close).

## One-paragraph thesis

KLA is the closest thing to a monopoly in semiconductor equipment, and the dip is pure multiple compression on AI-capex fear, not a loss of position: fiscal Q1 2027 revenue was \$4.14bn (+28.9% YoY, third straight quarter of accelerating growth), operating margin 45.0% GAAP (46.1% non-GAAP, a record), and management expects to grow sequentially through 2027 — yet the stock is 51.5% off its 52-week high and 19.6% below its 200-day SMA. Its inspection franchise (>80% mask inspection, >60% brightfield, >50% darkfield) is protected by decades of proprietary defect data and switching costs no fab will pay, and AI makes the moat stronger, because rising chip complexity raises inspection intensity. At 20.5x forward earnings the market has repriced a monopoly as if it were a cyclical; the rebound is a re-rating back toward a quality multiple on a still-growing earnings base, with wafer-fab equipment spending above \$200bn in 2027 as the corroborated driver.

## Why the dip is temporary, not permanent

The single most compelling evidence: nothing in KLA's own outlook changed on the down days — Q1 beat, Dec-quarter guide \$4.7bn ± \$0.2bn, lead times averaging ~12 months with demand exceeding supply — while trailing net income actually rose to \$5.26bn from \$4.24bn a year earlier as the trailing multiple compressed from 48.4x at end-September to 34.4x by November 6. KLAC fell 13.3% on November 5 with the SOX index, on Nvidia-leadership anxiety, not on a KLA miss. The business did not break; the multiple did. The drawdown is a sector repricing of AI-capex fear, and the Nov 23 relief rally (KLAC +14.96% to \$145.85, from a \$125.72 intraday low on Nov 20) shows how fast that fear reverses when the narrative cracks.

## The dip depth

KLAC is 51.5% below its 52-week high (\$307.37, Jun 30, 2026) and 19.6% below its 200-day SMA, after a 12-month return of +33.0% — the stock round-tripped a first-half run and then some. The cause: a sector-wide AI-capex repricing in October–November 2026 (fears that hyperscaler AI spending would pause, Nvidia leadership turmoil), not a KLA-specific miss — Q1 FY2027 was a record quarter reported Oct 28, 2026.

## Why it's the category leader / why the moat is intact

KLA holds 53–80% shares across inspection categories (>80% mask inspection, >60% brightfield, >50% darkfield, >56% of the process-control market — market-research estimates, confirmed across independent secondary sources). The moat is data as much as hardware: trillions of data points and ML algorithms trained on every major fab, a >20,000-system installed base, and a services segment with 52 consecutive quarters of growth. **Why AI cannot disrupt it:** AI cannot substitute for physically inspecting wafers, and it actively increases inspection intensity per wafer as nodes shrink to 3nm/2nm/1nm and packaging gets more complex (hybrid bonding, backside power, advanced packaging). Switching costs are extreme — swapping out KLA's KLARITY software means losing 18+ months of process-baseline data. The slow-burn threats are domestic Chinese metrology suppliers scaling from a small base and High-NA EUV measurement complexity — neither is existential on a 12–18 month horizon. The honest weakness: at ~20.5x forward earnings KLAC is the least cheap name in the semi-equipment group (vs AMAT ~14.1x, LRCX ~17.7x), so the pick pays up for quality.

## The rebound catalyst & timing

WFE above \$200bn in 2027 — KLA's own forecast on the Q1 FY27 call (Oct 28, 2026), corroborated by Lam and by Bernstein at \$217bn — plus H2 FY2026 revenue ~25% above H1 as supply-chain capacity improves. The next hard checkpoint is Q2 FY2027 earnings on January 28, 2027, with gross margin guided back toward the 63–64% Investor Day target in mid-to-late 2027. The Nov 23 reinstatement of Jensen Huang as Nvidia CEO (NVDA +31.5% that day) removed the leadership anxiety that drove the November sell-off.
