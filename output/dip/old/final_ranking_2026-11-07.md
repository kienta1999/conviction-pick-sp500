## Per-name thesis

**1. ANET — Arista Networks.** The dip is a sentiment story, not an operating one: a 3 Nov print of +58.5% revenue (fastest since IPO), a raised Q4 guide and a first-ever FY2027 outlook of about \$18.5bn, followed by a 10.8% fall two days later on politics — with \$14.36bn cash, zero debt and a 9/10 moat (EOS + hyperscaler qualification lock-in) untouched, and Friday's +7.31% reclaiming the 200-day SMA. The catalyst is the Feb 2027 Q4 print confirming the FY2027 guide; the value-trap case is a real hyperscaler capex pause or Nvidia Spectrum-X capping Arista's share in AI fabrics — verification confirmed Spectrum-X took the #1 data-centre Ethernet revenue share in Q1–Q2 2026 (IDC), so the share question is documented, not hypothetical. The only name in every lens's top 3, and the single pick (see final_pick_2026-11-07.md).

**2. APH — Amphenol.** The steadiest AI-levered compounder here: record sales \$10.23bn (+65% YoY, +37% organic), record orders \$12.2bn (book-to-bill 1.19x), adjusted operating margin 29.7%, a doubled \$4bn buyback and a 20% dividend raise, at about 20x forward (PEG about 0.94–1.00) — the cheapest forward multiple in the field relative to growth. The moat is physical: copper/optical/power interconnects designed into server architectures years ahead, which AI cannot route around, only demand more of. Catalyst: the Q4 print (guide \$10.3–10.5bn) confirming the order book converts. The trap case is the order book stalling if hyperscaler builds pause — but the book, not hope, de-risks the next two quarters. Watch item: CEO Norwitt sold 799,576 shares on 2 Nov (no filing context found).

**3. ## Risk lens & leverage-safety note

Sector concentration is the list's main risk: 7 of the top 10 ride the AI-infrastructure buildout (ANET, APH, VRT, MU, HWM, FIX, CAT), so a single hyperscaler capex pause hits the list together — the diversification is across the stack (memory, switching, interconnects, power/cooling, contracting, aero, machinery), not across the demand driver. Drawdown reality: the top 3 are 19–53% off their highs, and a beaten-down name can keep falling — "catching a knife" with margin is brutal; size for the possibility the dip deepens before it turns. This is risk education, not a leverage recommendation and never a specific leverage multiple or position size.
