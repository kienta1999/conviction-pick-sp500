## Per-name thesis

**1. LRCX — Lam Research (\$209.76).** The dip is temporary: a record fiscal Q1 (revenue \$8.22bn, +54% YoY, record 40.2% operating margin, record \$3.24bn FCF) was sold off because cleanroom/component supply capped the guide — deferred shipments, not lost demand — and the board answered with a \$25bn buyback (~9% of shares) on Nov 5. The rebound catalyst is WFE above \$200bn in 2027 (Bernstein \$217bn) with deferred shipments landing in the March quarter; the etch moat (#1, 45–50% share, served-market share up ~4pts) is intact and AI-irreplaceable in the same way KLAC's is — nobody etches 3nm wafers without Lam. The honest caveat: it is still a WFE cycle call, and the multiple (17.1x forward vs 24.4x 5-year average) only re-rates if the cycle confirms.

**2. KLAC — KLA Corporation (\$145.85).** The dip is temporary: Q1 FY2027 was a record (\$4.14bn, +28.9% YoY, 45.0% GAAP / 46.1% non-GAAP operating margin) with ~12-month lead times and demand exceeding supply, while the stock halved on sector sentiment — the multiple compressed from 48.4x trailing to 34.4x as trailing net income rose. The catalyst is WFE >\$200bn in 2027 plus margin recovery toward 63–64% gross margin by mid-to-late 2027; the moat (53–80% inspection shares, decades of defect data no rival can rebuild) is the strongest in the field and AI *increases* inspection intensity. The honest caveat: at 20.5x forward it is the least cheap equipment name, so it needs WFE growth to persist. Full writeup: `final_pick_2026-11-24.md` — KLAC is also the single pick.

**3. FIX — Comfort Systems USA (\$1,158.53).** The dip is temporary: a record \$17.81bn backlog (+90% YoY, 8th consecutive sequential increase) on a net-cash balance sheet (\$2.11bn net cash) with Q3 FCF of \$806m, while the forward P/E (17.2x) sits below its 5-year median (23.6x) and estimates were revised *up* over the past 30 days even as the price fell — the de-rating is entirely multiple, not earnings. The catalyst is backlog conversion at guided mid-20% 2027 growth; the moat is the most AI-proof work in the batch (AI cannot install HVAC and piping), with a skilled-labour bottleneck as the real constraint. The honest caveat: the CFO transition and the sheer pace of backlog growth (+90%) invite execution risk.

**4. AMAT — Applied Materials (\$346.87).** The dip is temporary: two consecutive record quarters (Q4 FY2026 revenue \$10.41bn, +53% YoY, non-GAAP EPS \$4.16, +92%) met with selling, at 14.1x forward — the cheapest equipment name, and Bernstein's top US equipment pick at a \$600 target. The catalyst is WFE growth >30% in 2027 and the "double output by 2028" investment converting; the moat is the broadest equipment portfolio, though less concentrated than KLAC's inspection monopoly or LRCX's etch lead. The honest caveat: breadth cuts both ways — AMAT is exposed to every WFE segment, so a broad capex pause hits it everywhere at once.

**5. APP — AppLovin (\$373.92).** The dip is temporary on the numbers: Q3 revenue +55% YoY to \$2.18bn, 84.3% adjusted EBITDA margin, \$1.42bn quarterly FCF, and the SEC closed its data-practices inquiry with no action in Aug 2026 — the stock halved on a narrative the numbers kept refuting. The catalyst is the Q4 print (Feb 10, 2027, guided +43–44%) plus the next-generation model rollout and a stepped-up buyback. The honest value-trap caveat is the one that kept it at #5: the moat is a single AI engine competing against Meta and Google, who own the user graphs, with a binary data-legitimacy overhang (class action status unconfirmed) — if AXON's edge rested on unauthorised data extraction, the performance edge evaporates fast.

**6. VRT — Vertiv (\$186.35).** Fundamentals accelerating (orders +100%, revenue +61%) with the price halved; the Feb 2027 guide is the test. Trap caveat: management has stopped sharing orders data in dollars, so the market's visibility into the backlog is now worse than the price assumes.

**7. GOOGL — Alphabet (\$282.10).** Cloud +103% with a \$561bn backlog at the cheapest megacap multiple (13.9x trailing); the rebound waits on FCF, and the HIGH_ACCRUALS flag is mostly benign timing (TPU inventory build ahead of contracted deliveries). Trap caveat: Search monetisation in an agentic world is still the open question.

**8. NEM — Newmont (\$100.73).** Record Q3, fortress net-cash balance sheet, 10.1x forward; a gold pullback plus cost guide, not a broken miner. Trap caveat: it is a pure gold-price bet — if gold rolls over, there is no second thesis.

**9. BKNG — Booking Holdings (\$144.94).** The #1 OTA at 11.8x forward with the AI threat verified at <1% of room nights (Nov 3 Q3 call) and a \$12.4bn buyback. Trap caveat: the discovery layer is genuinely contestable by AI agents over time (IDC forecasts agents executing 30% of travel bookings by 2030).

**10. MU — Micron Technology (\$707.70).** ⚠️ VALUE-TRAP FLAG — kept in the ranking, not vetoed, because ranked mode flags rather than vetoes: 3.4x forward earnings with 75% of 2027 output sold and ~\$134bn of remaining performance obligations is the cheapest name in the field, and lens A made it its #1 on the Dec 17 catalyst.
