# Final pick — dip — 2026-10-30

## THE PICK

**KLAC — KLA Corporation, Information Technology / Semiconductor Materials & Equipment, market cap 218.5B USD.**

## One-paragraph thesis

KLAC is the rare dip whose rebound catalyst has already printed rather than being a hope for a future quarter: fiscal Q1 2027 delivered record revenue of 4.136B USD (+28.9% year-on-year), a record 46.1% operating margin, and a December-quarter guide of 4.7B USD (about +42.5% year-on-year) that sits roughly 4.8% above consensus, on top of a 12.5B USD backlog (+58.2% year-on-year at end-FY2026). The 44.4% drawdown from the 52-week high was a multiple and sector-sentiment de-rating — the same equipment selloff that hit AMAT — not a moat break, and the post-print price of 167.43 USD leaves most of the gap to the analyst mean target of 227.19 USD still open. With management raising the 2026 wafer-equipment market view to the mid-150B USD range and previewing over 200B USD for 2027, KLAC has the fastest credible path to a sharp re-rating in this field.

## Why the dip is temporary, not permanent

The single most compelling evidence is that, after the selloff, KLAC printed a record quarter and then guided the next quarter to roughly 42.5% growth: fiscal Q1 2027 revenue 4.136B USD (+28.9% year-on-year, above guidance and consensus), non-GAAP EPS 1.22 USD versus 1.18 USD expected, and a 46.1% operating margin — its first quarter above 46%. A permanently impaired business does not do that. The drawdown drivers were a higher starting multiple, a sector-wide 22 Oct 2026 selloff on shipment-constraint and AI-spending concerns, memory pricing, tariffs, and China export-control friction (29.8% of FY2026 revenue from China, with a guided 300–350M USD CY2026 export-control revenue impact) — all cyclical or friction items, with no moat break identified in the dossier.

## The dip depth

KLAC is 44.4% below its 52-week high and 7.9% below its 200-day SMA (screen, 29 Oct 2026 close prices). On the split-adjusted series cited in research, it fell from a 30 Jun 2026 high of 307.37 USD to 145.61 USD by 25 Oct 2026 (about −52.6% on that series), including −9.55% on 22 Oct 2026 alone. The cause is a multiple de-rating on top of a sector de-rating, amplified by a premium starting multiple — not a demand collapse, as the Q1 FY2027 print subsequently proved.

## Why it's the category leader / why the moat is intact

KLAC is the near-monopoly in semiconductor process control — the tools that find defects, measure wafers, and manage yield — with a majority/dominant share of the global process-control market (verified: PitchBook "majority share"; Zacks "dominant process control market share"). As nodes shrink and packaging gets more complex, inspection and metrology content per wafer rises, so AI accelerators are KLAC's tailwind, not its replacement: there is no credible substitute for physically inspecting wafers, and in-house efforts by chipmakers have historically failed to match KLAC's defect sensitivity at volume. The moat is process technology and IP in optics and algorithms, qualification lock-in (a defect missed costs a fab far more than the tool), and a growing services installed base (850M USD services revenue in Q1 FY2027, +14%). AI disruption risk: low.

## The rebound catalyst & timing

The catalyst is already delivered and has a dated second leg. Fiscal Q1 2027 printed 28 Oct 2026 (record revenue, record margin, beat on both lines). The December-quarter guide — revenue 4.7B USD ± 200M USD, non-GAAP EPS 1.44 USD ± 0.11 USD — is the near-term re-rating engine, with the Q2 FY2027 print in Jan 2027 (screen: 2027-01-28) as the next confirmation. The medium-term leg is management's preview of a wafer-equipment market above 200B USD in 2027 and advanced packaging on track for at least 1.1B USD in CY2026 (+70%+). Timing: re-rating through the December-quarter delivery and the January print, with the 2027 WFE cycle carrying the base case to 2027-Q4.
