## One-paragraph thesis

Copart is the physical tollbooth of U.S. salvage auctions — a duopoly built on 250+ mostly-owned, permitted yards near metros and a global buyer network (buyers in 185 countries; international buyers are roughly 38% of U.S. unit sales) — whose stock has fallen -35.3% from its 52-week high and de-rated to 16.1x forward, roughly a decade-low multiple, because insurance assignment volumes shrank. The panel's judgment, confirmed by the skeptic lens that exists to kill exactly this kind of name, is that the hole is mostly cyclical and bounded: excluding one lost insurer account, domestic insurance assignments would have risen +2.3% in Q4 FY2026 (verified verbatim from the call), earnings already fell in FY2026 ($1.55 vs $1.59) so the low multiple sits on trough rather than peak earnings, the balance sheet is net cash (-2.36x net debt/EBITDA), FY2026 operating cash flow of $1,604.5M exceeded net income of $1,480.4M, and the name raised prices into falling volume (revenue per unit +5.7%, global ASP +5.5%) — behavior a business losing its moat cannot produce. The ACV Auctions acquisition (closed 2026-10-16, verified) adds a dealer-wholesale growth leg, and $1.63B of FY2026 buybacks say management is buying the same de-rating.

## Why the dip is temporary, not permanent

- The feared permanent impairment — a ~15-point insurer share collapse to IAA — decomposes to a bounded ~3-point reallocation from one account, an industry-wide shrink in collision claim frequency (-3.4% YoY), and client-mix arithmetic. Ex-the-lost-account, assignments grew +2.3% (Phase 3.5: CONFIRMED verbatim, Jay Adair, Q4 FY2026 call, 2026-09-10).
- Record total-loss frequency (23.3%) and rising severity are structural tailwinds that offset frequency decline over time; vehicle complexity (more electronics per car) raises the total-loss rate mechanically.
- The RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN earnings-quality flag was re-checked against the FY2026 10-K for this run — not copied from yesterday: the receivables build was only $80.6M, CFO/NI is 1.08, accruals are negative. The flag is a near-zero-revenue-growth denominator artefact (Phase 3.5: CONFIRMED). It is not earnings running ahead of cash.
- No guidance collapse, no leverage event, no technology displacement was found by any of the three research batches.

## Why it's the category leader / why the moat is intact

Copart and IAA/RB Global split roughly 80% of the U.S. salvage market; Copart's edge is owned, permitted land near metros (years to replicate through zoning), integrated title/tow/storage, catastrophe surge capacity insurers depend on after hurricanes, and the deepest global buyer base, which is precisely what maximizes recovery values — the metric insurers choose auctioneers on. **Why AI cannot disrupt it:** there is no software substitute for physical yards, tow networks and title processing; AI is an internal pricing/data lever for the incumbent holding decades of auction data, and vehicle complexity is a total-loss tailwind, not a threat. Honest weakness: the moat binds market structure, not every contract — one insurer did move volume, and RB Global/IAA now has a 50-state footprint, so dual-sourcing is a live watch item, not a dismissed one.
