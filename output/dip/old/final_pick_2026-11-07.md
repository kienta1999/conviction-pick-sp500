## One-paragraph thesis

Arista is the cleanest "great quarter, falling stock" in the field, two days running: Q3 2026 revenue grew 58.5% year over year — the fastest since its 2014 IPO — with a 49.1% non-GAAP operating margin, and management raised guidance and gave a first FY2027 outlook of about \$18.5bn revenue (+38–40%), yet the stock sits 26.6% off its 52-week high after the 5 Nov AI-politics rout. The moat is software — the EOS operating system plus multi-year hyperscaler qualification lock-in — which AI strengthens rather than threatens, since every GPU cluster needs high-speed Ethernet plumbing. With \$14.36bn cash, zero debt, and a forward multiple that has de-rated while earnings nearly doubled, this is a blowout business repriced as if its moat were cracking. It isn't. Friday's +7.31% bounce reclaimed the 200-day SMA (\$165.79) — the only name in its research batch to do so.

## Why the dip is temporary, not permanent

The sequence is the evidence: on 3 Nov Arista printed its fastest growth since IPO (revenue +58.5%, beat its own \$3.3bn guide by over \$350m), raised Q4 guidance and issued a first-ever FY2027 outlook of about \$18.5bn (vs \$16.54bn consensus) — and two days later the stock fell 10.8% on the midterm / data-center-moratorium rout, with no company-specific negative event in the quarter and no moratorium law enacted (Congress does not take power until January). A business does not print its fastest growth since IPO and raise guidance the week its moat breaks. The drawdown is a multiple compression on a sentiment shock, not an operating break: fundamentals moved the opposite way from the price for the entire drawdown. Phase 3.5 verification confirmed the print, the guide, the cash/debt figures, and that the 5 Nov fall was sentiment, not company news.

## Why it's the category leader / why the moat is intact

Arista is the #1 player in cloud/AI Ethernet switching by the panel's read, with the moat in the EOS software and telemetry stack and the installed base, not the chip underneath: hyperscalers qualify vendors over multi-year cycles and rarely switch mid-build. Irreplaceability score 9/10 — the joint-highest in the field. AI cannot route around Arista; AI *is* the demand driver, since every AI accelerator cluster needs high-speed Ethernet, and the 800G switch portfolio is the bottleneck hardware for AI fabrics. The honest second-order threats are Nvidia bundling Spectrum-X with its own GPUs and white-box/SONiC commoditisation of the hardware layer — and verification added a material correction the dossier underplayed: **Nvidia Spectrum-X took the #1 data-centre Ethernet revenue share from Arista in Q1 2026 and held it in Q2 2026 (IDC)**. No named hyperscaler exit was found, and Arista still grew 58.5% while Nvidia rose, but the share loss is documented and is the first thesis-break watch item below. No model can write, qualify and operate a hyperscaler's network fabric.

2. **Hyperscaler capex** — a real pause (order cancellations, not delays) is the bear case; continued builds are the base case.
