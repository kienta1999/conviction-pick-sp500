## Why the dip is temporary, not permanent

The dossier's central fact: fundamentals printed fine *while* the narrative collapsed.
FY26's 8% recurring revenue and 12% adj EPS growth (reported 8/4/2026 — independently
verified CONFIRMED) came **after** the closed-sales scare quarter. If the franchise
were being hollowed by AI, the recurring base — the proxy/regulatory annuity — would
be decaying; instead it compounded and management guided forward with confidence. The
AI-disintermediation thesis collides with the fact that proxy forwarding is a
regulatory obligation brokers must perform (Reg 14A) — the *function* cannot be
repealed by a better model. What AI can change is *who performs* it, and that is the
bear's real (weaker) argument, adjudicated in the risk section.

## Why it's the category leader / why the moat is intact

Broadridge is the dominant #1 in investor communications and proxy services — the
registered agent behind most banks'/brokers' proxy forwarding, with very high client
retention and economies of scale. **Honest correction to the dossier (per Phase 3.5
verification):** the dossier overstated the moat as a statutory monopoly. SEC Reg 14A
obligates *brokers/dealers* to forward proxy materials — it does not mandate
Broadridge specifically, and EDGAR filing composition can be done by issuers directly.
"Regulation is the moat" is analysis, not law; the real moat is **network scale +
deep broker integration + switching costs + regulatory plumbing**, on top of a
*function* AI cannot repeal. The verifier confirmed the factual core (9/10 claims
CONFIRMED, this one qualified, none contradicted). Closed-sales softness is pipeline
timing per management; recurring-revenue outlook was *raised* to the upper end of
5–7% in the Q1 call.
