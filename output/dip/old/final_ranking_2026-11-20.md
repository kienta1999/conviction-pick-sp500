## Per-name thesis

**1. LRCX — Lam Research.** The dip is temporary: Lam reported its best quarter on record on 21 Oct 2026 (memory systems revenue +120% YoY) and fell 16.4% on 5 Nov alone on the SOX's 12.25% political de-rating, with no company-specific setback. The rebound catalyst is the Q2 FY27 print on 27 Jan 2027 (guided revenue ~\$8.6bn, EPS \$2.33) against a WFE market management sees above \$200bn in 2027, with a \$25bn buyback as a floor. The moat holds: #1 etch (~45–50%), cryogenic etch the only proven high-volume path for HBM4 TSVs, irreplaceability 9/10 — AI raises etch content per wafer rather than threatening it. Full thesis and scenarios in output/dip/final_pick_2026-11-20.md.

**2. AMAT — Applied Materials.** The dip is temporary: Q4 FY2026 (reported 12 Nov) was a record — revenue \$10.41bn (+53% YoY), non-GAAP EPS \$4.16 (+92% YoY, beating consensus) — and management guided Q1 FY27 *above* consensus (\$11.1bn revenue, \$4.35 EPS). The catalyst is the 11 Feb 2027 print proving the record quarter was not a one-off, with Bernstein naming AMAT its top US pick (Outperform, \$600 target). The moat holds: #2 overall WFE vendor, #1 in deposition, broadest portfolio; irreplaceability 8/10. The watch item is receivables up 73.5% YoY to \$9.0bn — worth monitoring, not a distress signal. Cheapest equipment multiple in the field at 13.8x forward.

**3. KLAC — KLA Corporation.** The dip is temporary with a slow grind: revenue growth accelerated three straight quarters to +28.9% YoY in fiscal Q1 2027 while the stock fell 30% from 8 Aug to 6 Nov — a de-rating, plus a real but slow-moving margin headwind (memory component costs that cannot be repriced on existing backlog, with gross-margin recovery to the 63–64% model expected mid-to-late 2027 into 2028). The catalyst is the Q2 FY27 print in late Jan / early Feb 2027 (guided revenue \$4.7bn, non-GAAP EPS \$1.44, gross margin 62.5%) — note the date is unverified (screen 28 Jan; vendors estimate 4 Feb). The moat is the widest in the field: ~50–55% of process control, >80% of mask inspection, irreplaceability 10/10. Least cheap of the top 3 at 19.9x forward — buy the moat, not the bargain.

**4. VRT — Vertiv.** The dip is temporary: Q3 revenue +61% YoY with organic orders more than doubling and guidance raised, yet the stock fell 14% on 5 Nov with no company event — a political/sentiment shock on a stock priced for perfection (fwd P/E hit 55x in May). The catalyst is the Q4 print on 10 Feb 2027 (guided sales ~\$4.4bn, adjusted EPS \$2.25) plus any data-centre policy de-escalation. The moat holds (co-#1 in data-centre power/thermal, liquid-cooling lead) but is narrowing: Eaton's Boyd acquisition and the open 800 VDC standard cap pricing power over time. INVENTORY_BUILD flag judged mostly benign (inventory pre-sold against a firm-order backlog, funded by \$4.12bn of customer prepayments).

**5. FIX — Comfort Systems USA.** The dip is temporary: a blowout Q3 (revenue +53.7%, EPS \$13.91, backlog a record \$17.81bn, +90% YoY) sold off on a sequential margin slip (gross margin 24.8% vs 25.9% in Q2), no explicit EPS guidance, and a CFO transition — plus the November data-centre scare (technology customers are now 59% of revenue). RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN flag judged benign (receivables track the 90%-larger backlog and are more than covered by \$3.87bn of customer prepayments, with cash flow exceeding net income).
