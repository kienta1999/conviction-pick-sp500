## One-paragraph thesis

Arista is the cleanest great-business-on-sale name in the field: Q3 2026 was the best quarter in company history — revenue \$3.66bn, +58.5% YoY, non-GAAP operating margin 49.1% — yet the stock is -22.7% off its 52-week high after falling 11.6% in a single session on 20 Nov in the broad AI rout, with no Arista-specific bad news. It carries zero debt and \$14.36bn of cash and marketable securities, and its 2027 framework has revenue of ~\$18.5bn (+38–40%) with AI fabrics roughly doubling to ~\$7.0bn. At 27.77x forward earnings it is still a growth multiple, but it is below its own ~36.5x historical average forward P/E, and the growth behind it is accelerating, not decelerating. This is a compounder whose dip is a multiple compression, not a broken story.

## Why the dip is temporary, not permanent

The single most compelling evidence is the juxtaposition the panel kept returning to: Arista fell ~22% from its \$217.41 record (9 Oct 2026) to ~\$170 by 4 Nov in an AI-infrastructure selloff with no Arista-specific news, and then reported its best quarter ever on 3 Nov — revenue +58.5% YoY, beating its own \$3.3bn guide by ~11% and consensus by ~10%, with Q4 guidance (\$3.9–4.0bn) itself above the ~\$3.6bn consensus. The numbers got better while the stock fell. Verification confirmed the Q3 figures, the 2027 framework, and the zero-debt balance sheet against primary sources.

## The dip depth

ANET is -22.7% off its 52-week high and only -0.6% below its 200-day SMA — the shallowest dip in the researched set, which is the honest caveat on this pick: it is a momentum-reversal dip, not a deep-value dislocation. The drawdown is entirely a multiple compression in a crowded AI trade (the stock fell 11.6% on 20 Nov alone, the day Nvidia fell 14%), not a fundamentals break.

## Why it's the category leader / why the moat is intact

Arista held the #1 data-centre switching share into 2025 (~21.5%) and is the #1 independent Ethernet vendor, with EOS — a single modular operating system across all products — creating deep switching costs, plus CloudVision management stickiness and hyperscaler co-engineering relationships. AI needs Arista: in large training clusters networking is 20–30% of build cost, and Ethernet is displacing InfiniBand in AI back-end networks. The moat is contested, not unassailable — Nvidia's Spectrum-X overtook Arista in data-centre Ethernet switching in Q2 2026 (Nvidia 20.4% vs Arista 18.7%, IDC, confirmed by verification) — but Arista's defence is vendor-neutrality: customers building around non-Nvidia accelerators (AMD, Broadcom, Meta/Microsoft silicon) need open Ethernet, and Arista is advancing rack-scale architecture with AMD, Broadcom, Meta and Microsoft.
