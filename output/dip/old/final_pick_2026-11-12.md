At 19.3x forward earnings with a \$25bn buyback authorised the same day the panic peaked, this is multiple compression on an accelerating franchise, not a broken business.

## Why the dip is temporary, not permanent

The single most compelling evidence is that fundamentals accelerated *into* the sell-off: revenue +54%, EPS +75%, record margins, 4/4 EPS beats (average surprise +6.4%), and net cash of \$3.70bn — while no customer was lost, no fab was cancelled, and no share was lost (Lam has outperformed WFE for three straight years). The fall was a supply-constrained Dec-quarter guide (\$8.60bn, which management said would have been higher without near-term supply-chain constraints) plus a political sentiment shock about *future* data-center permits, not a demand print. The Dec guide barely beat consensus (\$8.60bn vs \$8.57bn FactSet) and the China mix drove part of the de-rating — the "fell anyway" framing is fair but incomplete without that context (verification note 6).

## The dip depth

LRCX is −45.5% off its 52-week high and −16.8% below its 200-day SMA. The cause, in sequence: a record quarter sold off on a supply-capped guide (22 Oct, ~10–12% down), then the 5 Nov AI rout (SOX −12.3%, Lam −16.4% with no company news) on the midterm sweep and data-center moratorium fears, including the Texas governor-elect calling for a pause on new data-center approvals.

## Why it's the category leader / why the moat is intact

Lam holds ~45–50% of the global etch market in a 3–4 firm oligopoly (Applied Materials, Tokyo Electron), with fab-by-fab qualification lock-in — a failed etch step scraps whole wafer lots, so customers do not switch suppliers casually. AI is the demand driver, not the disruptor: HBM needs roughly 3x the wafer area of conventional DRAM for the same bits (over 4x for HBM4E) and involves 200–300 process steps, so AI *increases* Lam's content per wafer. (The dossier's "2–3x more etch/deposition steps" ratio was NOT verified — do not quote it as sourced.) The soft spot is China: China was 35% of September-quarter revenue and management guides it "meaningfully down" in December — a real demand cliff, not a sentiment one, and the main bear-case driver. Irreplaceability scored 9/10.
