# DIP — Final Pick — 2026-11-27

## THE PICK

**FIX — Comfort Systems USA** (Industrials / Construction & Engineering), price **\$1,099.58** (2026-11-25 close; market closed 2026-11-26 for Thanksgiving), market cap **\$38.5B**.

**One-paragraph thesis:** Comfort Systems is the cleanest dip in the field: it printed the best quarter in company history — revenue \$3.77B (+54%), EPS \$13.91 (+69%), a record \$17.81B backlog (+90%) — and the stock fell 17% in two days anyway, because the whole AI-infrastructure complex repriced and its longtime CFO's retirement was announced the same night. It sits on ~\$2.1B of net cash, so there is no survival risk to survive, and 65–75% of that record backlog converts into revenue within 12 months — contracted demand, not hope. At 16.3x forward earnings (FY2027E) against a mid-40s same-store growth guide, the market is pricing a hyperscaler capex pause that the backlog does not support; the rebound is backlog conversion into cash, not a bet on a new story.

## Why the dip is temporary, not permanent

The single most compelling evidence is that the dip happened *on* the best quarter in company history: revenue, EPS and backlog all set records and the stock still fell 17% in two sessions (close 21 Oct \$1,401.27 → 22 Oct \$1,279.37 → 23 Oct \$1,160.86, -17.2%, confirmed by Phase 3.5 verification against Finnhub candles). A broken business does not print its best quarter ever and guide mid-40s growth; a derated sector does exactly that. The 9M cash conversion of 195% (operating cash flow \$2.54B vs net income \$1.30B) and \$3.87B of customer prepayments (billings in excess of costs) confirm the earnings are real cash, not accounting — the screen's RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN flag is benign. The company-specific cause verification added — the announced retirement of co-founder and longtime CFO Bill George at year-end — is a leadership-transition headline, not an operating break, and FIX issues no formal numeric guidance, so there was no guidance cut to fear (and no reassurance either).

## The dip depth

FIX is **-46.7% off its 52-week high** (\$2,073.99 intraday high, 14 May 2026) and **-31.2% below its 200-day SMA**, with a 12-month return of +16.6% — the drawdown is a give-back of a vertical run (the stock had run ~60x in ten years and traded at ~42x earnings at the May peak), not a collapse from a low base. The cause: a multiple collapse on a flawless quarter, plus profit-taking, the CFO-retirement headline, flat gross margin (24.8%, unchanged YoY), and the October–November AI-infrastructure de-rating that took CAT, GEV and VST down with it.

## Why it's the category leader / why the moat is intact

FIX is the largest-scale mechanical/electrical contractor for data centres, with the largest backlog in its peer set (Carrier's data-centre backlog ~\$8.5B+, AAON's ~\$1.9B). Its moat is physical: skilled-craft labour scarcity across ~45 autonomous operating companies at ~197 US locations, modular MEP fabrication (~40% faster than traditional methods, ~3M sq ft of capacity rising to ~4M by end-2026), and scale/bonding — and **AI cannot disrupt it, because AI is the customer**: technology (overwhelmingly hyperscale data centres) supplied 58–59% of 2026 revenue (60.8% in Q3 alone), up from 40–42% a year earlier, and technology accounted for ~89% of the year-on-year revenue gain. Service/maintenance is 35%+ of revenue — recurring, sticky, and AI-proof. The only erosion vector is customer concentration, not substitution: no substitute technology exists for installed mechanical/electrical systems.

## The rebound catalyst & timing

The catalyst is **backlog conversion + estimate gravity**, not a single event: the Q3 10-Q states 65–75% of remaining performance obligations (\$11.6–13.4B of the \$17.81B backlog) are recognised within 12 months, and management guides mid-40s same-store revenue growth for 2026 and mid-20s for 2027. The next hard checkpoint is the **Q4 print expected 18 Feb 2027** (vendor estimate; screen date 2027-02-18). Analysts have raised estimates over the past 60 days, and KeyBanc, UBS and Oppenheimer all reiterated Overweight/Buy/Outperform on 26 Oct 2026, with no recent downgrades. Modular capacity additions and frontier-AI-lab/colocation customer wins are the incremental upside.

## Key risks

- **Concentration (the trap door):** 59% of revenue from tech customers; a hyperscaler capex pause hits FIX first and hardest, and the backlog conversion window has already lengthened to 18–24 months.
- **Backlog ≠ next year's revenue:** only 65–75% converts within 12 months; the headline 90% backlog growth overstates near-term sales.
- **Execution at scale:** revenue +54% strains labour, modular capacity and project management; a single large-project margin miss would be punished at this beta (1.71).
- **The multiple may be right:** if 2025–26 growth was a one-off AI-buildout spike, mid-20s growth in 2027 followed by normalisation leaves a contractor at a contractor's multiple (~12–15x), not a re-rating to \$1,772.
- **Leadership transition:** co-founder/CFO Bill George retires at year-end; a new CFO inheriting a \$17.8B backlog at peak growth is an execution variable.
- **Multiple-comparison caveat (verification):** the 16.3x-vs-42x pairing mixes forward years — like-for-like FY2026E is ~21.6x now vs ~42x at the peak. The halving direction is real, but the exact compression is smaller than the headline pairing suggests.
