## Per-name thesis

**1. LRCX — Lam Research.** The "miss" was an in-line guide capped by supply, not demand: FQ1 FY27 was a record (\$8.22bn revenue, +54% YoY, adj. EPS \$2.20 vs \$2.18 expected), management said clean-room space and component supply limit the industry's ability to meet demand into 2027, and Lam is #1 in etch in a de-facto oligopoly with ~\$3.70bn net cash and a \$25bn buyback authorised 5 Nov 2026. The dip is temporary — a valuation reset on a demand-capped business — and the rebound catalyst is deferred shipments landing in early 2027 plus a WFE year Lam sees above \$200bn in 2027. The moat is intact: etch share, installed base, and memory leverage. Value-trap caveat: if WFE is cancelled rather than delayed, the most memory-levered name falls furthest.

**2. KLAC — KLA Corporation.** Record FQ1 FY27 (revenue \$4.14bn, +29% YoY, record 46.1% non-GAAP operating margin, record \$1.34bn FCF) with a Q2 guide of \$4.7bn (+43% YoY), yet −52% off its high as the trailing P/E halved from ~71x to ~31x. The dip is temporary — the multiple, not the model, broke — and the rebound catalyst is the Q2 FY27 print (~27 Jan–4 Feb 2027) plus a CY2027 WFE/services update. The moat is the panel's best (10/10 irreplaceability): AI cannot substitute for physical wafer inspection, and every node shrink raises process-control intensity per wafer. Value-trap caveat: at 20.53x forward it is the priciest equipment name, and a return to the low end of its 5-year P/E range would be another ~40% down even with earnings flat.

**3. AMAT — Applied Materials.** The cheapest equipment name on forward earnings (14.09x) with the broadest franchise: record FQ4 revenue \$10.41bn (+53% YoY), adj. EPS \$4.16 vs \$4.06 expected, Q1 FY27 guide \$11.1bn above consensus — yet −52% from its June high because management declined to quantify 2027 equipment spending at its 13 Oct investor event, a communication vacuum the market filled with fear while customers tried to pull tool deliveries forward. The dip is temporary and the rebound catalyst is a single 2027 WFE number from management. Bernstein named it its top US pick (\$600 target). Value-trap caveat: targets were cut sharply on 13 Nov (e.g. JPMorgan \$660→\$500), so the +61% screen upside is a moving target.

**4. VRT — Vertiv.** Orders doubled, guidance raised past the beat, record Q3 — the demand story is the strongest in the panel — yet −50% on a political de-rating (Texas permitting/financing sentiment) and the loss of order disclosure. The dip is temporary on the business, but the rebound is gated by politics and credit conditions VRT does not control. INVENTORY_BUILD flag: largely benign — inventories doubled to \$3.29bn but customer prepayments rose \$2.31bn to \$4.12bn over the same period, funding the build.

**5. GOOGL — Alphabet.** Cloud revenue doubled (+103% YoY to \$30.71bn) with a \$561bn backlog, while the stock fell on an EPS miss driven by an \$8.66bn mark-to-market loss on equity securities (SpaceX/Anthropic) — operating income actually rose 30% to \$40.6bn. The dip is temporary in the operating business, but the capex super-cycle (Q3 capex \$51.4bn, FCF −\$9.1bn, full-year guide \$195–205bn) is a multi-year overhang, not a one-quarter blip. HIGH_ACCRUALS flag: mostly benign — the wedge is unrealised equity marks, not operating earnings running ahead of cash. The rebound waits on capex discipline, not demand.

**6. APP — AppLovin.** The strongest fundamentals in its batch: record quarter, 84% EBITDA margin, \$1.4bn quarterly FCF, near-zero net leverage, forward multiple at roughly half its five-year average — with the rebound already underway. The dip is temporary (a 2025 valuation meeting decelerating sequential growth), but conviction is capped by the unproven e-commerce leg and the lumpy AI-model cycle, and AI is both its moat and its threat.

**7. FIX — Comfort Systems USA.** Record \$17.8bn backlog, record cash flow, net cash, forward multiple at half its historical average — the cleanest "growth at a de-rated price" name in the batch. The dip is temporary with evidence still owed: the rebound needs Q4/H1-2027 proof that the 25% gross margin is a floor, and the stock is a geared bet on hyperscaler capex continuing. RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN flag: benign — billed receivables +49% vs 9M revenue +53%, and 9M operating cash flow \$2.54bn vs net income \$1.30bn.

**8. Cheap only if 2027 pricing holds; the Dec 17 print is the test.

## Risk lens & leverage-safety note

Sector concentration is the list's biggest risk: the top 3 are all semiconductor equipment, all exposed to the same WFE/AI-capex cycle — a WFE cut hits the whole top of the list at once. The deepest drawdowns (AMAT −51.9%, LRCX −51.5%, KLAC −51.5%, VRT −50.4%) are also the names most likely to keep falling if the November repricing was the start of a capex bust rather than a sentiment washout. The steadier compounders lower down the list (BKNG at 11.85x forward, VST with 98%-hedged 2027 cash flow) carry less cycle risk but slower re-rating paths. MU is the highest-risk name in the top 10 on the trap dimension — peak-cycle earnings at 3.39x forward — and is flagged, not vetoed, in ranked mode. Leverage-safety: beaten-down names can keep falling, and "catching a knife" with margin is brutal — all picks are cash-only per POLICY.md §2. This is education, not advice: no specific leverage multiple or position size is recommended here.
