## Per-name thesis

**1. KLAC — the pick.** Quasi-monopoly process control (50–55% share, 80%+ reticle) with revenue accelerating (+29% YoY, record Q1 FY27) while the price halved on a sector de-rating. The dip is multiple compression, not a broken business; the Dec-quarter guide (~$4.7B) is the falsifiable test, and Nvidia's Nov 17 print corroborates the demand leg. See `final_pick_2026-11-18.md`.

**2. LRCX — cleanest rebound, newest to the screen.** Record Q1 FY27 ($8.22B, +54% YoY, record FCF ~$3.24B), a $25B buyback — the largest in company history — announced on the night of the Nov 5 rout, plus a 27% dividend hike and a preliminary 2027 WFE outlook above $200B. Etch/deposition moat 9/10, share of WFE still expanding. The only hair is China (35% of Q1 revenue, guided down on export controls).

**3. FIX — the backlog is the thesis.** A record $17.81B backlog (+90% YoY, eighth consecutive sequential increase) converting over 18–24 months at guided mid-20% same-store growth in 2027, with Q3 revenue +54% and FCF of $806M (+121% YoY), net cash, and a forward P/E of 16.3x — roughly half its own ~31x historical average. AI cannot pour concrete or pull cable. The single real risk is tech-customer concentration (59% of 9M revenue) if data-center capex actually pauses.

**4. GOOGL — deepest quality dip outside semis.** A −30% drawdown on a 25%-growth, net-cash franchise whose worst day was driven by equity-security marks, a $5.2B EU fine payment and a $51.4B capex quarter — not by losing Search (query growth at a five-year high per management). Cloud revenue doubled to $30.7B with a $561B backlog. The rebound needs the February print to show Search monetisation holding and Cloud margins surviving the build-out.

**5. AMAT — cheapest equipment, share question open.** Record Q4 FY26 ($10.41B, +53% YoY, EPS $4.16) and a quantified >30% 2027 WFE call at 14.2x forward — the cheapest forward multiple among the equipment names, with a +60.9% mean-target upside. The dip is partly a share-loss discount (2025 WFE share reportedly fell 20.0% → 17.9% while Lam gained); the Nov 12 print is the first evidence of reversal, and Q1 FY27 (Feb 11) is the test.

**6. VRT — biggest growth, binary guide.** Record Q3 (+61% sales, orders doubled, guidance raised) sold off purely on midterm politics and the SemiAnalysis slope cut. Cheap on 2027 earnings if the Feb 10 guide validates — that guide is the make-or-break test, and the orders blackout means the market learns about a demand air-pocket late. Inventory more than doubled; Q4 conversion decides whether the build stays benign.

**7. CAT — highest-quality, lowest-volatility name.** Record $78B backlog, $9.6B LTM FCF, a dividend, and a historically normal multiple (~18x forward vs ~17–18x history) after giving back a parabolic AI-premium rally. The Anthropic $3.4B / 1.7 GW order (reported, not confirmed) validates the scarcity value of its delivery slots. A grinder, not a snap-back: the rebound depends on margins recovering and the data-center order book holding.

**8. SCHW — record asset-gathering at 12x forward.** The cheapest, highest-moat name outside the top 3 (irreplaceability 8/10): $13.33T client assets, record $190.4B quarterly net new assets, 51.1% operating margin — punished for a funding-mix miss (NIM 3.04% vs ~3.09% expected, deposits down three straight quarters). The rebound hinges on the January print showing deposits and NIM stabilising. The shallowest dip in the top 10 (−18.9% off high).

**9. MU — the fastest re-rate, the biggest trap risk.** A's single pick: 3.4x forward, +119% analyst upside, a record $35B buyback usable Dec 9 and a guided record print Dec 23, with >75% of 2027 output already contracted and Nvidia's memory-procurement commitments underwriting HBM demand. But the 3.4x P/E prices an earnings halving, NAND spot is down ~20% since mid-October, and memory history says that pricing is often right. Only two of four ballots ranked it — the panel's conviction stops at the trap question.

**10. INTU — deepest de-rating, binary print tomorrow.** A high-quality compounder (QuickBooks +19–23%, ~$7B+ recurring FCF) at 11.9x forward — the bottom of its own 5-year P/E range — because the market has priced AI disruption of tax/accounting software as a certainty. The Q1 FY27 print Nov 19 (after close) is a genuine binary: QBO customer growth re-accelerating retires the bear case; a guide cut confirms it. Effectively an earnings bet until the print passes.
