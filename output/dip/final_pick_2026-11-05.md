## THE PICK: LRCX — Lam Research, Information Technology / Semiconductor Materials & Equipment, market cap ~\$299bn (screen)

## One-paragraph thesis

Lam Research printed a record quarter and got sold off for a guide that was capped by supply, not demand — the classic temporary-dip setup. Fiscal Q1 2027 revenue of \$8.22bn (+54% YoY) with non-GAAP operating margin of 40.2% (the first time above 40%; GAAP was exactly 40.0%) met a stock down ~44.8% from its 52-week high, because the December-quarter guide of \$8.60bn ± \$400m merely met consensus and management blamed component and clean-room constraints. With net cash of ~\$3.7bn, record quarterly free cash flow of \$3.24bn, and a preliminary calendar-2027 wafer-fab-equipment view above \$200bn, the dip prices a demand collapse that the quarter itself refutes; delivery against the December guide at the late-January 2027 print, plus a firmer 2027 WFE outlook, should re-rate it sharply. The panel was unanimous: all four lenses picked LRCX, the same pick as yesterday's run — an independent re-confirmation, not a carry-over.

## Why the dip is temporary, not permanent

The single most compelling evidence is management's own statement that the December-quarter guide would have been higher absent component and clean-room supply constraints — on top of a record quarter with memory revenue +120% YoY and no evidence of share loss. A business losing its franchise does not print records while complaining it cannot ship fast enough. The sharpest leg of the sector's October fall was sentiment (AI-financing anxiety, sector rotation) and a supply narrative, not a Lam-specific miss, demand warning, or lost qualification. Phase 3.5 verification confirmed the record print and the supply-capped guide from Lam's own release and call transcript (2026-10-21), with one precision correction: the \$300–400m of deferred shipments is an analyst estimate that CFO Doug Bettinger declined to confirm — the deferral of those shipments to the March quarter is company-confirmed, the size is not.

## Why it's the category leader / why the moat is intact

Lam is the etch leader and a co-leader in deposition, with tools qualified fab-by-fab over years and embedded in customer process recipes, serviced through a Customer Support Business Group that did \$2.49bn in the quarter (+40% YoY). AI cannot disrupt it: AI increases etch/deposition intensity through more layers, advanced packaging, and HBM stacks. Phase 3.5 confirmed the moat ranking from independent sources (Intelligent Investor; Cestrian Capital, Oct 2026): Lam holds the top market share in etch and the clear second share in deposition. The genuine threats are customer concentration, cyclical WFE orders, and China export controls (foreign-affiliate rule renewal 2026-11-10, US–China trade-truce expiry 2027-01-10) — not a substitute technology. The dossier's irreplaceability score is 9/10.

## The rebound catalyst & timing

December-quarter delivery at or above the \$8.60bn guide, reported late January 2027 (estimated Jan 27, 2027 per Zacks/TipRanks — not yet company-confirmed), with delayed shipments slipping to the March quarter rather than lost — plus a sharper calendar-2027 WFE view on that call (management's preliminary view is already above \$200bn, led by DRAM, foundry logic, and NAND, with about 10 new Tier-1 fabs in 2027) and easing of the clean-room/component constraints. A secondary leg comes from the 2026-11-12 AMAT print: a strong peer print would validate the WFE outlook the whole trio trades on.
