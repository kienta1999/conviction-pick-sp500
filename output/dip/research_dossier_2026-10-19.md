## CPRT — Copart, Inc.

### 1. Why it's down — temporary vs permanent

A two-decade compounder has de-rated from ~35–40x to ~16–18x because growth stopped. Hard numbers (Q4 / FY26, reported 2026-09-10):

- FY26 revenue $4.67B, **+0.4% YoY only** (+2.4% ex-hurricane comps); Q4 revenue $1.15B, +2.4%; Q4 net income $327.4M, **−17.4%**, EPS $0.35 vs $0.38 consensus; FY net income $1.48B, −4.4% (Copart Q4 release / Fool transcript, 2026-09-11).
- **Volume pool shrank:** Q4 global units −2.9% (US −5.7%, international +10.0%); global insurance units −4.2%, **US insurance units −7.5%** — the sixth consecutive quarter of shrinking US insurance volume (Speedwell, 2026-09). Collision claim frequency −3.4% YoY; management and prior calls attribute this to consumers retrenching on auto-insurance coverage (liability-only, higher deductibles) as premiums rose, plus lapping FY25 hurricane/CAT volume (Q1 FY26 call; TIKR, 2026-08-24). Adair: "cyclical and, in our view, unprecedented dislocation… consumer retrenchment is cyclical, not structural."
- **One-customer loss:** ex a single domestic customer loss, US insurance assignments would have been **+2.3%** in Q4 (management, 2026-09-10). Management did not name the customer and said it would not accept the pricing demanded. Independent analysis identifies the dynamic as Progressive — now the largest US auto insurer, historically ~75% (recently reportedly up to ~90%) allocated to IAA — shifting volume to RB Global's IAA, while insurers that favour Copart lose policyholder share to Progressive (The Investors Podcast intrinsic-value revisit, ~2026-09-25). IAA has also narrowed Copart's land-capacity lead from ~70% to ~25% over ~5 years (same source).
- **Cost squeeze:** Q4 operating expense per car **+12.7% YoY** (+13% per Speedwell), gross profit −5.5%, operating income −10.6% — discretionary investment (long-haul delivery, Title Express, wholesale facilities) landing on falling units (AInvest, 2026-09; Q4 call).
- **ACV headline (2026-09-10):** $1.9B all-cash acquisition of ACV Auctions announced the same day as the weak print (see §3/§7) added a "thesis drift" discount; the stock slipped ~2.6% the next session.

**Verdict: MIXED — roughly 2/3 temporary, 1/3 permanent.** Temporary: hurricane comps, coverage retrenchment (management's cyclical claim is plausible — severity and total-loss rates are rising, see §3), and investment-phase costs. Permanent/structural: the Progressive→IAA volume shift is a share loss that does not automatically come back, IAA's capacity catch-up is real, and ADAS/safer vehicles are a slow secular drag on accident frequency (safer vehicles shrink the supply of wrecked cars — Insider Monkey moat review, 2026-10-12; the ~1%/yr frequency-drag framing from yesterday is consistent with the −3.4% frequency print moderating, but the exact 1% figure was **not found** in fresh sources and should be treated as an estimate, not a sourced fact).

### 2. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 8.5/10

The moat is physical and contractual: 250+ owned yards on scarce, zoned land near metros (a digital entrant cannot conjure storage/tow capacity), insurer contracts routing total-loss volume, and a global buyer network whose liquidity produces record returns for sellers — international buyers were 45.7% of US sales dollars in Q4; US insurance ASPs rose +6% (+9% ex-CAT) in Q2 and global ASPs +3.5% in Q4, outpacing the Manheim index (+2.8%) (Q2/Q4 2026 calls). The genuine threats are not AI substitution but (a) ADAS/AV reducing crash supply over a decade and (b) IAA competition. **Irreplaceability: 8.5/10** — docked for the demonstrated IAA share take.

### 7. Earnings-quality flag — RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN (recv_rev_divergence 3.203, accrual_ratio −0.014, CFO/NI 1.09)

**Rebound score: 6.0/10 — Verdict: A fortress-balance-sheet duopolist at half its decade-average multiple whose rebound needs the one-customer lap and a coverage-cycle turn over 2–4 quarters, with the Progressive/IAA share loss and the unprofitable $1.9B ACV bet as the permanent hair on an otherwise cyclical dip.**
