## Per-name thesis

**1. LRCX — the dip is a record quarter meeting a halved multiple.** Fiscal Q1 2027 revenue \$8.22bn (+54% YoY) with operating margin above 40% for the first time, yet the stock fell ~47% from its 52-week high because the December-quarter guide (\$8.60bn ± \$400m) merely met consensus. Management said the guide would have been higher absent component and clean-room supply constraints — supply, not demand, capped the outlook — with memory revenue +120% YoY and no evidence of share loss. The moat is etch leadership plus deposition co-leadership, qualified fab-by-fab over years; AI increases etch/deposition intensity rather than substituting for it.

**2. KLAC — the twin, on margins.** Fiscal Q1 2027 revenue \$4.14bn (+28.9% YoY), record 46.1% non-GAAP operating margin and 62.7% gross margin, a \$12.57bn backlog (+60% YoY), and a December-quarter guide at or above expectations — yet the stock fell on sector rotation, with target trims reflecting tempered multiples, not deteriorating fundamentals. Process control is the near-monopoly choke point AI makes more valuable, not less.

**3. AMAT — the binary.** Fiscal Q3 2026 beat (revenue \$9.12bn, +24.8% YoY) followed by an 8.16% fall on a day it reported nothing, driven by Lam's supply flag and AI-financing anxiety, with its own fiscal Q4 guide calling for ~+51% revenue YoY into the 2026-11-12 print. The broadest WFE portfolio and net cash argue the dip is sentiment; the withheld 2027 framework and China/export exposure argue the market is pricing a visibility gap, not noise.

**4. VRT — the rebound has already fired.** Q3 2026 (2026-10-28): sales \$4.31bn (+61.2% YoY), adjusted EPS \$2.21 vs \$1.82 consensus, organic orders +100%+ YoY, full-year guidance raised. The dip's causes (timing slips, quality chatter) are answered by the print itself; the swing risk is the inventory build (inventories \$3,288.4m at 2026-09-30 vs \$1,456.5m at 2025-12-31): growth stocking if orders convert, a cash drain if they stall.

**5. FIX — the backlog compounder.** Record backlog levered to data-center buildout, net cash, and a beat sold off on sentiment rather than operations. The receivables flag is judged benign by the research (strong cash conversion, accruals negative), but backlog conversion pace and customer concentration remain the live risks.

**6. META —** Ad revenue +26% YoY across a 3.60bn daily-active-person family, yet the stock fell ~20% from its October high on a raised \$135–145bn capex range and a first quarterly cash burn since 2012; a slow sentiment reset, not a snapback.

**7. SCHW —** Record Q3 revenue \$7.17bn (+17% YoY), record core net new assets \$190.4bn, total client assets \$13.33tn; the receivables flag mirrors disclosed margin-loan growth, not uncollected sales.

**8. CAT —** Record backlog and pricing power against macro/tariff fears; 2.21x net debt/EBITDA tempers the survival case versus the cleaner balance sheets above it.

**9. SNDK —** The field's purest rebound-or-trap test: deepest dip, peak-cycle margins, commodity NAND with no Morningstar moat; the catalyst lens loves the snapback, the moat lens calls it the clearest trap in the field.

**10. TPR —** Coach executing at full price with Kate Spade as the drag; the 2026-11-05 print decides whether the de-rating is a buying opportunity or a warning.
