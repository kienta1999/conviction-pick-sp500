# Dip conviction pick — 2026-10-13

## THE PICK
**CPRT — Copart, Inc.**, Industrials / Diversified Support Services (salvage-vehicle auctions). Market cap ~\$25.3B. Price at pick: **\$26.48** (2026-10-13 screen).

## One-paragraph thesis
Copart is the deepest genuine dip in the field: -42.1% off its 52-week high at a screen forward P/E of 15.1 versus a verified ~31x five-year average, on a business whose FY26 slowdown was a volume trough — revenue +0.4%, US insurance units -8% on lower claim frequency and hurricane comps — not a broken franchise. The balance sheet removes survival from the debate (no debt, ~\$5.7B liquidity), trough cash generation proves the model (FY26 operating cash flow \$1.604B exceeded net income \$1.480B, both verified), and the moat — zoning-scarce salvage yards feeding a two-sided auction network — is the highest irreplaceability score in the dossier (9/10). Both the moat lens and the falling-knife skeptic lens picked it first.

## Why the dip is temporary, not permanent
The verified cause is volume, not impairment: FY26 revenue grew just +0.4% as US insurance units fell -8% (claim frequency -3.4% plus hurricane comparisons), with one disclosed customer loss as a caveat. Crucially, verification corrected the panel's margin claim: FY26 operating margin was 35.4% versus 36.5% in FY25 — only ~110bp of compression, not a collapse (the ~28.5% figure in the ballots was Q4 net margin). A franchise holding a 35% operating margin and converting more cash than it earns at the trough is cyclically depressed, not structurally impaired. The receivables flag was judged real but benign-leaning: DSO drifted only ~60 to ~63 days and operating cash flow exceeded net income.

## The dip depth
-42.1% off the 52-week high, -20.1% below the 200-day SMA — the deepest drawdown in the kept field. Screen forward P/E 15.1 versus a verified ~31.15x five-year average (Public.com); analyst mean target \$39.00 (+47.3%, verified, 12 analysts).

## Why it's the category leader / why the moat is intact
Copart's yards are permitted, zoning-scarce physical assets next to major metros that a rival cannot replicate at any reasonable cost or timeline, and they feed a two-sided insurer-buyer auction network with entrenched relationships. **AI cannot disrupt it** — no model creates permitted land, tow and logistics capacity, or a salvage title process; AI is a pricing and matching tool Copart can apply to its own asset base. Irreplaceability 9/10, the field's highest.

## The rebound catalyst & timing
Volume and mix normalisation off the FY26 trough, visible in coming quarterly prints; the next earnings date is **2026-11-19** (estimated, verified across MarketBeat/Zacks/StockAnalysis; TipRanks alone says Nov 18). The ACV Auctions deal closing (expected end-2026, per prior research) is a secondary catalyst. No single binary date — the re-rate builds as total-loss volumes recover and the multiple moves from 15.1x toward history.

## Scenarios & expected value (12–18 months, research scenarios not guarantees)
- **Bear: \$20 (-24%) by ~2027-Q2.** The volume trough is structural: claim frequency keeps falling, margins stay at the trough, earnings power is permanently lower and ~13x on trough earnings is fair. This is the stop — a close breaching it exits the position.
- **Base: \$39 (+47%) by ~2027-Q2.** Volumes normalise partway, operating margin holds near 35%, and the multiple re-rates partway toward history — the verified analyst mean target.
- **Bull: \$44 (+66%) by ~2027-Q3.** Volume recovery plus a fuller multiple re-rate toward the mid-20s; note the verified margin correction removes the old margin-rebuild driver, so bull now needs volume and multiple to do the work together — say so plainly.

Base is modal: cash conversion, no debt, and a halved multiple on a held 35% operating margin. Bull is capped at 0.25 because it needs two things at once (volume recovery and re-rating).

- **Market-implied:** \$26.48 sits between bear and base, much closer to bear — the market prices the trough as near-permanent, which is the opportunity.
