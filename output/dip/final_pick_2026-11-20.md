## One-paragraph thesis

Lam Research is the number-one etch supplier in the world, and it fell 52.9% from its 52-week high and 28.1% below its 200-day moving average after reporting its best quarter on record — a pure sector de-rating on post-midterm data-centre politics and AI-capex fear, not a company miss. Consensus normalised EPS is expected to rise from \$5.82 in FY2026 to \$9.65 in FY2027 (+66%), management has raised the calendar-2026 WFE outlook three times to the mid-\$150bn range with a preliminary 2027 view above \$200bn, and the board announced a \$25bn buyback authorisation on the evening of 5 Nov 2026. The business is supply-constrained, not demand-constrained, net cash, and generating record free cash flow — the dip is about the price of fear, and the 27 Jan 2027 print is the proving ground.

## Why the dip is temporary, not permanent

The single most compelling evidence is the sequence: Lam reported its best quarter on record on 21 Oct 2026 (memory systems revenue at a record, +120% YoY) and then fell 16.4% on 5 Nov alone and ~16% in the week ended 6 Nov, with the dossier recording explicitly that Lam "did not announce a company-specific operational setback." The 5 Nov trigger was the SOX falling 12.25% — its worst day since March 2020 — after the 3 Nov midterm sweep raised data-centre construction-restriction fears, plus a December rate-hike scare. Management said supply-chain constraints, not weak orders, held the guide down. Then Nvidia's 17 Nov beat-and-raise (revenue \$115.8bn, +103% YoY; Q4 guide \$130bn) confirmed AI demand rather than a collapse. A permanently impaired business does not report record revenue, raise its market outlook for the third time in a year, and then guide the next quarter up.

## Why it's the category leader / why the moat is intact

Lam is the #1 etch supplier (~45–50% of dry etch; TEL a clear second at ~18–22%) and #2 in deposition behind Applied Materials, and the #3 overall WFE vendor by revenue. The moat is process know-how plus >8,000 patents, qualification lock-in (a tool qualified for a step on a node tends to stay the tool of record), and a large installed base with a growing services stream (CSBG \$2.49bn in Q1 FY27, +40% YoY).

**Why AI or a substitute cannot disrupt it:** AI cannot substitute for etch — every chip pattern must be physically etched, and there is no software or alternative-material shortcut around it. Every node transition (GAA at 2nm, 3D NAND beyond 300 layers, TSV for HBM) *raises* etch intensity, and Lam's cryogenic etch is described as the only proven high-volume path for HBM4 TSVs. The AI build-out raises Lam's content per wafer rather than threatening it. The residual threat is share loss at the margin to TEL/AMEC/Naura, not obsolescence. Dossier irreplaceability score: **9/10**. The panel's moat lens (C) and skeptic lens (D) both rated this the lowest AI-disruption risk in the field.

## The rebound catalyst & timing

- **Q2 FY27 earnings, 27 Jan 2027** (screen date) — the proving print. Guidance given on the Q1 call: revenue ~\$8.6bn ± \$400m, EPS \$2.33.
- **WFE step-up:** management raised calendar-2026 WFE to the mid-\$150bn range (third raise in 2026) and gave a preliminary 2027 view above \$200bn; Bernstein forecasts \$217bn in 2027 (+39%) and \$279bn in 2028.
- **Buyback floor:** \$25bn authorisation announced the evening of 5 Nov 2026 (~10% of market cap at the time), plus a \$0.33 quarterly dividend; Q1 FY27 FCF was a record \$3.24bn, so the authorisation is fundable. The stock rose 7.7% the day after the announcement.
- **Demand confirmation already in:** Nvidia's 17 Nov beat-and-raise (Q4 guide \$130bn, FY2028 growth raised to ~75%) supports the demand side.
- Timing: sentiment can turn in weeks on any data-centre policy de-escalation; the fundamental proof lands 27 Jan 2027.

## Key swing factors

2. WFE spending in 2027 — the preliminary outlook above \$200bn must be confirmed by customer capex (Bernstein: \$217bn).
3. Supply-chain constraints — management cited component supply, not demand, as the guide limiter; easing is a tailwind, persistence caps the EPS step-up.
4. China export controls — China supplied ~75% of Q1's sequential growth; further tightening compresses the fastest-growing revenue leg.
5. Memory cycle — ~46% of systems revenue is memory-exposed, tying Lam to the same NAND/DRAM cycle as MU; a NAND glut would hit the WFE outlook.

## Key risks

- **WFE cyclicality (the trap case):** if the 2027 WFE step-up above \$200bn does not materialise (data-centre delays, hyperscaler capex cuts, China export controls), Lam's operating leverage cuts both ways and the FY27 EPS step-up evaporates.
- **Customer concentration:** a small number of memory and foundry customers drive systems revenue; a single customer's delay moves the quarter.
- **China exposure:** China supplied ~75% of Q1's sequential growth; export-control tightening is the most-cited company-specific risk.
- **Memory-cycle linkage:** ~46% of systems revenue is memory; the same NAND-glut risk that dogs MU (GF Securities: NAND prices flat in Q1 2027, then down for three quarters) would hit Lam's order book.
- **Multiple risk:** the stock is cheap on FY2028 earnings (16.6x) but ~22x on a standard NTM basis per TIKR — the "cheapness" depends on the FY27/FY28 EPS step-up actually arriving.
