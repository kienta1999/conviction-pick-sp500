## Per-name thesis

**1. KEYS — Keysight Technologies.** The dip is temporary in the cleanest sense in the field: the strongest quarter in company history (Q4 FY26 revenue +41%, orders +53%, EPS \$3.61 vs \$3.37) met with a 7.07% selloff on a Nasdaq-wide down day, because the AI trade unwound. The rebound catalyst is dated — Q1 FY27 earnings in February 2027, guided \$2.04–2.06bn revenue vs \$1.925bn consensus — and the moat holds: ~29.9% share of electronic measurement, calibration/certification lock-in, and the Spirent deal removed the closest rival. AI multiplies test demand (AI bookings ~2.5x prior year); no model can certify a 1.6T optical link. The trap case is an AI-capex peak turning the record order book into a pull-forward — the one risk the veto lens weighed and discounted, because demand exceeding supply is the opposite of a trap signature.

**2. FIX — Comfort Systems USA.** The dip is temporary: a record Q3 (revenue \$3.77bn +53.7%, EPS \$13.91 vs \$12.71, backlog \$17.81bn +90% YoY, eighth straight sequential increase) sold off 9.26% on the print and a further 15.08% on 4 Nov on Texas data-centre politics. The rebound catalyst is backlog conversion plus margin normalisation in H1 2027 (management: same-store revenue mid-40% in 2026, mid-20% in 2027), with Q4 earnings 18 Feb 2027 as the first test. The moat is physical — branch density, skilled trades, modular prefabrication — and AI cannot install HVAC. Balance sheet is the strongest in the field (net cash ~\$2.11bn). The trap case is the central one for this name: if the data-centre build-out is a cycle, not a structural shift, tech at ~60% of revenue is concentration, and a contractor multiple on lower earnings is a double de-rating.

**3. CAT — Caterpillar.** The dip is temporary on a cyclical base: Q3 sales \$20.68bn +17.3%, adj. EPS \$7.20 +45.5%, record backlog \$78.1bn +96% YoY — yet shares fell 9.3% because management held guidance rather than raising it. The rebound catalyst is the Q4 print and January 2027 outlook (2 Feb 2027) confirming ~\$32.18 FY27 EPS. The moat is the widest in the batch (9/10): ~160 dealers, ~2,700 branches, 190 countries, a services flywheel, and large-engine orders booked to 2029 / turbines to 2030. AI is a tailwind (data-centre power demand, autonomous mining), not a threat. The honest caveat: at ~19x forward earnings CAT has normalised to its historical average — fairly valued, not cheap — so the upside is entirely in whether \$32.18 EPS survives a data-centre pause. The trap case is cyclical peak earnings: CAT has fallen 50% before and stayed down for years in a construction recession.

**4. APH — Amphenol.** The dip is temporary: record Q3 (sales \$10.2bn +65% YoY, +37% organically, adj. EPS \$0.80 vs ~\$0.73, orders \$12.2bn at a 1.19x book-to-bill) gave back its entire post-print gain within days on profit-taking, a CEO Form 144 filing and AI-infra risk-off — no miss, no guidance cut, no demand break. The moat holds and is strengthening: Amphenol just overtook TE Connectivity as the world's #1 connector maker for the first time in 45 years, and AI is a tailwind (IT datacom organic growth +77% in Q3). The rebound catalyst is already guided: Q4 sales \$10.3–10.5bn and EPS \$0.81–0.83, confirmed 27 Jan 2027. The caveat is valuation: the least washed-out name in the field (forward P/E disputed, 20.2x on the screen vs ~35–41x in coverage), so the rebound depends on AI capex staying strong rather than on a cheap multiple — and debt is up 165% since FY2024 on the \$10.5bn CommScope deal.

**5. GS — Goldman Sachs.** The dip is temporary with a real cyclical overlay: the world's best deal franchise fell 14% in a week to ~21 Nov 2026 on the AI sell-off plus headlines about the \$44bn SpaceX GPU financing it priced with Apollo — while its own news was good (Anthropic IPO fees ~\$113m, ROE 21% annualised, ~60% of revenues now "more durable"). The moat is judgement-led advisory and bespoke structured finance, which AI creates work for rather than commoditising. The rebound catalyst is Q4 results 19 Jan 2027 — the test of whether IB fees held up — plus any AI-equity stabilisation. The trap case: if the AI-financing boom is 2026's leveraged LBO debt, or a genuine 2027 deal drought cuts IB fees from record levels, 10.8x forward is a peak-earnings illusion. Earnings-quality flag LOW_CASH_CONVERSION judged benign (bank broker-dealer cash-flow artefact).

**6. VST — Vistra.** The dip is temporary with a real 2028 cliff: record Q3 adj. EBITDA \$2.378bn +50% YoY, 2027 EBITDA guided \$7.7–8.5bn, 98% of 2027 hedged — but the stock fell 13.8% on 4 Nov on the Texas election (Hinojosa's data-centre moratorium platform) and weak Texas power (~\$30/MWh). The moat is physical generation that cannot be coded around (~50 GW, nuclear + gas + PPAs with Meta/Microsoft/AWS), and existing grid connections gain value when new ones are frozen. The rebound is gated by Texas politics (inauguration 19 Jan 2027) and the 2028 hedge cliff (only ~78% hedged). The trap case: a multi-year moratorium strands the Texas growth pipeline and the 2028 earnings run-rate misses. Targets imply a re-rating to ~19–20x that the market has not yet granted while Texas is unresolved.

**7. META — Meta Platforms.** The dip is a self-inflicted capex cycle, not a broken ad business: Q3 revenue \$64.6bn +26% YoY, DAP 3.64bn, but capex \$34.91bn in the quarter drove FCF to −\$2.12bn (first negative quarterly FCF in a decade), buybacks were \$0 in 2026, and a \$37.5bn stock raise at \$625 plus a planned \$60bn ATM (now suspended by Delaware litigation) spooked holders. The moat holds (8/10): network effects plus AI-strengthened ad targeting (GEM lifted conversions). The rebound needs a credible 2027 capex ceiling at the Q4 print (late Jan 2027) — JPMorgan's 2027 capex forecast is \$255bn (+78%). The trap case: capex becomes a treadmill with no incremental return, FCF stays negative, and dilution via the ATM becomes routine. Note: META is on the repo's basket exclusion list (owner's employer compliance) — it is ranked, not executed.

**8. EXPE — Expedia Group.** The dip is a one-quarter guide reset plus an AI multiple scare: Q3 beat (revenue \$4.94bn +12%, adj. EPS \$9.39 +24%) but Q4 guidance missed on election/geopolitical softness, and OpenAI's acquisition of AI travel agent Odessia (10 Nov 2026) revived the disintermediation fear. The moat is split (5/10): B2B distribution (bookings +19%, revenue +23%) is sticky plumbing; the consumer front end is exactly what an AI agent commoditises. **VALUE-TRAP FLAG:** if AI agents complete bookings at scale, Expedia becomes a wholesale inventory supplier at thinner take rates and the 48%-of-revenue marketing line becomes a tax paid to Google/Meta/OpenAI. Cheap at 9.7x forward on a still-growing, cash-generative business — but the AI question caps the re-rating until answered. Earnings-quality flag RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN judged benign (merchant-model artefact; CFO/NI 2.16x).

**9. CSCO — Cisco.** A real AI beneficiary whose record quarter was sold because margins and order momentum, not revenue, are the binding constraints: Q1 FY27 revenue \$18.52bn +24% (fastest in >15 years), EPS \$1.40 +40%, FY27 guide raised (AI hyperscaler revenue \$9bn) — yet −6.2% on 13 Nov on gross margin −250bp to 65.6% (memory costs/hardware mix) and order growth slowing 35% → 21%. The moat is installed-base switching costs and campus dominance (53.5% share), but Cisco is #3 in AI data-centre Ethernet (18.2% vs Nvidia 20.4%, Arista 18.7%) — competing for share, not defending a monopoly. The rebound needs a Q2 margin hold (10 Feb 2027); even then the upside is earnings-growth-plus-dividend, not multiple re-expansion, since the stock now sits only ~10% above its own 5-year forward average (15.7x). Earnings-quality flag INVENTORY_BUILD judged mostly benign (pre-build against a \$9.3bn AI order book), but the \$17.2bn purchase-commitment book is the number that would turn it into a problem.

**10. APP — AppLovin.** The operating rebound already printed: Q3 revenue \$2.18bn +55%, EPS \$4.30 vs \$3.94, shares +~51% in the days after — the remaining upside is a re-rating on Q4 proof (10 Feb 2027) that model timing and e-commerce are durable, at ~19x forward. The dip was a multiple collapse (35x → ~16x) on a <1% Q2 revenue miss, not an earnings collapse. **VALUE-TRAP FLAG:** AppLovin owns no inventory — it routes above Meta/Google/TikTok, which are building their own AI tools; Unity is a resurgent competitor; advertisers follow ROI quarter to quarter. The moat scored 5/10, the joint-lowest in the field, which is why only one ballot ranked it (at #5) despite the deepest drawdown (−48.3%) and the highest TTM revenue growth (44.6%).
