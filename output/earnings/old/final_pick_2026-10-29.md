## The fallback

If the print gaps down and the exit is ugly, what you are left holding: a qualified, process-critical consumables supplier (CMP pads/slurries, advanced-packaging films) whose products are designed into fabs' processes — requalification risks yield on multi-million-dollar wafer lots, which is real switching friction (research irreplaceability 7/10; Phase 3.5 notes the "high switching costs" phrasing is analyst characterisation, not a company disclosure). AI increases materials intensity per wafer (more layers, more stacking), and the local-for-local footprint (Taiwan, Korea, Vietnam, Delaware) fits supply-chain regionalisation. The docks: it is a materials supplier, not a process owner — if wafer starts fall, consumables fall with a lag and no pricing power offsets volume; China concentration (~33% of sales, verified from the Q2 10-Q geographic table: \$478m of \$1,429m) is a single-region failure mode; standalone execution (IT independence, transformation costs, new CFO) is unproven over a full cycle. Net debt ~\$3bn sharpens any demand shock. A hold here is a hold on a real business with a real single-region risk — not a disaster, not a bargain to average into blindly.
