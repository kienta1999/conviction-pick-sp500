## 5. THE GUIDE — the decisive section

### (a) Does PepsiCo guide, and at what granularity?
Yes — **annual only, ranges, no quarterly guide.** Granularity: (i) organic revenue growth range, (ii) core constant-currency EPS growth range, plus core tax rate (~22% for 2026), capex (<5% of net revenue), FCF conversion (≥80% in 2026 incl. ~$1B final TCJA tax payment; ≥90% in 2027), FX translation assumption (~+1pt to reported revenue and core EPS for 2026), M&A contribution (~+1pt to reported revenue), dividend and buyback (~$8.9B total returns in 2026: ~$7.9B dividends + ~$1B buybacks). The reported-USD translation given at Q4 2025 was FY26 core EPS **$8.55–$8.71** and net revenue $97.7–99.6B (MLQ/MarketBeat summaries of the guide).

### (b) The numbers the Q3 guide will be judged against
- **Company FY2026 guide (unchanged since Dec 2025): organic revenue +2% to +4%; core constant-currency EPS +4% to +6%.**
- **Street FY2026:** EPS **$8.57** (Barchart/MarketBeat, Oct 1) — i.e. the Street is already at the **low end / just above the floor** of the $8.55–8.71 translated range. Zacks FY26: $8.58 (newest crawl) / $8.57 (prior).
- **Street Q4 2026:** EPS **$2.43–$2.44** (Zacks: $2.43 newest / $2.44 prior; high $2.55, low $2.35), vs Q4 2025 actual $2.26 → **+7.5–8.0% YoY growth expected in Q4** — by far the fastest quarter of the year.
- **Street FY2027:** EPS **$8.96–$9.01** (Barchart $8.96, +4.6%; Zacks $8.99–$9.01, +~5.0%). No company FY27 guide exists yet (first given Dec 2026 / Feb 2027) — Q3 commentary on 2027 (pricing, costs) is what TD Cowen explicitly flagged as the risk.

### (c) Does the run-rate support the forward numbers?
**No — the FY26 guide is explicitly back-half loaded, and Q4 must accelerate sharply.**
- H1 2026 core cc EPS growth: **+3% YTD** (Q1 +5% cc, Q2 only **+1% cc** — Q2 call / Zacks), against a full-year guide of **+4–6%**. Arithmetic: to land even the **floor (+4%)**, H2 cc EPS must grow ~+5%; to land mid-point (+5%), H2 must grow ~+7%.
- Q4 consensus (+7.5–8.0% reported EPS YoY) embeds exactly that acceleration. Supports cited by management: (i) **tariff-refund claims ≈ +1pt of full-year EPS growth** (Schmitt, Q2 call — see §8), (ii) record productivity savings, (iii) easier comps, (iv) international strength (H1 net revenue +7% reported; international guided to exceed $40B in FY26).
- Organic revenue run-rate (+2.4–2.6% in H1) sits inside the +2–4% guide but gives **no cushion** — any Q3 organic print below +2% puts even the revenue floor in question for the year.
- **Verdict on (c): the forward numbers imply an acceleration not yet shown in any 2026 quarter.** That is the central guide risk, and JPMorgan’s Sep 29 note says it in so many words: PEP must “lean more heavily on productivity in Q4 to deliver guidance,” with higher costs potentially offsetting those savings.

## 7. MOAT / AI-IRREPLACEABILITY

- **Distribution scale — the core moat.** Direct-store-delivery (DSD) for Frito-Lay plus a bottling/fountain network reaching >200 countries; products consumed >1B times/day; nearly $94B net revenue in 2025 (company). A challenger cannot replicate shelf presence, route density, cold-drink placement and foodservice contracts on any investable timescale. International is now >$40B of revenue, ~2/3 of beverage volume and >50% of food volume (Q2 call).
- **Brand portfolio:** 22+ billion-dollar brands (Lay’s, Doritos, Cheetos, Gatorade, Pepsi-Cola, Mountain Dew, Quaker, Tostitos, SodaStream, plus Poppi / Alani Nu exposure). Category captaincy in salty snacks in North America.
- **Switching costs / substitution:** Low formal switching costs (a consumer can buy Coke or private label tomorrow) but very high **habit, shelf and availability** friction — the moat is distribution + brand salience, not lock-in. Real substitution risks are private label on price (the affordability problem) and category shrinkage (GLP-1, sugar/health regulation), not a competitor routing around the network. Coca-Cola is out-executing PEP in North America beverages (KO Q2 organic +6.0% vs PEP +2.4%) — the moat is intact but currently being out-run inside it.
- **AI-irreplaceability:** Essentially total. Physical manufacturing, trucking/DSD logistics (now adding autonomous middle-mile via Gatik), retail shelf negotiation and taste/brand equity are not disintermediated by AI; AI/productivity is a cost tailwind (the Elliott plan: ~20% US SKU cut, plant closures, automation, record productivity savings targeted 2026).
- **Irreplaceability score: 8.5 / 10** (deducted for: no true switching-cost lock-in, NA share losses to KO/private label, and a portfolio concentrated in categories — salty snacks, full-sugar soda — facing structural health/GLP-1 headwinds).

**18-month frame — if this gaps down 15% (≈ $107) and must be held 18 months: OPPORTUNITY, not disaster.** At ~$107 PEP would trade at ~12.5× the $8.57 FY26 consensus and yield ~5.5% from a dividend raised for 54 consecutive years, backed by ~$96.9B TTM revenue, CFO/NI 1.29 and net debt/EBITDA 2.25× (screen) — none of the balance-sheet or cash-flow markers of a broken staple are present. The disaster case requires the moat itself to fail (sustained NA volume/share collapse + guide cut below flat EPS), not a bad quarter. The honest caveat: it could be a **slow** opportunity — the 2025 precedent is a year spent walking a guide down, and a 15% gap-down on a guide cut likely means 2–3 dead quarters before re-rating.

## 8. RISKS SPECIFIC TO THIS PRINT

1. **The back-half-loaded guide (biggest risk).** H1 cc EPS +3% vs FY guide +4–6%; Q2 alone +1% cc. Q3 must show cc EPS accelerating or the FY floor becomes arithmetically implausible without an heroic Q4 — and the market now knows it (JPMorgan, Sep 29).
2. **Tariff-refund dependence.** ~**1 full point of FY26 EPS growth** is expected refund claims for tariffs paid last year (Schmitt, Q2 call). That is a one-off, timing-dependent item: if refunds slip or are smaller, the low end is missed. It also flatters the growth rate — ex-refund, underlying cc EPS growth is +3–5% guided, +2% H1 underlying.
3. **North America foods — the Q1 recovery stalled.** PFNA: Q1 volume +2% / revenue +2% → Q2 volume **flat** / revenue **−2%** (unfavourable net pricing from the Feb price cuts of up to 15% on Lay’s/Doritos/Cheetos). JPMorgan channel data (Sep 29) says salty-snacks softness continued into Q3. Any Q3 PFNA volume ≤0 kills the recovery narrative the Elliott plan is sold on.
4. **The pricing U-turn.** After cutting prices up to 15% in February, PEP confirmed (Bloomberg/Reuters, late Sep) **low-to-mid-single-digit price increases** on some chips, dips and sodas for late 2026 / early 2027, still below pre-cut levels. Deutsche Bank: successive interventions suggest the problem is “more difficult to solve”; TD Cowen: “shifting narrative on affordability” raises 2027 risk. Q3 Q&A on this is a headline risk in both directions.
5. **North America beverages volume.** PBNA Q2 revenue +7% reported but **unit volume −4%**, margin −90bp (Alani arrangements, weak convenience channel). NA beverage volume has now declined for years (Q3 2025 was the 12th consecutive quarterly decline). No inflection found in Q3 channel commentary.
6. **Tough / flattering comps both ways.** Q3 2025 base: organic only +1.3% (easy comp — a Q3 organic miss vs +2% would be damning), but Q3 is PEP’s seasonally strongest EPS quarter ($2.29 year-ago is the highest quarter of 2025), and consensus $2.30 demands matching it with weaker NA trends.
7. **Commodity/cost pressure rising into H2.** Management guided to “more pressure from a commodity standpoint” in H2 (Q2 call), with fuel, ingredients, packaging and shipping cited by Deutsche Bank (Sep 28). PBNA/PFNA margin already −40 to −90bp.

### Earnings-quality flags — JPM

The cash test for a bank is capital generation and distribution: JPM holds $303B CET1 (14.1%), just raised the dividend to $1.65/qtr, and runs a ~73% LTM net payout (Q2'26: $4.0B common dividend + $6.2B buybacks). **Verdict: structural, not a trap.**

The screen flag likely fires on loan/accrued-interest growth outpacing revenue growth, which is simply balance-sheet expansion in a credit-growth phase, backstopped by a $303B CET1 fortress and NCOs at 3.34% card (improving). **Verdict: balance-sheet growth, not phantom revenue.**
