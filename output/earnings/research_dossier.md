### 5. Guidance and What's Changed — Why the Beat Record Stayed Clean While the Stock Collapsed (the central question)
Intuit's Q4/FY26 quantitative record is spotless — 4/4 beats on both EPS and revenue. The stock's ~49% 12-month collapse and 20% single-day crash on May 21, 2026 were **not caused by a numbers miss**. Three things did it:
1. **TurboTax pricing failure, admitted by management.** At the May 20, 2026 call, Intuit said it "did not have the overall tax season we expected," having misjudged its price positioning against cheaper DIY alternatives for price-sensitive filers, and cut the TurboTax revenue forecast.

3. **A sector-wide "SaaSpocalypse" valuation reset.** Morningstar downgraded Intuit's economic moat rating from **Wide to Narrow** amid AI-disruption concerns for guided, per-seat software. Intuit's forward multiple compressed from a historical ~35–40x down toward the current 13.3x — a structural re-rating, not a one-quarter event.
4. **Compounding litigation overhang.** Starting mid-June 2026, multiple securities-fraud investigations/class-action solicitations (BFA Law and others) were announced over Intuit's TurboTax pricing representations ahead of and during the 2026 tax season, keeping headline pressure on the stock into late June even with no new fundamental data.

Net: this is a "beat the number, miss the narrative" stock — the quantitative track record has never broken, but management's own commentary and strategic moves have repeatedly been the actual swing factor. Since the May 21 trough (~$307), the stock has rallied hard — **+23.4% over the trailing 30 days** into this print — suggesting some of the pessimism has already unwound, but litigation and moat-downgrade narratives remain live going into Investor Day on Sept. 17. Morningstar's Wide→Narrow moat downgrade reflects this bifurcation. **Irreplaceability score: 6/10** (would be 8–9 for QuickBooks alone, materially lower for TurboTax).
