# PANELIST C — Quality / moat & irreplaceability — Earnings panel, RUN 2026-10-14

My lens weights the fallback above the trade: margins, returns on capital, balance sheet, and above all whether a customer could in-source the product or a substitute — including an AI-native one — could route around it. The question I ask of every name is: would I be happy to be stuck holding this for 12–18 months after a bad print?

## SINGLE-PICK BALLOT

- **Top pick:** CME
- **Runner-up:** BNY
- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** CME is the one name in this field where the quarter is substantially pre-disclosed and the franchise cannot be replicated: CME's own release shows record Q3 ADV of 29.4M contracts, +16% YoY, with record September ADV of 31.8M, +22%, led by rates (+13%) and equity index (+23%). There is no EPS or revenue guide to cut, so the usual earnings-season trap — beat the quarter, cut the guide, sell off — is structurally absent. That is not already in the price: the stock closed $278.03 on 2026-10-13, −15.5% vs its 52-week high of $329.16, flat over the last month (−0.87%), Hold-rated with consensus targets essentially at the price (~$278.83–$289.75). The only live question is rate-per-contract conversion, and at ~65% operating margin and a 63.3% net margin (Q2 2026) CME can absorb a soft RPC print without the earnings model breaking.
- **Key evidence from the record:** Record Q3 ADV 29.4M contracts, +16% YoY (CME release, 2026-10-02) — the volume that drives the quarter is already public, at a record, before the print.
- **Priced-in risk (THE TRAP):** low — flat for a month, −15.5% off the high, Hold consensus with ~no target upside, no run into the print, and the screen's realised reaction proxy is only 1.6%; the dossier scores priced-in 3/10. The trap here is not crowding, it is assuming record volume automatically means record revenue (see Top risk).
- **The guide:** There is no formal EPS/revenue guide — the de facto guide is monthly ADV plus the annual expense/tax framework, which has been reaffirmed with no material adverse revision found. The number the market is carrying for next quarter is Q4 2026 EPS of ~$2.97–$2.98 (FY2026 ~$12.27–$12.29). The run-rate supports it explicitly: H1 actuals were $3.36 + $2.99 = $6.35, so FY $12.29 minus H1 minus the ~$2.97 Q3 bar implies Q4 of ~$2.97 — flat versus Q3, squarely inside the last four quarters' $2.68–$3.36 range, requiring continuation, not unseen acceleration.
- **Plan A** — hold through the print, because the volume is pre-disclosed at a record, guide-risk is structurally the lowest in the field (dossier 2/10), positioning is not crowded, and the reaction asymmetry (1.6% priced proxy vs a +5.0% realised upside tail in Q2) favours being in before the number.
- **Fallback if the print disappoints:** Yes — this is the name I would most happily be stuck with for 12–18 months. The moat is the deepest liquidity network in listed derivatives (dossier 9.5/10): liquidity begets liquidity, clearing is a natural monopoly, a customer cannot in-source the clearing/liquidity pool, and AI does not intermediate a central-limit order book away — volatility and electronic flow feed it. A −15% gap would almost certainly mean a low-volatility regime, a macro condition, not franchise damage; the ~65% operating margin and the $1.30/quarter dividend persist through it while you wait.
- **Event scenario:** beat-and-raise (strong RPC on record ADV) ~+3% to +5% / in-line ~0% to +1.5% / miss (RPC collapse) ~−2% to −4%.
- **Top risk:** RPC / mix — record ADV converting to mediocre revenue, exactly as in Q2 2026 when near-record ADV produced revenue growth of only +0.8% YoY as mix shifted toward micro / lower-RPC products; the $0.02 late creep in consensus to $2.98 (MarketBeat, 2026-10-14) on pre-disclosed volume is how a "known" quarter still disappoints.
- **Conviction (1-10):** 8

### Top 3 — detail

**1. CME.** The most compelling record point is the pre-disclosed record Q3 ADV of 29.4M contracts (+16% YoY, September 31.8M / +22%) sitting on a ~65% operating margin — volume risk is already retired, only RPC conversion remains. The reaction record is the field's cleanest: four straight small EPS beats, beat_up_rate 1.00, last-four reactions +5.0% / +0.5% / +0.5% / −0.3%. Guide risk is the lowest here (2/10 — no EPS guide exists to cut, and the implied Q4 of ~$2.97 is flat continuation), and priced-in risk is low (3/10 — −15.5% off the high, flat month, targets at price). Rough moves: beat ~+3% to +5% / in-line ~0% to +1.5% / miss ~−2% to −4%. **Plan A.**

**2. BNY.** The quality evidence is a custody franchise administering ~$59–63T of assets — roughly a fifth of the world's investable assets, embedded in >90% of the Fortune 100 — printing a 39.8% pretax margin and 31.1% ROTCE in Q2 2026, with revenue, NII (+20% YoY) and margin all accelerating into a Q3 bar ($2.26) that is a sequential step *down* from Q2's actual $2.46. Its reaction record is the best beat-to-up conversion in the field (beat_up_rate 0.875; last four +5% / +2% / +2% / −2%), but guide risk is real (5/10): BNY is a true numeric guider and must reaffirm the July raise (FY revenue +10–11%, NII +12–13%, expenses 6–7%) and hold CET1 at ≥11.0% — Oct 13 proved this tape pays for the guide line, not the EPS beat, and sold even Goldman's smash beat. Priced-in risk is low (3/10 — −15.0% off the $165.84 high, −13.13% over 90 days), though options imply ±4.7% vs a 3.0% realised proxy. Rough moves: beat-and-reaffirm ~+4% to +6% / in-line ~0% to +2% / guide trim or capital slip ~−5% to −7%. **Plan B** — A is earned only by the guide-reaffirmation plus capital lines on the call, not by the beat alone.

**3. BLK.** The compelling data point is quality compounding at scale: adjusted operating margin 45.9% in Q2 2026 (near a 5-year high, +260bp YoY), AUM of $15.34T with $192B of Q2 net inflows, and organic base-fee growth of ≥~5% for eight straight quarters — clients outsource *to* BlackRock, and Aladdin is the industry's operating system, so in-sourcing and AI-substitution risk are minimal (moat 9/10). The reaction record is strong (4/4 beats averaging +7.3%, beat_up_rate 0.875, Q2 +3.5% premarket), guide risk is low (3/10 — no numeric guide; the Q4 bar of $15.11 EPS is the normal seasonal performance-fee step-up, though it requires a normal Q4 fee season), and priced-in risk is moderate-low (4/10 — flat for a month, −12.6% off the $1,219.94 high, implied move 3.3% *below* its 4.5% realised proxy). Rough moves: beat with HPS clearing ~+3% to +5% / in-line ~0% to +1% / HPS re-acceleration or markdown ~−5% to −7%. **Plan B** — the HPS redemption queue is easing (11.5% of the Corporate Lending Fund, down from 13.3%) but is still >2× the 5% repurchase cap, so the fallback stays excellent only if the print confirms the queue keeps clearing rather than becoming a franchise-trust event.

**Deliberately left at the bottom:** VRT, FAST and LVS — VRT because its power/thermal hardware has real substitutes (Eaton, Schneider, hyperscaler in-housing) and AI is its demand driver, not its protection; FAST because a distributor is an intermediary its large customers continuously re-bid on price — the gross-margin slide from 45.3% to 44.6% is that substitutability in the numbers, at 36.8× forward with no valuation airbag; and LVS last because, while the Macau/Singapore licences themselves are irreplaceable, the equity sits behind ~$19B of gross debt (D/E 15.27) and an $8B IR2 spend into a Macau market whose GGR fell year-on-year for four straight months — the fallback holder is paid last, and 12–18 months may not be long enough.
