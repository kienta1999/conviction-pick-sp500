### 6. How much is priced in
- Last 1 month: **-7.6%** (Finnhub, as of 9/18 close region); last ~3–6 months: rebounded +24–27% off the April lows, now $116.14 vs 52-week high $132.87 (Finnhub) / $141.89 (Barchart 52w high Aug 2025). **-8.8% below the 52-week high** (screen).

- Options-implied move (TipRanks, 9/21/26, ATM straddle): **±7.63%**. Screen reaction_avg_abs_move: **3.65%**. The options market prices **2.1× the stock's own average absolute move** — elevated event uncertainty relative to a calm mover (EVR 2.1/10 per optionslam).

### 7. Moat / AI-irreplaceability: 7/10
Payroll is compliance-critical, recurring, and sticky for 840,000 SMB customers; switching payroll/HR providers mid-year is genuinely painful (tax filings, benefits enrollment). Paycor integration + proprietary HR dataset + WISE agentic AI engine are offense, not defense. Could customers in-source? Large enterprises can (ADP/Workday already compete there), but PAYX's core SMB base cannot easily. AI substitutes: agentic AI *assists* HR work; it doesn't remove the legal/compliance requirement to run payroll correctly — AI-native competitors are not a credible threat to this moat today. A 15% gap held 18 months: with a ~4% dividend yield, ~44% margins, and a reversion to ~8% EPS growth, that is an **opportunity** (you'd be buying a high-quality compounder at ~15× forward), not a disaster.

### 8. Risks specific to THIS print
- The quarter isn't the risk — the **FY27 guide reaffirm** is. If management trims organic growth commentary or the Paycor integration slips, the Q4 selloff repeats (that selloff was entirely about 5–6% vs double digits).
- "Interest on funds held for clients" guided $195–205M — a rate-sensitive line that could come in soft.
- Beats are shrinking (+0.8% last time); a headline miss on a $1.32–1.33 band would be an event.
- Macro: SMB employment softness would show up in Management Solutions revenue (the largest segment).

### 9. Scores
- **Beat-likelihood: 6/10** — 4/4 streak intact but surprise margins narrowing to +0.8% and the bar is set exactly on guide run-rate.
- **Guide-risk: 5/10** — consensus FY EPS ($5.96) already sits at guide midpoint; a reaffirm should suffice, but management has no room to be coy after the Q4 growth-slowdown selloff.
- **Priced-in: 4/10** — not priced for good news (-8.8% off highs, cheap vs own P/E history), though the 7.63% implied move is 2× the stock's typical 3.65% absolute move, so expect a swing either way.
- **Verdict:** A quality compounder priced like a growth-scare; the print matters less than whether management reaffirms the 7–9% EPS guide without new caveats.

### 10. Plan A or Plan B?
**Plan B** — wait for the number. Two of the last four beats sold off, and the June print proved the quarter is not the trade; the FY27 growth slowdown narrative is. A hold-through case (Plan A) requires: a beat *plus* a clean reaffirm of 7–9% EPS growth *plus* Paycor/organic momentum commentary — only knowable after the call.

### 7. Moat / AI-irreplaceability: 8/10
Route density is the moat: 1M+ of 16–20M addressable North American businesses are customers; uniform rental is a logistics business where route economics get better with scale and bundling uniforms + mats + first aid + safety with one vendor makes switching costly and operationally disruptive. No credible AI-native substitute for renting and laundering physical uniforms; AI (route optimization, SAP) is a cost tailwind for CTAS, not a threat. Pricing power: record 51% gross margins speak for themselves. A 15% gap held 18 months: **an opportunity** — 43 straight years of dividend growth, ~11% EPS growth compounding at 8–9% revenue growth; you'd be buying the dip in a top-decile compounder.

- UniFirst timing/FTC headlines could add noise on the call.
- Zacks flags: higher SG&A/employee expenses and a stronger USD as headwinds into the quarter.

- **Guide-risk: 3/10** — the forward consensus ($5.42–5.49) sits inside the guide ($5.36–5.50); CTAS has raised 3× in FY26 and the guide excludes UniFirst noise. Risk is only that a mere reaffirm disappoints relative to the raise-every-quarter habit.
- **Priced-in: 5/10** — mid-range on its own forward multiple, 9% off highs after retracing most of the July spike, implied 5.32% only slightly above its 4.41% average move. Some good news priced, not all.
- **Verdict:** The classic beat-and-raise machine with accelerating EPS; the danger is not a miss but a "good not great" quarter at a full multiple.

### 10. Plan A or Plan B?
