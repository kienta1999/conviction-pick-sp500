## 5. THE GUIDE (load-bearing)
**(a) Does PepsiCo guide, and at what granularity?** Yes — percentage-based, not absolute dollars. FY2026 official guide (from the Feb 3, 2026 Q4/FY25 release and reaffirmed Apr 16 and Jul 9, 2026): organic revenue growth **+2% to +4%**; core constant-currency EPS growth **+4% to +6%**. (Zacks 2026-07-10; MarketBeat also lists company FY26 guidance as EPS $8.550–8.710, which appears to be MarketBeat's derived absolute presentation, not company wording — flag as secondhand.) Also commits to ~$8.9B shareholder returns in FY26 (~$1B buybacks + $5.92 annualized dividend; 54th consecutive annual dividend increase). Sources: quartr.com Q1/Q2 2026 release PDFs; fool.com 2026-04-19.
**(b) Forward bars:** FY2026 consensus EPS **$8.57** (Zacks, Sep 4, 2026); FY2026 revenue consensus **$98.97B** (+5.4% YoY) (ainvest, Aug 2026). Q4 2026 consensus EPS not found; derived arithmetic: YTD core EPS $3.81 (Q1 $1.61 + Q2 $2.20) → H2 consensus ≈ $8.57 − $3.81 = $4.76, and with Q3 consensus $2.30, implied Q4 ≈ $2.46. Treat as arithmetic, not a published figure.
**(c) Does the run-rate support it?** YTD core constant-currency EPS is **+3%** (Q1 +5%, Q2 +1%) vs a full-year guide of +4–6% — the guide is **back-half loaded**; the CFO explicitly said earnings are tracking toward the low end (Jul 9, 2026). The guide needs an H2 acceleration (including ~1 pt from tariff-refund claims and tariff-related items noted by Zacks, 2026-07-10, plus expected NA improvement in H2) — that acceleration is **not yet shown in the data**. Organic revenue YTD +2.5% sits inside the +2–4% range. Verdict: revenue guide supported; EPS guide leans on unshown H2 acceleration.
**(d) Guide history, last 8 quarters:** FY2025: initial guide (Feb 2025) low-single-digit core constant-currency EPS decline → Q2'25 trim → Q3'25 (Oct 8, 2025) **raised** to −0.5% decline from −1.5% decline (stock +4.2%). FY2026: set Feb 3, 2026 (+4–6% core CC EPS, +2–4% organic rev; stock +5%) → Q1'26 Apr 16 **reaffirmed** (stock +2.3%) → Q2'26 Jul 9 **reaffirmed but "low-end" caveat** (stock −3%). No cuts in the last 8 quarters; pattern is reaffirm-and-manage-down, not raise-and-beat.
**(e) Has the guide been raised between quarters?** No — reaffirmed three times in FY2026; the direction of travel is the CFO's "low end" framing, i.e., a soft pre-trim.
**Segments / peers / FX / pricing:** Q2'26: international carried the company (+7% organic revenue) while PFNA net revenue declined on lower net pricing and core CC op profit −8%; NA beverage volume −4% (stocktwits Sep 2026; minichart.com.sg Jul 2026). **FX:** Q1'26 revenue got a translation benefit (net +8.5% vs organic +2.6%); Q4'25 guidance raised partly on favorable FX (ainvest Oct 2025). **Pricing:** PepsiCo spent 2026 cutting prices up to 15% per Elliott's affordability push (Frito-Lay affordability program; fool.com Apr 2026) — but in late September 2026 announced **price increases** on selected chips, dips and soda after the cuts "failed to generate sufficient sales growth" (marketbeat/americanbankingnews, 2026-09-23/26), a risk signal for volumes. **Peer:** Coca-Cola, the direct peer (Yahoo Finance noted Sep 14, 2026: PEP −2% YTD vs KO +29% YTD), had not reported Q3 2026 as of Sep 28; no peer read-through yet. **Other items:** $1B Colombia investment announced Sep 27, 2026 (stocktwits); Maryland bottling plant closure eliminating 143 jobs, Sep 2026 (marketbeat, Sep 25); Joaquin Duato joins board Dec 1.

## 7. MOAT / AI-IRREPLACEABILITY
Downside insurance: global DSD (direct-store-delivery) network, owned/partner bottler system, shelf-space economics in both beverages and salty snacks (Frito-Lay share), and a portfolio where many brands each do >$1B retail (Lay's, Doritos, Cheetos, Gatorade, Pepsi, Mountain Dew, Quaker, SodaStream). Customers cannot easily route around it: retailers need the category draw and the DSD service; bottlers are contractually bound. Morningstar (Sep 2026, via stocktwits) still cites strong brands, distribution network, and international growth for the long term. AI-irreplaceability: high — physical manufacturing + distribution + retail relationships; AI doesn't disintermediate salty snacks and cola. **Irreplaceability score: 9/10.** If it gaps down 15% and you're stuck holding 18 months: opportunity, not disaster — 54 consecutive annual dividend increases, current yield ~4.5–4.6% ($5.92 annualized), the cash return is the backstop. (Sources: pepsico.com press releases; ad-hoc-news.de Sep 2026; marketbeat Sep 2026.)

## 8. RISKS SPECIFIC TO THIS PRINT
1. **Guide-risk realization:** CFO already telegraphed "low end" of +4–6% core CC EPS; a trim on Oct 8 is the consensus fear. The −3% Q2 reaction came *despite* a beat because of that caveat.
2. **NA volume/pricing whiplash:** 15% price cuts failed to move volume (0% volume response, −2% PFNA revenue per a Sep 2026 executive breakdown via youtube.com/andrewdremin), and management is now **raising** prices again (Sep 2026) — the worst of both worlds for Q3 volumes vs margin.
3. **GLP-1/snacking pressure:** structural demand overhang on salty snacks (oral obesity pill approvals driving sell-offs across food in late 2025/2026; archyde.com); private-label share gains (Walmart/Kroger 47–63% cheaper per the Sep 2026 pricing breakdown).
4. **FX:** Q1'26 got a translation tailwind; any reversal hits reported revenue, which is running hot (+6–8%) vs organic (+2–2.5%).
5. **Regulatory/litigation:** proposed class action (filed Aug 31, 2026) over "Avocado Oil" labeling on Siete and Tostitos chips (ad-hoc-news.de, Sep 2026); India Supreme Court ordered a binding warning-label framework within 10 days (reported Sep 11, 2026); Mumbai counterfeit raid involving PepsiCo brands (early Sep, per Reuters via ad-hoc-news.de); background UPF litigation (San Francisco city suit naming PepsiCo, Dec 2025; Arkansas class action naming PepsiCo, filed Mar 5, 2026) — none likely material to Q3 numbers but headline risk on the call.
6. **Management bandwidth:** Elliott engagement intact (no 13D, position flat four quarters) with the NA supply-chain review "now due" (late 2026) per the third-party wiki — the first hard margin-program test may land on or after this call.

## 10. PLAN A OR PLAN B?
**PLAN B — wait for the number.** The case for holding through (Plan A) would need: (a) options-implied move (2.1%) comfortably below the 4.18% historical average absolute move — true; (b) a strong beat-gets-rewarded record — true (5/6 beats up); (c) nothing priced in — true. But the disqualifier is guide-risk: the swing factor on Oct 8 is the FY guide the CFO already flagged as "tracking to the low end," and the last two prints show the market sells reaffirms (−3%) and buys raises (+4–5%). **What would flip it to A:** a credible path where management reaffirms the full +4–6% range with conviction (volume inflection in NA, pricing actions holding) *and* the quarter itself beats cleanly — i.e., the low-end caveat gets walked back, not confirmed.
