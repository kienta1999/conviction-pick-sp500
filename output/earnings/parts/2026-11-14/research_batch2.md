## 7. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 7/10
Micron is one of only three scaled HBM suppliers (SK hynix ~50% of HBM revenue, Samsung ~a third, Micron ~18% — Counterpoint, Q2 2026) and one of three scaled DRAM makers. HBM is a genuine AI chokepoint: every Nvidia accelerator needs it, supply is sold out, and qualification cycles are 12–18 months. Customers cannot in-source memory fabrication — the capex ($25B in two quarters; FY27 capex >$40B) and process know-how are prohibitive. Substitutes: CXMT (China's #4 DRAM maker, HBM yields ~20–30% vs ~70% at SK hynix, at least a generation behind — Reuters via TipRanks Oct 26) is the real long-term threat, plus Samsung/SK hynix capacity additions landing mid-2027+ (Idaho first wafers mid-2027; competitor fabs ramping same window). AI does not replace memory; AI *is* the demand. **If it gaps down 15% and I hold 18 months: opportunity — with a caveat.** At 4x forward earnings with sold-out supply, a 15% gap is a buying opportunity *unless* it signals the pricing cycle has turned (DRAM contract prices rolling over, or CXMT qualifying HBM at scale). The 18-month disaster case is a memory glut, which is a 2027–28 story, not a December story. Score reflects that the moat is real but cyclical, not permanent.
