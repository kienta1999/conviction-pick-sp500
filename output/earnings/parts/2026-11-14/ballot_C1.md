## 1. Single-pick ballot

- **Top pick:** AZO
- **Runner-up:** A
- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** AutoZone is the most holdable business in the field at the least demanding price, and that is exactly what this lens is asked to judge. The bar for the Dec 8 print is genuinely low: Q1 same-store sales are guided "relatively flat," revenue has already missed four straight quarters so the market expects little, and the stock is 18.4% below its 52-week high and down 15.5% over one year at ~17x forward earnings. If Q1 SSS comes in positive against that flat guide, with DIY transactions stabilising and commercial still growing high-single-digit, the EPS beat that has occurred in 3 of the last 4 prints finally lands on a revenue line that does not disappoint — and the buyback machine (shares −3.3% YoY) plus the Mega Hub network do the rest over the following year. What is not in the price is the commercial engine: the market is pricing AZO as a fading DIY retailer, not as an 8,031-store distribution network that repair shops increasingly cannot work without.
- **Key evidence from the record:** The single most compelling data point is the combination of durability and cheapness: an 8/10 irreplaceability score (the joint-highest in the field), a 12.65% net margin, commercial sales growing +8.6–10.4% while DIY SSS was −0.6%, and EPS beats in 3 of the last 4 prints despite revenue missing all four — the earnings engine works even when the top line disappoints.
- **Priced-in risk (THE TRAP):** Low. The 1-month +12.0% bounce is off a 52-week low ($2,730.65, Oct 5) and reads as defensive rotation, not AZO-specific euphoria; 6-month return is −3.6%, 1-year −15.5%, forward P/E ~17.0 is the cheapest in the field, and the dossier records no crowding. The trap here is not overpricing — it is the opposite: the stock has fallen on 3 of the last 4 prints, so even a decent print has recently been sold.
- **The guide:** AutoZone gives no EPS or revenue guide — qualitative only (SSS, commercial growth, stores, gross-margin direction, LIFO). The market is carrying FY27 consensus EPS of $170.30 (+11.6%) on revenue of $21.86B (+7.5%), with SSS guided flat-to-low-single-digit; the bridge is buybacks + new stores (a record 374 openings in FY26) + commercial mix, not comps. Q2 FY27 consensus is EPS $32.32 / revenue $4.60B. The run-rate supports that bridge only if commercial stays high-single-digit and DIY SSS does not go negative. **The loss case, named explicitly: "EPS beat but SSS negative / DIY transactions down again"** — that is the exact pattern of the last three prints (EPS beat, revenue miss, stock −9%, −6%, −7% in three of four), and a fourth would read as deterioration, not a one-off. The pre-announced $40m Q1 LIFO charge (~80bp margin / ~$1.85 EPS) also compresses reported margin optics regardless of the underlying business.
- **Plan A or Plan B:** Plan B. Revenue has missed consensus four straight times and the stock has fallen on three of those four prints despite EPS beats — the burden of proof for holding through the print is not met, and a buyback-and-valuation fallback is a reason to own the stock, not a reason to own the event.
- **Fallback if the print disappoints:** Yes — I would hold AZO for 12–18 months. It is the most holdable business in the field: 8/10 irreplaceability, 8,031 stores and a Mega Hub network (172 hubs → ~300) no online rival matches for same-day breadth, structurally AI-proof, a buyback machine shrinking the share count 3.3% a year, and a 12.65% net margin. A gap-down of 15% would be an opportunity, not a disaster — the dossier's own verdict.
- **Event scenario:** Beat-and-raise in substance (SSS positive vs "relatively flat" guide, commercial still high-single-digit): +5% to +8%. In-line (EPS beat, revenue miss again, SSS flat): −2% to −5% — the market has sold that exact outcome twice by 6–9%. Miss (SSS negative, DIY transactions down again, commercial decelerating): −7% to −10%.
- **Top risk:** DIY demand deteriorates further — a negative DIY comp with transactions down 5%+ would break the "flat SSS" story, and it would land on the first call with a finance-leadership vacuum (CFO departs Nov 25 for FedEx), so there is no trusted voice to reframe it.
- **Conviction (1-10):** 5

### Top 3 in detail

**1. AZO —** The record's most compelling point is the moat-plus-price combination: 8/10 irreplaceability, 12.65% net margin, commercial +8.6% while DIY was −0.6%, at ~17x forward earnings and 18.4% below its high. Rough moves: beat (SSS positive) +5% to +8% / in-line −2% to −5% / miss −7% to −10%. **Plan B.**

**2. A (Agilent) —** The setup is the best reaction record in the field (beat-up rate 0.857, average +2.5%, worst print only −5.5%) on a real moat: chromatography/mass-spec and CrossLab consumables embedded in regulated pharma QC, 7/10 irreplaceability, with Q3 delivering a +9.5% EPS beat, a revenue beat, a raised FY26 guide, and +3.0% the next session. But the priced-in risk is real (dossier score 6): +53.6–55.7% in six months, only 4.9% below its 52-week high, ~26x forward earnings, and the bar is a twice-raised guide with consensus sitting at the top of Q4 guide (EPS $1.72–1.73 vs company $1.71–1.74) — an in-line Q4 reads as disappointment, and no FY27 guide is due on this print to rescue it. Guide risk is therefore the whole event: Q1 FY27 consensus was not even found, and FY27 consensus EPS of $6.77 (+9.2%) needs continued Ignite margin expansion. Rough moves: beat-and-raise +4% to +7% / in-line −2% to −4% / miss −5% to −8% (worst in the record is −5.5%, so the downside is the most bounded in the field). **Plan B** — it would need a pullback toward ~$160 or a credible FY27 framework that will not come on Nov 23.

**3. CTAS (Cintas) —** This is the best business in the field and I rank it third only because the price and the calendar punish it: Q1 FY27 revenue +10.9% YoY (organic +8.9%), gross margin 51.5% and operating margin 23.6% — both all-time highs — with the FY27 guide raised on Sep 23 and a 4/4 beat record. Rough moves: beat + second raise with FTC clearance +5% to +8% / in-line reaffirm −3% to −6% / FTC suit or margin mean-reversion −8% to −12%. **Plan B** — it would need the FTC to close its investigation without a suit before Dec 11 plus a Q2 beat with a second guide raise.
