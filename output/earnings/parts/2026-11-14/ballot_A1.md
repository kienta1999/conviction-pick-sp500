## 1. Single-pick ballot

- **Top pick:** MU
- **Runner-up:** NTAP
- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** MU is the only name in the field whose beat is a near-certainty on the record rather than a hope: 10/10 EPS and revenue beats over the last 10 reports, with surprises averaging +20.1%, and five consecutive record quarters culminating in Q4 FY26 revenue of $54.23B (+379% YoY). The Q1 FY27 guide management issued on Sep 30 was itself ~9% above the then-consensus, and the Street has simply adopted it — so the print's real test is the Q2 guide, and HBM supply is sold out with >75% of FY27 shipments committed and memory described as "tight through 2028," which supports continued sequential acceleration. That is not fully in the price: the stock is 38% below its $1,255 June high, down 14.9% in the last month, and has chopped violently ($725.11 on Nov 10 to $811.30 on Nov 11) on Nvidia-resignation and memory-cost headlines rather than on any deterioration in MU's own order book.
- **Key evidence from the record:** 10/10 EPS beats and 10/10 revenue beats over the last 10 reports, average surprise +20.1% (including +38.8% in Q2 FY26 and +22.6% in Q3 FY26), with FY27 consensus EPS exploding from $45.51 (Feb) to $176.55–$189.15 — the largest surprise trend and the steepest estimate-revision ramp in the field by a wide margin.
- **Priced-in risk (THE TRAP):** **High.** The Q1 numbers are effectively pre-committed — consensus ($38.20 / ~$61.7B) is management's own guide — so a mere Q1 beat earns nothing; the de facto whisper is the top end of guide ($39.15 / $63.0B), and FY27 consensus of $176–$189 implies Q2–Q4 must average ~$46–$50 per quarter, i.e. acceleration every quarter. The stock is also the most violently crowded name in the field (beta 2.43, 30-day IV 68.5%, 5 sessions +18.6%), and its own history punishes holders through the print: median next-day move −1.9% over 10 prints, up only 4 of 10 days, and down 6 of 10 times one week later (median −5.4%).
- **The guide:** There is no published Street bogey for Q2 FY27 — that missing number is itself the risk. The Q1 guide annualises to ~$152 EPS; FY27 consensus of $176.55–$189.15 requires Q2–Q4 to average ~$46–$50/qtr, supportable only if DRAM/HBM pricing keeps rising. The loss case, stated explicitly: **beat Q1 but guide Q2 soft/flat** — a Q2 guide that merely holds Q1's run-rate, or HBM pricing commentary that hints at normalisation, is a "beat but guided soft" loss even on a clean Q1 beat.
- **Plan A or Plan B**, with one sentence of justification. **Plan B — emphatically.** Holding through the print means paying 68% IV to flip a coin the stock has lost 6 of the last 10 times, with an unconfirmed date (Dec 16 vs Dec 23), Christmas-week liquidity, and Nvidia's Nov 17 print (post-Huang-resignation) able to reprice MU first.
- **Fallback if the print disappoints:** Yes, I would hold it 12–18 months — MU is one of only three scaled HBM suppliers (~18% share vs SK hynix ~50%), HBM qualification takes 12–18 months, >75% of FY27 shipments are committed, and a $35.16B buyback authorisation becomes purchasable from Dec 9; the 18-month disaster case is a memory glut (a 2027–28 story), not a December story, so a print-driven dip is a buying opportunity unless DRAM contract prices actually turn.
- **Event scenario:** Beat-and-raise (Q1 beats the top end of guide AND Q2 guides to re-acceleration): **+10%** (roughly +8% to +15%, in line with the +15.7% after the Q3 +22.6% beat). In-line (Q1 beat, Q2 guide flat/soft): **−6%** (roughly −5% to −10%). Miss (Q1 disappoints or Q2 guide cut / pricing turns): **−18%** (roughly −15% to −20%, Christmas-week liquidity amplifying the move).
- **Top risk:** The Q2 guide / HBM pricing commentary for CY27 disappoints — Q1 is pre-committed, so the guide IS the print — compounded by any AI-capex wobble out of Nvidia on Nov 17.
- **Conviction (1-10):** **5** — conviction in the *beat* is high (10/10 record), conviction in the *hold-through-print payoff* is low (median −1.9% next day, 6/10 down), so the blended conviction on the event trade is middling.
