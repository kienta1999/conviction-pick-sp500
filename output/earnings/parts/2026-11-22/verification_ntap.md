## 5. The moat's central factual claim — CONFIRMED (with a sourcing caveat on "only")

Claim: NetApp is the only storage vendor with native first-party services on all three hyperscalers (FSx for NetApp ONTAP on AWS, Azure NetApp Files, Google Cloud NetApp Volumes); support revenue \$2.636bn in FY26 at ~92.5% gross margin.

### (a) First-party services on all three hyperscalers

- NetApp's own site (netapp.com/cloud-services): "We provide native, first-party storage services in all three major clouds: Amazon FSx for NetApp ONTAP, Azure NetApp Files, and Google Cloud NetApp Volumes. These are co-engineered with each provider and built into their marketplaces, not third-party add-ons."
- NetApp blog (GigaOm Radar): "This is the first year that NetApp technology is also available as a first-party offering in all three major hyperscalers: AWS, Microsoft Azure, and Google Cloud."
- Independent trade press: CRN (2023-10-24, Insight conference): NetApp VP Jeff Baxter — "having that same storage environment available as a first-party native service on all the major clouds like we do with AWS, [Microsoft] Azure and Google Cloud. And we're the only ones that do that." Blocks and Files: "No other storage vendor's technology sits behind the user consoles of three big cloud service providers and is treated as a first-party service, sold, supported, and billed by the CSPs themselves." WWT: "NetApp is the only data platform available as a first-party, natively embedded service in all three major clouds."
- The three named services exist as stated: FSx for NetApp ONTAP (AWS), Azure NetApp Files (Microsoft), Google Cloud NetApp Volumes (Google Cloud, announced Aug 2023).
- Caveat: the "only" is a company assertion repeated by trade press and a channel partner, not the result of an exhaustive audit of every storage vendor's cloud arrangements. No competing vendor with first-party services on all three hyperscalers surfaced in these checks, and the claim is corroborated by multiple independent outlets, so I mark it CONFIRMED with that sourcing caveat rather than downgrading it.

### (b) Support revenue \$2.636bn in FY26 at ~92.5% gross margin

- NetApp Q4 FY26 supplemental data (company 8-K exhibit / Q4 tables, 2026-05-28): Hybrid Cloud segment revenues — Support \$2,636m for FY2026 (vs \$2,512m FY2025); Q4 FY26 Support \$688m. Gross profit by segment — Support \$2,438m for FY2026 (vs \$2,315m FY2025).
- Arithmetic: 2,438 / 2,636 = 92.49%, i.e. **~92.5% gross margin**. The revenue figure and the margin both reproduce exactly from the company's own filing tables. (The 10-K for FY2026, filed 2026-06-05, is the same fiscal year; the Q4 supplemental tables are the source of the split.)

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**
