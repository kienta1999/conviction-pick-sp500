### 7. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — score 8/10

Route density is the moat: 48,100 employees serving 1M+ businesses from a fixed route network — each incremental stop on an existing route is nearly pure margin, and a challenger must replicate the *routes*, not just the uniforms. Contracts, garment-rental switching costs (a customer changing supplier re-fits its whole workforce), and cross-sell (First Aid at 57.9% gross margin, Fire Protection at 50.8%) compound it. AI threat is low: this is a physical, route-based service — no AI-native competitor can launder uniforms over the internet, and Cintas's own tech (SAP, SmartTruck) is the efficiency lever. The main substitutes are in-sourcing (a customer buying its own uniforms — capex-heavy, no service) and regional players (including UniFirst itself — hence the merger logic). **If it gaps down 15% and I hold 18 months: an opportunity** — a 23% operating-margin compounder with a 40-year raise-and-buyback record at a temporarily cheaper multiple is a buy-the-dip name, *unless* the gap is caused by an FTC block plus a guide cut together.
