### 5. THE GUIDE

(a) **Does it guide?** Yes, granularly: next quarter (total revenue, subscription revenue, services revenue, non-GAAP operating income, non-GAAP EPS) and full year (total revenue, subscription revenue, non-GAAP operating income ~\$1.64B, non-GAAP EPS ~\$9.21). It does *not* give GAAP operating income or GAAP EPS guidance, citing stock-based compensation unpredictability — a standing limitation, not a new one.

(b) **Forward bar.** Next quarter Q4 FY2027 consensus was not found as a published figure — **not found**. Implied from the FY27 guide: FY27 revenue midpoint \$3.6845B less Q1 \$0.883B, Q2 \$0.928B and Q3 guide \$0.9335B = Q4 implied ~\$0.940B; FY27 non-GAAP EPS \$9.21 less Q1 \$2.24, Q2 \$2.35 and Q3 guide \$2.335 = Q4 implied ~\$2.29. So the Q4 guide will be judged against ~\$940m revenue / ~\$2.29 EPS — a modest sequential step-up from Q3 that requires no acceleration. Full-year FY27 consensus (\$9.22 / \$3.69B, Zacks) is already at the guide.

(c) **Does the run-rate support it?** Yes, comfortably for Q4: Q2 revenue \$928m growing 17.6% with 45% margins supports a Q4 at ~\$940m (+~13% YoY vs Q4 FY2026 \$836m) without heroics. The risk is not Q4 arithmetic but the *growth rate*: the FY27 guide implies ~15% full-year growth, and the Investor Day 2030 plan (\$6B run rate by end of calendar 2030, from ~\$3.71B today) requires only ~11% CAGR — i.e. management's own long-term plan implies growth *slowing* from today's 15–17% to ~11%. A Q4 guide that merely hits the implied ~\$940m confirms the deceleration; the market has to decide whether to pay ~28x forward earnings for a decelerating grower.

(d) **Guide history, last 8 quarters.** Raised every quarter in the last four: Q3 FY2026 (Nov 2025) raised FY26 to \$3.166–\$3.169B / \$7.93; Q4 FY2026 (Mar 2026) set FY27 at \$3.585–\$3.600B / ~\$8.85; Q1 FY2027 (Jun 2026) raised FY27 to \$3.635–\$3.645B / ~\$9.05 (+\$35–60m revenue, +\$0.20 EPS); Q2 FY2027 (Aug 2026) raised FY27 again to \$3.682–\$3.687B / ~\$9.21 (+\$42m revenue at the high end, +\$0.16 EPS). A serial raiser with a consistent beat-the-guide-by-\$25m habit. The reactions show the market does not reward the raise mechanically (+15% once, −10% once on similar scripts) — the differentiator in Q2 was the Vault CRM acceleration narrative, not the size of the raise.

(e) **Already raised between quarters?** Yes — effectively. At the Investor Day on 2026-11-05, CFO Brian Van Wagener said Veeva expects Q3 to **meet or exceed** its guidance on all metrics (8-K, 2026-11-06: Q3 revenue \$932–935m and adjusted operating income \$417–420m). That is a soft pre-announcement: it pulls the "beat" forward and leaves the print with less to deliver, since a mere meet is now the floor. The Investor Day also reaffirmed the \$6B 2030 revenue run-rate target, raised TAM to \$30B (from \$20B) largely on industry-specific AI, and disclosed Vault CRM progress: 200+ customers live (from 100+), 5 of top 20 live, 14 of top 20 committed (from 9 a year ago), ~halfway through migration, completion not until end of 2029. New items with no financial targets: Falcon agents (live at only a few customers, six- and seven-figure deals still pipeline), Vault AI (priced at 10% of Vault subscription), Aspen CRM outside life sciences. The 2030 goal explicitly *excludes* all three — so the print cannot disappoint on AI revenue that was never guided, but it also cannot surprise positively on AI revenue that does not yet exist in any filing.

### 7. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — score 8/10

Veeva is the system of record for life-sciences R\&D, quality and commercial: Vault is embedded in regulated workflows (clinical, regulatory, safety, quality) where switching means re-validating processes with the FDA/EMA in the loop, and the data model is industry-specific in a way generic CRM/ERP cannot replicate without years of work. 14 of the top 20 pharma are committed to Vault CRM; the migration runs to 2029, which is itself a multi-year revenue lock-in. AI is a tailwind here, not a threat: Vault AI and Falcon are Veeva's own products, priced at 10% of subscription, and the TAM expansion to \$30B is *because* of industry-specific AI. What caps the score: Salesforce remains the credible alternative for the uncommitted 6 of the top 20; the CRM migration is only halfway done, so execution risk persists for three more years; and services growth (which flatters the top line) is lower-margin and lumpier than subscription. If it gaps down 15% and I hold 18 months: an opportunity — a 15–17% grower with 45% non-GAAP margins, \$6.5B+ of cash/investments (Q4 FY2026) and a buyback, at a price that would then be ~24x forward earnings, is a hold-through-the-pain name, not a disaster — provided Vault CRM commitments do not start reversing.

### 8. Risks specific to THIS print

The Investor Day "meet or exceed" remark removes the surprise: a print that merely meets the \$932–935m guide after that remark will read as a soft guide, and a Q4 guide at the implied ~\$940m confirms deceleration to ~13–15%; the −10% reaction to the Q3 FY2026 beat-and-raise shows this name punishes prints that do not *accelerate* the narrative; Vault CRM: any slowdown in new commitments (stuck at 14 of 20) or a large customer delaying migration would hit the multi-year story, not just the quarter; AI revenue is zero in filings — any hint that Falcon/Vault AI monetisation is further out than hoped could deflate the TAM story; the stock fell ~9% in five sessions into the print, so momentum is already negative and a weak guide could cascade.

### 9. Scores and verdict

- **Beat-likelihood: 8/10** — consensus equals guidance, management has already said "meet or exceed", Q1/Q2 beat guide by \$25m+, and the beat pattern is the most consistent in the batch.
