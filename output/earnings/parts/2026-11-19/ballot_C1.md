### 1. VEEV — Veeva Systems

The most compelling record in the field: 4/4 beats with EPS surprises of +4.6% to +6.2% and revenue beats of +2.4% to +3.1% every quarter, revenue accelerating +16.0% to +16.3% to +17.6%, at a flat ~45% non-GAAP operating margin with no earnings-quality flags and cash conversion (CFO/NI 1.64) backing every dollar. The reaction record is the wildest in the field — last four +15%, −2%, +4%, −10%, average absolute move 9.1% — but the swings track the guide and the Vault CRM narrative, not the beat, which is almost a constant. Guide risk is moderate: the Q4 implied bar (~\$940m / ~\$2.29) is undemanding and the run-rate supports it, but a guide that merely confirms deceleration to ~13–15% reads as a cut at 28.1x forward earnings. Priced-in risk is the lowest in the field — down 9–12% in a month, 11–15% below the high, implied move below its own history. Rough moves: beat-and-raise +10% to +15%, in-line −2% to −5%, miss −10% or worse. Plan A.

### 2. ADSK — Autodesk

The second-strongest moat in the field and the cheapest quality name in it: AutoCAD/Revit/Fusion are the file-format and workflow standards for architecture, engineering, construction and manufacturing, with decades of DWG files, trained users and plug-in ecosystems — a customer cannot in-source CAD, and AI-native design tools are complements Autodesk itself ships rather than substitutes. The record is a 4/4 (indeed 8-for-8) beat streak with the largest average surprise in the field (+6.37%), Q2 revenue +16.1% with non-GAAP operating margin at 41%, 97% recurring revenue, and net cash on the balance sheet at 18.0x forward earnings. The reaction record is the warning: the stock fell on the last two beats (−3.7%, −4.0%) despite raises both times, and billings — the leading indicator — slowed from +18% to +10% while cRPO growth halved from 18% to 12%. Guide risk is the highest in the field (8/10): a reaffirmation of FY27 guidance reads as a cut, and the implied Q4 (~\$2.21B / ~\$3.20) must absorb MaintainX dilution. Priced-in risk is mixed — 20% off the high and YTD −14.5% on price, but the whisper of \$3.16 sits above the top of guidance, so the beat-and-raise is expected. Rough moves: beat-and-raise +5% to +7%, in-line/reaffirm −4% to −7%, billings miss −8% or worse. Plan B — but the first name I would buy on a gap down: a 15%-growing subscription franchise at ~18x forward earnings is a hold-18-months name, not a flee name, unless billings have structurally broken.

### 3. A — Agilent Technologies

A genuine moat, one notch below the top two: analytical instruments embedded in regulated pharma/QC workflows, where method validation and regulatory filings name the instrument and the installed base drives a consumables annuity; customers cannot in-source a mass spectrometer, and AI routes around data analysis, not physical measurement. The record is accelerating — core growth +4.4% to +6.3% to +7.3%, non-GAAP operating margin 27.2% ex-refund and expanding, three straight FY26 guide raises, peers Thermo Fisher and Danaher both beating and raising in October. The reaction record is the best in the field (beat-up rate 0.857, average +2.5%), but the Q3 beat-and-raise only earned +1.7%, and the stock is +60% in six months, ~3–6% below its high, with analyst targets only ~2–3% above the price. Guide risk is high: this is the fiscal year-end print, so the market gets first-ever FY27 guidance against a \$6.77 consensus (+9.2%), and a mere reaffirmation after three straight raises reads as a disappointment — with Q3 EPS flattered by a \$0.06 one-off tariff refund that does not repeat. Priced-in risk is high (7/10). Rough moves: beat-and-raise +3% to +6%, beat with FY27 guide at consensus −3% to −5%, soft FY27 guide −7% to −10%. Plan B — a quality hold-18-months business (clean balance sheet, 1.15x net debt/EBITDA, ~96% free-cash-flow conversion) whose print is a guide verdict, not an earnings verdict.
