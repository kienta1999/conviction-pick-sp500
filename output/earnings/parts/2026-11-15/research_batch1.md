### 7. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — score 6/10
Micron is one of only **three HBM suppliers in the world** (SK Hynix ~50–58% share, Samsung, Micron ~18% HBM / ~21% DRAM per Counterpoint). HBM is capital-intensive (FY27 capex ≥$50B; ~$11.5B in Q1, ~$25B in H1), takes years of fab build, and is now sold under long-term contract: **26 strategic customer agreements with take-or-pay volume commitments, $32B of customer financial commitments (up from $22B a quarter earlier), >75% of FY27 shipments already committed, >35% of projected sales through 2030 under take-or-pay**. That is real lock-in — customers prepay because they cannot get the memory elsewhere. But the product is a priced commodity, not a proprietary platform: JPMorgan's Nov 13 warning is that HBM is now 70–75% of GPU cost, so Nvidia/AMD/hyperscalers have every incentive to trim HBM content per GPU; CXMT (China) is making progress on density; and the "moat" is really a shortage, which new capacity eventually ends. **If it gaps down 15% and I'm stuck 18 months: an opportunity IF the contracted backlog holds and pricing only moderates — a disaster if it is a cycle top**, because at 87% gross margin the earnings base can halve faster than the multiple can expand. The 18-month question is entirely whether peak-cycle margins are durable; the contracts argue yes for 2027, the history of memory cycles argues no for 2028.
