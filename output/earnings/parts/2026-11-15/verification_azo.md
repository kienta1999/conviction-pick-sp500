The underlying fact pattern — a positive report-day reaction that faded the next session — is the opposite of a clean "+1 to +6% next-session" read.

## 5. Moat claims and CFO departure — store count and CFO CONFIRMED; "largest retailer" CONTESTED; mega-hub delivery CONFIRMED with a sourcing caveat

### 5a. "Largest US aftermarket auto-parts retailer" — UNVERIFIED / CONTESTED (definition-dependent)
- **Supporting:** DeepVal's O'Reilly moat analysis (Nov 2026) estimates AutoZone ~12–14% of US aftermarket hard-parts demand, **ranked No. 1 nationally**, ahead of O'Reilly ~11–13%, Genuine Parts/NAPA ~9–11%, Advance ~5–7% (author estimates, medium reliability, not audited). Ainvest/CSIMarket data as of Q2 2025: AZO 12.99% vs ORLY 11.76% vs AAP 5.22%. Placer.ai Q1 2024 foot traffic: AutoZone >40% of visits across the four big chains vs O'Reilly 32.1%.
- **Contradicting:** IBISWorld's "Auto Parts Retailing in the US" industry analysis (July 2026) states **"The company holding the most market share in the Auto Parts Retailing industry in the United States is O'Reilly Automotive, Inc."** — the opposite ranking under IBISWorld's retailing-industry definition.
- **Verdict:** the claim is plausible and supported on a revenue-share-of-aftermarket-parts basis, but a reputable industry source ranks O'Reilly first under its own definition, and the gap between AZO and ORLY is within estimation error (~1pp). Do not state "largest" as an unqualified fact; the safe phrasing is "roughly tied with O'Reilly as the largest US aftermarket parts retailer, ~12–14% share by most estimates." This is a moat-framing softening, not a thesis-breaker.

### 5b. Store count 8,031 at Aug 29, 2026 (6,863 US / 1,001 Mexico / 167 Brazil) — CONFIRMED
- Exact figures from the company's own releases: the Oct 26, 2026 CFO-departure press release (GlobeNewswire, carried by Nasdaq) states "As of August 29, 2026, the Company had 6,863 stores in the U.S., 1,001 in Mexico and 167 in Brazil for a total store count of 8,031." The Nov 11 Q1-earnings notice repeats the same counts. The Q4 FY26 8-K/10-K is consistent (fiscal year ended Aug 29, 2026).

### 5c. Same-day commercial delivery via dense footprint / mega-hubs — CONFIRMED (company-described)
- AutoZone's own job postings (Hub/Mega Hub Operations, current Oct–Nov 2026 listings) describe the tiered network: "Hubs stock fast-moving inventory for daily replenishment, while Mega Hubs carry an expanded assortment of up to 110,000 SKUs — including hard-to-find parts — to support retail and commercial customers with same-day or next-day availability."
- Substack analysis of the Q4 FY26 call (Sep 23, 2026): **172 Mega Hubs** (~30,000 sq ft, ~5x a standard store, 100,000+ SKUs), each serving ~90 satellite stores plus commercial customers (collision centres, repair shops, dealerships); ~2,000 commercial programmes linked to Mega Hubs sell 16% more annually; target ≥300 Mega Hubs at full buildout; commercial (DIFM) is now ~1/3 of revenue vs ~15% in 2012. CFO Jackson reiterated Mega Hubs' importance on the Q4 FY26 call.
- **Caveat:** "same-day" is the company's own description of availability ("same-day or next-day"), not a verified SLA or a standalone press release; the strongest sourcing is company job descriptions plus call commentary. The mechanism (dense stores + Mega Hubs feeding commercial) is real and current.

### 5d. CFO Jamere Jackson resigned effective Nov 25, 2026 to become FedEx CFO; J. Scott Murphy interim — CONFIRMED
- Form 8-K filed **Oct 26, 2026** (event date Oct 20, 2026), Item 5.02: "On October 20, 2026, Jamere Jackson, Chief Financial Officer, Customer Satisfaction, notified AutoZone, Inc. that he has resigned from his position as Chief Financial Officer of the Company effective November 25, 2026 … for a professional opportunity outside of the automotive aftermarket industry." Exhibit 99.1 press release (Oct 26, 2026): "after which he will assume the position of Chief Financial Officer of FedEx Corporation."
- Same 8-K: "Beginning on the Effective Date … J. Scott Murphy, the Company's Vice President and Controller, will serve as both principal accounting officer and principal financial officer" while an external search runs. The dossier's "interim principal financial officer" matches the filing (Murphy holds both principal-officer roles, not the CFO title).
- FedEx side (Reuters, Oct 26, 2026): FedEx appointed Jackson CFO **effective November 30**, succeeding interim CFO Claude Russ. Jackson was AutoZone CFO since January 2021 (previously Hertz, Nielsen).
- Note: the Q1 FY27 print on Dec 8 will therefore be the first earnings call without Jackson as CFO — Murphy will be the financial voice, with a permanent successor search ongoing. This is a real (if modest) execution/communication risk for the print, not a factual problem with the dossier.

## Verdict

Most claims held: the Dec 8 BMO date and 10:00 a.m. ET call are confirmed from the company's own Nov 11 release, the $35.41 / ~$4.94B consensus and the Zacks $36.06 / $34.65 / −3.58% ESP figures check out, the Q4 surprise (+3–4% EPS beat, −1.6% revenue miss) is confirmed, the forward numbers ($32.32 / $4.60B Q2, $170.30 FY27) and the no-numeric-guidance policy are confirmed, and the store count (8,031) and CFO departure (Jackson → FedEx Nov 25, Murphy interim) are confirmed exactly. Two claims failed or softened: (1) the Q4 reaction as labelled — the close-to-close next-session move was **−1.8%**, not +1 to +6% (the +6% was an intraday peak, the +1.4% a two-session move), so the dossier overstates AZO's last-print momentum; and (2) "largest US aftermarket retailer" is contested by IBISWorld ranking O'Reilly first and should be softened to roughly tied for largest. Neither failure changes AZO's standing at #3: the date, the bar ($35.41 vs Zacks $36.06 with a negative ESP), and the forward numbers are all intact, the Q3 precedent (−9% on an EPS beat with a revenue miss) still warns that a revenue miss can dominate a beat, and the CFO transition is a watch item for the Dec 8 call, not a ranking input. Recommendation: correct the Q4 reaction line in the dossier to "report-day close +3.3%, next session −1.8%, two-session +1.4%" and soften "largest" to "roughly tied with O'Reilly as the largest"; AZO stays #3.
