## 5. THE GUIDE
(a) **Does the company guide at all? NO — AutoZone issues no numeric guidance.** The Q4 release "contains no numeric guidance" (SkipAndGrow, Sep 22). Management gives qualitative colour (quarter cadence, LIFO charge expectations, store-opening targets, capex) and nothing else. **A non-guider cannot disappoint on guidance — but that does not make it safer:** the market substitutes its own bar (comps, revenue, gross margin ex-LIFO), and the last three prints show it punishes a revenue/comp miss brutally even on an EPS beat. The "guide" risk here is a *consensus-expectations* risk, not a management-guide risk.
(b) **Forward consensus:** FY27 EPS **$170.30** (Zacks, Nov 6; Clearank $169.37; Forbes $170.13 — tight cluster), FY28 **$193.51** (Zacks, +13.6%). **Q2 FY27 (Feb 2027) consensus EPS $32.32, revenue $4.60B** (Zacks). FY27 revenue consensus ~$21.86B (Zacks) / $21.82B (Forbes). No quarterly guide exists to compare these against — the Q2 FY27 number is purely the Street's own expectation, and it implies EPS +17% YoY vs Q2 FY26's $27.63, a big step-up from a quarter that itself missed.
(c) **Run-rate support:** Q1 FY27 consensus ($35.41–36.06) implies +14–16% EPS YoY off a Q1 FY26 base that was depressed by the $98M LIFO charge — an easy comp on EPS, but revenue consensus (+6.7%) requires comps to re-accelerate from Q4's 1.6% domestic comp toward the 4–5% range. The run-rate does **not** support that: domestic comps have decelerated for three straight quarters and DIY just went negative. The FY27 EPS consensus of ~$170 implies +11% YoY (Clearank) on ~7% revenue growth — achievable only if LIFO charges normalise (FY26's $207M LIFO drag does not repeat at the same scale) and buybacks keep shrinking the share count (−3.0% YoY in Q4; diluted shares 16.62M vs 17.18M). The EPS path is supportable via buybacks + LIFO normalisation; the **revenue path is not supported by the comp trend**.
(d) **Guide history over the last 8 quarters: N/A — no guide to raise, reaffirm, trim or cut.** What exists instead is a *call-commentary* history: management has consistently flagged LIFO charges in advance (Q2 call: ~$60M per quarter for Q3/Q4; Q3 call: ~$30M in Q4, ~$207M for the year) and consistently described DIY softness as weather/traffic rather than structural. The closest analogue to a "guide event" is the Q3 call's expectation of a "hotter than normal summer" rebound — Q4's domestic comp of 1.6% did not deliver it.
(e) **Already raised between quarters? NO.** No pre-announcement, no analyst-day raise, no conference remark lifting expectations. The only inter-quarter news was negative: Oppenheimer's Sep 18 valuation cut ($4,300 → $3,500) and the CFO resignation (see §8).

**Peer read (the best available leading indicator):** O'Reilly Automotive (ORLY) reported Q3 on Oct 28, 2026 — revenue $5.12B (+8.8% YoY, +2.1% vs consensus), EPS $0.97 vs $0.93, **comparable store sales +7.2%** (pro +10%+, DIY mid-single-digit), and **raised** full-year guidance (EPS $3.25–3.35 from $3.20–3.30; comps 5.5–6.5% from 4–6%). Stock rose ~3.7% after results and is flat since (StockStory, Nov 9). The peer read is **mixed-to-negative for AZO**: the pro/commercial market is strong (good for AZO's commercial engine), but ORLY's DIY grew mid-single-digits while AZO's DIY *fell* 0.6% in Q4 — AZO is losing the DIY leg to its closest peer. Both companies benefited from tariff refunds (ORLY $39M, AZO $96M) that will not recur. Genuine Parts (GPC) Q3: revenue $6.52B (+4.2%, in line). Advance Auto Parts Q3: revenue $2.01B (−1.2% YoY).

## 7. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — 7/10
The largest US aftermarket auto-parts retailer (8,031 stores, ~132,000 employees) with a distribution moat that is physical, not digital: 300 mega-hubs planned, dense store footprint for same-day commercial delivery, and a private-label (Duralast) assortment. Customers cannot in-source parts retail; repair shops need parts within the hour, and DIY customers need the part today. AI does not replace a brake pad, and e-commerce (Amazon) has repeatedly failed to displace the immediacy + advice model — though it caps pricing power on commoditised SKUs. Threats are demand-side, not substitution-side: an aging vehicle fleet (average age ~12.6 years, a tailwind) vs deferred maintenance in a weak consumer environment, plus tariff/LIFO margin noise. **If it gaps down 15% and I hold 18 months: opportunity, not disaster** — a mid-teens grower on EPS (buyback + LIFO normalisation) with a 7%-ish revenue grower underneath, bought at ~17x forward off a 52-week low region; the disaster case is a multi-quarter DIY demand collapse, not obsolescence. Score is lower than Cintas's 8 because the DIY leg is cyclically exposed and the moat is distribution, not contract lock-in.

## 8. Risks specific to THIS print
- **Four straight revenue misses.** AZO has missed revenue consensus in all four of the last four quarters (−0.2%, −0.8%, −0.4%, −1.6%). The market has now learned to sell the revenue miss even on an EPS beat. A fifth consecutive miss — even a small one — is the single most likely way this print loses.
- **DIY comp negative and decelerating.** Domestic DIY −0.6% in Q4 (first negative in the recent record), transactions down >5%; management's "traffic will normalise" is an assumption, not a demonstrated fact (Aardvark Labs). If Q1 DIY is negative again, the comp story breaks.
- **CFO departs Nov 25 — CONFIRMED, and it lands 13 days before the print.** Jamere Jackson resigned effective **November 25, 2026** to become CFO of FedEx (effective Nov 30), after six years as AZO CFO (8-K filed Oct 26, 2026; press release Oct 26). The company says the departure is "not related to financial or operating results or any disagreement" and has commenced an external search; VP & Controller **J. Scott Murphy** will serve as principal accounting *and* principal financial officer in the interim. The Dec 8 call will therefore be fronted by an interim CFO with no permanent successor named — expect cautious, non-committal commentary and no new disclosure on the call. This is a headline/communication risk, not an accounting risk, but it removes the one executive who has been explaining the LIFO story for six years.
- **LIFO/tariff margin quality.** Q4's +182bp gross-margin expansion was +145bp tariff refund (one-off, $96M) +105bp non-cash LIFO — the underlying margin was *down*. If Q1 gross margin reverts toward ~51–52% without a refund, EPS beats become hard even if revenue is fine. Q1 FY26's comp base (51.0% gross margin, −203bp) is an easy comparison, which helps.
- **FX:** Q1 FY26 got a $37M sales / $0.44 EPS tailwind from peso strength; management said at spot rates Q2 would see ~$57M revenue benefit. Peso moves cut both ways and are outside management's control.
- **Diesel/fuel costs:** Oppenheimer's Sep 18 cut cited record diesel costs hitting the commercial delivery model; watch for margin commentary on delivery costs.
