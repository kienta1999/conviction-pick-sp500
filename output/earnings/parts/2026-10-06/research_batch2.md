## EARNINGS-QUALITY FLAGS

### MS — LOW_CASH_CONVERSION
**Benign business-model artifact.** MS is a broker-dealer/bank: net income includes unrealized mark-to-market gains on trading inventory and prime-brokerage receivables that expand mechanically when client activity surges (Q2 equities revenue was a record $6.3B). Cash-flow statements of broker-dealers are dominated by changes in trading assets, securities borrowed/lent, and client balances — GAAP operating cash flow is not comparable to net income the way it is for an industrial. Verdict: expected for the model; watch credit provisions and NII trends instead of the cash-conversion ratio.

### MS — RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN
**Benign business-model artifact, with one watch item.** For MS, "receivables" are principally client margin loans, fee receivables, and securities-financing balances that grow *with* market activity — a record trading quarter mathematically inflates receivables alongside revenue. This is the balance sheet doing its job, not channel-stuffing. Watch item: confirm receivables growth is matched by revenue growth (it is — +27% revenue in Q2) and by stable credit provisions; a divergence would be the real flag. Verdict: benign at this print.

### BLK — LOW_CASH_CONVERSION
**Benign business-model artifact.** BLK's management fees are billed quarterly in arrears on average AUM — revenue is recognized before cash arrives, creating structural fee receivables that scale with AUM growth ($15.3T). Non-cash stock comp (RSUs), deferred taxes, and lumpy performance fees ($754M in Q4 2025, $272M in Q1 2026) further decouple GAAP cash flow from earnings — Q2 2026 operating cash flow was $1.2B against much larger adjusted operating income of $2.9B (per MarketBeat standardized financials). Verdict: expected for an asset manager with arrears billing and acquisition-driven non-cash charges; the informative numbers are net inflows, organic base-fee growth, and the raised buyback guide.
