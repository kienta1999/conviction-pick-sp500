## EARNINGS-QUALITY FLAG appendix

- **GS: LOW_CASH_CONVERSION** — Benign accrual artifact. For a trading bank, GAAP net income includes unrealized mark-to-market gains on trading inventory and principal investments that have not settled in cash, while operating cash flow is whipsawed by period-end changes in trading assets/liabilities, securities borrowed/lent, and broker receivables. The cash "conversion" looks low precisely when trading is strong (as in 1H 2026). Cross-check with capital return instead: $5.36B returned in Q2 ($4.0B buybacks + raised $5.00 dividend) — a cash-short business doesn't do that. Verdict: benign business-model artifact.
- **MS: LOW_CASH_CONVERSION** — Same benign artifact as GS (trading MTM + broker balance-sheet swings dominate the cash-flow statement). Verdict: benign.
- **MS: RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN** — Benign for a prime broker. MS's receivables are overwhelmingly client/broker-dealer receivables — margin loans, securities-lending collateral, and settlement balances — which mechanically grow when client trading activity and balance-sheet usage expand (exactly what record $5.15B equities / strong FICC quarters produce). These are collateralized, short-dated, and matched against payables; growth alongside revenue is the business working, not channel-stuffing. Verdict: benign balance-sheet scaling with activity.
- **BNY: LOW_CASH_CONVERSION** — Benign. A custody bank's operating cash flow is dominated by changes in client deposits and settlement balances (which can swing tens of billions quarter to quarter) plus non-cash amortization; meanwhile net income includes fee accruals. The signal to watch instead is the dividend: raised to $0.63 in Q2 alongside the guide raise. Verdict: benign.
- **BLK: LOW_CASH_CONVERSION** — Benign. Asset managers collect fees in cash, but GAAP earnings include unrealized carried interest and mark-to-market on seed/co-investment portfolios that don't convert to operating cash in the period; working-capital swings (performance-fee receivables, comp accruals) further decouple OCF from net income. BlackRock's economics are fee-on-AUM — among the most cash-generative models in finance. Verdict: benign accrual artifact, not earnings running ahead of cash.
