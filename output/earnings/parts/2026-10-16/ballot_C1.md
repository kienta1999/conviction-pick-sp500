# Panelist C — Quality / Moat & Irreplaceability

## SINGLE-PICK BALLOT

- **Top pick:** CME
- **Runner-up:** ISRG
- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** CME is the name I would least mind owning if the print goes wrong, because the business itself cannot be routed around: liquidity goes where liquidity already is, open interest cannot be ported by a competitor's marketing, and no client can in-source a regulated central counterparty at economic cost. It pairs that 9/10 irreplaceability with the batch's best economics — operating margin 64.9% in Q2'26 (61.8–69.7% over the last four quarters), net margin ~61–63%, and H1'26 operating cash flow of $2.207B against net income of $2.196B, i.e. ~100% cash conversion. AI does not substitute for it; if anything it raises hedging and data demand, and market-data revenue has set eight consecutive quarterly records. Unlike ISRG and PM, there is no EPS guide to disappoint and no premium multiple that needs a perfect quarter to defend.
- **Key evidence from the record:** Q3 ADV already disclosed at a record 29.4M contracts, +16% YoY (September 31.8M, +22%; international ADV +22% to 9.0M), yet consensus revenue of ~$1.71B is flat versus Q2's $1.706B, so the bar does not pay for volume translating. Four consecutive small adjusted beats of ~+1% to +3% (Q2'26 $2.99 vs $2.91; Q4'25 $2.77 vs $2.74–$2.75; Q3'25 $2.68 vs $2.63). Forward P/E 21.4x sits below CME's own history (5-year average forward ~23.25x; 3-year average trailing 24.2x vs 23.5x now). The RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN flag is explicitly benign: receivables spiked with record Q1 activity ($639.2M → $935.5M), partly reversed in Q2 ($753.1M), with ~100% H1 cash conversion. Moat / irreplaceability scored 9/10 in the dossier.
- **Priced-in risk (THE TRAP):** medium, and why — valuation and positioning are not crowded (screen price $278.03 was −15.5% vs the $329.16 52-week high, analyst consensus only Hold), but the stock ran ~+10% from the Oct 2 close of $263.14 to ~$289 by Oct 15 on ADV data that has been public since Oct 2. The known good news is already paid for; the only unknown left for the print is RPC / mix / collateral, which the ADV release does not disclose — a revenue miss on record volume is the one real trap.
- **The guide:** CME barely guides — no EPS, revenue or volume guide exists, only the February FY26 guide of adjusted opex ex-license fees ~$1.695B, capex ~$85M and adjusted tax rate 23.5–24.5%. The market is carrying Q4'26 EPS of $2.98 and FY26 EPS of $12.29 (FY27 $12.87); the run-rate fully supports it as pure continuation — H1 printed $3.36 + $2.99 = $6.35, plus Q3e $2.96–$2.98 gives $9.31–$9.33, so hitting $12.29 needs Q4 of ~$2.96–$2.98, identical to Q4 consensus, with no acceleration assumed anywhere. "Beat but guided soft" cannot occur in the formal sense here, because there is no EPS guide to soften; the soft-guide equivalent would be management signalling that September's volume was the peak, or reinvestment opex running ahead of the $1.695B guide.
- **Plan A or Plan B:** Plan B — the dossier's default, because at the screen price and above the already-disclosed record volume is in the price and only the unknown RPC remains, though CME is the closest name in the batch to Plan A.
- **Fallback if the print disappoints:** Yes — hold 12-18 months, happily, and this is the decisive test for my lens. A disappointing print here would most plausibly be a volume-cycle, RPC/mix or collateral-income shortfall, none of which impairs the franchise: the exchange, the clearinghouse, the benchmark contracts and the data exhaust are still the venue, margins are structurally ~65% at the operating line, cash conversion is ~100%, and capital return (44% payout ratio plus buybacks, per the dossier) pays you while you wait. Customers cannot in-source it, substitutes (ICE, Cboe, Eurex) have coexisted for decades in their own niches without dislodging CME's core complexes, and there is no AI-native way to replace a regulated central counterparty. The dossier's own fallback verdict is that a −15% gap is an opportunity unless caused by a regulatory break (loss of a clearing mandate, punitive fee/transaction tax, forced contract portability) — that regulatory break, not a soft quarter, is the only condition under which I would change my mind, and June 2026's $218.31 low-to-October recovery is this year's own example of a buyable CME drawdown.
- **Event scenario:** beat-and-raise equivalent (small beat with firm volume/RPC commentary) +3% to +5% / in-line +0% to +1% / miss −3% to −5% — anchored on the last four reactions of +5%, +0%, +1%, +0%, a screen average absolute move of 1.6%, and an indicative expected move of 3.7% that already prices more than double the typical reaction.
- **Top risk:** RPC / mix disappointment despite record ADV — if mix shifted to lower-rate products or incentives rose, CME can miss on revenue while volume headlines look perfect, compounded by any commentary that September's 31.8M ADV was the peak.
- **Conviction (1-10):** 7

**2. Plan B.

**3. PM** — Genuine quality on my lens: gross margin expanding to 68.4% and operating margin 40.5% in Q2, smoke-free at ~42% of revenue, FDA marketing and Modified Risk authorisations for IQOS and 20 authorised ZYN variants that rivals cannot copy quickly, and a dividend raised +8.8% to $1.60/quarter — no customer can in-source regulated nicotine and AI is irrelevant as a substitute. It ranks only third because the fallback is bought at the worst price in the batch: priced-in 8/10, new 52-week highs on Oct 15 after a $199.29 screen, trailing P/E 28.6–28.7x versus a ~21.3–21.9x five-year average, guide-risk 5/10 with the September FY raise ($8.35–$8.50) delivered for currency only after a July operating trim, and ZYN US shipments up just +2% YoY in Q2; reactions were +3%, +7%, −1% and −4% on a beat a year ago against a ~2.7% expected move versus 5.9% average absolute move, so rough moves are +2% to +4% on a clean beat-and-intact-guide, −1% to −3% in line, −5% to −8% on an ex-currency trim. Plan B.
