### 8. Risks specific to THIS print — including the earnings-quality flag
- **EARNINGS-QUALITY FLAG — RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN: VERDICT — mostly event-timing, not yet a value trap, but the cash-conversion deterioration is real and Q1 collection is the test.**
  - The numbers (Fox Q4 FY2026 release / 10-K balance sheet, 30 Jun 2026): receivables, net **$3,455m vs $2,472m a year earlier (+39.8%)**, while FY2026 revenue rose only **+5.1%** ($17,126m vs $16,300m). On a full-year basis, receivables massively outran revenue — that is exactly the flag.
  - The benign explanation, stated by the company itself: Q4 FY2026 free cash flow was $726m, "impacted by the timing of working capital related to World Cup rights payments landing in fiscal 2026 while advertising receivables will be collected in fiscal 2027" (AlphaSense Q4 summary). The receivables build is concentrated in Q4: $2,948m at 31 Mar 2026 → $3,455m at 30 Jun 2026 (+$507m in the World Cup quarter), on Q4 revenue of $4.21bn (+28% YoY, advertising +78%). A quarter in which advertising surges 78% will mechanically balloon receivables at quarter-end; collection, not recognition, is the question.
  - Why it is not fully benign: full-year operating cash flow fell to **$1.97bn from $3.32bn** and free cash flow to **$1.47bn from $2.99bn** (TipRanks financials) while adjusted EPS rose 13% — cash did lag earnings in FY2026, and part of that is the same World Cup timing (rights paid in FY2026, ads collected in FY2027). If Q1 FY2027 shows receivables staying near $3.4bn+ while revenue normalises, or operating cash flow again lagging, the flag upgrades from "timing" to "earnings ahead of cash". If receivables fall back toward ~$2.9bn on collection, the flag clears.
  - **One-line flag verdict: World Cup timing explains the spike, but FY2026 cash conversion fell by half, so treat Q1 receivables/cash flow — not EPS — as the print's real quality test.**
