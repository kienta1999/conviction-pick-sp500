### 7. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — score 8/10
- Howmet makes flight-critical, certified engine parts, fasteners and titanium structures. Certification,
  metallurgy know-how, and OEM qualification (Boeing/Airbus/GE/Pratt) take years; an airline or OEM
  cannot in-source a turbine blade overnight, and an AI-native competitor cannot route around a
  physical, safety-certified forging. Spares (high-margin, recurring, engine-installed-base driven)
  are the moat's compounding leg — spares demand grew through Q2 and GE's Q3 confirms the aftermarket
  is still the profit pool. Substitutes: additive manufacturing and alternative suppliers (e.g. PCC,
  ATI, TransDigm in adjacent niches) nibble at the edges, and a prolonged air-travel downturn would
  hit volumes, but none replicates the installed-base + certification stack.
- **If it gaps down 15% and I am stuck holding it for 18 months: opportunity, not disaster** — provided
  the gap is a guide/multiple event and not a certification or OEM-production halt. The business
  (backlog, spares, gas-turbine demand) does not disappear in 18 months.
