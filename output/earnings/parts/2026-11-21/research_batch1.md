### 7. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — score 8/10
- AutoCAD/Revit file formats and AECO workflows are the industry standard: architects, engineers and contractors exchange DWG/RVT files; switching costs are measured in retraining whole firms and breaking project data. Subscription model (~$2.01bn of the $2.13bn quarter is subscription revenue), EBA enterprise agreements, and a 100–110% net revenue retention band (top of band in Q2) show pricing power — the company is *removing* multi-year discounts and still retaining customers. MaintainX extends the platform from design/make into operate/maintain — the "design-make-operate" story.
- Limits: this is exactly the workflow AI-native entrants target — generative design, automated drafting, and cheaper cloud-native CAD (Figma-style disruption is the standing fear; AInvest's Q4 note flags "whether AI will eventually reshape the core design workflow"). Autodesk's answer is its own agentic AI built on proprietary project data — credible, but monetisation is unproven and the MaintainX deal ($3.6bn, debt-funded, ~18x forward revenue per Aardvark) adds integration and leverage risk at the moment the sales org is being reorganised.
- **If it gaps down 15% and I'm stuck 18 months: an opportunity, not a disaster** — at ~$208 that would be ~16.5x FY27 non-GAAP EPS for a subscription software leader growing revenue mid-teens with 39–41% margins and a $2.7bn+ FCF guide. The disaster case needs billings to keep decelerating *and* AI to commoditise design — a two-factor bear case, not a one-print miss.
