# SINGLE-PICK BALLOT — Panelist D (Contrarian / risk skeptic)

- Top pick: CME
- Runner-up: BNY
- Thesis: Most of this field is a beat-but-sell trap — banks whose beats stopped paying (JPM, MS), a name priced at its high for a margin inflection that has not appeared (FAST), and guides that were already raised into the print (PM, META, BAC). CME is the only name where the quarter is substantially pre-disclosed, there is no numeric EPS/revenue guide to cut, and the forward bar asks for flat continuation rather than acceleration. The genuine mis-modelling risk is not volume — it is rate per contract: the market may be converting record ADV into revenue at a rate Q2 already disproved, so the edge is real but thin and conviction is capped accordingly. This is a least-bad pick, not a high-conviction one.
- Key evidence from the record: CME's own 2026-10-02 release reported record Q3 ADV of 29.4M contracts, +16% YoY, with record September ADV of 31.8M, +22% — the volume half of this print is already public and at records before the stock has to be held through it.
- Priced-in risk (THE TRAP): medium — price is mid-range (-15.8% vs the $329.16 52-week high, +0.6% in a month, forward P/E 21.3 inside its normal low-20s band, Hold consensus with only ~+3–5% target upside, not crowded), but the priced move is only ~1.6% and the beats themselves are tiny (four straight, averaging ~+1.4%), so there is very little compensation if the one unknown — RPC/mix — disappoints.
- The guide: CME gives no EPS or revenue guidance at all; it guides only the frame (annual expense ranges, capex, effective tax rate, non-transaction lines), and that frame has been reaffirmed with no material adverse revision found — there is structurally nothing to guide down, which is why research scores its guide-risk 2/10, the lowest in the field. The forward number the market carries is Q4 2026 EPS of $2.98 (FY26 $12.27–12.28, FY27 ~$12.82–12.84), and the run-rate supports it on paper: H1 actual EPS of $6.35 plus the Q3 bar of ~$2.96 implies a Q4 of ~$2.97, i.e. the $2.98 is flat continuation of the Q3 bar ($2.95–2.97), not an acceleration. The "beat but guided soft" case for CME is therefore not a guidance cut — it is a beat on volume that arrives with soft conversion and soft forward colour: mix has shifted toward micro/lower-RPC products (Micro E-mini Nasdaq-100 +73% in September), and Q2 already showed volume does not convert 1:1 (near-record ADV of 29.8M produced revenue of only $1.71B, +0.8% YoY, against Q1's $1.88B). If Q3 prints an EPS beat but RPC is weak, expense/tax framing ticks up, or October ADV commentary sounds calm, the Q4 $2.98 stops being run-rate-supported — because it was never supported by a revenue run-rate, only by a volume run-rate — and the stock can fall on a beat, plausibly to the flat-to-negative end of its recent reaction range. That is the guide-down case I own: not a cut that management announces, but a forward EPS number the market is carrying on a conversion assumption the last quarter already falsified once.
- Plan A or Plan B: Plan A — it is the batch's only Plan A in the research, because the bar is pre-disclosed, guide-risk is structurally minimal, and the flat Q4 bar needs no heroics.
- Fallback if the print disappoints: Yes, hold 12–18 months — moat is scored 9.5/10 (liquidity network and clearing natural monopoly, ~65% operating margin, dividend persists), and a disappointment here would most likely reflect a low-volatility/RPC-mix regime, a macro condition, rather than franchise damage; the dossier explicitly calls a -15% gap an opportunity.
- Event scenario: beat with cooperative RPC ~+3% to +5% (Q2's +5.0% is the realised upside tail) / in-line ~0% to +1% (three of the last four reactions were +0.5%, +0.5% and -0.3%) / revenue or RPC miss ~-2% to -4%, worse only if October volume commentary also sours.
- Top risk: RPC/mix — record ADV converting into mediocre revenue, exactly as in Q2, leaving a small EPS beat with nothing underneath it.
- Conviction (1-10): 6

Plan B.
