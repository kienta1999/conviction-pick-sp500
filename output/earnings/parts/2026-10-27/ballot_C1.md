# Panelist C — Quality / moat & irreplaceability — Earnings ballot, 2026-10-27

## SINGLE-PICK BALLOT

- Top pick: GOOGL
- Runner-up: CBOE
- Thesis (3-5 sentences): GOOGL is the only name in the 15 that combines irreplaceability 9/10 with a genuinely de-rated price — it owns every layer of the AI stack (Search distribution, YouTube, Android, TPUs, frontier models, Cloud) and no customer can in-source it and no AI-native rival can route around its distribution. The operating business is accelerating, not coasting: revenue growth has risen four straight quarters to +24% YoY, Cloud grew +82% in Q2 with a $514B backlog, and group operating margin was 34.0% despite +42% D&A growth. Unlike the other 9/10-moat names (CBOE, LLY), it is not priced for perfection — it sits −21.6% below its 52-week high with defensive, put-heavy positioning. My lens asks which name I would be happy to be stuck holding after a bad print; a capex-driven gap in GOOGL leaves the franchise, the backlog and the margins untouched, which is exactly the fallback I want.
- Key evidence from the record:
  - Irreplaceability 9/10 — highest in batch 1 by a wide margin; the only player that owns every AI layer, with paid/default Search distribution on iOS and Android/Chrome.
  - Revenue $102.3B → $113.8B → $109.9B → $119.8B, YoY growth accelerating +16% → +18% → +22% → +24%; Cloud +82% in Q2, Cloud operating income tripling to $8.8B, backlog $514B with >50% converting within 24 months.
  - Normalised earnings quality must be judged correctly: Q2 GAAP EPS $9.11 included ~$6.26/share of unrealised equity gains (~$98–99B); normalised EPS was ~$2.85, a slight miss — so I am underwriting revenue, Cloud, operating income ($40.8B, +30%) and backlog, not a fake GAAP beat streak.
  - Price already pays part of the toll: −21.6% below the $408.61 high, ~20.8× forward on normalised earnings, RSI ~31, and the company itself sold equity at $355.20 in June — above today's $320.42 screen price.
- Priced-in risk (THE TRAP): low — priced-in scored 4/10 in the dossier; the stock has de-rated, positioning is hedged rather than crowded-long, and options already charge ±7.58% versus a 4.4% recent realised average. The trap is not the multiple, it is a single line: a third capex raise.
- The guide: Alphabet guides no revenue or EPS — the market carries Q4 EPS ~$3.05 and, operatively, the FY26 capex guide of $195–205B plus the steer that 2027 capex will "increase significantly." Run-rate supports the demand side (Cloud +82%, $514B backlog) but not the spending pace or any FCF recovery: Q2 capex of $44.9B annualises to ~$180B, below the guide floor, so hitting the guide requires H2 capex stepping up to roughly $55B+/quarter, and management itself says FCF "will remain under pressure" (Q2 FCF was −$5.9B). The forward number I actually need is capex held at $195–205B with no shocking quantification of 2027.
- Plan B: Plan B — the fallback is A-grade (irreplaceability 9), but Plan A requires a low-variance event and a binary capex reveal two hours after the Fed decision, on the same evening Microsoft prints, is the definition of Plan B.
- Fallback if the print disappoints: hold 12-18 months? Yes, unambiguously — a −15% gap to ~$272 would be ~17× normalised forward earnings for a business growing revenue 24% at 34% operating margins with a contracted $514B backlog; the stuck scenario requires the capex to earn no return at all, which the backlog conversion schedule already contradicts. This is the strongest fallback in the field.
- Event scenario: beat-and-raise (revenue/Cloud beat with capex held at $195–205B) +6% to +9% / in-line (solid print, capex merely reaffirmed, 2027 steer unchanged) −2% to +3% / miss (a third capex raise, a quantified-shocking 2027 number, or Cloud decelerating on capacity rationing) −8% to −12%.
- Top risk: Capex escalation destroying returns on capital — a third raise on top of negative FCF and the June $80B equity raise (share count already +0.9% YoY, dilution replacing buyback shrink) would turn a quality compounder into a capital sink, and it is the only path that has actually moved this stock for two quarters.
- Conviction (1-10): 8

### Top 3 — detail

**1. GOOGL.** The most compelling record point is the combination, not any single line: irreplaceability 9/10, revenue growth accelerating to +24%, Cloud +82% with a $514B backlog, and a price −21.6% off its high at ~20.8× normalised forward earnings — quality this scarce almost never comes de-rated into its print. Reaction record is genuinely two-sided and capex-driven (+10% in Q1, −7.1% in Q2 when the capex guide rose $15B at the midpoint and FCF printed −$5.9B), and guide risk is the highest in its batch (8/10) because the only guidance is capex, which has only ever moved up. Priced-in risk is low (4/10; options ±7.58% vs 4.4% realised); rough moves: beat with capex held +6% to +9% / in-line −2% to +3% / capex-raise miss −8% to −12%. Plan B.

**2. CBOE.** On pure quality this is arguably #1: a licensed, network-effect monopoly in SPX/VIX/0DTE that literally cannot be replicated by a competitor or by AI, GAAP operating margin 65.1%, adjusted EBITDA margin 72.2%, expenses up only ~3% against +25% revenue — and Q3 is the most knowable quarter in the field, with volumes pre-reported on 2026-10-05 (quarterly SPX ADV 4.9M, record quarterly SPX 0DTE ADV 3.2M) and consensus revenue of $702–709M set *below* Q2's actual $731.6M. Reaction record is the calmest in the 15 — four straight beats, four positive reactions (+5%, +9%, +3%, +4%), worst only −1.7% — and guide risk is low (4/10; FY organic growth was just raised to mid-to-high teens and Q4 consensus EPS $3.42 is below Q1/Q2 actuals of $3.70/$3.56). What keeps it off the top spot on my lens is priced-in risk 9/10: +31.3% in 21 days, straight through the ~$306–318 consensus target, means a good print may have no marginal buyer; rough moves: beat +2% to +5% / in-line −3% to −6% on multiple compression / miss (RPC capture slippage — Q3 projected total-options capture $0.307 vs $0.317 in June) −7% to −10%, with any −15% gap an emphatic 18-month opportunity. Plan B.

**3. LLY.** The franchise evidence is elite: tirzepatide is the world's best-selling drug franchise, gross margin 85.8–86.3%, Q2 volume +60% overwhelming realised price of −13%, Mounjaro $9.94B (+91%) and Zepbound $4.93B (+46%), plus the Foundayo oral and retatrutide pipeline that only Novo can even attempt to answer — irreplaceability 9/10. Reaction record is the best in batch 3 (last4 +5%, +10%, +10%, +4%, five straight beats, two FY raises already in 2026), and the FY26 revenue guide of $85–87B is already covered by the H1 run-rate of ~$42.8B with zero H2 growth required. But priced-in risk is 9/10 — an all-time intraday high of $1,311.21 on 2026-10-20, consensus FY revenue of $88.68–88.94B sitting *above* the guide top, and guide risk 5/10 precisely because a mere reaffirmation (no third raise) reads as a miss, while Novo pricing and Medicare Bridge headlines are live routes around the moat's pricing, if not its volumes; rough moves: beat-and-third-raise +7% to +10% / beat-but-no-raise −4% to −7% / volume-or-price miss −10% to −14%. Plan B.
