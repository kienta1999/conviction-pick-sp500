# Panelist C (Quality / moat & irreplaceability) — ballot 2026-10-12

**Lens applied:** which of these would I be happy to be stuck holding after a bad print? Weighted margins, returns on capital, balance-sheet durability, and above all irreplaceability — can customers in-source it, or can AI-native competition route around it? Every Plan defaults to B per the dossier's disqualifier note (all 13 carry Plan B).

## PART 1 — Single-pick ballot

- **Top pick:** CME
- **Runner-up:** BNY

- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** CME is the purest toll booth in the set: a 65% operating-margin quasi-monopoly on US derivatives clearing whose network effects make it structurally un-in-sourceable — no AI-native entrant clears a listed futures contract. Roughly 70% of the quarter is already pre-disclosed (Q3 ADV +16%, September a record +22%), so the revenue side has hard floors rather than hope. The market isn't crowded in: Hold consensus, −11% off highs, priced-in only 5 — the tape isn't positioned for the record quarter that is already half-published. Beat_up is a perfect 1.000 over four quarters with a tiny 1.6% average absolute move, meaning the reaction record is boring in the good way: the market reliably under-prices the volume print. There is no forward guide to disappoint, which removes the single biggest tail risk haunting this tape (sell-the-peak / held-guide selloffs at MS, BNY, ISRG).

- **Key evidence from the record:** perfect 4/4 beat streak (beat_up 1.000, the only 1.000 in the dossier) at 65% operating margins — the highest margin of any name in the set — with Q3 ADV +16% y/y already disclosed and a September ADV record of +22%.

- **Priced-in risk (THE TRAP):** medium. The September record is public, so the tape knows volume was strong; priced-in scores 5, not 3. Against that: Hold consensus (no herd), −11.1% off the high (not extended), and a history of the market selling even strong prints (Q1) — meaning positioning is sober, not euphoric. The trap isn't over-positioning; it's the tape deciding records are "expected."

- **The guide:** there is no forward guide — that's the feature. The bar is $2.96–2.97 EPS (+10.5% y/y), and with ADV +16% for the full quarter already disclosed, the revenue run-rate arithmetically supports it: exchange revenue is volume × rate card, and the volume side is already printed.

- **Plan A or Plan B:** **Plan B** — wait for the number. The default holds: even a monopoly doesn't need to be held through an event when the reaction record (avgabs 1.6%) offers almost no event upside to capture. Plan A would carry the burden of proof and nothing here meets it.

- **Fallback if the print disappoints:** Yes — happily hold 12–18 months. This is the centerpiece of my lens and CME is its best answer in the set. The moat: CME's clearinghouse and its network-effect liquidity pool are a natural monopoly — no customer can "in-source" futures clearing, and AI-native competition cannot route around a listed, cleared, regulated derivatives market; AI actually *helps* (more hedging flows, more volatility, more volume). The economics: 65% operating margins on a variable-cost-light model, returns on capital among the best in the S&P, and a dividend-funded balance sheet. The duration of the trade: the current regime — Fed hiking to 3.75–4.00%, 10y at ~5.3%, geopolitical noise — is a volatility regime, and volatility is CME's secular revenue driver; a bad print would be a sentiment event, not a franchise event. I would own this monopoly through 18 months of a miss far more comfortably than any bank franchise or growth name on this list.

- **Event scenario:** beat-and-clean-print (no guide to "raise") ~+3% to +4%; in-line ~flat to +1% (volume already known, EPS in-line should be absorbed); miss ~−4% to −5% (the market would read it as rate-card/mix weakness). Ranges anchored to the dossier's reaction denominator (avgabs 1.6%) scaled for a modest surprise.

- **Top risk:** rate-card/mix compression — ADV was record-strong but if it came in low-margin product (micro contracts, off-peak), revenue misses despite the volume beat, and the market reads "volume without monetization."

- **Conviction (1-10):** 7.5
