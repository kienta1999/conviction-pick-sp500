# Ballot — Lens C (quality / moat & irreplaceability) — earnings 2026-09-21

**Analyst:** Agent C — the quality/moat & irreplaceability investor. Weighting: margins, returns on capital, balance sheet, and above all **irreplaceability** — rejecting names customers could in-source or that a substitute (including AI-native competition) could route around. AI-disruption exposure is a first-class filter. The question: which of these would I be **happy** to be stuck holding after a bad print? All figures grounded in `research_dossier.md` / `triage.md`; nothing invented.

**Standing note:** this lens does not hunt the best beat probability or the cheapest setup — it nominates the name whose business you'd most want to *own through* a bad print: highest irreplaceability, balance-sheet quality, and AI-resistance. Every researcher returned Plan B; implied moves exceed realized averages wherever found. This ballot follows that verdict: the fallback is the thesis, but a priced-in print is still a priced-in print. No name in this field earns Plan A from this lens either.

## SINGLE-PICK BALLOT

- **Top pick:** BNY
- **Runner-up:** CTAS
- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** BNY Mellon is the name I'd most want to be left holding if the trade goes wrong: the world's largest custodian ($59.3T in AUC/A) running the market plumbing its institutional clients cannot rip out — custody, clearing, settlement, a regulatory charter — with pre-tax margins near 40% (39.8% in Q2 2026) and a CET1 ratio of 11.9%, a fortress by any definition. AI is an efficiency tool here (100+ deployed solutions, the Google Cloud Eliza partnership), not a substitute for trusted clearing infrastructure — nobody replaces a custodian with a chatbot. The print record is intact (4/4 beats, 87.5% of measured beats rewarded, the smallest average absolute move in the researched field at 3.01%), and the forward message — not the beat — is what moves the stock, which is exactly the property you want in a fallback: boring, durable, compounding. That isn't all in the price: the bar was raised in Q2 (FY26 revenue growth 10–11%, NII +12–13%), but the bar is a *growth-rate* guide from a soft guider, not a number it must hit to the penny, and nothing about the custody oligopoly breaks if a quarter prints merely in-line.
- **Key evidence from the record:** Q2 2026 (2026-07-15): adjusted EPS $2.45–2.46 vs $2.16–2.20 consensus (+12–14%), revenue $5.698B vs ~$5.35B (+13%), NII +20%, pre-tax margin 39.8% — on which the company *raised* FY26 guidance to revenue growth 10–11% and NII +12–13% with ~400 bps of operating leverage, and the stock rose +5%. Behind it: eight consecutive quarters of positive operating leverage through Q4 2025 and AUM/AUC compounding ($57.8T → $59.3T).
- **Priced-in risk (THE TRAP):** Medium-high. +33.8% over six months and 6.7% below the 52-week high says expectations are elevated, and the Q2 FY26 raise pulled the good news forward — a merely in-line Q3 reads as a guide-cut risk. But the realized-move denominator is the friendliest in the field (3.01% average absolute; worst −2.03%), so being wrong costs less here than on any other name, and the Q3 2025 template (a +8.5% beat sold −2% on sustainability worries) is a known, bounded risk, not a mystery.
- **The guide:** BNY is a soft guider: no quarterly EPS/revenue guide — it guides revenue-growth and NII-growth *rates* and medium-term margin targets (pre-tax margin 38%, ROTCE 28%, raised in Q4 2025). The market carries FY26 consensus EPS ~$9.26 against the July-raised outlook (revenue growth 10–11%, NII +12–13%, expenses +6–7%). Run-rate check: revenue grew +13% in both Q1 and Q2 2026 with NII +20% in Q2 and AUC/A compounding — the run-rate supports the raised outlook, and a raise to "upper end" territory is the only way management can surprise; the base case is a hold. The loss case is "beat but guided soft": a Q3 beat followed by language that walks back the 10–11% revenue-growth frame gets sold — the Q3 2025 precedent (beat, sold on sustainability questions) names it explicitly. This stock sells off on the *forward message*, not on the EPS line.
- **Plan A or Plan B**, with one sentence of justification: **Plan B** — the date is unconfirmed (ESTIMATED ~2026-10-15, no IR announcement, a doctrine disqualifier), the ticker-specific implied move was not found, and the bar was just raised in July, so there is no burden-of-proof case for taking the gap; enter only after a beat with a held-or-raised forward message and a positive first session.
- **Fallback if the print disappoints:** Happily — yes, 12–18 months without hesitation: custody oligopoly with $59.3T AUC/A is the deepest economic moat in the field, CET1 11.9% is a fortress balance sheet, pre-tax margins near 40% leave enormous cushion, and AI cannot displace market plumbing — a gap-down on guide tone would be an entry on a compounding franchise, not a broken thesis. This is the single strongest "stuck holding it" name in the 15.
- **Event scenario:** Beat + raised/held forward message: **+3% to +6%** (the Q2 template ran +5.08% on a raise; moves here are small by construction). In-line beat with merely reaffirmed growth frame: **−1% to −3%** (the market already demonstrated it sells sustainability doubts). Miss or a walk-back of the 10–11% frame: **−4% to −6%** (worst measured −2.03%, but a guide walk-back is the one way this name moves).
- **Top risk:** The forward message weakens: management softens the 10–11% revenue-growth or +12–13% NII frame (NII is Fed-sensitive), or AUM/AUC net outflows accelerate — in which case the moat is intact but the print trades like Q3 2025 (beat, sold) and the position becomes the 18-month hold immediately. Secondary: the date itself is estimated and unconfirmed.
- **Conviction (1-10):** 7
