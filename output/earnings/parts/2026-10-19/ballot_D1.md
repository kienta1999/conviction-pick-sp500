## SINGLE-PICK BALLOT

- **Top pick: CAT**
- **Runner-up: CINF**
- **Thesis (3–5 sentences):** CAT is the one name in this field where the market is mis-modelling pessimism, not optimism: the stock has been sold −31.3% off its 52-week high and −8.5% in 21 days on an AI-power factor unwind while estimates were being *raised*, and the Q3 bar of $7.09 sits ~13% *below* the $8.17 CAT actually delivered in Q2 — seasonality and the tariff bill are already in the number. The $72B backlog (+92% YoY, 59% deliverable within 12 months) mechanically covers the forward revenue number, so the beat does not require a demand surprise, only demand not collapsing. That asymmetry is not in the price: forward P/E 22.4× against a $951.11 mean target (+30%), the lowest priced-in score in the field (4/10), and the smallest event tail (avg abs 5.04%, worst −4.6%). Everywhere else in this field the trap runs the other way — consensus above management's own guide (PM, SNDK, STX, FFIV), a record price on zero revisions (V), a negative average reaction (META), or an undisclosed nation-state breach (FFIV) — which is why CAT is the pick and the rest of the field is a pass.
- **Key evidence from the record:** Q3 consensus EPS $7.09 (Longbridge, 2026-10-17) vs Q2 delivered adjusted EPS $8.17 — the bar is set below the demonstrated run-rate, backed by a $72B backlog; CAT is 4/4 beats with an *improving* surprise trend (+16.1) and all four of its last reactions positive (+6/+10/+3/+12).
- **Priced-in risk (THE TRAP): low** — the only low in the field. The stock de-rated into the print (−31.3% off the high, ret_21d −8.5%) on factor selling, not company news, while the bar rose modestly in October. The residual trap is not valuation; it is margin optics (see The guide).
- **The guide:** The forward number the market is carrying is **FY26 consensus EPS $27.42 (MarketBeat), with Q4 2026 standalone at ~$6.88 (Zacks) and FY27 at ~$32.44** — CAT itself gives no EPS guide, only outlook language (mid-to-high-teens FY revenue growth on FY25's $67.6B, i.e. ~$77–79B; adjusted operating margin near the bottom of its target range *excluding* IEEPA recoveries; FY tariff cost ~$2.2B). **Run-rate verdict: the revenue leg is SUPPORTED, the EPS leg is only CONDITIONALLY supported — and the condition is the guide-down case nobody is leading with.** Q2's $20.54B revenue and the $20.7B Q3 bar are a repeat, not a stretch, and the backlog covers them. But Q2's 21.9% adjusted operating margin was flattered by **$392M of IEEPA tariff recoveries that do not repeat in Q3**, while Q3 carries the full **~$600M guided tariff cost** — so the clean, ex-recovery margin run-rate is materially below the Q2 headline, and $27.42 (and especially FY27's $32.44, which implies ~+18% EPS growth) holds only if the Q3 tariff bill lands at ~$600M and the FY tariff estimate stays at ~$2.2B. If the tariff line overshoots or the FY tariff guide rises, the outlook language gets cut to/below the bottom of the range and both forward numbers come down — that, not demand, is how this print disappoints.
- **Plan B** — even the field's best setup does not earn Plan A from a skeptic, because the one number that decides the margin leg (actual Q3 tariff cost vs the ~$600M guide, with no IEEPA credit this quarter) is unknowable before the print; enter only after a confirmed beat with the outlook language and the ~$2.2B FY tariff estimate held.
- **Fallback if the print disappoints: Yes, hold 12–18 months.** A tariff-driven miss leaves the $72B backlog, the parts-and-services annuity on the installed base, and order visibility into 2029–2030 intact; the holder owns backlog conversion, not a broken story. The exception that would change this answer is order cancellations / a falling backlog — that would be a demand break, not a margin miss.
- **Event scenario:** beat-and-raise (beat + outlook/tariff language held or raised) **+5% to +8%** / in-line **+1% to +3%** / miss (tariff overshoot or outlook trim) **−4% to −6%** — anchored on avg abs 5.04%, worst-ever −4.6%, and a last-four record of +6/+10/+3/+12.
- **Top risk:** The tariff line — Q3 tariff cost above the ~$600M guide, or a raised FY tariff estimate, compressing the ex-recovery margin that management already pre-softened to "near the bottom of the range," leaving no rhetorical room.
- **Conviction (1–10): 6**
