### 5. THE GUIDE

(a) **Does it guide?** Yes, granularly for the full year and the next quarter: total revenue growth, Management Solutions growth, PEO and Insurance Solutions growth, interest on funds held for clients, adjusted operating margin, effective tax rate, adjusted diluted EPS growth. Q2 FY2027: revenue growth ~4%, adjusted operating margin ~40%.

(b) **Forward bar.** Q2 FY2027 consensus: EPS \$1.27–\$1.30, revenue ~\$1.6–\$1.65bn (above). FY2027 consensus: EPS \$5.96 (MarketBeat / bannercoop summary), +8.2% vs FY2026's \$5.51 — i.e. the Street is at the *top* of management's +7–9% adjusted EPS growth guide. FY2027 revenue consensus is ~\$6.85bn at +5–6% on FY2026's \$6.512bn. The forward bar is therefore: Q2 at ~4% (already guided) and the full year at the top of the guided range.

(c) **Does the run-rate support the forward number?** No, not comfortably. Q1 revenue +6% with Management Solutions +4% and a Q2 guide of ~+4% implies H1 at ~+5% — the *bottom* of the +5–6% FY range, requiring H2 acceleration to reach the midpoint, let alone the top where consensus EPS sits. Management Solutions at +4% vs its +5–6% FY guide requires the same H2 acceleration, while Schrader himself flagged it could trend to the *low* end. The FY guide is supportable only if PEO strength (+12%, raised to +7–8%) offsets core softness and if the Q2 margin trough (~40% vs 42% in Q1 and ~44% FY) reverses in H2. That is a hope, not a run-rate.

(d) **Guide history, last 8 quarters (verifiable).** Q3 FY2026 (Mar 2026): FY2026 reaffirmed. Q4 FY2026 (Jun 2026): FY2027 initiated at +5–6% revenue / +7–9% EPS. Q1 FY2027 (Sep 2026): FY2027 *reaffirmed* — total revenue, Management Solutions, adjusted margin and adjusted EPS all "No change"; only PEO (+7–8%, from +6–7%) and interest on funds (\$200–\$210m, from \$195–\$205m) were raised. The market's verdict on that reaffirmation was −8.8% in a day. A serial reaffer is a serial disappointment in this tape: each reaffirmation is priced as a de facto cut because the Street sits at the top of the range.

(e) **Already raised between quarters?** No — the PEO/interest raise happened *on* the September print, not between prints. Nothing has been raised since. ADP, the large-cap peer, *did* raise on 2026-10-28 (see below), which makes Paychex's reaffirmation look worse by comparison, not better.

**Rest of the quarter — peer read:** ADP Q1 FY2027, reported 2026-10-28 (BMO): revenue \$5.49bn (+6%, beat \$5.47bn), adjusted EPS \$2.82–\$2.83 vs ~\$2.78 (+1.8%), adjusted EBIT margin +130 bp to 26.8%, US pays-per-control +2% (above expectations), record Q1 new-business bookings — and ADP **raised** FY2027 guidance to ~6% revenue (from 5–6%) and +10–12% adjusted EPS (from +9–11%). ADP's options implied ±5.2% that week vs a ±2.2% historical average, and the stock fell −2.6% despite the raise. The peer read is mixed-to-negative for Paychex: the category is growing ~6% and the leader is raising, while Paychex is guiding Q2 to ~4% and reaffirming — Paychex is lagging its own peer on the guide, which is the number that matters.

### 7. Moat / AI-irreplaceability — score 6/10

Paychex serves ~800k+ SMB clients with payroll, HR, benefits and PEO; switching payroll providers is painful (tax filings, compliance, employee data), retention is high, and the Paycor acquisition (April 2025) added upmarket HCM depth. But the moat is *process*, not technology: ADP is larger and just raised guidance, Paycom/Workday attack from above, and AI-native payroll/HR tools (including Paychex's own WISE Hire agentic recruiting) lower the cost of switching over time by automating migration. The PEO business is genuinely sticky; the core Management Solutions business is a price-and-service grind. If it gaps down 15% and I hold 18 months, that is an opportunity *if* the dividend (~4% yield at recent prices) and the PEO engine hold — a disaster only if SMB employment rolls over and Management Solutions growth goes to low-single-digits permanently. Score 6.

### 8. Risks specific to THIS print

The Q2 guide is already ~4% revenue growth with ~40% adjusted operating margin — a margin trough that must be explained, not just reported. Management Solutions at +4% vs a +5–6% FY guide: a second consecutive +4% (or lower) forces a guide trim. Interest on funds held for clients (\$200–\$210m FY guide) is rate-sensitive; the September Fed cut is already in the guide, but further cuts are not. Free cash flow in Q1 was only \$357.4m, 87% of reported net income and down significantly YoY (Motley Fool, 2026-09-23) — cash conversion is weakening alongside the receivables build (see flag). SMB employment is the macro swing factor: ADP's pays-per-control +2% is a modest positive read, but Paychex's SMB mix is more cyclically exposed than ADP's enterprise mix.

### Earnings-quality flag — RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN

**Verdict: mostly timing noise around Paycor, but the cash-flow corroboration is weak — not a value trap on its own, not clean either.** Accounts receivable (net) rose to \$1,615.9m at 2026-08-31 from \$1,507.6m at 2026-05-31 (+7.2% in the quarter), and PEO unbilled receivables rose to \$680.2m from \$664.2m; total receivables (Zacks) \$2,335m at 2026-08-31 vs \$2,208m at 2026-05-31 and \$2,003m at 2025-08-31 (+16.6% YoY vs revenue +5.9% YoY in Q1). The 10-Q cash-flow statement shows a \$73.3m drag from accounts receivable and PEO unbilled receivables in the quarter. The benign read: Paychex's receivables are largely *pass-through* (PEO payroll, client funds, insurance premiums) and Paycor integration changed the billing mix — the 10-Q states no single customer was material. The non-benign read: Q1 free cash flow was only 87% of net income and down YoY, and receivables growing ~3x revenue growth for a full year is not a one-quarter timing quirk. If Q2 receivables grow in line with the ~4% revenue guide, the flag clears; if they outrun again while FCF stays below net income, earnings quality becomes the story of the print.
