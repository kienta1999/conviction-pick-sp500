## 5. Moat — MIXED: standard status and switching costs CONFIRMED; "duopoly" CONTRADICTED

**Confirmed — AutoCAD/Revit as standards, switching costs enormous:**
- Morningstar (Moat Ratings Guidebook, March 2026): *"We also maintained wide moat ratings on the broader engineering software companies: Autodesk, Bentley Systems, PTC, and Dassault Systemes… they benefit from proprietary data models, complex workflows, and high switching costs in their respective engineering domains."* Morningstar separately: *"We think Autodesk will remain the industry standard, as its switching costs and network effect continue to reinforce one another"*; *"We view Autodesk as the global industry standard computer-aided design (CAD) software… switching costs to be significant… In AutoCAD, we estimate that learning the basics takes roughly three to six months, with several years required for one to become fully adept."*
- DWG as de facto standard: multiple 2026 sources — DWG is "often regarded as the industry standard for technical drawings and blueprints" and "the default way a drawing gets stored and passed around" in AEC/manufacturing; AutoCAD is the most widely deployed CAD platform (~38–39% market share, ~242,683 companies per 6sense; AutoCAD + Revit + Inventor combined well over 50% of tracked CAD seats).
- Revit is the leading BIM platform (Revit ~4.8% of tracked CAD seats as a standalone product, but it anchors the AEC Collection that is Autodesk's largest segment).

**Contradicted — "Dassault is the only peer of similar scale (effectively a duopoly)":**
- The same Morningstar passage names **four** wide-moat engineering-software peers — Autodesk, **Bentley Systems, PTC, and Dassault Systèmes** — not two.
- Market-share data (Verified Market Research, Mar 2026, PLM & CAD ecosystem): Dassault 16.5%, Autodesk 14.2%, **Siemens 12.8%** — Siemens is the same order of magnitude. Jon Peddie Research (Oct 2026, 2D CAD): Autodesk holds the largest share, **Bentley second at ~9.7%**, and "roughly 62% of the market stays effectively locked inside a top group of direct-sales majors — Autodesk, Bentley, Dassault, and Siemens."
- **Verdict on §5:** the moat's *mechanism* (standards + switching costs) is well supported, but the market structure is an **oligopoly of 4–5 scaled vendors** (Autodesk, Dassault, Siemens, Bentley, PTC), not a duopoly. The dossier's 8/10 moat score is a judgment call and is not itself falsifiable, but its stated factual underpinning should be softened from "duopoly" to "oligopoly led by Autodesk, with Dassault the closest peer in mechanical CAD". This does not weaken the fallback case (hold 12–18 months) in any material way.
