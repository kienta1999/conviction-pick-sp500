- **Thesis (3-5 sentences):** KO is the name I would be happiest to be stuck holding if this print disappoints, and that fallback — not event upside — is what my lens is paid to weight. What is NOT in my favour is the entry: at $94.51, at the 52-week high and only +0.7% to the $95.13 mean target, the likely beat is largely already paid for, so I am choosing KO for capped downside and franchise permanence, not for a large beat-day payoff.
- **Key evidence from the record:** Irreplaceability 9/10 in the dossier — the widest moat in the set — backed by hard quality numbers: comparable operating margin 35.6% in Q2 2026, CFO/NI 1.14 with no earnings-quality flags, ~$12.4B of guided FY free cash flow, and 64 consecutive years of dividend increases.
- **Priced-in risk (THE TRAP):** high — dossier priced-in score 9/10. Price $94.51 is 0.0% from the 52-week high, +7.3% in 21 days, +32.6% YTD, at 26.8x forward earnings versus PepsiCo at roughly 16–17x trailing, with only +0.7% to the mean analyst target and a slightly negative Zacks Earnings ESP of −1.31%. An 8/8 beat streak and a twice-raised guide mean beat-and-raise is the expectation, not the surprise; the only direction with room is down.

My run-rate verdict on that FY27 $3.53 is SUPPORTED as a continuation, not an acceleration: H1 comparable EPS growth ran ahead of guide, and the raised FY guide already embeds the guided H2 fade — tougher comparisons, six fewer selling days in Q3, and a ~1% divestiture headwind partly offset by FX — so $3.53 requires H2 not to undershoot the fade, not a new leg of growth. The explicit beat-but-guided-soft loss case: KO beats the Q3 bar of $0.87–$0.88 but trims the twice-raised FY guide (organic ~5% / EPS +9–10%) or flags weaker unit-case volume beneath pricing-led growth, and the stock falls anyway because at the high the market was paying for beat-and-raise, not for a beat alone.
- **Plan B** — wait for the number: Plan A carries the burden of proof and fails it here, because at 0.0% from the high with +0.7% to target there is no valuation cushion, and KO's low realised volatility (worst reaction in the last eight only −2.1%) means a good print can be chased afterwards at limited cost while a soft-guide dip is the better entry.
- **Fallback if the print disappoints:** Yes — happily hold 12–18 months, and this is the decisive reason KO tops my ballot. A bad print here would almost certainly be a valuation event, not a franchise event: the downside case in the dossier is multiple compression from 26.8x, not impairment of the brand, the bottler system or the cash machine. With ~$12.4B of guided free cash flow, H1 2026 operating cash flow of $7.5B, expanding 35.6% comparable operating margins, a 64-year dividend-increase record and the lowest earnings volatility in the set, a −15% gap-down would be an opportunity to add to the most irreplaceable business on the list, not a thesis break. No customer can in-source the concentrate, no substitute has routed around the distribution system in decades, and AI is a marketing and productivity tool KO deploys itself rather than a competitor that bypasses physical shelf space. Of all eight names, KO is the one where being wrong on the quarter leaves me owning exactly what I wanted to own anyway.
- **Event scenario:** beat-and-raise ~+2% / in-line −1% to −2% / miss −3% to −5% (anchored on the ±3.7% options-implied move, the 2.9% average absolute reaction, last-four reactions of +2% to +5%, and a worst reaction of only −2.1%).
- **Top risk:** A trim to the twice-raised FY2026 guide — or weak unit-case volume confirming that growth is pricing-only — at a stock priced at its high for perfection; that turns a likely EPS beat into a down day.
