## Appendix A — Deterministic earnings-quality flags

### GS — LOW_CASH_CONVERSION + RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN
**Likely benign model mismatch, not standalone evidence of trouble.** For a trading bank, "operating cash flow" is dominated by changes in trading assets/liabilities, secured financing, deposits, and settlement balances — it is not a meaningful measure of earnings quality. Likewise "receivables" (reverse repos, securities borrowed, brokerage receivables) mechanically outrun revenue in active markets. Assess GS on ROE/ROTE, CET1, and net interest/trading revenue quality instead.

### MS — LOW_CASH_CONVERSION + RECEIVABLES_OUTRUN
**Same verdict as GS: likely benign.** Trading assets, securities financing, and client settlement balances make cash-conversion screens structurally unreliable for a broker-dealer. No inference about earnings quality should be drawn from the flag alone; monitor ROTCE, wealth fee-based flows, and credit provisions.

### BNY — LOW_CASH_CONVERSION
**Likely benign.** A custody bank's balance sheet is dominated by client deposits, central-bank placements, and securities-financing flows; operating cash flow is not where earnings quality shows up. Evaluate fee growth, NII trajectory, AUC/A, CET1, and ROTCE instead.

### BLK — LOW_CASH_CONVERSION
**Potentially benign, with a monitoring note.** For a fee-based asset manager, GAAP earnings and operating cash can decouple temporarily via accrued fees, performance-fee timing, acquisition-related items, and investment marks. Before dismissing, compare multi-quarter cash generation: the dossier confirms strong multi-quarter cash flow at APH but did not verify BLK's multi-quarter operating-cash trend — flag as "verify, not verdict."

### APH — no flag requested
For reference: APH's cash conversion is strong (Q3'25 operating cash flow $1.47B = 117% of net income; FCF $1.2B = 97% of net income; Q2'26 FCF $1.2B) — no quality concern evidenced.
